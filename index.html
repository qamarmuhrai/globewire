Latest · Europe · Ukraine War30 October 2026

Ukraine Strikes a Refinery in Russia's Samara Region, Ending a Two-Week Pause and Defying US Pressure

Ukraine hit the Novokuibyshevsk oil refinery in Russia's Samara region early on Friday, ending a two-week halt in its campaign against Russian refining capacity, the Wall Street Journal reported, with social-media footage showing columns of smoke rising over the plant. Kyiv had last struck a refinery on 15 October and any Russian oil infrastructure on 20 October, after weeks of pressure from President Trump, who has publicly blamed Ukraine's strikes for high global fuel prices — a link energy experts mostly attribute instead to the US–Iran war — and who earlier this month called for President Zelensky's removal. The pause also coincided with SpaceX limiting Ukraine's military use of Starlink on 14 October, and with European allies urging Kyiv to hold off until after Tuesday's US midterms. Defence Minister Evgeniy Khmara had told Zelensky the military judged it best to be careful given the delicate diplomacy; Zelensky said his forces had no order not to strike, and that Ukraine wants stronger protection for its own energy network, which Russia is systematically attacking ahead of winter, in return for restraint.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Oleshky Truce30 October 2026

Evacuations Begin From Occupied Oleshky as a US-Brokered Local Truce Holds Into Its Fourth Day

Evacuation and aid distribution began on Friday in Oleshky, the Russian-occupied town in Ukraine's southern Kherson region, under a localised ceasefire that officials on both sides describe as the longest pause in fighting anywhere on the front since 2022. Ukrainian negotiator David Arakhamia confirmed the operation had started, with 25 buses offering about 750 seats, plus ambulances, standing ready; Ukraine's ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets travelled into the town to meet residents alongside his Russian counterpart Yana Lantratova — the first such open visit by a senior Ukrainian official to occupied territory during the war. The White House credits First Lady Melania Trump with mediating the truce, which took effect on Tuesday and is due to run until Sunday evening. Oleshky, seized in 2022, has fallen from about 24,000 residents to roughly 2,000, without electricity, heat or running water, and former residents say some people had been reduced to hunting pigeons for food. Demining the approach routes was completed on Thursday, at a cost of two lives, and the Kremlin has stressed the local truce should not be read as a step toward a wider ceasefire.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Dnipro Strike29 October 2026

Thirteen Killed as a Russian Jet-Powered Drone Hits Two Trains at Dnipro's Main Station

At least 13 people were killed and at least ten injured when a Russian jet-powered drone struck a platform at Dnipro's main railway station on Thursday, where a suburban commuter train and a scheduled passenger service — train No. 866 to Zaporizhzhia — were standing, Ukrainian officials said. Dnipropetrovsk regional governor Oleksandr Ganzha confirmed the toll, and the state railway operator Ukrzaliznytsia said the drone hit as passengers waited to board despite an elevated threat warning already in force. The air force said four jet-powered drones were launched at the area, three of them shot down; four of the wounded, including an 11-year-old boy, were reported in serious condition. President Zelensky called the strike horrific and urged the European Union, the United States and the G7 to impose tougher sanctions on Moscow. Russian forces have repeatedly targeted Ukraine's rail network since the 2022 invasion, destroying hundreds of locomotives and killing railway workers, and separate strikes the same day killed two 15-year-old girls in Chernihiv and three civilians in Kremenchuk, according to Ukrainian authorities.

Latest · Europe · Russia — Press Freedom30 October 2026

Novaya Gazeta, Russia's Last Independent Newspaper, Announces Its Closure, Saying 'We Were Killed'

Novaya Gazeta, the independent newspaper founded in 1993 and long led by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Dmitry Muratov, announced on Friday that it is ceasing to exist, blaming a coordinated campaign by Russia's authorities and security services. Editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov, who was arrested on 15 October, said in a video statement that the paper will switch off its servers and leave its Moscow home of 30 years on 9 November. The paper had its media licence revoked in 2022, was branded a foreign agent in September, and has seen journalists questioned, detained and their homes searched; six of its journalists and staff have been murdered over its history, including Anna Politkovskaya in 2006. Muratov, who shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize for safeguarding freedom of expression, had to step down after being declared a foreign agent himself. An exiled sister title, Novaya Gazeta Europe, will continue publishing. More than 30 journalists from various outlets are currently imprisoned in Russia, where independent reporting has been driven almost entirely abroad or underground since the invasion of Ukraine.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen & Saudi Arabia30 October 2026

Houthis Deny Launching Any Drone Toward Mecca After the Saudi-Led Coalition Reports an Interception

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said on Friday morning that its air defences had intercepted and destroyed a drone launched by the Houthis over the Mecca region, home to Islam's holiest city, with spokesman Turki al-Malki describing the protection of the holy sites and pilgrims as a red line. The Houthi movement flatly denied the account: military spokesperson Yahya Saree dismissed it as false, said the group had carried out no operation toward Saudi territory on Friday, and insisted its cross-border attacks are aimed only at Saudi oil facilities and military bases. The Iran-aligned Houthis have repeatedly denied targeting Mecca or Medina whenever the coalition has reported interceptions near them, including a similar allegation in September that their leader Abdul-Malik al-Houthi called a fabrication. The exchange comes amid a renewed series of Houthi attacks on Saudi military sites, airports and energy infrastructure, and as regional diplomacy over Yemen and the Strait of Hormuz intensifies.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China Purge29 October 2026

China's Party Plenum Confirms a Record 33 Expulsions, Half of Them Military, as Xi Tightens Discipline

The fifth plenum of China's Communist Party Central Committee closed in Beijing on Thursday after confirming the expulsion of 33 senior officials — a record for a single meeting — including 16 from the military. Among those removed were former Central Military Commission vice-chairman Zhang Youxia, former joint staff chief Liu Zhenli, former Xinjiang party chief and Politburo member Ma Xingrui, diplomat Liu Jianchao and former securities regulator Yi Huiman. The purge leaves the seven-member Central Military Commission with just two members: President Xi Jinping and Zhang Shengmin, the anti-corruption chief who was the meeting's sole major promotion, elevated to the Politburo. Only 279 members attended the four-day session, the first plenum turnout below 300 in Xi's 14 years in power. The expulsions bring the total removed in Xi's third term to 51, and the party's watchdog says 680,000 people were disciplined in the first nine months of this year alone. The plenum also set the next party congress for the second half of 2027, when Xi is widely expected to seek a fourth term.

Xi Tells Steinmeier China and Germany Should Forge a New Model of Cooperation, as Trade Imbalance Shadows the Visit

Chinese President Xi Jinping held talks with German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier in Beijing on Friday, saying the two countries should lead in building a model of cooperation based on equality, mutual benefit and strategic stability, and urging Germany toward a rational, pragmatic and friendly China policy and the European Union to work with China in the same direction. He also raised the bilateral imbalance directly: China's trade surplus with Germany ran to almost €90 billion last year. Steinmeier's five-day visit ends on Saturday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Afghanistan29 October 2026

Afghan Opium Production Jumps Fivefold as Farmers Find Ways Around the Taliban's Poppy Ban, the UN Finds

Opium production in Afghanistan has risen fivefold in a year, the UN Office on Drugs and Crime reported on Thursday, in the first significant increase since the Taliban banned poppy cultivation in 2022. The area planted grew by 330% to more than 43,000 hectares, up from about 10,000 hectares in 2025, and estimated output rose from roughly 300 tonnes to just under 1,500 tonnes, spreading to 16 of the country's 34 provinces. The figures remain far below pre-ban levels, when some 232,000 hectares were cultivated. UNODC chief Monica Juma said the rebound shows how hard it is to sustain reductions when poor farmers have no alternative way to feed their families; its country representative said many feel they have no other choice, with legal crops paying a fraction of poppy's returns. Prices eased from $570 to $500 a kilogram but remain far above the pre-ban level of under $100. The Taliban authorities say they eradicated 22,000 hectares this year, rejected the UN's estimate as roughly double the real figure, and acknowledged the ban cannot be a long-term answer without alternative livelihoods.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab30 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab's Remnants Bring Rain and Himalayan Snow to Nepal, Easing From Saturday After Battering India

The weather system left by Cyclone Arnab, which made landfall in India's Andhra Pradesh on Wednesday, is bringing widespread rain and Himalayan snowfall across Nepal through Saturday, forecasters said, with the effects expected to weaken from Saturday afternoon. Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology recorded light rain across most of Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati and Gandaki provinces, with Kavrepalanchowk logging the country's highest 24-hour rainfall at 50.2mm, and warned of heavy snow at altitude that could affect trekkers; Nepal had earlier suspended high-mountain trekking permits for three days over avalanche risk. Officials stressed the weakened system is unlikely to cause major damage in Nepal, though harvest-ready paddy could suffer. In India the storm killed at least three to five people, according to differing official counts, forced the evacuation of more than 180,000 people across Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, destroyed at least 180 houses, uprooted hundreds of trees, knocked out power and, by state estimates, caused at least ₹500 crore of damage to electricity infrastructure, with dozens of trains cancelled or diverted on the Howrah–Chennai route.

Latest · Americas · US Debt & Midterms30 October 2026

Investors Say America's Fiscal Slide, With Debt Above $40 Trillion, Is the Headache No Midterm Result Will Cure

Investors see the deteriorating US fiscal position as their main worry heading into Tuesday's midterm elections, with no easy fix under either outcome, Reuters reported on Friday. The national debt has passed $40 trillion, the deficit is expected to stay near 6% of GDP with a budget gap of about $2 trillion this year, and persistent shortfalls and rising interest costs are expected to keep Treasury borrowing heavy for years, weakening confidence in US government bonds. If Republicans hold Congress, investors expect more of the same pressure; if Democrats win, gridlock may restrain new spending but meaningful consolidation is still seen as unlikely, and Pimco expects spending and the deficit to grow under both scenarios. Prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket currently make Democratic majorities in both chambers the most likely outcome, and a recent Reuters poll gave Democrats an 11-point advantage. A separate German Economic Institute study seen by Reuters found President Trump's tariffs have failed to cut the US trade deficit — the goods gap reached $130 billion in August, above its level two years earlier — while foreign direct investment fell 8.2% to $248 billion in 2025. Congress has pushed the fiscal 2027 spending fight to a deadline of 11 December.

Latest · Africa · Russia–Africa Summit30 October 2026

Putin Closes the Russia–Africa Forum in Moscow Pledging to at Least Double Trade With the Continent

The Russia–Africa Economic and Humanitarian Forum wrapped up in Moscow with President Vladimir Putin calling for trade between Russia and African countries to at least double in the near future, and setting out plans to deepen cooperation in energy, food security, technology and humanitarian affairs. The summit is Moscow's flagship pitch to a continent where it is competing for influence and contracts with China, the United States and the European Union, all of which are also courting African governments over critical minerals, energy routes and port access — a contest visible this week in parallel deals linking Atlantic and Indian Ocean rail corridors across southern Africa. For African delegations, the practical tests remain financing, delivery timelines and food and fuel security at home. The forum closed without a single headline figure for new investment, leaving the doubling pledge as an ambition to be measured against future trade data rather than a signed programme.
