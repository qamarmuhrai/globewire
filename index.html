Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz17 October 2026

Second Supertanker Strikes Naval Mine in Strait of Hormuz, Iran’s Guards Say

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy said on Saturday that a second supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil had struck a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, causing a major explosion in its engine room and a fire that left the vessel immobilised. The force said the tanker had switched off its positioning and radar systems and was using a route Tehran has not authorised; a first tanker was reported to have hit a mine on Friday. The IRGC did not name the ship or give casualty details, and repeated that the strait is closed to vessels breaking its rules. The United States and its allies reject Iran’s restrictions and insist on freedom of navigation through the waterway, which carries about a fifth of the world’s oil.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said on Saturday that his presidency will end in 2030 and that the constitution will not be amended to keep him in office, calling the question “completely settled.” Sisi, 71, made the remarks at a Military Academy graduation ceremony in the New Administrative Capital, dismissing social-media calls for another constitutional change. Egypt’s largest opposition coalition welcomed the statement as a step toward respecting the constitution and a peaceful transfer of power.

Police fired tear gas and water cannon as tens of thousands of people protested across France on Saturday over the cost of living and declining public services, in some of the country’s most violent demonstrations in years. Latest · Americas · Brazil16 October 2026

Amazon Deforestation Falls to Lowest Level Since Records Began in 1988

Deforestation in Brazil’s Amazon rainforest fell to its lowest level since satellite records began in 1988, according to data released on Friday by the national space research agency Inpe. Some 3,335 square kilometres of forest were cleared in the 12 months through July, about 42% less than in the previous period, while clearing in the Cerrado savanna also dropped to a record low of about 5,517 square kilometres. Climate campaigners called the figures a historic achievement, and they hand President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva a boost days before he faces Flavio Bolsonaro in a presidential runoff on October 25. Forest loss has fallen about 71% since Lula returned to office in 2023, after surging under his predecessor.

Anthropic Pitches Draft Bill That Would Let US Block Dangerous AI Model Releases

AI company Anthropic has quietly pitched US lawmakers a draft bill that would let the government block the release of a frontier AI model only if it poses a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than $1 billion in damage, or of killing or seriously injuring more than 50 people, Politico reported. The danger would have to arise from a biological weapon, a model escaping its developer’s controls, or a cyberattack. Under the proposal, independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department would periodically assess labs and their models and report any imminent risk within 72 hours, and only a federal judge could order a model suspended. Anthropic said the draft reflects the “Advanced AI Framework” it published earlier this year.

Sri Lanka’s 22 million people were left without grid electricity for about six hours on Saturday after the national grid tripped around noon, in a failure officials suspect was caused by solar generation exceeding demand on a quiet weekend. Power was restored in stages by dusk after the energy minister appealed to rooftop solar owners to disconnect their systems to help stabilise the network. The island has more than 3,000 megawatts of installed solar capacity against daytime demand of 2,000 to 2,500 megawatts, and a similar solar-driven imbalance shut the entire grid for more than six hours in February 2025.
