First Time in 80 Years: The World Court Finds a State Committed Genocide

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya — the first time in the court’s 80-year history that its judges have found a state responsible for the crime. In a 9–6 decision read out over three hours on Thursday by President Yuji Iwasawa, the court found Myanmar’s security forces killed around 10,000 Rohingya in northern Rakhine State during the 2017 clearance operations, committed widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and systematically destroyed villages, with the “only reasonable inference” being an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group. More than 730,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh in that crackdown. The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court ordered Myanmar to stop all genocidal acts, investigate and punish those responsible, allow the safe, voluntary and dignified return of refugees, and submit a reparations plan within two years — compensation itself could not yet be determined. The judgment is final and binding but the court has no enforcement arm; The Gambia can now take it to the UN Security Council. In Cox’s Bazar, refugees watched the verdict on screens, while the general who oversaw the 2017 campaign, Min Aung Hlaing, now sits as Myanmar’s president. Lawyers also note the ruling will be read closely in South Africa’s separate genocide case over Gaza.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bay of Bengal — Landfall Day20 November 2026

Noon at Gopalpur: Cyclone Akvan Comes Ashore After a Night of Evacuations

Severe cyclonic storm Akvan is due to cross India’s east coast around noon on Friday, between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, close to Gopalpur, with sustained winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110. The India Meteorological Department tracked the storm moving north-northwest over the west-central Bay of Bengal, and red warnings for extremely heavy rain cover districts including Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur, Puri, Ganjam, Gajapati and Kandhamal, with very heavy to extremely heavy rain expected across 11 districts through Friday. Odisha’s response scaled up through Thursday: early counts put more than 46,700 people evacuated, later state roundups cited over 1.20 lakh — 120,000 — moved, more than 300 rescue teams deployed, schools closed in six districts for three days, and dozens of trains cancelled or rescheduled, with figures varying by bulletin as the operation continued. Akvan is the third cyclone to threaten the coast in about a month, and forecasters expect it to weaken rapidly after landfall — leaving flood risk from the rain, rather than the wind, as the main danger into the weekend.

Kushner: Russia Is “Comfortable” With NATO-Style Guarantees for Ukraine

A striking claim closed the Berlin day. US envoy Jared Kushner, who took part in Thursday’s Coalition of the Willing meeting alongside Steve Witkoff, said Washington believes Russia is “comfortable” with security guarantees for postwar Ukraine modelled on NATO’s Article 5 — the principle that an attack on one member is an attack on all — and that having Europe at the table will be essential to ending the war. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he hoped to work “as a team with Kyiv and Washington” in talks with Russia, though no date was announced, and Kushner confirmed Donald Trump called into the Berlin discussions after his Shenzhen sessions with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping; the last round, in Abu Dhabi, produced no major breakthrough. What form any guarantees would take, and whether Moscow or Kyiv would accept them, remains unclear — and the claim sits against Putin’s own words hours earlier, vowing retaliation for the week’s three Ukrainian strikes on the Kerch Bridge and ruling out any truce. On the ground, Crimea’s authorities were due to start free Kerch–Kavkaz ferries on Friday to replace lost bridge capacity, Russia said it intercepted 53 Ukrainian drones overnight, and a Ukrainian strike on the Saratov region killed three people while Russian attacks killed two and wounded at least 23.

Latest · Africa · Yenne, Senegal — The Night Boat20 November 2026

Dozens Missing Off Senegal as Canary Crossings Surge to Record Levels

Relatives gathered on the beach at Yenne on Thursday looking for news that mostly did not come. A wooden pirogue carrying migrants toward Europe capsized off the town south of Dakar at about 2 a.m. on Wednesday after striking a rocky area, washing ashore badly damaged. Accounts of how many were aboard conflict — a survivor told one NGO about 50 people, other survivors said around 100 including five women — and so do the confirmed deaths: firefighters and the mayor reported one body recovered, while the head of the Horizons Sans Frontières group told Reuters four bodies had been found. Police recovered eight identity documents, phones and personal belongings from the wreckage. What is not in dispute is the surge around it: the UN refugee agency says nearly 8,200 people reached Spain’s Canary Islands in the first 12 days of November alone — more than 11,000 in the first half of the month by Spanish figures — with almost 1,500 arriving on a single Saturday, and nearly 600 people reported dead or missing on the West Africa route this year. Senegal’s navy rescued more than 1,400 people from pirogues in 48 hours this week. Less than two weeks ago, another sinking near Kebemer killed at least 39.

From “Most Aggressive Threat” to Partner: The US Signs a Military Deal With Venezuela

The United States has restored military ties with Venezuela after a break of more than two decades. Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Caracas on Wednesday — his first since US special forces deposed Nicolás Maduro in a January raid — and signed a cooperation agreement at the Miraflores Palace with Defence Minister Gustavo González López, witnessed by interim President Delcy Rodríguez, who has led the government since 5 January. Hegseth said Venezuela had gone from America’s “most aggressive regional threat to a compliant security partner,” and described the deal as historic: joint training, intelligence sharing, military equipment, systems exchanges and border security, aimed at drug-trafficking groups and armed organisations. Rodríguez said the renewed technical-military cooperation would confront common threats and strengthen Venezuela’s armed forces without compromising sovereignty. The full terms have not been published and the Pentagon has not answered questions on scope or timing. The pact follows August’s oil arrangement giving a US-linked producer long-term access to 17 fields holding about a fifth of Venezuela’s reserves.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Seoul — The Retrial20 November 2026

15-Year Sentence Overturned: South Korea’s Top Court Sends the Ex-Premier Back for Retrial

South Korea’s Supreme Court has overturned former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo’s 15-year prison sentence for his role in the December 2024 martial law crisis and sent the case back to the Seoul High Court. In a televised hearing on Thursday, Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae’s court ruled that Yoon Suk Yeol’s six-hour martial law declaration was itself unconstitutional and illegal, but found insufficient evidence that Han intended to subvert the constitutional order when, before the declaration, he proposed convening a cabinet meeting — a step lower courts treated as lending the decree false legitimacy, and which the Supreme Court called a legitimate official act for a prime minister. Other convictions stand: seeking ministers’ signatures on a martial law document after the declaration, discussing cutting power and water to certain media outlets, preparing a false official document, and perjury at the impeachment trial. Han, 77, was first sentenced to 23 years in January, reduced to 15 in May; he remains in custody at Seoul Detention Center, with detention extended two months. Yoon himself is serving a life sentence for leading the insurrection, with his appeal due on 26 January.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Pacific — The Dry Emergency20 November 2026

“Crisis Thresholds”: Fiji Declares a Disaster, Tonga an Emergency, as El Niño Bites

While one side of the Pacific floods, the other is running dry. Fiji’s government has declared a nationwide state of disaster, saying the El Niño drought has reached “crisis thresholds,” after cabinet approved the move on Wednesday on disaster-council advice; Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka said families across the country are worried about water, crops and livestock. Neighbouring Tonga has declared a 27-day state of emergency over water shortages across its islands. The pattern is regional: the World Meteorological Organization reported earlier this month that Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, Fiji and Tonga all recorded shortages after persistently low rainfall from May to October, and the outlook to January favours still-drier conditions across the southwest Pacific. Forecasters warn the current El Niño could become one of the most powerful on record and stay very strong into early 2027. Fiji’s disaster declaration, which runs up to 30 days at a time under its 2024 law, unlocks powers to move water, money and supplies to the communities running lowest.

Latest · Americas · Deep Space — One Light-Day20 November 2026

A Signal That Takes a Day to Arrive: Voyager 1 Crosses One Light-Day From Earth

Humanity’s most distant object has passed a distance no machine has reached before. NASA calculates that Voyager 1 became one light-day from Earth at 5:16 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday 18 November — about 25.9 billion kilometres, or 16.1 billion miles, the distance light travels in 24 hours — meaning a radio command now takes a full day to reach the probe and its answer another day to return. Launched on 5 September 1977, Voyager 1 left the Sun’s protective bubble in August 2012 and is flying through interstellar space at nearly 38,000 miles per hour toward the star Gliese 445, which it will pass within 1.7 light-years in about 40,000 years. The milestone changes nothing on board — NASA calls it symbolic — but the spacecraft is fading: its plutonium power source loses about 4 watts a year, eight of its ten original instruments are off, and only the plasma wave subsystem and magnetometer were still reported running this year. NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory scheduled a briefing for Thursday to discuss the crossing. Voyager 2, on a slower path, is not expected to reach one light-day until November 2035.

Latest · Europe · Kemerovo — The An-12 Inquiry20 November 2026

19 Dead in Siberia: Investigators Open a Criminal Case Over the An-12 Crash

Russia’s Investigative Committee has opened a criminal case over the military transport crash that killed everyone aboard in western Siberia. The Antonov An-12 came down on the evening of Tuesday 17 November in an unpopulated area near the village of Yagunovo, outside Kemerovo, about 3,000 kilometres east of Moscow, during what the Defence Ministry called a scheduled flight. The ministry says 19 people, crew included, were on board and none survived — an early wire figure of 17 was later superseded — and its preliminary assessment points to a technical malfunction, without naming any system. An Aerospace Forces commission is working at the site, regional governor Ilya Seredyuk confirmed emergency teams extinguished the fire, and the criminal case has been opened under the standard article covering violations of flight safety rules. The An-12 is a Soviet-built four-engine turboprop in service since the late 1950s, and the ministry has not released the aircraft’s variant, unit, age, route or the identities of those killed. The crash follows a Tu-95 loss in the Amur region in September that killed six crew.

Today the Lines Stop: 3.7 Million Workers Covered as IG Metall Strikes for 24 Hours

Friday is the walkout IG Metall promised. The 24-hour strikes called on Thursday hit hundreds of auto and steel plants across Germany today, escalating a pay dispute covering 3.7 million workers at companies including Audi, Mercedes-Benz and Tata Steel. The union, led by Christiane Benner, is demanding a 5% wage increase, safeguards for jobs and production sites, and profit-sharing where sectors are performing strongly. It dismissed the employers’ offer — a one-off 1% payment in July 2027 followed by a 1.3% rise from July 2028 — as meagre, arguing workers are being asked to wait nearly two years for a real increase while showing little commitment to keeping plants in Germany. With no date set for a fourth round of negotiations, today’s stoppage is both leverage and warning: a taste of what an open-ended dispute in Europe’s largest manufacturing economy would cost, in an industry already squeezed by the costs of the electric transition and weak demand.
