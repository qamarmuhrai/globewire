Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Tankers and Talks2 November 2026

Two More Tankers Explode in Hormuz, Trump Threatens 'the Hammer' — Yet Oil Falls More Than 3% as Qatar Talks Enter a Third Day

The strait gave markets two opposite signals at once. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said late on Sunday that two more oil tankers, one of them a supertanker, exploded after striking naval mines while using what Tehran calls an unauthorised route with navigation systems switched off; Britain's UKMTO separately reported a merchant vessel hit by an unknown projectile and relayed a Mayday call from inside the waterway. President Donald Trump renewed threats of military action, saying Washington may eventually have to give Tehran 'the hammer', while Iranian officials warned any new war could be longer and wider than the last. Yet crude fell: Brent dropped $3.68, or 3.2%, to $109.75 a barrel by mid-morning GMT and West Texas Intermediate fell 3.3% to $100.69, after Iranian and Qatari officials held a third straight day of talks on ending the US-Israeli war on Iran. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi described a two-day plan — US measures on day one, a reopened strait and restored navigation on day two. Analysts called the fall a correction after Brent ended October at a six-month high, and warned rhetoric could push Brent towards $120 once Tuesday's US midterm elections are out of the way. US Central Command, for its part, accused Tehran of killing more than 20 civilian mariners near the strait in recent months, a charge Iran rejects.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Final Polls2 November 2026

Eve of Polls: Democrats Lead in Five Republican-Held Senate States, Trump's Approval Sits at 38%, and More Than 51 Million Have Already Voted

America votes tomorrow, and the final surveys describe a Republican Party defending ground it won comfortably two years ago. New York Times/Siena polling puts Democratic candidates ahead in five Republican-held Senate battlegrounds — Texas, where James Talarico leads Ken Paxton 50% to 43%; Kansas, where Chris Hamilton leads Senator Roger Marshall 49% to 44%; Maine, where Graham Platner leads Susan Collins 51% to 45%; Ohio, where Sherrod Brown leads Jon Husted 49% to 44%; and Alaska — with Iowa effectively tied and Democrats also ahead in Michigan, the one Democratic seat in the group. Across the seven states surveyed, only 38% of likely voters approve of President Trump's performance, though he won five of those states by double digits in 2024; the NBC News average also puts his approval at 38% against 60% disapproval, and 52% of Americans say they prefer a Democratic Congress to 44% for Republican control. Nationally the generic ballot averages — RealClearPolling at 51.8% to 43.3%, NBC at 52–45, CBS at 53–45 — point the same way, and Fox News' final Power Rankings rate the Senate a toss-up while favouring Democrats to retake the House. Trump closes his campaign in Kansas and Iowa; House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries has declined to rule out impeachment proceedings if Democrats win the House. At stake on Tuesday: all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four.

After the Truce, the Alerts Themselves Become a Battlefield: Russia and Ukraine Accuse Each Other of Hacking Emergency Warning Apps

The war's longest pause ended on Sunday; by Monday both capitals were fighting over the phones in civilians' pockets. Latest · Europe · Bulgaria — Count Complete2 November 2026

99.95% Counted: Iotova Confirmed at 57.58% and 1,093,564 Votes — Bulgaria's Seventh President and Its First Elected Woman in the Post

The count is effectively finished, and the margin grew as it closed. Bulgaria's Central Election Commission, with 99.95% of polling-station protocols processed, gives Iliana Iotova and running mate Kiril Valchev 1,093,564 votes — 57.58% — against 749,543 votes, 39.46%, for Andrey Gyurov and Georgi Kandev. Iotova carried 28 electoral districts; Gyurov took three, including two in Sofia and Kardzhali, and won among Bulgarians voting abroad with 55.4%. Turnout was about 27%, up from the first round's record low of 23% but still meaning the winner was chosen by roughly one voter in six. Gyurov has accepted the result. Iotova, 62, became acting president in January when Rumen Radev resigned to run for prime minister — a post he won when his Progressive Bulgaria took an outright parliamentary majority in April — and she now holds the presidency in her own right from 22 January 2027. Backed by Radev's camp, she favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine; Gyurov ran with the pro-Western opposition. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen congratulated her, citing security, competitiveness and energy independence. The vote was held under the shadow of October drone attacks on commercial shipping off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast that left 21 people missing.

Latest · Americas · Florida Jet Crash — Investigation2 November 2026

It Fell 27,000 Feet in One Minute: Florida's Learjet Crash Is America's Second-Deadliest This Year, and 600 Neighbours Spent the Night Evacuated

New detail has sharpened the picture of Sunday's crash without explaining it. Flight-tracking data cited by CNN shows the Learjet 60, registration N722BD, descended 27,000 feet in about a minute before the signal cut off, striking Military Trail in Greenacres, Palm Beach County, shortly before 1pm and leaving a deep crater and burning debris feet from homes. Governor Ron DeSantis called the damage 'cataclysmic', with debris strewn over a significant distance; about 600 residents of the adjoining mobile-home community were evacuated while investigators work the site, and remarkably no one on the ground was reported killed or injured. All nine aboard died — Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed the toll — making this the second-deadliest US plane crash of 2026, after 12 people died in a June skydiving-plane crash in Missouri, and the worst accident involving a Learjet 60 in Flight Safety Foundation records. The jet had flown from Austin, Texas, bound for North Eleuthera in the Bahamas; the crew had reported turbulence and icing near 35,000 feet. Officials still have not identified the nine, nor said how many were crew, and no cause has been suggested. The National Transportation Safety Board's go-team arrived Sunday night and Monday, with a news conference expected Monday.

Latest · Europe · Germany — Gen Z Protests1 November 2026

Daylight Marches, Night-Time Looting: Germany's Gen Z Cost-of-Living Protests End With Frankfurt Shops Smashed and Dozens Detained

Two different protests occupied the same streets. During Saturday, tens of thousands marched in 34 German cities under the banner of 'Gen Z regelt' — 'Gen Z takes charge' — demanding better-funded schools and healthcare, relief from living costs, an end to public-spending cuts and opposition to compulsory military service. After dark the mood broke: in Frankfurt, hooded rioters looted at least two stores, damaged several more, smashed the windows of a gun shop — police seized two firearms and arrested a suspect — and threw Molotov cocktails at emergency services, while a large stretch of the main shopping street was cordoned off. Police used water cannon in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg against crowds throwing fireworks, stones and bottles; reports put detentions at 54 or more across the cities, with 27 officers injured in Berlin. Authorities in Frankfurt, Munich and Berlin put combined turnout at up to 18,000, about half the organisers' estimate and well below the 182,000 once expected. Hesse's interior minister said about 100 left-wing extremists had infiltrated the Frankfurt march; Federal Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt demanded harsh punishments and said police had prevented 'violence like that seen in Paris', where more than 320,000 had also demonstrated on Saturday. Organisers insist their demands are peaceful ones: investment, fairness, and no return to conscription.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea — Cluster Warhead1 November 2026

North Korea Says Its New Cluster Warhead Can Turn 13 Hectares to Ashes — and That All Four Types Will Reach Frontline Units This Year

Pyongyang is moving a banned-by-many weapon from test range to inventory. State media reported on Sunday that the Missile Administration test-fired a cluster-bomb warhead for tactical ballistic missiles on Saturday, overseen by General Kim Jong-sik of the Central Military Commission, to assess its combat adaptation and power. Research institutes said the Standard Type 2 warhead can devastate a target area of 12 to 13 hectares — about 32 acres — at what they called highest-density power, roughly double the area claimed in April, when Kim Jong Un and his daughter Ju-ae watched a cluster warhead launched on a Hwasong-11 missile. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said the missile was fired from near Chongju in the northwest towards the Yellow Sea, and that Seoul and Washington had tracked preparations in advance. The Missile Administration's director said cluster warheads are classified into four types, special-purpose and standard, and that all four will be made available to units before the end of the year, to be selected according to the target. The test follows a recent run of weapons trials — attack drones, a precision-guided torpedo and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile — and a salvo of about ten ballistic missiles fired on Thursday during German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's visit to South Korea. Cluster munitions scatter submunitions over a wide area and are banned by more than 100 countries under a convention North Korea, South Korea and the United States have not joined.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Settlement Day2 November 2026

Statement Day in Westminster: Mahmood Sets Out the Ten-Year Settlement Rules, as New Analysis Says Skilled Migrants Add £115 Billion Under the Current Ones

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood addresses the Commons on Monday with the government's final position on indefinite leave to remain, and the argument has acquired a new number. Analysis by the Institute for Public Policy Research, using the government's own methodology, finds that skilled migrants and their dependants due to settle under current rules would contribute £115 billion more to the public finances over their lifetimes than they cost — a direct counter to the Home Office estimate, cited by Mahmood in March, that settling some 350,000 lower-skilled care-route workers and dependants would cost £10 billion. The proposals double the standard wait from five years to ten, with shorter waits for higher earners and community contribution under an earned-settlement model, and — in earlier drafts — up to fifteen years for some lower-paid workers, including 117,000 care workers and 79,000 dependants. After a consultation drawing more than 200,000 responses and a revolt led from within Labour, care workers already in Britain are now expected to keep the five-year route; the BBC reports that exemption as likely, unconfirmed until Mahmood speaks. Up to 1.6 million people could become eligible for settlement between 2026 and 2030. Shadow home secretary Chris Philp calls the softening a surrender; Reform UK would abolish indefinite leave to remain entirely. Transitional rules for people already part-way through the five-year path remain the largest unanswered question — until the new rules take effect, the current route still applies.

Latest · Global Affairs · Binance — Proof Due Tuesday2 November 2026

Until Tuesday It's Binance's Word: Withdrawals Flow Out Through New Wallets While the Cryptographic Proof of the $6.3 Billion Cover Is Still Computing

The money is moving; the maths is not finished. Binance reopened withdrawals in phases from Sunday across 15 networks, routing them through newly created wallets after the 29 October breach, and says it has covered about 95% of the roughly $6.3 billion taken — more than $6 billion replaced from its corporate treasury, its Secure Asset Fund for Users and futures insurance reserves, with about $3 billion of stolen assets frozen by industry partners. The FBI has formally attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, North Korea's state hacking unit, previously blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft. A reserves snapshot — published Saturday and used in updated wallet listings — shows ten major assets including bitcoin, ether, USDT, BNB, Solana, USDC and XRP backed at 1:1 or better, but the margins are thin: reporting on the Ethereum wallets shows holdings exceeding customer balances by fewer than three coins after the top-up. The full zero-knowledge proof, which lets each customer verify their own balance is covered, takes 40 to 50 hours to generate and is now expected Tuesday, 3 November — a day later than first promised. Analysts note a proof of reserves is a snapshot, not an audit of liabilities. Sunday's flows underline the trust gap: about $4.65 billion withdrawn against $1.01 billion deposited, a net outflow near 2.1% of assets under management.

India's Voter-Roll Battle Moves to the Streets Again as Amit Shah Leads a Counter-March in Hyderabad and Attacks the Opposition Rally's Timing

The argument over India's electoral rolls now has rival processions. Union Home Minister Amit Shah was scheduled to lead a foot march in Hyderabad on Monday, from the Babu Jagjivan Ram statue at Nizam College to Mozamjahi Chowrasta, as part of the BJP's campaign against what it calls a false opposition narrative on the Special Intensive Revision of voter lists. The Election Commission maintains the revision removes duplicate, deceased and ineligible entries and that anyone wrongly deleted can re-register; Human Rights Watch has said the pattern of deletions raises serious questions about the fairness of future elections. Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand Votes Saturday2 November 2026

Five Days From Polling Day, New Zealand's Election Turns on a Two-Speed Economy: Booming Farms and Tourist Towns, Squeezed Cities

New Zealand votes on Saturday, and the campaign has narrowed to the price of ordinary life. In Lower Hutt, outside Wellington, 70-year-old part-time bus driver Liz Hartley told Reuters she still works because little is left at the end of the week, and plans a tactical vote in Hutt South aimed at unseating Chris Bishop, one of the National government's most senior figures. Three years after urban voters helped National win on promises to cut inflation and debt, the recovery has arrived unevenly: tourism and agricultural exports have lifted the rural South Island, where second-quarter retail spending rose 8.6% year on year and unemployment fell 0.6 points, while the North Island's cities lag — spending up 5.8%, unemployment up 0.7 points, and house prices down almost 30% from their peak in Wellington and 24.7% in Auckland. Data from more than 400,000 users of 1News Vote Compass tells the same story across generations: the cost of living is the top issue for voters aged 18–25 (30.9%) and for those over 70 (25.8%), with healthcare close behind among older voters at 23.8%. Polls show no clear path to government for any coalition, with voters drifting back towards Labour or towards smaller parties such as the centrist Opportunity Party. A left-leaning coalition promises a bigger state role on household costs, public services and housing; National argues it has laid the foundations for growth.
