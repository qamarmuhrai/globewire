Latest · Middle East · Iran — Trump–Netanyahu Call2 November 2026

Netanyahu Presses Trump to Strike Iran Immediately After the Midterms, as Tehran Warns a New War Would Have No Geographic Limits

The decision now sits between a phone call and a ballot box. US and Israeli officials told Axios that President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke about when — and whether — to resume combat operations against Iran, with Netanyahu urging strikes immediately after Tuesday's US midterm elections and Trump preferring to let negotiations run longer; one Israeli source called the exchange tough, an American source called it direct. The call followed word from Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei that a letter from Vice President JD Vance seeking to revive stalled talks had arrived with a completely changed tone, which he described as a positive sign, though he has not replied. Diplomacy continued in parallel: Iranian negotiators spent a third day in Doha with Qatari mediators, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a proposal developed with the mediators could fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days of an agreement, while stressing no conclusion has been reached. The warnings from Tehran hardened over the weekend. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered an updated target list and said any attack would meet an immediate response without geographic limits; army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia added on Sunday that a future war could last longer than the last and that Trump would not dictate when it ends. Trump, for his part, said the US still has one or two targets in Iran to take care of, including the Pickaxe Mountain complex. Late on Sunday a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, and Iran's Foreign Ministry designated Britain's Royal Navy and Royal Air Force as terrorist organisations and MI6 as a hostile group, in response to British support for the US-Israeli war and London's July move against the Revolutionary Guard.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Election Eve2 November 2026

Election Eve in America: More Than 51 Million Have Already Voted, Democrats Hold an Eight-Point National Lead, and Control of Congress Rests on a Handful of Senate Races

America votes on Tuesday, and by Monday morning the shape of the contest was set. More than 51 million people have already cast ballots early, well above the pace of the last midterm cycle, with candidates left one final day of appeals before polling stations open. The RealClearPolling generic-ballot average puts Democrats at 51.5% to Republicans' 43.1%; a THP/MM survey gives a wider 54% to 41%, and an Emerson College poll a ten-point gap. All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are contested. Democrats need a net gain of three seats to take the House and four to take the Senate outright, since a 50–50 Senate would still be broken by Vice President JD Vance. The Senate map explains why a large national lead may not settle the outcome: in Kansas, a Washington Post–Schar School poll has Democrat Adam Hamilton ahead of Senator Roger Marshall 52% to 43%, while Emerson finds a much tighter 50% to 47% and prediction markets still narrowly favour the Republican incumbent. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico leads Republican Ken Paxton — 49% to 43% in a CNN poll — in a state his party has not carried in a Senate race since 1988, and in Michigan Abdul El-Sayed leads Mike Rogers by up to seven points. President Trump spent Sunday campaigning in Iowa and Kansas; his approval stands near 37% against roughly 60% disapproval in aggregated polling, and analysts caution that the closest races may not be called on election night while mail and provisional ballots are counted. The cost of living, worsened by the war on Iran, has dominated the closing argument on both sides.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria Runoff Result1 November 2026

Exit Polls Make Iliana Iotova Bulgaria's First Elected Female President, Defeating Andrey Gyurov in a Low-Turnout Runoff Split Over Russia

Bulgaria appears to have chosen continuity — and a first. Exit polls published as voting ended at 8pm on Sunday put acting President Iliana Iotova ahead of former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov in the presidential runoff, on course to become the country's first elected female head of state. Alpha Research's exit poll for Bulgarian National Television gave Iotova 52.5% to Gyurov's 43.4%, with about 4% voting against both; Myara put her at 51.1% to 44.6% and Trend at 51.6% to 44%. A parallel count by Alpha Research, based on a fully processed sample, widened the margin to 57.4% against 39.6%, though the Central Election Commission had not announced the official result as counting continued on Sunday night, with the formal declaration expected within days. The runoff followed a first round on 25 October in which Iotova led with about 34.8% to Gyurov's roughly 32% among 27 candidate pairs — far closer than the 20-point lead polls had forecast for her. Turnout remained the story beneath the result: about 23% in the first round, a record low, rising only to an estimated 27–29% in the runoff. The two candidates offered opposite instincts on Russia and the war in Ukraine: Iotova, a former vice-president backed by Rumen Radev's Progressive Bulgaria, favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Kyiv, while Gyurov ran with the pro-Western Continue the Change and Democratic Bulgaria. The campaign's final weeks were shadowed by drone attacks on commercial shipping off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast that left 21 people missing. The winner takes office when the current term ends on 22 January 2027.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Kherson Evacuation2 November 2026

Ukraine Evacuates 167 More Civilians From Kherson as the Oleshky Ceasefire Expires, While Strikes Kill in Sumy and Vinnytsia

The longest local pause of the war ended the way it began — with buses. Ukraine's military said it evacuated 167 people from threatened areas of southern Kherson region on Sunday, hours before a six-day local ceasefire around the Russian-held town of Oleshky was due to expire at midnight Moscow time. The truce, brokered with the involvement of Pope Leo XIV and US First Lady Melania Trump, had held without violations since Tuesday, according to both Ukrainian and Russian officials, and allowed busloads of Oleshky residents — some of the fewer than 2,000 people left from a pre-war population of 24,000 — to reach Ukrainian-held territory after a 19-hour journey. Officials warned that the intensity of Russian strikes around Kherson remains high and the threat to life extreme. Elsewhere the war did not pause. Regional authorities reported at least one death in a strike in the border region of Sumy and at least one in central Vinnytsia; a conflict briefing recorded a Russian drone strike on the grounds of a vocational-school dormitory in Koziatyn district, Vinnytsia region, used to shelter displaced people, killing two civilians including a 13-year-old girl and injuring six, among them three five-year-olds, one critically. In Russia, officials said a Ukrainian drone attack set fire to the Lukoil refinery at Kstovo in Nizhny Novgorod region, one of the country's largest, and investigators opened a terrorism case; President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said hundreds of thousands of Ukrainian households were left without power after overnight Russian strikes on energy infrastructure in four regions. Diplomacy moved slowly in the background: a Kremlin aide said the next round of settlement talks should begin in a trilateral Russia–Ukraine–US format, and President Trump said the war would end in the not-too-distant future and that he was willing to invite Zelenskiy to December's G20 summit in Miami.

Latest · Global Affairs · Binance Heist — The Bill Comes Due2 November 2026

Binance Processes $4.65 Billion of Withdrawals in a Day as Customers Test the Exchange After the Record Heist, With a Proof-of-Reserves Report Due Monday

The reopening held, but customers voted with their wallets. Binance said it processed about $4.65 billion in withdrawals between 6am and 3:30pm GMT on Sunday, against $1.01 billion in deposits — a net outflow of $3.64 billion, equal to roughly 2.1% of the total assets the exchange manages. The phased restart began Sunday morning with 15 cryptoassets including bitcoin, ether, Solana, XRP and dogecoin, each network restored only after stability and security checks. The exchange has set aside more than $6.34 billion of company funds to cover the theft, saying it used its Secure Asset Fund for Users, its futures insurance fund and its corporate treasury to buy replacement tokens — about 90 to 95% of what was taken, with the remainder in less liquid tokens that will take longer to source — and insisting that Binance, not its users, absorbs the loss. The FBI formally attributed the 29 October attack on Saturday to TraderTraitor, the North Korean state-sponsored unit previously blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025, and investigators expect further laundering attempts through decentralised-finance and instant-swap services; industry partners have frozen about $3 billion of the stolen assets. The bulk of the $6.3 billion theft was executed in around 20 minutes, and Binance's preliminary assessment points to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer. The next test arrives Monday, when the exchange is due to publish a comprehensive proof-of-reserves update — the document customers and regulators will use to judge whether the reassurance matches the balance sheet.

Five Days From Polling Day, New Zealand's Election Turns on a Two-Speed Economy: Booming Farms and Tourist Towns, Squeezed Cities

New Zealand votes on Saturday, and the campaign has narrowed to the price of ordinary life. Spain Has Identified 56 of the 69 Urda Dead, the Transport Minister Refuses to Resign, and the Timeline Points to Six Minutes That Decided Everything

The identification work is becoming the measure of the disaster. Spanish authorities said 56 of the 69 people killed in Wednesday's high-speed collision near Urda, in Toledo province, had been identified by Saturday through fingerprints and DNA, with forensic teams and the Guardia Civil working through the weekend and more than 200 people injured. Transport Minister Óscar Puente rejected calls to resign, insisting he retains Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's support. The preliminary reconstruction is now precise about the sequence. At about 8:30am a computer failure knocked the LZB train-protection system offline across a 63-kilometre stretch, automatically stopping six trains. At 8:36am the Madrid control centre authorised the Renfe AVE service 2080 — 397 people aboard, travelling from Madrid to Seville — to resume normal running without a strict speed limit. At about 8:53am, travelling at between 160 and 200 km/h, it struck the rear of the stationary Iryo train near kilometre marker 118, between Mora and Urda. The two Renfe drivers, Antonio Acedo Meneses, 36, and trainee Daniel Cabanas Cob, 31, have been praised as heroes for braking seconds before impact, a decision credited with saving many lives. The drivers' union Semaf has demanded an urgent review of the rules that allow trains to run at speed when automatic protection has failed, and has called strikes at Renfe for 10 and 12 November. The crash is Spain's second major rail disaster of 2026, after January's near Adamuz killed 46, and its deadliest since Santiago de Compostela in 2013; investigators stress the two are not believed to be connected. Sánchez, who faces a general election on 29 November, has promised to establish every detail and strengthen safety so it never happens again.

Latest · Americas · Alberta — Certified Results29 October 2026

Certified at Last: Alberta Voted 74.95% to Stay in Canada — and Narrowly Backed Tighter Immigration Controls in the Same Breath

The official count has settled what the projections promised. Elections Alberta's certified results for the 19 October referendum, released on 29 October, show 1,556,316 votes — 74.95% — for Alberta to remain a province of Canada, against 520,233 (25.05%) for beginning the legal process towards a binding separation referendum, on a turnout of 70.4%. Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed the verdict as a clear choice for national unity in the middle of a trade fight with the United States; separatist campaigners with the Alberta Prosperity Project vowed to continue. But the ten-question ballot delivered a second, quieter message: all five immigration-related proposals passed, each narrowly — increased provincial control over immigration at 54.8%, and proof of citizenship to vote provincially at 58.1%, the widest of the five — while all four constitutional proposals failed, including abolishing the Senate (52.7% no) and giving provincial laws priority over federal ones (55.5% no). The distinctions matter legally: the immigration questions and the separation question are non-binding, and the constitutional questions would have bound the government only if passed, so the referendum by itself changes no rule, no eligibility test and no resident's status. Politically, it hands Premier Danielle Smith's government a complicated mandate — a province that decisively rejected the road to independence, measurably wants a harder line on immigration, and declined to expand its own constitutional powers. One in four Albertans still voted to start down the separation path, a constituency no government in Edmonton or Ottawa can treat as settled.
