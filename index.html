Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — Taiwan Landing Drills27 November 2026

China Tests New Taiwan Landing Tactic, Linking Civilian Ships Into a Floating Bridge

Satellite imagery and a geospatial analysis show China’s military running a weeks-long amphibious exercise in Dacheng Bay, in the Fujian province facing Taiwan, in which civilian vessels are lashed together to form a makeshift floating bridge about 800 metres long for moving heavy equipment ashore. Eight civilian vessels — barges and semi-submersible and powered craft — took part alongside landing craft and nearby naval ships, with no purpose-built bridging equipment observed, suggesting a rehearsal of improvised, low-signature logistics. A senior security official said the drill, part of this year’s All-Military Strategic Exercise, was designed to practise the swift seizure of small islands along the first island chain and was still under way on Friday; jamming activity was also detected in the area. Taiwan’s defence ministry said it was monitoring the activity, while Beijing described its training as legitimate and reasonable. Analysts cautioned that the barges would be exposed targets in wartime.

Latest · Middle East · Defence — Mecca Pact Ratified27 November 2026

Erdogan Completes Turkey’s Ratification of Mecca Defence Pact With Pakistan and Saudi Arabia

President Tayyip Erdogan has formally approved the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement between Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, Turkey’s Official Gazette said on Friday, completing Ankara’s ratification after parliament endorsed the pact on 5 November. The three countries signed the agreement in Mecca in August amid the US-Israeli war against Iran; under its terms, an attack on one party counts as an attack on all. Ratification clears the way for Turkey — holder of NATO’s second-largest military — to send support to Riyadh, which has faced attacks by Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis, and a ruling-party lawmaker has said a mandate to deploy Turkish personnel to Saudi Arabia will also be put before parliament. The three partners are due to meet in Islamabad on Friday and Saturday. Pakistan is the only nuclear-armed Muslim-majority state, while Saudi Arabia is both home to Islam’s holiest sites and one of the world’s top oil exporters.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — Budget Summit Ends Without Deal27 November 2026

EU Leaders End Summit Without a Budget Deal, Back Ukraine Loan in Principle and Pick Next ECB Chief

European Union leaders wrapped up a special summit in Brussels without agreement on the bloc’s 2028–2034 long-term budget, after more than 12 hours of talks exposed splits over the budget’s overall size, its spending priorities and new revenue sources. French President Emmanuel Macron set an end-of-year deadline for reaching a deal. On Ukraine, 24 of the 27 member states backed a proposed additional loan of up to €45 billion, about $49 billion, for 2027 — up to €25 billion of it military — though Bulgaria said it would join only the non-military part, and leaders deferred a final decision to December. The summit also put forward Spain’s former central bank governor, Pablo Hernández de Cos, to succeed Christine Lagarde as president of the European Central Bank when she leaves office on 7 February 2027, subject to consultation with the European Parliament and the ECB’s Governing Council.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — Ebola Outbreak26 November 2026

Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Passes 12,000 Cases as Fighting Forces Responders to Pull Back

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo have climbed past 12,000, with 5,904 deaths recorded, health ministry data showed on Thursday — the fastest-growing and deadliest Ebola outbreak on record. The epidemic, declared in May in the northeastern province of Ituri, is caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment, and cases have now been reported in eight of the country’s 26 provinces. Renewed fighting between the army and the Convention for the Popular Revolution rebels around Bunia has cut off some communities, disrupted monitoring of people exposed to the virus and left some treatment centres short of staff. The World Health Organization said about a dozen people, including two of its staff, were evacuated to Bunia after spending three days trapped in a hospital at Akwe amid intense crossfire. Delayed detection, poor roads and local mistrust have further hampered the response.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — Election Deadlock27 November 2026

Final Count Leaves New Zealand Deadlocked, With a Centrist Newcomer Holding the Key

New Zealand’s final election count, published on Friday, confirmed that neither major bloc won a majority, leaving the shape of the next government to negotiation. After some 400,000 special votes were tallied, Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s National party lost two seats to finish on 40; the right-wing bloc holds 65 of 130 seats, one short of the 66 needed, while the Labour-led left bloc rose to 58. That makes the centrist Opportunity party — a newcomer to Parliament with seven seats, led by 38-year-old business consultant Qiulae Wong — the decider. Wong has said she could work with either side, but Labour leader Chris Hipkins conceded her support could not install a left-wing government, leaving Luxon the only leader with a viable path, provided he can also win over three divergent minor parties. Labour actually outpolled National, 29.88 percent to 28.35 percent, in the tightest vote in years, with 2.8 million ballots cast.

Latest · Europe · Serbia — Government Still Unformed27 November 2026

Serbia’s Victorious Student Movement Still Has No Prime Minister as Deadlines Pile Up

A month after student-backed candidates won Serbia’s 25 October parliamentary election in a landslide — ending the rule of longtime populist Aleksandar Vucic after nearly two years of anti-corruption protests — the Student List has yet to name a prime minister or cabinet, and its parliamentary faction leader Jovo Bakic says a government is unlikely before the second half of December. Three deadlines fall before then: a US Treasury licence that lets Serbia’s only refinery, the Russian-majority-owned NIS, keep operating expires on 11 December; the 2027 budget must pass by 20 December or the country drops into three months of temporary financing; and the Gazprom gas contract ends on 31 December, with Serbia dependent on Russia for about 80 percent of its gas. Under the constitution, the movement has until 15 February to form a government or face a fresh election, and a presidential vote is set for 27 December in the meantime.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia — Sanctions Over Arson Attacks27 November 2026

Australia Sanctions Two Men It Accuses of Running Iran-Linked Antisemitic Arson Attacks

Australia on Friday imposed counter-terrorism financing sanctions on two men it publicly named for the first time, accusing them of orchestrating antisemitic arson attacks in Sydney and Melbourne on behalf of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Foreign Minister Penny Wong said Zohair Hassib and Kazem Hamad used a complex chain of proxies to arrange the firebombing of the kosher Lewis Continental Kitchen in Sydney in October 2024 and the Adass Israel Synagogue in Melbourne two months later. The sanctions make it a criminal offence, punishable by up to 10 years in prison, to use or deal with the pair’s assets. Canberra expelled Iran’s ambassador and three other diplomats in August 2025 and listed the IRGC as a state sponsor of terrorism after its intelligence agency, ASIO, linked Tehran to the attacks; Friday’s move brings Australia’s counter-terrorism financing list to 102 individuals and entities.

Latest · Global Affairs · Trade — US Tariffs26 November 2026

Trading Partners Push Back as Washington Unveils New Tariffs on 14 Economies

The United States has proposed fresh tariffs on imports from 14 major trading partners, including China and the European Union, arguing they are needed to counter excess industrial capacity abroad. Chinese goods would face the highest headline rate, 20 percent, though Washington held the total at an agreed cap by not stacking the new duties — a rate that could jump to 40 percent if the US-China trade truce is not extended before it expires on 10 January, according to US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer. Beijing said it reserved the right to respond, while rates for other partners hinge on their own negotiations: Vietnam faces 20 percent with a deal or 35 percent without, and caps of 15 to 18 percent apply to partners including Japan, South Korea, the EU and several Southeast Asian exporters. The duties would take effect only after a public hearing on 25 January, and markets were little moved, judging the announcement largely expected.

South Korea Sends Six North Koreans Home After Rescue From Drifting Fishing Boat

South Korea on Friday returned six North Korean fishermen to the North by sea after their boat was found drifting off the peninsula’s east coast earlier this month, the Unification Ministry in Seoul said.
