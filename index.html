Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya, Türkiye — No Deal After 44 Hours of Overtime22 November 2026

COP31 Collapses Without an Overarching Deal as the EU Refuses a Pact That Never Mentions Fossil Fuels

The United Nations climate summit in Antalya has ended in failure, the first breakdown of its kind since Copenhagen in 2009. After more than two weeks of talks that ran a record 44 hours past their scheduled close, COP31 president Murat Kurum told Sunday’s final plenary that there was no consensus to adopt the proposed Antalya Pact and that the decisions had therefore not been adopted. The breaking point was language on the main driver of warming itself. An early draft had called for an immediate transition away from fossil fuels, echoing the pledge made at COP28 in Dubai in 2023, but every later draft stripped the words out. More than 100 countries, and by some counts around 130, pushed to keep an explicit reference to coal, oil and gas, while Saudi Arabia and other oil producers opposed naming them. Ireland’s climate minister Darragh O’Brien, speaking for the EU presidency, said the bloc would not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels and would not accept a summit that greenwashes them. The European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway were among those rejecting the text, which Russia, China and Arab states had supported. The United States, under President Donald Trump, was absent from international climate action. Negotiations president Chris Bowen called the outcome very disappointing, though he noted more than 90 countries had signed a separate pledge to speed up electrification. Disputes over carbon capture, adaptation funding and how to respond to the now-expected overshoot of the 1.5°C threshold were never bridged.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv Region — Seven Killed in Latest Attacks22 November 2026

Zelenskyy Urges Washington to Release Paid-For Patriot Missiles as Rescuers Search Rubble Near Kyiv

Ukraine’s president has made a direct public appeal for air-defence missiles he says allies have already bought. Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Sunday that Russia had fired about 2,700 attack drones, nearly 1,460 guided aerial bombs and 165 missiles across 20 regions in a single week, and he urged the United States to approve delivery of Patriot interceptors funded through a NATO-coordinated programme. Ukraine has been receiving an average of about 12 interceptors a month and is running short of the only system that can reliably stop ballistic missiles. The appeal followed a deadly Sunday around the capital. In Dymer, north of Kyiv, a strike partly collapsed a five-storey apartment building; rescuers recovered bodies as crews searched for around 10 missing people, including two children, with later local updates putting the Dymer toll at four. In Vyshneve, southwest of Kyiv, a drone hit a grocery store, killing at least two people and wounding eight, with the toll later reported at three, while another person died when a strike set a home ablaze in Bila Tserkva. Overnight shelling also killed a man in Zaporizhzhia. Russia’s defence ministry said its forces had struck a cargo ship northeast of Odesa and a power facility in Zaporizhzhia, and claimed the capture of Sosnovyi Bir in Kharkiv region — claims that could not be independently verified. Separately, Ukrainian drone strikes wounded five people in Russia’s Krasnodar region, according to Russian reports.

Latest · Americas · Washington — 258 Seats and Counting22 November 2026

Democrats Reach 258 House Seats for Their Biggest Majority Since 1992 as a North Carolina Recount Tips the Milestone

House Democrats have secured the largest majority held by either party in more than three decades. The Associated Press call on Sunday in North Carolina, where Democrat Chuck Hubbard, a former newspaper reporter, defeated 11-term Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx after a recount, took the party to at least 258 of the 435 House seats. Republicans hold at least 173, with four races still outstanding: one each in Minnesota and Tennessee and two in Louisiana heading to December runoffs. Democrats have flipped 44 seats so far, four more than their gain in President Donald Trump’s first midterm in 2018, and won 70 of the 92 districts their campaign arm had targeted as battlegrounds. The tally matches the party’s 1992 result under Bill Clinton and edges past its 257 seats in 2008 under Barack Obama. Foxx chaired the powerful Rules Committee, which decides which bills reach the floor. Together with a Democratic Senate majority reported at 56 seats, the result sets up divided government with a Republican president in the White House — and, analysts note, heavy pressure on Democrats to deliver on the affordability promises that drove the sweep, which was fuelled in part by anger over living costs and the Iran war.

Latest · Middle East · Herzliya — Four Hours, No Deal22 November 2026

Netanyahu and Eisenkot End a Second Round of Unity Talks Without Agreement, Split Over Who Leads and Whether Ben-Gvir Joins

Israel’s government deadlock has survived another long meeting. Delegations from caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud and Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party talked for four hours at a hotel in Herzliya on Sunday and parted without a coalition deal, saying only that they had outlined their positions and that a follow-up meeting would be held, with no date given. According to Channel 12, the two camps remain divided on who would lead a unity government and on whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir’s Otzma Yehudit party would sit in it: Likud wants Ben-Gvir included, while Yashar insists on his exclusion. Former prime minister Naftali Bennett has floated a broad coalition with a premiership rotation — Eisenkot first, Netanyahu second — but Eisenkot opposes any rotation, Netanyahu is reported to insist on going first, and Avigdor Lieberman rules out joining any government that includes Netanyahu. Eisenkot, a former army chief tasked by President Isaac Herzog on 8 November, has until 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension, to assemble 61 seats in the 120-seat Knesset. His bloc holds 48 seats against 53 for Netanyahu’s alliance, and his camp is split over courting ultra-Orthodox or Arab parties instead. If unity talks fail, Yashar says it will seek a confidence vote on a minority emergency government before the mandate expires.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Serang, Indonesia — Search Resumes Monday22 November 2026

Search Teams Prepare to Re-Enter the Banten Candy Factory on Monday, With 24 Body Bags Recovered and Dozens Still Unaccounted For

The search at the burned-out PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in Cikande, Serang, is set to resume on Monday morning after an overnight suspension. Police said on Sunday that 24 body bags containing human remains had been removed from the three-storey plant, which makes Milkita milk-based sweets, while firefighters continued cooling hotspots more than a day after the fire began on Saturday afternoon. Officials caution that the bag count is not a confirmed death toll. The company said about 300 people were on the Saturday shift and that 65 workers were in the area it is tracking most closely: 24 had returned home, four were in hospital, 29 remained missing and eight more had yet to be confirmed, though earlier rescue updates put the missing at 36 to 37 and the confirmed dead at three. At least 15 people were rescued. Because the heat made visual identification impossible, police disaster victim identification teams are gathering DNA samples and personal belongings from relatives, work expected to take two to three weeks. Chief executive Steven Erwin Wijaya said a hotline had been set up for families. The cause of the blaze, which started in the office and warehouse area, is still under investigation.

Latest · Europe · Belgrade — 140 of 250 Seats22 November 2026

Serbia’s Student-Led Government Plans a 45-Minute Cap on Reality TV and a Four-Day Week Without Pay Cuts

The movement that toppled Serbia’s old order is now setting out how it wants to change daily life. The Student List, which won 140 of 250 parliamentary seats in October after two years of anti-corruption protests that followed the Novi Sad station canopy collapse that killed 16 people, plans to restrict reality television on free-to-air national channels to 45 minutes a day, shown only after 11 p.m. and never live. Shows in the mould of the Big Brother-style series Elita would still be available on subscription services with age limits and parental controls, and broadcasters would have to cut away immediately for violence, threats or hate speech. The plan also reserves at least 30 per cent of national channels’ annual schedules for educational, cultural, documentary and children’s programming. Beyond television, the movement wants employers to move toward a four-day, 32-hour week with no loss of pay. The outsiders, who have no single agreed candidate for prime minister and must form a government within 90 days of parliament convening, also promise to loosen Russia’s hold on Serbian institutions while keeping economic ties, to oppose sanctions on Moscow, and to halt arms trading with Israel. Analysts warn that winning the election and running the country are very different tasks.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Narail, Bangladesh — Dozens of Homes Burned21 November 2026

Dozens of Homes Belonging to Awami League Figures Are Torched in Narail After Two BNP Leaders Are Killed

Political violence has flared in southwestern Bangladesh. In Bendar Char village, in Kalia upazila of Narail district, houses belonging to leaders and activists of the Awami League — whose activities are banned — were vandalised, looted and set on fire from Friday afternoon until midnight. Fire service officer Humayun Kabir said crews who reached the village around 8 p.m., delayed by trees felled across the roads, found 20 to 30 houses ablaze and later estimated that more than 50 structures across 20 to 25 homesteads had been gutted, along with shops at Bardia Bazar. Residents put the number of houses and shops attacked at around 50. Among the properties hit were the ancestral home of former lawmaker BM Kabirul Haque Mukti, the homes of former mayor Mushfiqur Rahman Liton and local party general secretary Krishnapada Ghosh. The local administration gave lower counts, saying 15 to 20 houses were vandalised and several set on fire. The attacks followed Wednesday’s assault on a BNP motorcycle procession in which two BNP-linked leaders, Sohel Rana and Shahinur Islam, were killed. Police said relatives and supporters of the dead men gathered in the village on Friday and attacked properties of those they blamed. The BNP’s local leadership denied its activists carried out arson, calling the allegations baseless, and police said an investigation is under way while rejecting social-media claims that Hindu families had been targeted.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Manila Summit — Representation Denied22 November 2026

Myanmar’s Army-Backed Government Calls ASEAN’s Summit Decision “Unfair and Imbalanced” and Discriminatory

Myanmar’s military-backed administration has pushed back hard against its regional bloc. In a statement on Saturday, the foreign ministry called the decision taken at the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila unfair and imbalanced, saying it did not reflect the positive measures the government says it has taken and that continued exclusion from summits was discriminatory. ASEAN leaders said Myanmar’s full political representation could be restored gradually if it makes progress on the five-point consensus, the framework agreed in 2021 for ending the post-coup crisis. Naypyidaw said its views were often disregarded and its equal rights denied, insisted there are no political prisoners in the country, and said more than 126,000 prisoners had received pardons or amnesties since the coup. The statement followed remarks on Wednesday by President Min Aung Hlaing, who told parliament his administration was reconsidering its ASEAN ties while continuing to talk to individual members, and who has announced a peace conference for February. Myanmar has been in civil war since the military ousted Aung San Suu Kyi’s elected government in February 2021; the UN estimates about 100,000 people have been killed and more than 3.5 million displaced. Suu Kyi, 81, remains detained in Naypyidaw.

Latest · Americas · Halifax — “No Daylight” Between Allies22 November 2026

Canada’s Defence Minister Says Military Cooperation With the US Is Unchanged Despite a Reported Pentagon Memo Restricting Ties

Ottawa is playing down reports of a new rift with Washington. Defence Minister David McGuinty told reporters in Halifax on Saturday that he had seen no change in military cooperation with the United States and had not seen a classified memo that the Washington Post reported Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth circulated last week imposing new restrictions on defence ties. McGuinty said there was still no daylight between the two militaries, which continue to work hand in glove on Caribbean drug interdiction, integrated missile defence and scrambling jets for North American threats. Canadian military sources likewise said they had received no formal or informal notice of restrictions and that relationships were operating normally. The Pentagon said it was taking steps to ensure engagements with Canada advance tangible US defence objectives and support Canada in meeting its NATO commitments, while stressing that the guidance does not affect intelligence cooperation, the binational NORAD command or cooperation through NATO. The report lands amid strained relations under President Donald Trump, who has repeated annexation rhetoric about Canada, imposed tariffs and criticised Ottawa’s defence spending, and after Washington paused an 86-year-old joint defence board in May complaining that Canada had failed to make credible progress on its commitments. Canada now spends about 2 per cent of GDP on defence, while Washington presses for the newer 3.5 per cent benchmark.

Latest · Americas · Washington — “Bumps in the Road” Ahead22 November 2026

America’s Intelligence Chief Warns of More Tech Outages as Ageing Systems Are Upgraded for the AI Age

Americans should expect further glitches, the country’s top intelligence official has warned, days before the Thanksgiving travel peak. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN’s State of the Union on Sunday that as companies and government agencies replace ageing systems and invest in cybersecurity for a step change in computing, there will be bumps in the road, with glitches, gaps and interruptions in service. His remarks follow nearly two weeks of separate disruptions: a nationwide FAA ground stop — the first full grounding since 11 September 2001, affecting more than a million passengers and blamed on router upgrades by contractor L3Harris that flooded communications centres with data — a Microsoft Outlook outage tied to a network capacity upgrade, a glitch affecting several major banks, and a temporary disruption to the National Security Agency’s network. Officials, including Clayton and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, stress the incidents had different causes and show no signs of a coordinated or state-sponsored cyberattack; Bessent likened bank security patches to a vaccine. Clayton declined to discuss specifics of separately reported cyber incidents at the FBI and the Department of Agriculture, saying investigators would provide answers.
