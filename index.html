Latest · Americas · Economy — Payrolls +165,000, Jobless 4.1%6 November 2026

The Jobs Report That Beat Every Forecast: 165,000 Hired, Unemployment Down to 4.1%

America’s labour market blew through expectations on Friday. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that employers added 165,000 jobs in October — nearly three times the Dow Jones consensus of about 59,000 and above even the highest single forecast of 131,000 — while the unemployment rate slipped to 4.1 per cent from 4.2 per cent. The strength was not a one-month mirage: August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000, taking September to 60,000 and August to 186,000, against a 12-month average gain of 48,000. Professional and business services led with 70,000 hires, followed by healthcare with about 42,000 and transportation and warehousing with 25,000; manufacturing added 19,000. Pay rose faster than expected too, with average hourly earnings up 0.4 per cent on the month to $38.00 and 3.1 per cent on the year, while participation edged up to 61.9 per cent and some 7.1 million people remained unemployed, 2 million of them long-term. Released days after a midterm defeat the president himself blamed on the economy and the Iran war, the report cuts both ways politically — proof of resilience, but also fuel for the Federal Reserve: traders lifted the implied odds of a December rate hike from the 3.75–4.00 per cent range to roughly 76–78 per cent, and Treasury yields pushed higher, with the 10-year near 4.85–4.88 per cent. The next test is Tuesday’s October inflation report.

Dip-Buyers Wipe Their Eyes: Chips Lead a Fragile Bounce after the $2.5 Trillion Rout

Wall Street steadied on Friday after two of the wildest sessions of the year — Wednesday’s post-election surge, then Thursday’s crash, when the Nasdaq fell 5.52 per cent into correction territory, the S&P 500 lost 4.36 per cent and roughly $2.5 trillion of market value evaporated on fears that a Democratic Congress will slow the AI data-centre build-out. Even after the bounce, the week’s damage stands: the Nasdaq remains about 2.6 per cent below Tuesday’s close and the S&P about 2.1 per cent, with hot jobs data simultaneously calming growth fears and cementing the case for a December rate hike.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Delay Bid Rejected 39–36 November 2026

No 72 Hours, No Appearance: Senate Votes Down Peña’s Delay and Pushes the Trial On

Paraguay’s impeachment endgame accelerated on Friday. The Senate voted 39 to 3, with two abstentions, to reject President Santiago Peña’s request for a 72-hour extension to prepare his defence against the Ueno Bank charges — he had protested on X that a 15-page indictment with some 300 pages of appendices, delivered late Thursday, denied him due process. Senate President Basilio Núñez confirmed the trial would proceed immediately and summoned the 47-year-old president; when Peña did not appear within the allotted time, senators reconvened, voted to continue the juicio político and paused to settle the next procedural steps toward a removal vote, which requires 30 of the 45 senators. Formally, Peña remains president: he tendered his resignation on Thursday, but a joint session called to accept it collapsed when Colorado Party lawmakers walked out and destroyed the quorum — a distinction with real consequences, because an accepted resignation would leave him a senator for life with its legal protections, while impeachment conviction would not. Around the trial, the criminal track widened: the Attorney General’s three investigations into Ueno Bank’s supervision, the placement of public funds and the conduct of officials continue, six bank executives including founder Miguel Vázquez sit in preventive detention, 27 people face charges, and the central bank has begun capped deposit-guarantee payments to the first frozen-out customers. Vice President Pedro Alliana waits in the line of succession.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Seafarer Killed, Drone Claim6 November 2026

A Dead Seafarer on the Velos Sapphire: the Hormuz War Reaches Another Tanker

The shipping war in the Gulf claimed another life on Friday. Athens-based Velos Tankers confirmed that a crew member of its Marshall Islands-flagged products tanker MT Velos Sapphire, first reported missing after an explosion early on Thursday, had been found fatally injured on board. The ship was sailing empty toward Bahrain’s Sitra port when an unidentified projectile apparently struck its port quarter, starting a fire the crew put out themselves; no one else was hurt and no pollution was reported. The tanker has reached Bahrain’s Khalifa Bin Salman Port, where an investigation is expected; UK maritime authorities had reported two projectiles hitting a tanker about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar. No attacker has been identified. The same day, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two more oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines on unauthorised routes through the Strait of Hormuz — a day after claiming five others — and said air defences had destroyed a US MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island, neither claim independently confirmed. Saudi air defences intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles and a drone aimed at Najran, Iraq stepped up radiation monitoring near the Iranian border, and President Trump, who spoke with Qatar’s emir, warned Washington may have to hit Iran harder while Tehran insists Hormuz will not fully reopen except on its terms. Oil took a breather: Brent near $97.57 and WTI near $89.84 in Friday trading.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 87% of Districts Counted6 November 2026

Count Passes 87%: ANC at 31.7%, and a 20-Point Reversal in Gauteng

South Africa’s municipal count reached about 87 per cent of voting districts on Friday with the pattern of a political era ending intact. Provisional Electoral Commission figures put the ANC on 31.73 per cent — its worst national result since 1994, down from 45.6 per cent in 2021 — with the Democratic Alliance second on 23.99 per cent and Jacob Zuma’s MK Party third on 9.41 per cent. The metro map tells the sharper story. In Gauteng, which holds Johannesburg and Pretoria, an IEC snapshot at 3:18 p.m. put the DA on 34.22 per cent against the ANC’s 21.40 per cent and ActionSA’s 17.33 per cent — a swing of roughly 20 points from 2021, when the ANC led the province by 7.4. The DA leads in both Johannesburg and Tshwane, the MK Party leads in eThekwini, and most municipalities remain headed for coalition negotiations rather than outright control. Voters told canvassers the same story in province after province: failing water and power supplies in one of Africa’s wealthiest countries. The commission is still expected to announce the official outcome and seat allocations for the 257 councils on Sunday, 8 November, inside a legal deadline of 11 November, and the rand has stayed calm near 16.52 to the dollar while the coalition arithmetic takes shape.

Latest · Europe · France — 575,000 Out, 448 Arrested6 November 2026

France Counts the Cost of Its Biggest Protest Day: 448 Arrests, 53 Police Hurt, Talks on Monday

France’s autumn of discontent reached its peak on Thursday, and on Friday the government moved to talk. The Interior Ministry’s revised tally put turnout at 575,000 people across some 260 towns and cities — the movement’s largest day, above the 550,000 of 17 October — while the CGT union federation claimed nearly 1.6 million, including 350,000 on the Paris march from République to Nation, figures the ministry put at 65,000 in the capital. The ministry also counted 448 arrests, 53 injured police officers and 86 fire incidents, with tear gas used and vandalism reported in some cities against a deployment of about 70,000 security personnel. Nearly 16 per cent of teachers struck. What began in September as high-school students protesting overcrowded classrooms and staff shortages has fused with union, farmer and revived Yellow Vest anger over wages, living costs and public-service cuts, six months before elections to replace President Macron, with the far right ahead in polls. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s answer is a “social conference” at Matignon on Monday, 9 November, with unions and employers on pay and purchasing power; CGT leader Sophie Binet called the strike “extremely effective”, and organisers are threatening to march on through Christmas and toward the spring election.

Pope Leo over the Atlantic: a 13½-Hour Flight, Then Uruguay’s First Papal Welcome since 1988

Pope Leo XIV’s longest journey is under way. His ITA Airways flight left Rome’s Fiumicino airport at 7:05 a.m. on Friday for the 13½-hour, roughly 6,900-mile crossing to Montevideo, where the official welcome at Carrasco International Airport is scheduled for 4:30 p.m. local time — 12:30 a.m. Saturday in Pakistan — followed by a 5:30 p.m. ceremony at Plaza Independencia, meetings with President Yamandú Orsi, authorities and diplomats, and 7:10 p.m. Vespers at the Metropolitan Cathedral. The 12-day apostolic journey runs to 17 November across ten cities in Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, with nearly 40 addresses in Spanish, and comes barely three weeks after the 71-year-old pope’s 13 October lung-cancer surgery; the Vatican calls its medical precautions routine for a recent patient, not a permanent condition. Uruguay is Latin America’s most secular country, where Christians are a minority, and no pope has visited since John Paul II in 1988; Montevideo’s archdiocese has even suspended Saturday-evening and Sunday-morning Masses so the faithful can attend, while police warn of road restrictions around the airport. Ahead lie Saturday’s Mass in Paysandú and a youth vigil expected to draw some 60,000 to the Estadio Centenario, Sunday’s Mass in Florida, then Argentina’s first papal visit in 39 years and the long Peru leg, where Leo served more than two decades as missionary and bishop.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Bank Rate 4%, Inflation Seen near 5%6 November 2026

The Bank of England Blinks First: First Rate Rise since 2023, and a Warning of More

Britain has joined the tightening cycle. On Thursday the Bank of England’s Monetary Policy Committee voted 8–1 to raise Bank Rate by a quarter point to 4 per cent from 3.75 per cent — its first increase since August 2023 — and the lone dissent was for more: Catherine Mann wanted a half-point move to 4.25 per cent. The driver is the war economy: energy prices pushed up by the nine-month Middle East conflict lifted UK inflation to 3.5 per cent in the year to September, and the Bank now expects it to peak near 4.9–5 per cent around the middle of 2027 and not return to the 2 per cent target until 2028. Governor Andrew Bailey trod a careful line — the rise should not be read as the start of an “inevitable long sequence of hikes”, but unless the Middle East conflict is settled it is unlikely to be the last either. Brent crude averaged about $108 a barrel in the Bank’s latest forecasting window, 38 per cent above its July assumption, with UK gas prices 81 per cent higher. For households, the December-hold era that began in late 2025 is over: mortgage and savings pricing will follow the new 4 per cent anchor, and the committee’s next decision lands on 17 December, six days before Christmas.

Latest · Middle East · Lebanon — Mandate Ends 31 December6 November 2026

UNIFIL Confirms the End: Peacekeepers Start Leaving Lebanon When the Mandate Dies on 31 December

A mission born in 1978 now has a leaving date. UNIFIL, the UN Interim Force in Lebanon, confirmed on Thursday that under Security Council Resolution 2790 its mandate ends on 31 December 2026 and it will then begin an orderly drawdown and withdrawal, expected to run through 2027 with most uniformed personnel gone by mid-year. Until the deadline, peacekeepers from 45 countries — about 7,500 personnel — will keep up daily patrols, monitoring and reporting along the Blue Line, liaison work, support to the Lebanese Army and humanitarian facilitation. The confirmation comes despite Beirut’s repeated requests for an extension: in July, 86 Lebanese lawmakers signed a letter seeking a continued and expanded presence, and President Joseph Aoun has warned that withdrawal will leave a security vacuum in the south. The council voted last year, unanimously but under sustained US and Israeli pressure, not to renew a mandate critics say failed to deliver the disarmament of Hezbollah required by Resolution 1701. What replaces the force is undecided: the Security Council is weighing options for post-UNIFIL monitoring, France and Italy have floated co-leading a successor support coalition, and the EU has proposed a multi-year mission to train and advise Lebanon’s army and security forces — none of it yet agreed, with eight weeks left on the clock.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations — Eighth Straw Poll6 November 2026

Grynspan’s Race to Lose? 12–3 in the Eighth Poll, and a Turkish Debut at the Assembly

The contest to run the United Nations from 1 January is narrowing. In the Security Council’s eighth straw poll on Thursday, Costa Rica’s Rebeca Grynspan — former vice president and head of the UN trade body UNCTAD — drew 12 “encourage” votes against three “discourage”, her strongest showing yet and her seventh poll lead in eight rounds. Argentina’s Rafael Grossi of the IAEA stayed second at 9–4–2, ahead of Guyana’s Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett at 8–5–2 and the UAE’s Lana Nusseibeh at 7–6–2. The newest name, Türkiye’s OSCE secretary general Feridun Sinirlioğlu, nominated only on 1 November, debuted sixth at 3–8–4 and took his case to the General Assembly in an informal dialogue on Friday, with council meetings to follow on 9 November. The unresolved question hangs over every ballot: Thursday’s vote was undifferentiated, so no one outside the room knows whether any of Grynspan’s three discourages came from a permanent member holding a veto — in last month’s single colour-coded poll, two did. A successful candidate needs nine votes and no veto before the General Assembly formally appoints. António Guterres leaves on 31 December, and Russia has publicly restated the diplomatic convention that the post should now go to Latin America and the Caribbean — the region of the top three candidates.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pakistan — Hazara Motorway, Abbottabad6 November 2026

Eight Dead on the Hazara Motorway: a Burst Tyre, a Gilgit Bus and a Dumper Truck

Pakistan’s highways claimed another busload of travellers early on Friday. A passenger coach running from Gilgit to Rawalpindi collided with a dumper truck near the Havelian Interchange on the Hazara Motorway in Abbottabad district, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, killing at least eight people and injuring 30, rescue officials said. Rescue 1122’s initial account is that a tyre burst made the driver lose control before the impact; no final investigation finding has been issued. Seven ambulances and a rescue vehicle responded under District Emergency Officer Hafeezur Rehman. The injured, among them five children and six women, were taken largely to Ayub Medical Complex in Abbottabad, where four were reported critical and an emergency was declared at major hospitals; the dead, reported to be from Gilgit, were distributed among hospitals in Abbottabad and Havelian. KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi expressed grief and ordered the best possible care for the injured. The crash came a day after a bus–truck collision near Pakpattan in Punjab killed 11 people and injured 33 — two mass-casualty coach crashes in 24 hours, and a fresh entry in Pakistan’s long ledger of intercity road deaths.
