Latest · Middle East · West Bank24 October 2026

Israel Seals Off Hebron and Displaces Residents After Attack That Killed Two Soldiers

Israel imposed sweeping closures across the occupied West Bank city of Hebron on Saturday, a day after Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers near the Yehuda Junction outside the city. All gates in and out of Hebron were impassable by car, taxis queued behind military checkpoints, and Palestinians were forced to cross on foot; the Palestinian news agency WAFA said troops ordered dozens of residents to leave their homes in some neighbourhoods. The army said the two attackers, both from Hebron and armed with an M16 rifle and a handgun, were killed after a firefight of about 20 minutes, and that it had launched what it called a precise, targeted operation in the area the gunmen came from, expected to last several days. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered the city cordoned off and a wider West Bank closure prepared. Al Jazeera reported more than 2,000 people forced from their homes, and the United Nations said it was extremely alarmed, warning the closures severely restrict movement, access to services and livelihoods. The attack comes days before Israel's parliamentary election on Tuesday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Vanuatu24 October 2026

Powerful Earthquakes Strike Off Vanuatu; Tsunami Threat Declared Passed

A strong earthquake sequence struck offshore northern Vanuatu in the South Pacific on Saturday, beginning with a magnitude 6.9 quake at 02:31 UTC about 200 kilometres west-northwest of Port-Olry at a shallow depth of roughly 10 kilometres, followed minutes later by a magnitude 6.3 event and at least eight further tremors of magnitude 5.0 or more over the next three hours. The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center initially put the first quake at magnitude 7.2 and warned that hazardous waves were possible along coasts within 300 kilometres of the epicentre, with Espiritu Santo among the forecast locations. In a final message less than an hour later, the centre revised the magnitude to 6.9 and said the tsunami threat had passed, though minor sea-level fluctuations remained possible. Early rapid assessments suggested low expected humanitarian impact, with an estimated 40,000 people exposed to moderate shaking from the first event. There were no immediate reports of casualties or serious damage. Vanuatu sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where subduction between tectonic plates produces frequent earthquakes.

Latest · Europe · Serbia25 October 2026

Serbia Votes in Snap Election as Student Movement Challenges Vučić's Party

Serbians vote on Sunday in a snap parliamentary election that has become the most serious challenge yet to the Serbian Progressive Party (SNS), which has held power since 2012. The election follows nearly two years of student-led protests sparked by the November 2024 collapse of a concrete canopy at Novi Sad's renovated railway station, which killed 16 people and snowballed into a nationwide movement against corruption. Former president Aleksandar Vučić, who has dominated Serbian politics since 2014, resigned the presidency in September and is seeking to return as prime minister on the SNS ticket. Against him stands the Student List, a largely leaderless slate of 250 candidates, most of whom have never held public office. About 150,000 supporters rallied for the student movement in central Belgrade on Thursday. Activist Masa Perepeliuc, 21, told AFP she was sure voters would wake on Monday in a freer Serbia. The campaign has been shadowed by allegations of intimidation, unequal conditions and arrests that prosecutors link to alleged plans for election-day violence, drawing scrutiny from European institutions and international observers.

China Names New Provincial Party Chiefs in Six Regions Ahead of Key Plenum

China's ruling Communist Party announced senior personnel changes across six provinces on Saturday, two days before a closed-door plenum of its top leaders runs in Beijing from October 26 to 29. The secretive meeting is officially expected to focus on party governance and discipline, though further personnel moves may also feature. Among the appointments, State Council Vice Secretary-General Wang Zhijun becomes party secretary of Hunan, replacing Shen Xiaoming, while Mo Gaoyi, a vice minister in the party's publicity department, takes over as party secretary of Yunnan from Wang Ning. Several deputy party secretaries were positioned to step up as provincial governors: Xie Weijiang in Hunan, Guo Ningning in Fujian, Zhuge Yujie in Qinghai and Chen Yongqi in Jiangxi, where the former governor, Ye Jianchun, was placed under investigation in August. Lin Tao becomes Fujian's deputy party secretary, and Qin Haixiang succeeds Guo as party chief of the provincial capital, Fuzhou.

Latest · Europe · Czechia24 October 2026

Czech Coalition Pushes Public Media Funding Overhaul Through Overnight Vote

The Czech ruling coalition pushed changes to public media funding through a first parliamentary hurdle in a rare overnight vote, despite opposition warnings that the plan could weaken broadcaster independence. The populist government of billionaire Prime Minister Andrej Babis, backed by the far-right SPD, has frequently accused public and private media of bias. Its proposal scraps the monthly household broadcasting fee of 205 crowns (about $9.45), easing costs for households, and would instead fund public media from the state budget. Opposition parties have compared the approach to media reforms in Hungary under Viktor Orban, who oversaw a major expansion of pro-government outlets. The bill was brought to the floor outside the planned order of business on Friday; Speaker Tomio Okamura of the SPD limited debate and scheduled the vote for 4:36 a.m. on Saturday. The coalition majority then sent the bill to committees for further discussion.

Latest · Americas · Trade24 October 2026

Trump Lifts 50% Tariffs on Some Canadian Goods Ahead of the US Midterms

President Donald Trump signed an order on Friday removing 50% tariffs on a range of Canadian agricultural and forestry-related products, less than two weeks before the November 3 US midterm elections. The exemptions cover plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and refrigeration equipment, packing crates and boxes, and fresh and dried seaweed, the White House said, and take effect on October 30. At the same time, other Canadian goods were added to the 50% list, including carpets, paints, shampoos, cocoa powder, wooden coffins, ice cream sticks, air-conditioning machines, chest freezers, archery equipment and canoes. Republican Senator Susan Collins, in a tight re-election race in Maine, had urged the White House to act and said the relief would help the state's blueberry, potato, apple and seaweed producers, who rely on specialised Canadian equipment and packaging. Trump imposed the 50% tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian imports in August after trade talks broke down; roughly 70% of Canadian exports go to the United States. Ottawa's US-Canada trade minister, Dominic LeBlanc, said the Canadian government had taken note of the measures.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Philippines24 October 2026

Four Philippine Air Force Pilots Killed as Two Trainer Aircraft Crash

Four Philippine Air Force pilots were killed when two SF-260 trainer aircraft crashed during a training flight at Fernando Air Base in Batangas province, south of Manila, on Friday afternoon, the military said on Saturday. Both aircraft came down within the grounds of the base at about 3:51 p.m., and there were no other casualties. Local media reported the two planes collided in midair, though the cause has not been officially confirmed. The Air Force said it had temporarily grounded its entire SF-260 fleet while investigators determine what happened, and that it was coordinating with the pilots' families and honouring their service. The type has a recent history of fatal accidents in the Philippines: two pilots were killed when an SF-260 crashed in a mountainous area in the north in May, and another crashed during a training flight in 2023, also killing two pilots. The military has also used SF-260s in combat, including during the 2017 battle for Marawi City.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom24 October 2026

UK Tells Households to Keep Three Days of Emergency Supplies at Home

The British government advised the public on Friday to keep three days of emergency supplies at home, saying the message had been hammered home by this week's PHP software glitch and a record-setting summer of heat, drought and wildfires. Two critical flaws disclosed on Wednesday in PHP, one of the world's most widely used web programming languages, forced government and business services offline across Europe and triggered millions of hacking attempts as operators raced to patch systems. The government's new "Be Ready" campaign advises households to stock bottled water and non-perishable food, and ministers also announced a wildfire response plan that includes deploying military aircraft. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Britain had too often been "on the back foot" in emergencies and needed to act earlier. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting warned that cyber incidents would become more common as AI helps attackers find weaknesses, and said the threat level facing the UK was greater than at almost any point in his lifetime. The advice mirrors European Commission guidance last year for EU citizens to keep a 72-hour emergency kit.

Latest · Middle East · Shipping24 October 2026

Attacks on Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz Hit a Record High in October

October has been the worst month on record for attacks on commercial shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz, according to data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, which has tracked maritime security incidents worldwide since 2017. The institute's map recorded 28 confirmed and reported attacks in October and 113 since the US-Israeli war with Iran began on February 28, a tally broadly in line with the International Maritime Organization's count of 120 incidents in the region over the same period. More than 30 supertankers have been damaged, including 11 in October alone — together about 3.5% of the global fleet of very large crude carriers, each able to carry roughly 2 million barrels of oil. Greece-based consultancy Marisks called October the most intense period of maritime attacks since hostilities escalated in March, with a dozen ships attacked in a single week from October 5 to 11. Analysts say more owners have been willing to load cargoes inside the Gulf, drawn by daily freight rates above $1 million and steeper discounts from regional producers.

Latest · Africa · Sudan24 October 2026

El Niño Drought Raises a New Famine Risk in War-Torn Sudan, UN Agency Warns

An El Niño-driven drought is deepening hunger in war-torn Sudan, where the UN World Food Programme forecasts an additional 350,000 people will face severe hunger by the end of 2026, rising to 1.2 million more by August 2027, according to figures shared with donors. The worst-hit areas are Darfur and the Kordofan region. In North Kordofan, farmers returning to land they fled during the war are finding shrivelled crops in dry, sandy soil; one farmer and sesame trader near Um Rawaba estimated about 40% of the area's land would produce no harvest, and traders at the El Obeid livestock market described cattle left skinny by the scarcity. The WFP says it is focusing food deliveries on the 5 million most needy people but cannot reach all of them, and has cut rations to stretch limited funds. As of October 17 it had $276 million for its Sudan operation, less than half of last year's budget, and says it needs a further $134 million to tackle drought-driven hunger. Acting executive director Carl Skau called the simultaneous decline of aid and harvests a "recipe for disaster" after visiting Darfur.
