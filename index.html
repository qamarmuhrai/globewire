Latest · Middle East · Bahrain — Drone Attack Claim15 November 2026

Sirens Over Manama: Iran’s Guards Say Six Drones Hit a US Air-Defence Radar, and Bahrain Tells Residents to Shelter

The Gulf war reached Bahrain’s capital again before dawn. Explosions were heard around 4 a.m. local time on Sunday, with a second series of blasts reported at about 6:30 a.m., as alarm sirens sounded and the Interior Ministry urged residents to stay calm, head to the nearest safe place and follow official channels — without naming the source of the threat. The alert was later lifted and the danger declared passed. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said their Aerospace Force had sent six drones against the US air-defence radar system at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, in a statement carried by Iranian state broadcasting, and repeated that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed and that any further American action would draw a harsher response. Neither Manama nor Washington has confirmed a strike or reported casualties from Sunday’s incident. It follows a similar exchange on Friday, when the Guards said they targeted a US early-warning radar in Bahrain and the American military said it shot down two Iranian drones, with Central Command reporting no casualties or damage. Bahrain hosts a major US naval base and has repeatedly been caught in Tehran’s regional campaign during the eight months since the war began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Missiles Downed Over Riyadh15 November 2026

Saudi Air Defences Destroy Two Houthi Ballistic Missiles Aimed at Riyadh After a Brief Emergency Alert in the Capital

The Yemen front widened to the Saudi capital on Sunday. The Saudi-led coalition says its air defences destroyed two ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh, after a short emergency alert was sounded in the city; no casualties from the interceptions have been reported. The attempt comes as ground fighting spreads inside Yemen, with clashes reported in Marib and al-Jawf, and as the human cost on Yemeni civilians keeps mounting: a rights group cited in regional reporting says Houthi attacks in Taiz killed 51 civilians and injured 85 over just 12 days. The missile launch also lands a day after the UN Security Council voted to extend sanctions on the Houthis, strongly condemning their renewed military escalation inside Yemen. For Riyadh, the interceptions underline how quickly the region’s separate fronts — Iran and the Gulf states, Israel and Gaza, Yemen’s internal war — are bleeding into one another: within the same Sunday, Iranian drones were claimed over Bahrain, an Israeli strike killed a senior Hamas commander in Gaza, and the Strait of Hormuz stayed effectively closed to most shipping.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Arsenal Estimate15 November 2026

US Intelligence Estimates Iran Still Holds Between 1,500 and 2,500 Missiles Despite Months of Strikes

Months of bombing have not emptied Tehran’s magazines, on Washington’s own numbers. US intelligence assessments reported by the Wall Street Journal on Sunday estimate that Iran retains between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles despite sustained US and Israeli strikes on its weapons infrastructure, alongside significant numbers of drones and short-range missiles — a stockpile that sits uneasily beside President Donald Trump’s claims that Iran’s military capability has been largely destroyed. Officials caution that the estimate carries a low level of confidence, because it is hard to know how many missiles are ready to fire and how many sit in storage, and Israeli military estimates put ballistic missiles alone at more than 1,500. A former US deputy assistant secretary of defence for the Middle East, Simone Ledeen, noted that even the lower figure would mean Iran has rebuilt more than half of its pre-war stockpile, though the recovery is reported to be slower than before the war. The assessment helps explain Washington’s current posture: reporting this weekend says Trump has told advisers he may hold off on further attacks barring a new provocation, favouring sanctions and the naval blockade instead, while Gulf allies warn against renewed fighting and Israel continues to press for more military action.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Rafah Commander Killed15 November 2026

Hamas Confirms Its Rafah Brigade Commander, Samir Abu Younis, Was Killed in an Israeli Drone Strike on al-Mawasi

Israel has removed another of Hamas’s senior field commanders. Hamas confirmed on Sunday that Samir Abu Younis, 53, who led its Rafah brigade, was killed in an Israeli drone strike on the al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis in southern Gaza; Israel accuses him of involvement in planning the 7 October attack. The strike came two days after an Israeli attack on Nasser Hospital in the same city, which the European Union said killed six people including a surgeon and which sharpened the EU’s demand for immediate, unimpeded humanitarian access. The killing lands in the middle of Gaza’s worsening winter emergency: the UN’s humanitarian office says rain since 6 November has damaged more than 40,000 family shelters and potentially affected some 226,000 people, with about 70,000 tents flooded within 12 hours at the peak, while deliveries through Kerem Shalom fell sharply in the week to 8 November. Israel maintains that more than 93,000 tents and thousands of truckloads of winter supplies have entered since September and blames distribution failures inside Gaza; aid agencies and the EU say crossings and access remain the binding constraint.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flights Recover, Blame Game Starts15 November 2026

US Flights Head Back to Normal by Sunday’s Close — 1,200 Already Cancelled — as Washington Fights Over Who Pays for Ageing Systems

The recovery is real, and so is the argument about how the system failed. FlightAware had recorded more than 1,200 cancelled US flights by Sunday morning, after what FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford put at 12,000 cancellations on Friday and more than 6,600 on Saturday; Bedford predicted a full resumption of schedules by close of business on Sunday, and the nationwide ground stop — only the second in US airspace since the 9/11 attacks — had been lifted at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Friday once full functionality was restored. The administration’s account is that a botched upgrade from copper wire to fibre cable flooded routers elsewhere in the air-traffic system; Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy used the failure to appeal for $50 billion to modernise air traffic control and called on Congress to “meet this moment.” Democrats answered in kind: Illinois Governor JB Pritzker pointed out that Republicans control Congress and could have funded the system, New York Governor Kathy Hochul called on Duffy to resign or be fired, and lawmakers from both parties demanded investigations, including into whether the country is adequately protected against cyberattack. President Trump, unusually, had made no public statement on the outage by Sunday morning. Britain’s Civil Aviation Authority warned UK–US travellers that follow-on disruption could persist while airlines rebook passengers and reposition aircraft.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Defence Pact Signed in Manila15 November 2026

Australia and the Philippines Sign a New Defence Pact in Manila as the ASEAN Summit Moves Into Its Second Day

The summit’s most concrete security result so far was signed on Sunday. Australia and the Philippines concluded a new defence agreement on the sidelines of the leaders’ meetings in Manila, updating a decades-old memorandum to fast-track military cooperation and, in Manila’s words, bolster deterrence in a region shaped by China’s growing military presence. Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro said the pact makes coordination “faster, speedier,” strengthens the Philippines’ credible deterrent posture in areas under its jurisdiction, and lets its forces familiarise themselves with Australian equipment ahead of joint training; the two countries have held a visiting forces agreement since 2012 and already run joint maritime patrols in the South China Sea. Australian Defence Minister Richard Marles said the deal deepens practical cooperation — enhanced maritime activity, information-sharing and defence infrastructure — and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese attended the signing. The pact sits inside a crowded summit week: ASEAN leaders held their closed retreat on Myanmar, energy security and transboundary haze, dialogue-partner meetings follow on Monday and Tuesday, the East Asia Summit meets Tuesday, and the first Quad leaders’ summit since 2024 is expected Tuesday evening after President Donald Trump lands.

Latest · Europe · France — Fifth Weekend of Protests15 November 2026

106,000 March Across France in a Fifth Weekend of Protest — Unions Claim 200,000 — as a Police-Powers Bill Joins the Cost of Living on the Streets

France’s protest movement is smaller than at its peak but nowhere near finished. The interior ministry counted about 106,000 demonstrators nationwide on Saturday, including 24,000 in Paris — the lowest turnout in recent weeks — while student unions put the figure at 200,000, with nearly 100,000 in the capital. For a fifth consecutive Saturday, students, trade unions and Yellow Vest activists marched over the cost of living and inequality, and increasingly over a bill in parliament that would presume police acted in self-defence when they use their weapons — a measure backed by Marine Le Pen’s far right that opponents call a licence to kill and link to injuries, including reported mutilations, at recent protests. Police fired tear gas in Paris, Bordeaux and Nantes, and intervened in Lyon to protect a television crew; the rallies were otherwise largely peaceful. The politics behind the streets are stark: President Emmanuel Macron’s popularity is at its lowest since 2017, the far right leads polling for next year’s presidential election, and the government, struggling with debt, has little room to concede. Unions rejected a promised 0.6 percent minimum-wage rise as “crumbs” and have called a new day of strikes for Tuesday.

Latest · Europe · Spain — Canary Islands Record14 November 2026

1,488 Migrants Reach the Canary Islands in a Single Day — the Busiest Day on the Atlantic Route Since 2020

The Atlantic migration route has set a record Spain hoped not to see again. Spanish maritime rescue services brought 1,488 people ashore from 14 boats on Saturday, surpassing the previous single-day high of 1,461 set on 7 November 2020, according to figures reported by the EFE news agency. The smallest island took the largest share: 12 boats carrying 1,235 people reached El Hierro, with 203 people landed on Gran Canaria and 50 on Tenerife; one boat alone carried 240 people, including 12 women and 36 children, with those rescued coming from Senegal, Gambia and Guinea-Conakry. Together with Friday’s 843 arrivals, 2,331 people reached the islands in two days, and Spain’s territorial policy minister was due on El Hierro on Sunday to discuss the strain on reception and emergency services. The surge is part of a renewed rise in West African departures since October, in the autumn season when calmer seas make the crossing — one of the world’s deadliest, often from Mauritania in overcrowded boats — more likely to be attempted. The longer trend is less dramatic: 13,327 people reached the Canaries in the first ten months of 2026, 5.5 percent fewer than in the same period of 2025.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan — Church Attack Verdict15 November 2026

Jordan’s State Security Court Sentences Three Men to Death by Hanging for the October Church Massacre That Killed Nine

Jordan has delivered its verdict on the attack that shocked the kingdom last month. The State Security Court on Sunday sentenced three men to death by hanging for the 23 October assault on a Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud village, in the southern Karak governorate, where gunmen stormed a funeral, killed nine people and wounded 25 others. The Jordanian military said the three — two brothers and a relative by marriage, arrested three days after fleeing the scene — were convicted of carrying out terrorist attacks resulting in death, weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation. The attack stunned the Muslim-majority kingdom, where Christians make up around three percent of the population and communal violence of this kind is rare. The sentences were announced by the military on Sunday; death sentences in Jordan are subject to further legal process before they can be carried out.

Vigilantes Ambush a Kidnapping Gang in Nigeria’s Zamfara State — 53 Dead, Including Nine of the Ambushers

In Nigeria’s northwest, the line between community defence and open warfare keeps dissolving. Residents say village vigilantes ambushed an armed kidnapping gang near Zoma in Zamfara State on Thursday, killing 44 of its members and losing nine of their own men — 53 dead in a single clash, in a region where armed groups have taxed, kidnapped and raided rural communities for years while state forces struggle to hold territory. It is the latest in a series of mass-casualty confrontations across Nigeria’s northwest and north-central belts this year, where kidnapping for ransom has become an industry and where vigilante formations — sometimes tolerated, sometimes armed by local authorities — increasingly fight set-piece battles rather than simply guarding villages.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Cyclone Akvan Forms15 November 2026

Cyclone Akvan Is Taking Shape in the Bay of Bengal — a Severe Storm by Tuesday, Aimed at the Andhra Coast Around 19 November

The Bay of Bengal’s next cyclone now has a name and a track. India’s Meteorological Department says a depression over the southeast and adjoining southwest Bay of Bengal is likely to strengthen into a severe cyclonic storm — to be named Akvan — by 17 November, and to move toward the Andhra Pradesh coast around 19 November. Rain is expected first in the north coastal districts of Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and the Karaikal area as the system organises, with the department tracking a low-pressure area that formed over the southeast bay and is forecast to intensify through a depression stage on Sunday into the named storm on Monday or Tuesday. Coastal administrations in Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu are the ones to watch over the next 48 hours for evacuation and fishing advisories as the forecast cone firms up. The same Sunday brought a small piece of aviation normalisation in the region: Air India said it has resumed non-stop flights to New York and Newark from Delhi and Mumbai after the easing of airspace restrictions, cutting journey times by up to five hours.
