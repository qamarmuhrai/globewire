Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Exports Fall for Fifth Straight Week24 November 2026

Middle East Oil Exports Slide as Hormuz Attacks Rise, Despite Talk of a Stalemate

Oil shipments from the Middle East have been falling steadily even as markets treat the Iran conflict as a quiet stalemate, according to shipping data compiled by Kpler and reviewed by Reuters. Exports averaged 18.99 million barrels a day in October, above pre-war levels, after flows through Hormuz, the Gulf of Oman and Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea port of Yanbu recovered. Since a peak of 20.86 million barrels a day in the week beginning 12 October, however, every week has been weaker than the last, dropping to 13.96 million barrels a day in the week starting 16 November. The decline has tracked a rise in attacks on vessels in the strait: at least 12 ships were struck in November after 17 in October, with British maritime officials reporting another tanker hit by an unidentified projectile on 21 November. Brent crude, which touched a six-month high of $118 a barrel on 30 October, closed at $89.33 on Monday, a fall of about 24 percent that analysts say may understate how quickly the situation at sea is tightening.

Latest · Europe · Zaporizhzhia — Two Children Among the Wounded24 November 2026

Russian Attack Kills Two People and Wounds 22 in Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia Overnight

A Russian attack on the southern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia killed two people and injured 22 others overnight, including two children, Ivan Fedorov, co-head of the regional defence council, said on Telegram on Tuesday. Reuters said it could not independently confirm the report, and noted that both Russia and Ukraine deny deliberately targeting civilians. Separately, Russia’s defence ministry said its forces had struck Ukrainian drone control sites, Starlink satellite terminals and other military assets in the Sumy region in the north-east, according to the Interfax news agency. The Zaporizhzhia casualties add to a heavy toll from the weekend, when Ukrainian officials said Russian strikes across the Kyiv region killed at least 15 people, and come as Kyiv continues to press Western partners for more air-defence interceptors ahead of winter.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Ulleung Island — Alert Lifted Within Minutes24 November 2026

Russian Warplanes Trigger a Brief Shelter Alert on South Korea’s Ulleung Island After an Alert Mix-Up

Several Russian warplanes briefly entered South Korea’s air defence identification zone over the East Sea early on Tuesday, approaching Ulleung Island before leaving, South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said. South Korean fighter jets scrambled in response, and the military stressed the Russian aircraft did not violate South Korean territorial airspace. Confusion followed on the island itself: North Gyeongsang Province sent residents an emergency message at 7:52 a.m. telling them to take shelter in underground facilities or inside buildings, then lifted the warning about five minutes later — but the all-clear text mistakenly described the aircraft as suspected North Korean planes, using wording left over from a training template. Opposition politicians demanded the government disclose the full response process and explain how the wording was changed, saying residents had been left anxious and confused. Russia does not recognise Seoul’s air defence zone, arguing it has no basis in international law.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Jiuquan — Launch Set for Wednesday Morning24 November 2026

Pakistani Astronaut to Become the First Foreign Visitor to China’s Tiangong Station on Shenzhou-24

China is preparing to send its first foreign astronaut to the Tiangong space station, with Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali set to fly aboard Shenzhou-24 alongside commander Tang Hongbo and flight engineer Wu Yi. The China Manned Space Agency said the crew is in good condition and launch preparations are complete ahead of lift-off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in north-west China at 8:18 a.m. local time on Wednesday aboard a Long March-2F rocket. Ali will carry out a short-term mission focused on scientific cooperation between China and Pakistan, including four projects in biotechnology and materials science and an in-orbit experiment involving photosynthetic microorganisms, alongside routine station work. The flight, framed as part of Belt and Road cooperation, will also complete a crew rotation with the Shenzhou-23 team already in orbit, as Beijing accelerates work toward a first crewed lunar landing by the end of the decade.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Dhaka and Delhi — Return Planned for 16 December24 November 2026

Ousted Leader Sheikh Hasina Plans a 16 December Return to Bangladesh as Dhaka Vows to Arrest Her

Ousted Bangladeshi prime minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return home from more than two years of exile in India on 16 December, the anniversary of the end of Bangladesh’s 1971 liberation war, her spokesperson told Reuters on Tuesday. Hasina has asked Dhaka to arrange her return on the national carrier, Biman Bangladesh Airlines, and is seeking travel paperwork that was cancelled months after she fled to Delhi during a deadly uprising against her government. Her plans set up a direct confrontation with the authorities now in power: a senior Bangladeshi official was quoted in Tuesday’s press as saying Bangladesh will arrest and jail Hasina if she returns from India. The standoff leaves New Delhi in a delicate position, caught between its long relationship with Hasina and pressure from Dhaka, and it remains unclear whether the paperwork for any return flight will be granted.

Latest · Middle East · Jabalia and Gaza City — Three Killed on Monday24 November 2026

Israeli Strikes Kill a UNRWA Worker and Two Others in Gaza

Israeli airstrikes killed at least three people in Gaza on Monday, including Adel Al-Helo, a staff member of the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, UNRWA, who was working near a water well in the Jabalia refugee camp in the north of the strip. Three colleagues were wounded in the same strike, which the agency said hit a street near the well where its team and a guard were carrying out regular duties providing water services. Later the same day, a separate strike on a vehicle in western Gaza City killed two more people and wounded others, medics said. Relatives mourned Al-Helo outside a hospital morgue, his body wrapped in a white shroud with his blood-stained blue UNRWA vest visible. The agency says the number of its staff and support workers killed in Gaza since the war began has now risen to 397, and it has repeated calls for humanitarian workers to be protected at all times.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cikande, Banten — About 30 People Still Missing24 November 2026

Indonesia Confirms 24 Dead in a Candy Factory Fire as Rescuers Search for Dozens Missing

Indonesian authorities have confirmed 24 deaths in a fire that tore through a confectionery factory in Cikande, in Serang regency, Banten province, on Saturday evening, while rescuers continue to search unstable, still-smouldering wreckage for about 30 people who remain missing. The plant, run by PT Fonusa Agung Mulia and part of United Family Food, makes the Milkita milk candy exported to more than 40 countries, and about 300 people were on shift when the blaze broke out at around 4:30 p.m. The company said 24 of the 65 workers directly involved had returned home and four were in hospital, with 29 confirmed missing and the whereabouts of eight more still unclear; officials cautioned that the 24 body bags recovered may not map neatly onto individual victims until forensic work is done. Presidential labour adviser Said Iqbal said early findings point to a short circuit in an air-conditioner, that safety protocols at the site are being assessed, and that he will summon the factory’s chief executive.

Latest · Europe · Kehl — Two Children Survived and Are in Hospital24 November 2026

Five Members of a Family Found Dead in a Suspected Carbon Monoxide Leak in Western Germany

Two adults and three children have been found dead in a flat in Kehl, the German town across the Rhine from Strasbourg, in what police describe as a suspected gas leak. Officers forced entry at about 11:30 a.m. on Monday after several of the children failed to appear at school, and first responders detected carbon monoxide at the scene, according to the regional Badische Zeitung newspaper, citing fire brigade official Viktor Liehr. The children who died were aged between nine and 13. Two other children survived and were taken to specialist clinics, at least one of them in France, and two first responders were treated in hospital for minor gas-related effects. Firefighters checked every other flat in the building and found no further casualties, and residents were offered temporary shelter. Criminal police and prosecutors are now investigating whether a technical defect or a criminal act caused the deaths, with appliance testing and toxicology under way.

Latest · Middle East · Damascus — Demining Training at the Centre of the Plan24 November 2026

US Discusses a First Training Mission for Syrian Forces Since Damascus Changed Hands in 2024

The United States is discussing plans to train Syrian soldiers in mine clearance, in what would be the first American training mission with Syria’s military since former rebel fighters took power in Damascus in 2024, five people familiar with the proposal told Reuters. The idea is for a multinational mission built around the Damascus Coordination Center, set up last year in Jordan to link Syrian authorities, US forces and regional allies, and Syria’s defence minister met the operation’s commander in Damascus on 12 November, according to the state news agency SANA. The plan still faces legal hurdles in Washington and scepticism among some Syrian officers. It follows a rapid thaw: the US lifted broad economic sanctions on Syria in December 2025, removed its state sponsor of terrorism designation in August 2026, and eased arms-export restrictions in October so licences can be considered case by case.

Latest · Global Affairs · Taipei and Wall Street — Taiwan Benchmark Jumps 9 Percent24 November 2026

Jensen Huang’s Return to Nvidia Sparks a Market Surge and Lifts Taiwan Stocks

Taiwan’s stock market surged 9 percent on Tuesday, rebounding from an eight-month low, as investors welcomed the return of Jensen Huang to the top job at Nvidia, the chipmaker he co-founded 33 years ago and which was born out of his Taiwanese roots. Nvidia announced on Monday that Huang had been reinstated as president, chief executive and board director, only 11 days after he resigned amid scandal; its shares jumped about 31 percent the same day, helping lift the Nasdaq by 6.5 percent, the S&P 500 by 4.7 percent and the Philadelphia chip index by 12 percent in one of the most extraordinary sessions in recent US market history. Reuters reported that President Donald Trump called into an Nvidia board meeting, a move some US lawmakers criticised, while the Commerce Department separately added Alibaba’s Singapore-based subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions to a trade blacklist alongside Megaspeed, the cloud company at the centre of the Nvidia affair. Analysts cautioned that the relief for China’s artificial intelligence sector may be brief, with shares in Hong Kong-listed Z.AI flat on Tuesday morning.
