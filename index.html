Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Into the Weekend20 November 2026

COP31 Runs Past Its Deadline, Still Arguing Over Two Words: Fossil Fuels

The climate summit that was supposed to end on Friday has instead spilled into the weekend. Nearly 200 delegations in Antalya remained deadlocked after a late plenary managed only technical business, while the political deal — a slimmed-down draft released by negotiations chair Australia on Thursday — still contains no mention of coal, oil or gas at all. An earlier, longer draft had called for faster action to phase fossil fuels out; the revision dropped the language, and a dozen countries from France to Panama and the Solomon Islands have written to demand a clear transition away from fossil fuels be restored. What the draft does offer is Türkiye’s electrification push: lifting electricity to 35% of global final energy demand by 2035, which chair Chris Bowen defends as the practical route off fossil fuels, alongside further talks on tripling adaptation finance by 2035 — short of the $120 billion developing nations sought. A third draft is expected Saturday. The talks unfold against a record El Niño, floods in Nepal, heatwaves in Europe and an oil-price shock from the Iran war; one veteran campaigner warned a deal silent on fossil fuels would make this the worst COP yet.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — The Reset, With an Asterisk20 November 2026

Britain and the EU Seal Food, Youth and Carbon Deals — Then Trip Over Tuition Fees

A decade after the Brexit vote, London and Brussels signed their broadest package of cooperation yet — and immediately disagreed about whether it can take effect. Friday’s summit, postponed from July after Keir Starmer’s resignation brought Andy Burnham to Downing Street, produced three agreements: a food and agriculture deal ending most post-Brexit checks on goods such as sausages and seeds, which Britain estimates could add up to £5.1 billion a year to its economy and takes effect from March 2028; linked emissions trading systems from the start of 2028, exempting some £6.5 billion of British exports including steel and aluminium from EU carbon border taxes; and a youth experience scheme offering 100,000 visas a year — double Britain’s opening offer — for 18-to-31-year-olds to live and work on either side for up to three years, with the first travellers by January 2030. Burnham put the total prize at £17 billion a year and announced a year of talks on Britain’s long-term relationship with the bloc. But EU member states ambushed the closing hours, insisting implementation depends on cutting tuition fees for EU students from as much as £38,000 a year to the domestic £9,970 — a concession worth roughly £450 million a year that London resists. Both sides set a six-month deadline to settle it; Ursula von der Leyen still called the day a turning point.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — Drones Over the Embassy Quarter20 November 2026

Brussels Summons Moscow’s Envoy After Drones Shatter Embassy Windows in Kyiv

The diplomatic fallout from Thursday’s rare daytime drone assault on central Kyiv widened on Friday. Waves of Russian drones worked over the capital’s Podil district for hours while Presidents Putin, Xi and Trump met in Shenzhen, killing a 26-year-old man and injuring five people. Falling debris and blast waves damaged the embassies of Austria and the Netherlands, shattered a large window of St Volodymyr’s Cathedral — its cornices were also damaged, though its murals appear intact — and scattered debris across the grounds of Brazil’s embassy and Ukraine’s own foreign ministry. About ten people were inside the Austrian mission when the blast hit at around 4:30 p.m.; windows and vehicles were damaged but no staff were hurt, and the Dutch foreign minister, Tom Berendsen, confirmed minor damage and that his colleagues were safe. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas noted the timing — strikes landing as Putin walked a red carpet in China — and said the bloc would step up support for Ukraine and pressure on Moscow; Brussels has summoned Russia’s envoy over the damage. The daytime attack was followed within hours by the massive overnight missile barrage on Kyiv’s bridges, which Moscow confirmed on Friday morning it had launched against military-industrial and transport targets.

Latest · Americas · Washington — The Outage Purge20 November 2026

Trump Fires His Cyber Chief After a Week of Outages Grounded Flights and Froze Banks

The first senior head to roll for America’s week of technological chaos belongs to the man in charge of defending against it. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is out, administration officials confirmed Thursday, after a string of disruptions that included a Microsoft outage affecting hundreds of millions of users, an FAA nationwide ground stop that disrupted more than 18,000 flights, banking problems and a temporary failure on the National Security Agency’s network. A person close to the White House said President Trump fully supported firing Cairncross, judging that he lacked the strong cyber credentials the job now demands; replacing him was among the first tasks of new chief of staff Stephen Miller. Experts say the outages were likely triggered by companies rushing to patch vulnerabilities newly exposed by powerful AI tools, and the top US intelligence official said Wednesday there is no evidence of a coordinated or state-sponsored attack — an assessment Cairncross himself repeated in Manila this week, saying there was “zero evidence” the incidents were connected. Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, takes over in an acting capacity; a permanent successor must be confirmed by a Senate that Democrats will control from January, and they have already accused the administration of slow explanations and deference to tech giants. In his farewell statement, Cairncross said he was proud of work to position America “to lead the world in Super Intelligence.”

Latest · Americas · New York — Closing Rikers, Five Years Late20 November 2026

Mamdani Sets 2032 to Shut Rikers Island, Trading One Notorious Jail for Four Borough Ones

New York City finally has a dated, costed plan to close the jail complex that has symbolised its carceral failures for generations — though the date is five years later than the law currently demands. Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Friday released the city’s first comprehensive closure strategy: Rikers Island shuts by the end of 2032, replaced by four smaller borough-based jails beginning with Brooklyn in 2029, then Queens and the Bronx in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032, each with about 1,040 beds. The plan pairs construction with shrinking the population itself, from roughly 6,600 people today to 5,500 by 2029 and 4,100 by 2032, through reduced pretrial detention, faster case processing and restored online bail payments. It adds 236 hospital-based therapeutic beds for people with serious mental illness, on top of 106 already opened, and commits $146 million next fiscal year to mental health care, substance-use treatment, education, job training and victim services. Mamdani blamed his predecessor’s delays and the pandemic for missing the August 2027 legal deadline, and the City Council will now be asked to legislate the new timeline — a negotiation expected to draw opposition from neighbourhood groups and the correction officers’ union, which has long fought to keep Rikers open.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Across the Yellow Line20 November 2026

Israel Ends Its First Tank Raid Beyond Gaza’s Ceasefire Line, and the Truce Looks Thinner

For roughly 24 hours, the boundary that has divided Gaza for thirteen months stopped holding. Israeli tanks rolled west of the Yellow Line into central Gaza on Wednesday — the first publicly announced ground incursion across the ceasefire demarcation since the October 2025 truce — advancing along the Salah al-Din road near the Nuseirat and Bureij camps to dismantle what the military called a Hamas tunnel threatening its troops. Families who had already been displaced fled again with belongings piled on bicycles, wheelchairs and motorcycles; Gaza’s Hamas-run media office put the number who left at about 6,000, while UN humanitarian officials estimated up to 18,000 displaced from the Deir al-Balah area. By Thursday the army said the operation was complete and the road reopened. Hamas called the raid a flagrant violation and appealed to mediators; the timing sharpened the charge, coming a day after the US-backed Board of Peace reported to the Security Council that both sides are failing their commitments on disarmament, withdrawal and forming a technocratic administration. Defence Minister Israel Katz has said Israel will not withdraw until Hamas is dismantled and is prepared to seize more territory — a line that already, by some measures, has quietly expanded from about 53% of Gaza to nearer two-thirds.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Vienna — Watching by Satellite20 November 2026

North Korea Is Extending Its Uranium Plant Again, and the World’s Watchdog Can Only Look On

Pyongyang is building more capacity to make the core of a nuclear weapon, and the agency charged with policing that fact has not set foot in the country since 2009. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi told the agency’s Board of Governors in Vienna this week that a further extension to the original uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon has been under construction since mid-September and was externally complete by late October, calling the continued expansion of North Korea’s enrichment programme a cause for serious concern and a clear violation of Security Council resolutions. Satellite imagery from 6 November, analysed by the Wall Street Journal, shows the new annex attached to the main enrichment hall; specialists say that if it houses additional centrifuges it could yield more than 35 extra kilograms of highly enriched uranium a year, on top of an estimated existing capacity of up to 375 kilograms, itself boosted by a large new hall reported in June. Grossi added that Yongbyon’s five-megawatt plutonium reactor probably remains in operation, in its seventh irradiation cycle, and that a light-water reactor there has run since April — meaning North Korea continues producing both bomb fuels at once. Enrichment is also believed to proceed at Kangson and Kusong, beyond the reach of any inspector.

Latest · Europe · Frankfurt — 15 Million Files20 November 2026

The Deutsche Bank Leaks Reach the Boardroom: Shares Down a Tenth, a Regulator Investigating

Germany’s biggest bank is spending the week answering for its own filing cabinets. More than 15 million internal records — some nine million emails, board minutes, compliance files and chat logs dating to the early 2010s — were copied by two whistleblowers and shared with more than 230 journalists across some 50 outlets in the “Inside Deutsche Bank” investigation, and the material was also passed to US prosecutors and financial-crime authorities. Among the findings: about $582 million in transactions linked to Jeffrey Epstein, flagged internally as suspicious — more than double the figure a US Senate inquiry had previously established — with internal emails indicating senior executives approved taking Epstein on in 2013, five years after his Florida conviction, and kept his accounts despite warnings about Ghislaine Maxwell. The files also show that in 2017 the bank accidentally sent tax statements for 1,586 private accounts, including Donald Trump’s, to another client’s accountant, told the Federal Reserve but not the clients, and that as recently as 2021 it weighed expanding in Russia before the invasion of Ukraine. Deutsche Bank shares traded at €29 on Friday morning, down more than 10% since the week began; Hesse’s data protection commissioner has opened an investigation, and the revelations have sharpened an already live debate about chief executive Christian Sewing’s succession and reform record. The bank disputes the framing of much of the reporting.

Latest · Africa · Dakar — The Yenne Wreck20 November 2026

Dozens Missing Off Senegal as the Atlantic Crossing to Europe Surges to Record Levels

The cries from the water could be heard from the beach. Around 2 a.m. on Wednesday, a wooden pirogue carrying migrants toward Spain’s Canary Islands struck a rock near Yenne, south of Dakar, as people were being transferred from small fishing boats into the larger vessel; it capsized and later washed ashore badly damaged, with identity documents, mobile phones and a school enrolment certificate recovered from the wreckage. Accounts of how many were aboard range from about 50 to as many as 100, including five women; four bodies had been recovered by Thursday and dozens remain missing as searches continue. The wreck is the second in a month on this coast — a sinking off Thieppe less than two weeks earlier killed at least 39 people — and it comes amid an extraordinary surge in departures: Senegal’s navy says it rescued or intercepted 1,424 people in a single 48-hour period this week, and the UN refugee agency counts more than 10,800 arrivals in the Canary Islands in the first half of November alone, over four times last November’s pace and the highest monthly level since October 2023. Nearly 1,500 people landed on Saturday, the most in a single day since 2020. Close to 600 people have died or vanished on the route this year.

Latest · Africa · South Kordofan — The War Over Gold20 November 2026

Four Missiles on a Gold Mine: Sudan’s Drone War Follows the Money

In Sudan’s civil war, the gold is a target in itself. Witnesses in Gadeer locality, South Kordofan, say a drone fired four missiles into the Bajoun mining site on Wednesday, killing at least 10 people and injuring several others, with the true toll still unknown; the site’s operating company could not be identified and no side has been formally blamed for this strike. It was not the first: the UN Human Rights Office has documented earlier attacks on the same mining area on 9 and 16 October, in which drone strikes attributed to the Rapid Support Forces killed 42 civilians and injured 25. UN investigators say the pattern is deliberate — mining areas are struck because of their economic value to the opposing side, making gold part of the war economy that sustains the fighting. The wider numbers describe a weapon that has transformed the conflict: the UN human rights office counted 2,230 civilian deaths between January and the end of October, more than 1,630 of them caused by drones, which have hit markets, displacement sites, hospitals and infrastructure across the country. The Kordofan region, rich in farmland and minerals, has become the war’s eastern front since the RSF seized El-Fasher in late October and pushed toward army-held territory.

Latest · Americas · San Salvador — Term Limits, Removed20 November 2026

Bukele Registers for a Third Term That the Old Constitution Forbade

Supporters gathered outside El Salvador’s Supreme Electoral Tribunal on Thursday as President Nayib Bukele arrived to do something his country’s constitution once made impossible: register as a candidate for a third consecutive term. Bukele, 45, filed ahead of the deadline alongside Vice President Félix Ulloa for the election of 28 February 2027, having secured his Nuevas Ideas party’s nomination in July. The path was cleared by his own movement: after his 2024 landslide gave Nuevas Ideas control of Congress, lawmakers amended the constitution in 2025 to allow indefinite presidential re-election, lengthen terms from five years to six, abolish runoff votes and pull the election forward to coincide with legislative and municipal contests — changes that could keep Bukele in office until 2033. His popularity rests on a hardline crackdown that crushed the gangs terrorising Salvadorans, and polls put his support between 55% and 70%, against four opposition candidates whose parties poll in single digits. Critics answer that the popularity is being converted into permanence: Human Rights Watch’s Juan Pappier said Bukele is running “after packing the court, rewriting the constitution to his liking and jailing or forcing into exile those who dare criticise him.”

Latest · Americas · Caracas — From Threat to Partner20 November 2026

Ten Months After Capturing Its President, the US Signs a Military Pact With Venezuela

The transformation is now formal, and signed. On an unannounced visit to Caracas on Wednesday, US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth concluded an agreement re-establishing military ties with Venezuela — joint training, intelligence sharing, defence equipment and cooperation against drug trafficking — ten months after US forces seized President Nicolás Maduro in a January commando raid and flew him to New York to face drug charges. “Venezuela has gone from our most aggressive regional threat to a compliant security partner,” Hegseth said at the US Embassy, crediting interim President Delcy Rodríguez, who hosted him at the Miraflores Palace and described a resumption of the two countries’ technical-military relationship. The pact follows oil deals and joint operations against armed groups inside Venezuela, part of a US campaign that since September 2025 has struck dozens of suspected drug boats, some launched from Venezuelan shores. What Washington has not provided is the fine print: the Pentagon offered no timeline or detailed scope for the agreement, and the partnership-of-equals presentation sits alongside an obvious imbalance — a government in Caracas that governs with American approval, and a predecessor in a US jail cell awaiting trial.
