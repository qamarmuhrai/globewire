Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — America Answers over Qeshm17 November 2026

Five to Six Explosions over Qeshm: Washington Strikes Back as a Fifth Tanker Is Reported Hit

The Hormuz crisis crossed a new line on Tuesday evening. Iranian officials said the United States carried out several strikes on Qeshm Island, the large island guarding the strait’s entrance: Hormozgan deputy governor Ahmad Nafisi, quoted by the state agency IRNA, said the attacks caused no damage to residential, commercial or infrastructure areas and that the island was under control, while IRNA reported five to six explosions heard in the evening. The strikes followed Iran’s drone attack earlier in the day on a US-operated radar system at Bahrain’s Sheikh Isa Air Base — Tehran’s third attack on Bahrain in four days — which Bahrain said it intercepted and US Central Command said caused no damage. By nightfall, live coverage counted at least five vessels hit in and around the strait in 24 hours, after Iran’s army said another tanker caught fire on a naval mine east of Hormuz and Tehran warned ships off what it calls “fake American routes.” The Revolutionary Guards also claimed to have downed a US MQ-1 drone south of Qeshm — the 18th such interception it claims since the war began — and a US-made LUCAS attack drone over the strait. A third US carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, has joined two others in the region, and Europe’s aviation safety agency has extended its Gulf conflict-zone advisory through 31 January 2027.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — One Bridge Breathes Again17 November 2026

A Span Down at Kerch, a Lane Back in Kyiv: The Bridge War Produces Its First Verdicts

By Tuesday afternoon Kyiv had begun to recover movement. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said traffic was partially resuming on the Southern Bridge, with vehicles under the weight limit allowed from the eastern bank toward the city centre, while the Northern Bridge stayed closed and under inspection after seven ballistic missiles — four at the Southern, three at the Northern, by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s count — struck the two Dnipro crossings overnight. Two people were killed in the Obolonskyi district, fires burned in four districts, and 317 buildings in Darnytskyi remained without heating. Zelenskyy called Ukraine’s retaliatory operation against the Kerch Bridge to annexed Crimea “unprecedented” and weeks in the making: the General Staff said an arch and a span of the road section had collapsed, severing the road link, while the railway arch was damaged but standing. Traffic on the Kerch crossing was halted for hours for a second consecutive day. Moscow said it downed 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight, the second-highest daily figure this year, and envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected in Berlin on Thursday to work with Ukrainian and European officials on security guarantees and a postwar recovery plan.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Manila — Five Outcomes, One Exercise17 November 2026

Mumbai Gets the Forum, Guam Gets the Aircraft: The Quad Leaves Manila with a Working Programme

The 7th Quad Leaders’ Summit closed in Manila on Tuesday with five announced outcomes and a calendar. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, President Donald Trump, Japan’s Sanae Takaichi and Australia’s Anthony Albanese launched an inaugural Quad Business Leaders’ Forum, which Mumbai will host in early 2027; agreed an Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration, under which P-8 and P-3C patrol aircraft will fly together in a Quad exercise for the first time, on the sidelines of Exercise Sea Dragon in Guam in March 2027; extended their cable partnership from telecoms to undersea energy cables; stepped up cyber cooperation; and condemned terrorism and violent extremism in all forms, including cross-border terrorism. The joint statement reaffirmed a free, open and prosperous Indo-Pacific, opposed unilateral moves to change the status quo by force or coercion, voiced grave concern over economic coercion and arbitrary export restrictions, and backed ASEAN centrality and secure supply chains. Trump separately described a Super Intelligence Accelerator backed by more than $85 million for trusted AI ecosystems. It was the first Quad leaders’ meeting in two years and marked a decade since the free-and-open-Indo-Pacific vision was first articulated.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Pork, Seafood and Steel17 November 2026

C$400 Million of Tariff Relief: Xi and Carney Turn a Thaw into a Trade Schedule in Shenzhen

Canada and China converted their rapprochement into numbers on Tuesday. Meeting President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen ahead of the APEC leaders’ gathering, Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada was well ahead of schedule toward raising exports to China by 50 percent by the end of the decade, and his office said Beijing will extend and expand tariff relief covering about C$400 million (US$280 million) of Canadian exports, including frozen pork cuts and fish and seafood, under a widened arrangement for farmers and seafood harvesters. In return, Ottawa will extend by two years its existing remission measures for certain Chinese steel and aluminium products, currently due to expire at the end of 2026. Reducing dependence on the United States, which has imposed heavy tariffs on Canadian goods, is a central plank of Carney’s economic strategy, and he described the relationship as gaining “greater predictability and stability.” The APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting opens Wednesday in the same city, ahead of Thursday’s first Xi–Trump–Putin trilateral, and the Kremlin has said President Vladimir Putin could also meet North Korea’s Kim Jong Un in Shenzhen if Kim attends.

Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — Debate into the Night17 November 2026

67 Names on Paper, Hours of Speeches Ahead: The Knesset’s Speaker Vote Runs Long

Israel’s parliament spent Tuesday evening in debate ahead of the vote that will decide who controls its agenda. The plenum convened at 4 p.m. to replace Speaker Amir Ohana of Likud with Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party, and by nightfall the outcome was arithmetically settled but procedurally unfinished: the Arab-majority Ra’am and Joint List parties confirmed they will vote for Kahana, giving him 67 of 120 seats — the 48 of Eisenkot’s four-party bloc plus their 19 — against Likud’s Ofir Katz, whose own camp, with Haredi support, holds 53. Katz told Arab lawmakers not to vote for him, saying he would not accept election with what he called the votes of terror supporters. The Arrangements Committee moved in the morning to cap speaking time amid fears of a filibuster that could push the ballot deep into the night, and lawmakers will also elect nine temporary deputy speakers, including two Arab members. A Kahana win completes the Eisenkot bloc’s capture of the Knesset’s machinery — arrangements committee, a temporary foreign affairs and defence panel, and now the chair — and opens the way to legislation repealing parts of the judicial overhaul as early as Wednesday, even though no governing coalition yet exists.

Latest · Europe · Paris — The March, Counted Two Ways17 November 2026

Peaceful in Paris, Argued over Everywhere Else: France’s Strike Day Ends in Competing Numbers

France’s third united public-sector strike day since September wound down on Tuesday evening with the argument shifting from the streets to the statistics. The Paris march from Place de la Nation to Place d’Italie proceeded peacefully, police said, after more than 4,000 preventive checks by mid-afternoon; police had forecast 40,000 to 80,000 in the capital and 220,000 to 300,000 nationwide across nearly 180 demonstrations. The regional counts split as they always do: Marseille 4,900 by the prefecture, 80,000 by the CGT; Toulouse 5,000–5,500 against the union’s 20,000; Lyon 7,000 against 14,000, where the base of the Louis XIV statue at Place Bellecour was graffitied; in Nantes a water cannon was used around midday before calm returned. The Education Ministry put striking teachers at 3.76 percent; the Snes-FSU union counted 20 percent, and 27 percent of higher-education institutions reported disruption. Seventeen Paris stations stayed shut at the police prefecture’s request, regional rail ran reduced, and mainline trains ran normally. The unions’ demand is unchanged — unfreeze the pay index frozen for a fourth year — and the next fixed point is talks with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s government on 11 December.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Oceans Enter the Text17 November 2026

Bob Marley at the Door, Oceans in the Draft: COP31’s Ministers Inherit a Maze of 144 Paragraphs

Ministers arriving at COP31 in Antalya on Tuesday were greeted by protesters playing Bob Marley’s “Get Up, Stand Up” and by an 18-page draft cover decision that UN coverage describes as a maze of competing proposals. Two developments nevertheless moved from the margins toward the text. Ocean measures now feature prominently in the draft, backed by new analysis showing more than 95 percent of coastal and island nations include ocean action in their national climate plans — though about 60 percent of those commitments depend on outside finance or technical support. And 23 countries endorsed the Antalya Action Plan on Integrated Fire Management, developed with the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, shifting wildfire policy from suppression to prevention and drawing on Indigenous knowledge. The core fights remain open: rival fossil-fuel clauses — “transitioning away” versus “phase out” — a proposed tripling of adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year, and Turkey’s push to raise electricity to 35 percent of final energy demand by 2035. EU climate chief Wopke Hoekstra told delegates the talks must send the strongest possible signal on clean energy; negotiations president Chris Bowen spent the day taking responses before a revised draft later this week, against Friday’s deadline.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Manila — 19 Points Against the Scam Compounds17 November 2026

Nineteen Governments, One Fraud Industry: East Asia’s Summit Adopts a Plan Against Online Scams

The 21st East Asia Summit gave Manila’s diplomatic week a law-enforcement legacy on Tuesday, adopting a joint statement and a 19-point action plan against online scams and the trafficking networks behind them. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., hosting the 11 ASEAN members plus Australia, China, India, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, Russia and the United States, argued that threats now move across borders, sectors and institutions, so the response must connect police, financial regulators, telecoms, digital platforms and courts while protecting people vulnerable to exploitation. The plan centres on intelligence-sharing to dismantle scam centres, cross-border operations through ASEANAPOL and Interpol, and stronger financial and digital safeguards. The harm is documented at scale: a 2025 UN report cited at the summit put global scam losses somewhere between $8.3 billion and $114.1 billion, and the Philippines alone repatriated at least 839 citizens from Myanmar in 2025, many lured by fake job offers into compounds. Nikkei Asia reported the United States pressed to soften the chair’s language on climate change, sustainability and diversity even as it signed on to the anti-scam push.

$824 Million from Streaming, $3 Billion to Find in Costs: Disney’s Quarter Points Both Ways

Disney opened America’s results day with a clean beat and a hard promise. Fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 16 percent to $25.9 billion and adjusted earnings reached $1.77 a share, ahead of expectations near $25.2 billion and $1.67, helped by an extra 53rd week, record ESPN audiences for the NFL and college football season, and fuller parks in Florida and Paris. The counterweight: roughly $3 billion of annual operating-expense savings targeted by fiscal 2029, further layoffs expected, and a decision to stop disclosing streaming operating profit separately from 2027 — investors cheered the numbers, sending shares up about 2 percent, while being told they will see less of the detail behind them.

Latest · Americas · Caracas by Ledger — $12.26 Billion, Accounted17 November 2026

99 Percent Disbursed, $168.6 Million Left: The KPMG Ledger of Venezuela’s Oil Money under US Custody

The fullest public accounting yet of Venezuela’s post-Maduro oil finances is now on the record. A KPMG report published by the US State Department — dated 29 October and sent to Congress in early November — says sales of Venezuelan crude, petroleum products and natural gas, plus smaller amounts of gold, minerals and fertiliser, produced $12.26 billion in the first nine months of 2026, deposited by buyers into a US Treasury account under the arrangement built after Washington seized Nicolás Maduro in January. Of that, $12.11 billion — nearly 99 percent — has been disbursed, mostly to individuals and businesses inside Venezuela through foreign-exchange transactions with local commercial banks, leaving $168.6 million in escrow at the 30 September fiscal year-end. The report stresses the funds remain Venezuela’s sovereign property, held pending disposition for public, governmental or diplomatic purposes as determined by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and it redacts the names of the 20 companies that bought the oil and gold. The interim government in Caracas, led by former Maduro allies now working closely with Washington, receives the releases.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 17 Dead, No Arrests17 November 2026

Nine in a Shack, Eight in a Car, 530 Kilometres Apart: South Africa Counts Another Weekend of Mass Shootings

South African police are investigating two mass shootings that left 17 people dead on Sunday, with no arrests and no established motive in either case by Tuesday. In Jeffsville, an informal settlement near Pretoria, officers alerted early on Monday found nine bodies — six men and three women — inside a four-room shack, all with gunshot wounds; Gauteng commissioner Tommy Mthombeni said detectives are examining a possible link to last year’s shooting in nearby Saulsville, where 12 people were killed and 13 wounded. In Sweetwaters, outside Pietermaritzburg in KwaZulu-Natal, eight people were found dead inside a bullet-riddled vehicle after reports of gunfire. KwaZulu-Natal premier Thamsanqa Ntuli blamed the killings on the proliferation of illegal firearms, calling their availability one of the region’s most pressing security threats. The cases, handled by the serious and violent crimes unit, land in a country where mass shootings at homes, taverns and gatherings have become a recurring feature of a persistently high murder rate — and where, in both of this weekend’s attacks, the gunmen simply left.
