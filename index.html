Oil Climbs as US Pulls Diplomats From Saudi Arabia After Houthi Strikes on Airport and Oil Field

Oil prices pushed higher on Thursday after a fresh wave of Houthi attacks inside Saudi Arabia prompted Washington to order some diplomats and family members out of its missions in the kingdom, with plans to withdraw families from other US missions in the region as well. Brent crude rose $1.29, or 1.2 percent, to $110.11 a barrel in early trading, while US West Texas Intermediate crude gained 97 cents to $98.12. Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis struck several targets on Wednesday: at least one person was killed and eight wounded at a civilian airport in Dammam, and two people died in a drone attack on a Saudi oil field. Iranian media also reported explosions of unknown origin in Sirik, in southern Iran. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araqchi, told the Financial Times that Tehran was fully prepared to defend itself, while President Masoud Pezeshkian said the United States could not expect dialogue while blockading and threatening Iran. Shipping remains under pressure too — data from Kpler counted at least 18 attacks on vessels over the past two weeks, and LSEG analysts warned that Iran is targeting tankers well beyond the Strait of Hormuz, into the Gulf of Oman.

Turkey Brings Seven Foreign Ministers Together in Istanbul for First Talks of Their Kind on Regional Wars

Turkey's foreign minister, Hakan Fidan, will host his counterparts from Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Britain, France and Germany in Istanbul on Friday, a Turkish diplomatic source said on Thursday — the first time the seven countries have met in this format, at Ankara's initiative. The agenda spans the Israeli–Palestinian issue, the latest situation in the Strait of Hormuz, developments in Yemen, efforts to end the Russia–Ukraine war, and closer cooperation between regional and European countries, as wars involving Iran, Yemen and Ukraine threaten shipping routes and regional security. Fidan is expected to press for a diplomatic solution to the war between the United States and Iran, stress the importance of freedom of navigation in the Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb straits, and restate Turkey's support for Saudi Arabia's security against Houthi attacks under the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signed by Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia. He will also highlight Turkey's work to stop the Ukraine war spreading into the Black Sea, and the regional risks the conflict poses.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War21 October 2026

Putin Agrees to Resume Three-Way Peace Talks With Ukraine and the US as Russia Frees American Teacher

Russian President Vladimir Putin has agreed to resume US-mediated peace talks with Ukraine as soon as next month, the Kremlin said after he met US envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg on Wednesday — the ninth time the two have met in person. The encounter opened with a goodwill gesture from Moscow: the release of Stephen Hubbard, a retired American schoolteacher detained since April 2022, whose freedom President Donald Trump had personally requested; Trump said the United States was not required to give anything in return. The Kremlin said the new talks, at a technical level, could take place in Abu Dhabi on or after 10 November and would focus on how to guarantee the security of Ukraine and Russia once the war ends. The last US-mediated round was held in February, before the United States and Israel launched their war against Iran. The meeting came amid concern in Kyiv and among its allies that Washington is backing away from Ukraine, at times withholding real-time intelligence on Russian attacks. Moscow was cooler about Witkoff's earlier talks in Oslo with President Volodymyr Zelenskiy and European and NATO representatives: Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said the ideas discussed "do not entirely satisfy us".

Ebola Infections Rebound in DR Congo as Cross-Border Spread Heightens Concerns, WHO Says

Ebola infections in the Democratic Republic of the Congo have rebounded after a brief decline, while the virus's spread to other African countries is complicating efforts to contain an outbreak that has already claimed more than 4,600 lives, the World Health Organization warned in its latest situation report on Wednesday. The DRC recorded 1,466 confirmed cases between 29 September and 19 October — up 10.4 percent from the preceding 21-day period — taking its totals to 9,637 confirmed cases and 4,646 confirmed deaths across eight of its 26 provinces. Kenya has reported nine confirmed cases, including one death, with all eight secondary infections linked to an imported case and detected among people already under quarantine. Nigeria confirmed its first case on Tuesday, in a woman who had travelled from Uganda earlier this month; the WHO warned that the long interval between her symptoms appearing and laboratory confirmation raised concerns about potentially undetected exposures and secondary transmission. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention said on Tuesday that the outbreak had become the most geographically widespread Ebola epidemic on record, with infections reported across Central, Eastern and Western Africa. The outbreak is caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain of the virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment.

North Korea to Put Border Loudspeakers on Standby Within Days, Kim Yo Jong Warns Seoul

North Korea will place anti-South Korea loudspeakers on standby along the entire border within days and has put unspecified new countermeasures on alert, Kim Yo Jong, the powerful sister of North Korea's leader, said on Thursday, warning Seoul of "the most horrible and distressing situation" if South Korea continues what Pyongyang calls interference in the North's internal affairs. In a statement carried by state media KCNA, Kim said she was responding to a special adviser to President Lee Jae Myung on defence policy, who had accused North Korea of planting mines that wounded South Korean soldiers and told it not to escalate tensions. On Wednesday evening, after visiting fortification sites with senior military officials, she said North Korea would activate the loudspeakers across the border by the end of October, adding that tactical units had taken up new positions and accusing South Korean troops of warning broadcasts and warning shots at several locations near the border; South Korea said its warning shots were "legitimate and defensive measures". She warned that North Korean troops would "mercilessly eliminate" South Korean forces if warning fire harmed North Korean territory or personnel. The threats raise tensions along the heavily fortified frontier to their highest level since Lee took office in June last year pledging to ease inter-Korean hostility.

Latest · Americas · Technology21 October 2026

Meta Ordered to Pay $25 Billion in New Mexico After Jury Found It Misled Users Over Privacy

A judge in New Mexico has ordered Meta Platforms to pay $25 billion after a jury found the company misled consumers about the privacy of their data — one of the largest financial penalties in US corporate history. The case stems from the Cambridge Analytica scandal, in which the British political consulting firm involved in Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign obtained data from millions of Facebook users without their consent through a third-party app. New Mexico, which sued in 2021, accused Meta of lying to users in the state about who had access to their data, and of tolerating hate speech and other harmful content when it benefited the company's bottom line; in September a jury found more than 43 million violations of the state's consumer protection law. "Facebook's wrongdoing — over the course of years and systematically broadcast to the public — goes to the heart of Facebook's core business practices," Judge Francis Mathew wrote, finding that a penalty of more than $219 billion was warranted under state law before reducing the figure to $25 billion. Meta said it will appeal: chief legal officer C.J. Mahoney called the ruling a "tax on speech" that makes it all but impossible to do business in the state. Attorney General Raúl Torrez said technology companies should view the verdict as a warning that their size does not put them beyond the law.

European Car Sales Rise 10 Percent in September as Electric Surge Offsets Petrol and Diesel Slump

Strong demand for electrified cars helped Europe's new-car market grow in September, offsetting another sharp decline in petrol and diesel sales, data from the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association showed on Thursday. New-car registrations, a proxy for sales, rose 10.2 percent year on year to 1.36 million vehicles across the European Union, Britain and the European Free Trade Association. Battery-electric, plug-in hybrid and hybrid registrations jumped 59.9 percent, 24 percent and 5.3 percent respectively, and together accounted for 76.2 percent of all new vehicles, while petrol registrations fell 20.6 percent and diesel 28.2 percent. Chinese automakers continued to gain ground: Chery nearly tripled its sales, BYD doubled its own, and Geely and SAIC rose by more than 33 percent and 23 percent, lifting the four brands' combined market share to 14.6 percent from 9.6 percent a year earlier. Registrations at Volkswagen and Renault declined, while Stellantis gained 4.5 percent; the three European groups' combined market share fell to 43.7 percent from 48.4 percent.

Latest · Middle East · Economy22 October 2026

IMF Sees Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan Output Shrinking 1.3 Percent in 2026 on War's Impact

The International Monetary Fund expects economic output in the Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan to contract by 1.3 percent this year because of the US–Israeli-led war against Iran, before rebounding to 6.6 percent growth in 2027 — though it warned that further disruption to energy production or trade routes could worsen the outlook. In its regional outlook released in Dubai on Thursday, the Fund said the war's impact had been substantial but uneven: oil-exporting economies directly hit by disruptions to energy production and shipping suffered most, while oil importers proved more resilient, with domestic demand supported by remittances and tourism, favourable financial conditions and prompt policy responses that shielded consumers from higher commodity prices. Even so, output across the region would generally remain below pre-war projections. The baseline assumes the conflict abates in early 2027 and trade gradually normalises during the year, but the IMF said the outlook remained clouded by "exceptionally high uncertainty", and that external financial conditions could also tighten if investors reassess AI-driven productivity gains while interest rates rise amid inflation and fiscal concerns. Growth in the Caucasus and Central Asia, meanwhile, is moderating toward a more sustainable pace, forecast at 5.2 percent in 2026 and 5 percent in 2027.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology & Economy22 October 2026

IMF and OECD Warn AI Could Widen the Divide Between Rich and Poor Countries

Artificial intelligence could boost global growth, but poorer countries, smaller companies and younger workers risk missing out unless access to power, digital infrastructure and skills improves, officials from the IMF and the OECD have warned. The IMF estimates that effective AI adoption could add up to 0.5 percent to annual global growth over the medium term. "For many developing economies, the critical question is whether they can adopt and diffuse AI," said Era Dabla-Norris, deputy director of the Fund's Fiscal Affairs Department, though she added that "the aggregate productivity evidence, so far, is much less". The OECD estimates AI could add 0.4 to 1.2 percent to annual productivity growth across its member countries, but its chief economist, Stefano Scarpetta, said workplace use trails consumer use, with only limited adoption in core business functions: "There is a large gap between exposure and actual use." The IMF estimates about 40 percent of jobs worldwide could be exposed to AI, and reports of mass layoffs are gathering pace — Barclays is cutting around 5,000 jobs this year as it uses technology including AI to reduce costs, eBay is reported to be laying off 9 percent of its workforce, and Qualcomm is expected to cut jobs globally. The warnings come as massive investment in chips and AI infrastructure supports global growth and record stock markets, while raising questions about whether companies can earn enough from the spending as borrowing costs rise.

Most Container Lines Expected Back in the Suez Canal by Early Second Quarter, Kuehne+Nagel Chief Says

Most container shipping lines should resume using the Suez Canal route by late in the first quarter or early in the second quarter of 2027, the chief executive of Swiss freight forwarder Kuehne+Nagel said on Thursday, though he cautioned that geopolitics remained volatile. "There is a clear intention among carrier partners to return to the route," Stefan Paul told Reuters after the company raised its 2026 profit outlook for the third time this year. He pointed to higher oil prices as a factor making shorter voyage times more attractive, while adding that reopening the route "would not materially affect customer demand". Most carriers abandoned the Asia–Europe corridor through Suez in November 2023 after attacks in the Red Sea by Yemen's Houthis forced ships onto the much longer trip around Africa, and hopes of a return were dampened again earlier this year by the US–Israeli strikes on Iran in late February. Maersk has already restored selected services through the canal, but analysts say the broader resumption of Red Sea routes is likely to be gradual and phased, and unlikely to materially affect this year's earnings.
