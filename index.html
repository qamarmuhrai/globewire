Latest · Europe · Ukraine Evacuation1 November 2026

Oleshky Evacuees Reunite With Family at the Border After Fleeing the Occupied Town Under a Rare Truce

Residents of Russian-occupied Oleshky, in Ukraine's southern Kherson region, crossed into Ukrainian-held territory on Saturday in an evacuation carried out under the most significant local truce since Russia's full-scale invasion began four and a half years ago. Wrapped in blue-and-yellow flags, evacuees stepped off buses at the Ukrainian-Belarusian border in the Chernihiv region and were met by relatives, along with volunteers from the Red Cross, Doctors Without Borders and World Central Kitchen. Their route home was long and indirect: buses through Crimea to Anapa in Russia, then four planes to Gomel in Belarus, because the front line sits only a few kilometres from Oleshky itself. The ceasefire began early on Tuesday and is due to run until Sunday evening; the White House says First Lady Melania Trump helped broker it, while Pope Leo XIV pressed both sides to allow aid and evacuations. About 2,000 people were still in Oleshky when the truce took hold and were offered the choice of leaving for Ukrainian- or Russian-held territory, or staying with replenished food and medical supplies. Ukrainian officials have not confirmed the final number evacuated, saying details are restricted for safety, though the convoy had capacity for more than 750 people. The evacuees ranged from just over a year old to 94, and some brought pets with them.

Latest · Global Affairs · Quad in Atlanta1 November 2026

Quad Ministers Pledge $85 Million for Trusted AI and Warn Against Economic Coercion in Atlanta

The foreign ministers of the United States, India, Japan and Australia closed their third Quad meeting of the year in Atlanta on Saturday with a package centred on supply chains, technology and maritime security. In their joint statement, Marco Rubio, S. Jaishankar, Toshimitsu Motegi and Penny Wong voiced grave concern about economic coercion through arbitrary export restrictions and non-market practices that create overcapacity — language aimed at supply-chain disruption without naming China directly. The four pledged more than $85 million in technical and financial help to widen access to trusted artificial intelligence across the Indo-Pacific, including through a Quad AI Accelerator, and agreed to map vulnerabilities in semiconductor supply chains before they cause disruption. Washington also committed $10 million for recycling critical minerals in India and ASEAN countries. The ministers announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum, a Sea Dragon 2027 maritime surveillance initiative, and a meeting of their export credit agencies in Tokyo on 18 November. Rubio said the grouping was not an anti-China coalition and said a leaders' summit in Manila later this month, alongside the ASEAN meetings, would hopefully be announced soon — it would be President Trump's first Quad summit.

Latest · Americas · UN on Boat Strikes1 November 2026

UN Rights Chief Tells the US to Halt Boat Strikes in the Caribbean and Pacific, Calling Them Unlawful Killings

The United Nations' top human rights official, Volker Türk, has urged the United States to stop its airstrikes on boats in the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific and to prevent what he called the extrajudicial killing of people on board. More than 60 people have reportedly been killed since early September in a continuing series of US attacks on vessels Washington says were linked to drug trafficking — circumstances that, in Türk's words, find no justification in international law. Secretary-General António Guterres supports that assessment, a UN spokesperson said, adding that none of those targeted appeared to pose an imminent threat to life. Under international human rights law, lethal force is permitted only as a last resort against an imminent threat, and Türk called for prompt, independent and transparent investigations, arguing that drug trafficking is a law-enforcement matter to be handled by intercepting boats and detaining suspects. The latest strike announced by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth killed four people in the eastern Pacific; the campaign has been backed by a large US deployment in the Caribbean, including warships and thousands of troops. President Trump has defended the strikes as necessary to stem the flow of drugs into the United States.

Latest · Europe · France Protests1 November 2026

Hundreds of Thousands March Across France as 70,000 Security Personnel Deploy and Students Vow New Blockades

France's third consecutive weekend of protests over living costs and inequality drew hundreds of thousands of people on Saturday, under an exceptional deployment of about 70,000 police and gendarmes determined to avoid a repeat of the looting and arson of two weeks earlier. Authorities put nationwide turnout at about 320,000, including 70,000 in Paris; youth organisations claimed nearly half a million, including 200,000 in the capital. The interior ministry described the day as generally calm by early afternoon, with 27 arrests by 2 p.m. compared with 217 a week earlier, though tear gas was used and clashes flared at the end of marches in Paris and other cities. Some demonstrators wore Halloween costumes — clowns, witches and a Marie-Antoinette with a placard reading “Give them brioche.” The movement brings together Yellow Vest activists, unions and students angry at deteriorating school conditions, and its demands have widened to civil liberties after injuries in earlier crackdowns. Student union spokeswoman Sabrine Kharbach said blockades of high schools and universities would resume on Tuesday and Thursday, ahead of a union-backed demonstration on 5 November. In Paris, only a march on the outskirts was authorised, more than 40 metro stations were closed, and night curfews were imposed in the République and Châtelet-Les Halles districts.

Latest · Global Affairs · G20 Atlanta1 November 2026

Rubio Says a Shift in Congress Won't Change Trump's Foreign Policy as the G20 Ends Without a Joint Statement

Closing the two-day G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta on Saturday, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said a change in control of Congress after Tuesday's midterms would not alter President Trump's foreign policy directives. The election had not happened yet, he noted, and divided government is a familiar feature of American politics; Congress has a role in funding and oversight, but the central role in foreign policy belongs to the executive branch. With Democrats able to use appropriations and oversight to challenge the administration if they win one or both chambers, the remark was a clear signal that Washington does not intend to change course. The Atlanta gathering itself ended in division: disagreements over the Ukraine and Middle East wars meant no joint communique, the traditional family photo was cancelled after European ministers refused to be photographed with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, and the United States instead planned a chairman's statement. Rubio declined to announce any further military action on Iran, saying that decision rests with the president, and said Trump would also decide whether Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy attends the G20 leaders' summit in Florida in December. He described Atlanta's focus on energy, technology and supply chains as itself a presidential directive.

Latest · Middle East · Iran Nuclear Watch31 October 2026

IAEA Says Iran Is Not Rebuilding Its Damaged Nuclear Sites — but 440 kg of Enriched Uranium Remains Unverified

The head of the UN nuclear watchdog, Rafael Grossi, told the Security Council this week that inspectors have seen no sign since June 2025 of Iran trying to repair or reconstruct nuclear facilities damaged in US and Israeli strikes in 2025 and 2026. The reassurance comes with a major caveat: the International Atomic Energy Agency still cannot verify the whereabouts of about 440.9 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60% — close to the 90% level associated with weapons-grade material and far above civilian limits — because it has lacked access to most sites since the June 2025 attacks. Grossi said remote monitoring cannot replace physical inspections, that the agency cannot confirm whether declared material has been diverted, and he urged Tehran to grant access without further delay, including to a planned enrichment plant at Isfahan announced in June 2025. The Bushehr power plant is the exception, where verification continues. The Security Council session followed the IAEA board's decision last month to refer Iran to the Council over non-compliance — the first such referral in 20 years. The verification gap is now central to the mediation track as well: proposals discussed by regional intermediaries would revive inspection arrangements in exchange for limited economic relief, without Iran immediately surrendering its stockpile.

Latest · Global Affairs · Qatar Mediation1 November 2026

Qatar Pushes for an Iran–US Deal After Marathon Tehran Talks, With Inspections at the Centre of the Bargain

Qatari mediators are pressing for a narrow agreement between Iran and the United States after more than twelve hours of talks in Tehran on Friday and follow-up meetings in Doha on Saturday, as fears grow that Washington could resume strikes on Iran once Tuesday's midterm elections are over. The emerging proposal, as described by regional reporting, would revive elements of the September 2025 Cairo arrangement between Iran and the IAEA: Tehran would let inspectors back into damaged facilities without immediately giving up its enriched uranium, while Washington would grant limited exemptions for Iranian oil sales and release some frozen funds for humanitarian purchases. That split — inspections now, harder nuclear concessions later — is the core of the compromise, because Iran's nuclear chief has previously rejected demands to halt enrichment or hand over the stockpile, and Tehran has already called Qatar's seven-day confidence-building proposal unsatisfactory. The diplomacy follows a letter from Vice President JD Vance to a senior Iranian security official, which Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi says Iran is studying, even as President Trump has publicly played down the initiative. The White House has not formally accepted the inspectors-for-relief proposal.

Latest · Business · Crypto Heist31 October 2026

Binance Counts the Cost of a $6.3 Billion Heist, the Largest Crypto Theft on Record, as Withdrawals Stay Frozen

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, says about $6.3 billion was moved out of its wallets on 29 October in what blockchain analysts describe as the biggest crypto theft in history — roughly four times the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit in February 2025. Most of the money left across ten blockchains in about twenty minutes, after smaller test transfers in the preceding day and a half. Two US analytics firms, TRM Labs and Chainalysis, attribute the attack with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-linked hacking unit associated with the Lazarus Group; TRM says the attackers used Binance's signing infrastructure, while Binance says it has found no evidence of a private-key leak and will publish technical details when its investigation is complete. About $3 billion has been frozen on-chain with partners, including more than $2.3 billion frozen by the stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle, but an estimated $2.4 billion in bitcoin and ether cannot be frozen by any issuer and remains under the attackers' control. Binance has suspended withdrawals, says deposits were unaffected, expects a gradual restart around Sunday, and has pledged to cover all user losses from its Secure Asset Fund and company funds.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Pope's Ballot31 October 2026

Pope Leo Becomes the First Pontiff to Cast a Ballot in a US Election

Pope Leo XIV has voted by mail in the US midterm elections, the Vatican press office confirmed on Friday — a historic first for a pontiff, made possible because the 71-year-old pope was born in Chicago and remains a registered voter in Illinois under his birth name, Robert Francis Prevost. He requested his ballot on 3 October, according to the Will County Clerk's office; the Vatican did not say how he voted. Illinois voters are choosing a successor to retiring Senator Dick Durbin and deciding whether to re-elect Governor JB Pritzker. The ballot lands in a difficult midterm climate for President Trump, whose approval has been dragged down by the cost of living and the Iran war, putting Republican majorities in both chambers at risk in Tuesday's vote. Trump criticised Leo repeatedly early in his papacy, and the pope said in March that God rejects the prayers of those who start wars. Leo, elected in May 2025 to lead the world's 1.4 billion Catholics, had part of his right lung removed in surgery on 13 October and was diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma; he is due to travel to South America next week.

Latest · Americas · Ports and Labour31 October 2026

Los Angeles and Long Beach Ports Avert a Watchmen's Strike as Talks Are Extended to 5 November

The employer group for the Los Angeles and Long Beach seaports and the union representing about 250 maritime watchmen have agreed to extend their negotiating deadline from 1 November to 5 November, averting a potential strike at the busiest container port complex in the United States. The Pacific Maritime Association said on Friday that talks with ILWU Local 26 will continue with help from the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service. The watchmen account for only about 2% of port workers, but a walkout could still have snarled cargo flows at a complex coming off all-time record volumes in September — shipments driven by holiday retail goods, equipment for AI data-centre construction and auto parts. The extension keeps freight moving through the peak import season while negotiations continue under federal mediation.
