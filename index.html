Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Trilateral Afternoon19 November 2026

Hours From the Table: Xi, Trump and Putin Prepare a First Three-Way Summit

The meeting is now a matter of hours. The first trilateral summit between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia is set to begin at 2:30 p.m. local time on Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC gathering in Shenzhen, and the Kremlin expects it to last no more than an hour. Donald Trump will first sit down with Xi Jinping for a bilateral and working lunch — their third substantial meeting this year, with tariffs, rare-earth controls and semiconductor restrictions in the background of a trade truce due to expire in January — and has added a separate one-on-one with Vladimir Putin, their first talks since Alaska last year. Trump wants nuclear arms control at the centre of the three-way agenda after New START expired in February; Beijing has repeatedly resisted joining arms talks with two far larger arsenals. Xi opened the APEC programme with a pitch for free and open trade and stable supply chains, and greeted Trump at Shenzhen airport on Wednesday, reciprocating the welcome Trump gave him at Joint Base Andrews in September. Officials on all sides describe the session as exploratory and symbolic, with Ukraine, Iran and energy stability in the room even where Washington says Trump does not plan to raise the Ukraine war himself. A draft joint statement has been under intensive consultation, but whether the three will adopt one remains unclear.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Shipping at 7%19 November 2026

Another Tanker Hit, Bases Struck Again: Hormuz Traffic Falls to a Trickle

The waterway is close to a standstill, and the exchanges around it widened again overnight. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre reported a tanker struck by an unknown projectile in its engine room about six nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, as it sailed outbound through the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday evening — the report reached authorities in the early hours of Thursday. Iran said three tankers had caught fire after hitting naval mines east of the strait, with its Revolutionary Guard saying the vessels had ignored warnings and entered restricted waters with navigation systems off; an ADNOC vessel attacked earlier in the week left one crew member missing and eleven injured. On land, Iran said its drones targeted Patriot batteries and launch platforms at Kuwait’s Ali Al-Salem Air Base and fired repeatedly at Bahrain, in retaliation for US strikes on a missile site on Iran’s Qeshm Island, while Washington reissued security warnings to its citizens in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a Houthi ballistic-missile launch platform in Saada, Yemen. Tracking data underlines the choke: no AIS-visible commercial ship had crossed the strait by early Thursday, the seven-day average is about 5.7 crossings a day — roughly 7% of normal — and LNG sailings out of the Gulf have fallen to about two in the past week from a recent average of eight.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Beyond the Yellow Line19 November 2026

Tanks Across the Ceasefire Line: Israel’s First Ground Push Into Hamas-Held Gaza

For the first time since the October 2025 ceasefire, Israeli ground forces have operated on the other side of the line that divides Gaza. Tanks and bulldozers advanced west of the Yellow Line in central Gaza on Wednesday, rolling onto the main Salah al-Din road near the Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps under artillery fire, as Israel’s military said troops had begun a targeted operation to dismantle a tunnel it described as a threat to its forces. Dozens of displaced families fled tents and shelters; Palestinian medics and Gaza’s civil defence said three people were killed in separate strikes during the day, near Nuseirat, in Deir al-Balah and in Gaza City. Israeli Army Radio reported the push — some 300 to 400 metres beyond the line — was coordinated with the United States in advance and could last several days. Hamas condemned the move as a breach of the truce, and the US-backed Board of Peace, in its second report to the UN Security Council, accused both Israel and Hamas of failing to meet their commitments to end hostilities. Israel says it controls more than 60% of the territory east of the line; its civilian-affairs body, COGAT, warned residents and aid agencies to stay clear of the operation area.

Latest · Europe · Berlin — Not Decided Over Our Heads19 November 2026

While Three Leaders Meet in China, Europe Gathers in Berlin to Hold Its Line

Europe’s answer to Shenzhen is being staged on the same day, in Berlin. Leaders of the Coalition of the Willing — Germany, France, Britain, Poland and Italy among them, with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy — meet on Thursday with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in the room, in a show of support timed, pointedly, only hours after Trump and Putin were expected to talk in China. The fear driving the choreography is explicit in European capitals: that decisions about the war, and about the continent’s security, could be taken without European input. German officials say the Berlin agenda centres on reaffirming robust, legally binding security guarantees for Ukraine that would take effect after a ceasefire, and Zelenskyy wants the session to work through the US-led negotiations and hear what Trump’s China talks produced. Money is moving in parallel: on Wednesday the European Commission proposed a further €45 billion loan for 2027, covering about two-thirds of Kyiv’s €70 billion funding gap, on top of the €90 billion package already being paid out — a proposal EU leaders will take up at a Brussels summit next week.

Latest · Europe · Kerch Strait — Third Strike in a Week19 November 2026

Hit Again After Midnight: The Crimean Bridge Takes a Third Attack in Seven Days

The bridge Vladimir Putin opened in 2018 as the symbol of Russia’s hold on Crimea has been struck for the third time this week. Crimea’s Russian-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said traffic over the 19-kilometre crossing was temporarily suspended after an attack just after midnight on Thursday, with emergency services at the scene and damage still being assessed; rail traffic was later reported resumed. The new strike follows Tuesday’s far heavier blow, when Ukrainian missiles and drones destroyed the bridge’s central road section and brought its main road arch down into the Kerch Strait, in what Ukraine’s SBU security service described as a seven-phase operation prepared over weeks. The timing is part of the message: the latest attack landed hours before Putin’s meetings with Trump and Xi in Shenzhen. The bridge is both a principal supply route for Russian forces and a flagship of the annexation Moscow proclaimed in 2014. Russia has vowed to rebuild, but specialists cited by Ukrainian media put full repairs at a year or more — a span that would leave Crimea’s logistics dependent on ferries and the land corridor through occupied southern Ukraine.

Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — Judgment Day19 November 2026

Seven Years On, the World Court Reads Its Rohingya Verdict

A judgment seven years in the making begins at the Peace Palace on Thursday morning. Judges of the International Court of Justice start reading their ruling at 10 a.m. local time — a reading expected to run up to 90 minutes — in the case Gambia brought against Myanmar in November 2019, alleging that the 2017 military offensive against the Rohingya Muslim minority, which drove at least 730,000 people into Bangladesh, breached the 1948 Genocide Convention. Myanmar denies genocide and calls the campaign a legitimate counter-terrorism operation. The stakes are precedential: in eight decades the court has never found a state responsible for genocide, and this is only its third genocide judgment ever, and its first in more than a decade. A finding against Myanmar would make it the first state held to account under the convention, with Gambia also seeking reparations including full citizenship for the Rohingya; the ruling is final, with no appeal, though enforcement would fall to the UN Security Council. In Cox’s Bazar, some 1.2 million Rohingya remain in the world’s largest refugee settlement, following a verdict that will also shape how lawyers argue the other genocide cases now before the court.

Priced at $83, Worth $1.5 Trillion: Anthropic Meets the Stock Market

The largest technology debut of the AI era starts trading on Thursday. Anthropic, the maker of the Claude models, priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday — above the $70-to-$78 range it had marketed — valuing the company at roughly $1.5 trillion fully diluted, below the $2 trillion figure discussed in September but still among the biggest listings in history. The sale of 825 million shares under the ticker ANT on the Nasdaq Global Select Market was reported around six times oversubscribed. The numbers behind the price are formidable in both directions: revenue of about $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, with third-quarter revenue near $17 billion, set against some $900.9 billion in committed computing and infrastructure spending over coming years, $451.6 billion of it due by 2030. Chief executive Dario Amodei framed the below-whisper valuation as deliberate — a preference for delivering more than promised. Under Nasdaq’s fast-entry rule, a listing of this size could join the Nasdaq-100 within about 15 trading days, around 10 December, pulling index funds into the stock almost immediately.

Latest · Americas · Washington — 251 to 1119 November 2026

One Floor Vote From History: Democrats Nominate Jeffries for Speaker

Hakeem Jeffries is a single vote of the full House away from becoming the first Black Speaker in American history. House Democrats, fresh from a sweeping midterm victory that gives them at least 257 seats in the new Congress, nominated the 56-year-old New Yorker in a closed-door ballot on Wednesday, 251 to 11, with two members not voting. Jeffries, who has led the caucus since 2023, ran unopposed — but chose a recorded ballot over nomination by acclamation, following the precedent Democrats set in 2018, which put his dissenters on the record. Eleven were: reporting identifies seven newly elected progressives among them, several of whom said his support for Israel and what they called insufficient action over Gaza kept them from backing him. The formal election comes on 3 January, when the new Congress convenes and 218 votes decide the gavel; Republicans have nominated Judiciary chairman Jim Jordan to lead their side. A Jeffries speakership would place a Democrat second in the line of presidential succession for the final two years of Donald Trump’s term, with his party promising to reverse Republican policies and open investigations of the administration.

“From Threat to Partner”: Hegseth’s Surprise Caracas Trip Seals a Military Pact

Ten months after US forces captured Nicolás Maduro in a January raid, Washington’s defence chief stood in Caracas and signed a military agreement with the government that replaced him. Latest · Americas · New York — Three Minutes, $190.4 Million19 November 2026

A Van Gogh Hidden for 50 Years Breaks the Artist’s Record

It took about three minutes of bidding to rewrite the Van Gogh record. “Châtaigniers en fleurs” — Chestnut Trees in Blossom, painted in May 1890 at Auvers-sur-Oise in the last weeks of the artist’s life — sold at Sotheby’s in New York on Wednesday night for $190.4 million including fees, far beyond the previous Van Gogh auction high of $117.2 million set in 2022, and now the fourth most expensive painting ever sold at auction. Auctioneer Oliver Barker opened at $135 million; New York dealer Bill Acquavella placed the winning $165 million bid. The canvas had barely been seen in half a century: the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which bought it in 1975, did not lend to museums. The sale nearly unravelled over its past — German publisher Samuel Fischer bought the painting in Paris in 1909, and his Jewish family parted with it in 1936, in the Nazi era; his heirs challenged the sale as made under duress, and only on Tuesday did Sotheby’s announce an agreement between the family and Fischer’s descendants that let the auction proceed, on undisclosed terms. The lot headlined a November season expected to generate about $2.4 billion across New York’s auction houses.

Akvan Wobbles, Models Split: A Coast Waits for Friday Noon

With landfall about a day away, Cyclone Akvan is refusing to settle its story. The India Meteorological Department shifted the expected crossing point north during Wednesday — from between Nellore and Visakhapatnam in the morning to between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha by afternoon, with sustained winds trimmed to 90–100 km/h gusting to 110 — and conceded unusually large disagreement among forecast models: some European and AI-based systems weaken the storm below cyclone strength before it reaches shore, while the official forecast still brings it in as a severe cyclonic storm around noon on Friday 20 November. What is not in dispute is the rain: red warnings for extremely heavy falls cover six Odisha districts on Thursday, including Puri, Ganjam and Kendrapara, with a possible storm surge of one to two metres. Odisha has cancelled officials’ leave in alert districts, shut schools in the most exposed areas and moved people to shelters; the storm’s speed has doubled from 3 to 7 km/h as it recurves. Akvan is the third Bay of Bengal cyclone to threaten this coast in roughly three weeks, after Arnab and Murasu.

Latest · Global Affairs · Demographics — The 2035 Crossover19 November 2026

2.57 Billion to 2.52 Billion: Pew Dates the Changing of the Guard

The world’s two largest faiths are both still growing — one is simply growing faster. Projections released on Wednesday by the Pew Research Center put Muslims ahead of Christians in total numbers by 2035, at about 2.57 billion to 2.52 billion, the first crossover in the modern demographic record. Pew’s baseline counts 2.27 billion Christians and 2.02 billion Muslims in 2020; by 2070 it projects more than 3.6 billion Muslims against just under 3 billion Christians, in a world population up 29%. The engine is fertility and age structure rather than conversion: Muslim women averaged 3.1 children in 2020–2025 against 2.5 for Christian women, and Pew’s senior demographer Conrad Hackett identifies birth rates as the main driver, with religious switching and migration factored in across 201 countries and territories. The study — part of a 2020–2070 project covering seven religious groups — also projects Hindus rising from 1.2 billion to 1.4 billion by 2070 while their growth rate slows, and Christians ceasing to be a majority in the United States by 2070. Pew stresses the figures are projections of current trends, not predictions of a world that cannot change course.
