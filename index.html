Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia & Yemen27 October 2026

Explosions Over Riyadh: Houthis Claim a 'Direct Hit' on the Saudi Defence Ministry, the Kingdom Reports Interceptions

Yemen's Houthis widened their campaign against Saudi Arabia on Tuesday, claiming a ballistic missile scored a direct hit on the Saudi Defence Ministry in Riyadh and that a drone strike on Abha International Airport in the south-west had disrupted air traffic. Military spokesman Yahya Saree also said missiles had been aimed at military transport aircraft at Najran Airport and at King Khalid Air Base in Khamis Mushait, on top of the group's earlier claim of a strike on the Ras Tanura refinery in the Eastern Province. Saudi authorities gave a different account: they said two ballistic missiles launched toward the capital had been intercepted and destroyed, after AFP correspondents in Riyadh heard explosions and residents received threat alerts that were later lifted without immediate reports of damage; Saudi Civil Defense said debris from intercepted missiles fell in the capital, damaging a building, a construction site and a vehicle. The Houthi claim of a direct hit on the ministry has not been independently verified. The exchange came a day after Egypt's parliament authorised President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi to send defensive military support — expected to involve aircraft and air defences, not ground troops — to help Saudi Arabia if Riyadh requests it, and as senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Iran, Pakistan and Turkey agreed to keep talking about regional security. The Houthis have stepped up missile and drone attacks on the kingdom through October, targeting airports, oil facilities and military sites.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab27 October 2026

Arnab 140 Kilometres From Kakinada: Highest Danger Signal Hoisted, 137,000 People Being Moved as Landfall Nears

Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab was about 140 km from Kakinada and 170 km from Visakhapatnam on Tuesday afternoon, closing on the Andhra Pradesh coast at its peak strength, with the India Meteorological Department expecting it to cross just south of Yanam by Wednesday forenoon with winds of 125–135 km/h gusting to 150 km/h and a storm surge of one to two metres that could flood low-lying areas around Kakinada and Yanam. The highest danger signal has been hoisted at Kakinada port. Nearly 89,000 people along the Kakinada coast are being moved into 101 relief camps, Odisha plans to evacuate 48,000 more, and sea water has already flooded parts of Uppada near Kakinada; all beaches in Visakhapatnam are closed after more than 100 mm of rain fell at several spots in the city since morning. The disruption is spreading across the region's transport network: the Railways Ministry said more than 90 trains — 95 by one evening count — have been cancelled with others diverted or short-terminated, and several flights at Visakhapatnam airport have been cancelled or diverted. Schools will stay shut on Wednesday and Thursday in Jharkhand's Ranchi and West Singhbhum districts as the storm's rain bands push inland, Chhattisgarh has put its districts on high alert for October 28–29, and heavy rain is also forecast for parts of Odisha, West Bengal and Chhattisgarh, with Nepal on high alert from October 29 to 31 for the storm's remnants.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia27 October 2026

More Than 60 Christians Killed in Ethiopia's Oromia Region: Church Calls It a Massacre, Blame Traded Over Who Did It

At least 60 Orthodox Christians were killed in two attacks in Ethiopia's conflict-hit Oromia region over the past week — 14 people in Merti Woreda on 20 October and 46 in Sherka Woreda on 24 October — the Holy Synod of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said in a statement issued on Monday, which also reported more than 20 homes burned and more than 20 people hospitalised with severe injuries. A resident whose 75-year-old father was killed told BBC Amharic the attackers surrounded them from all four directions in an ambush they were completely unprepared for, and an eyewitness said five of the dead were children; many residents have since fled the area, and the church said attacks on Christians have been rising and that it had repeatedly raised security concerns with authorities without a solution. Responsibility is disputed. The Oromia regional government said a rebel alliance launched the attacks to try to spark religious conflict, without providing evidence; a local official blamed the Alliance for Survival, a newly formed coalition of seven armed groups — announced last month with the stated aim of overthrowing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government — that includes the Oromo Liberation Army. A source linked to a rebel alliance in the northern Tigray region instead blamed the regional government itself, citing conversations with two local residents. The OLA, which the regional government has blamed for previous attacks on Orthodox Christians, did not immediately respond to a request for comment and has denied carrying out similar attacks before. The regional government said it would take action against those responsible.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War27 October 2026

UN Panel: Russia's Energy Attacks Are Crimes Against Humanity — as Ballistic Missiles Hit a Kyiv University Campus

A United Nations-backed commission of inquiry has concluded that Russian officials committed war crimes and crimes against humanity through sustained attacks on Ukraine's civilian energy infrastructure — a policy it says was meant to deprive civilians of essential supplies as winter approaches. In its report to the UN General Assembly, the commission named President Vladimir Putin, former defence minister Sergei Shoigu, his successor Andrey Belousov and Chief of the General Staff Valery Gerasimov as those most responsible for planning and overseeing the attacks, pointing to Putin's own statements and an August Russian defence ministry declaration that announced strikes on energy infrastructure and warned Ukrainians: "In winter you will freeze." One woman in Dnipro told investigators that cold "paralyzes you; there's simply no way to get warm." The International Criminal Court has previously issued arrest warrants for Putin, Shoigu and Gerasimov over alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity. The report landed as Russia launched another mass overnight strike — 16 missiles and more than 280 drones by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's count, with air defences downing two ballistic missiles and 255 drones. Ballistic missiles struck the campus of Kyiv's State University of Trade and Economics near the Lisova metro station, damaging academic buildings and dormitories where more than 200 students and staff lived, hitting the Southern Bridge and a hospital's windows in the Dniprovskyi district, and damaging a DTEK electricity office; a 46-year-old woman was killed and around 15 people injured across six districts, including two students seriously hurt and two ambulance workers injured while answering a call. The university's rector stressed that no students or staff were killed, correcting reports that circulated overnight. Zelenskyy said the missiles hit crowded areas with no military facility nearby and appealed for systems able to destroy Russian ballistic missiles before launch.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election27 October 2026

Final Hours of Voting in Israel: Record-Pace Turnout, Fraud Warnings From Netanyahu, Exit Polls Due at 10 p.m.

Israelis were voting into Tuesday evening in the first Knesset election since the 7 October 2023 attacks, with polling stations due to close at 10 p.m. local time and television exit polls to follow immediately — the first real test of whether Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving leader with 19 years in office, survives a challenge led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot. The final pre-election surveys converged on deadlock: Channel 12 projected Eisenkot's bloc on 56 seats and Netanyahu's on 50, Channel 13 put them at 54 and 50, and Kan 11 at 52 and 50 — all short of the 61 seats needed to govern the 120-member Knesset, with Eisenkot's Yashar party projected as the largest single faction ahead of Likud in two of the three polls. Turnout ran at its fastest pace in decades, reaching 31.5% by midday. Netanyahu spent the day urging right-wing supporters to turn out — "no beach, no barbecue," he told them — declared the day an emergency, and warned that high Arab turnout and alleged irregularities could "steal" the election, claims that drew warnings from the opposition that he was laying the groundwork to challenge the result. Voters at Tel Aviv's Hostages Square, where families of those killed or taken on 7 October gathered before casting ballots, illustrated the stakes: calls for an official inquiry into the 2023 attack, the ultra-Orthodox conscription dispute, efforts to curb the Supreme Court, national security and the course of the war all dominated the campaign. Counting runs overnight, official results are expected by 4 November, and whoever leads will still face weeks of coalition bargaining — Israel held five elections between 2019 and 2022 amid repeated failures to form a stable government — while Netanyahu's government continues in a caretaker capacity in the meantime.

Latest · Americas · Brazil27 October 2026

Flávio Bolsonaro Leaves Brazil for the United States Two Days After Losing the Runoff to Lula

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro has left Brazil for the United States following his defeat in Sunday's presidential runoff, an ally of the senator told Reuters on Tuesday, confirming earlier media reports. The departure came two days after Brazil's electoral court confirmed that President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva had won re-election with 51.8% of the vote — a lead of nearly 4.4 million votes, on about 64 million ballots — earning the 80-year-old a fourth, non-consecutive term that begins on 5 January 2027. The comeback was historic: Bolsonaro had led the first round on 4 October by 47% to 45%, and no candidate since Brazil's return to direct elections in 1989 had won the presidency after finishing second in the first round; Lula overturned the deficit by adding some 10.4 million votes between rounds, against 3.7 million for his rival. The result remains contested. Voting on Sunday was extended by two hours on a Supreme Court order after both campaigns petitioned over transport disruption and heavy rain, and Bolsonaro refused to concede, called the extension unprecedented and said he would seek to have the result annulled — even though his own campaign had also requested more time. His father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, is serving a 27-year jail term for attempting a coup after losing to Lula in 2022. Lula's win bucks a recent rightward trend across Latin America and leaves the region's two most populous countries, Brazil and Mexico, under left-of-centre leaders despite higher US tariffs under President Donald Trump, a frequent target of Lula's criticism.

Latest · Europe · Serbia26 October 2026

Serbia Wakes Up After the Landslide: Student-Backed Winners Hold 139 Seats — and Now Have to Work Out How to Govern

Serbia spent Monday absorbing a political earthquake: with almost 99% of votes counted from Sunday's snap parliamentary election, the student-backed list born of nearly two years of anti-corruption protests had won 52.7% of the vote and 139 of the 250 seats in parliament, against 39.2% and 97 seats for the alliance led by Aleksandar Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party (SNS), ending the SNS's 14-year grip on power. Vučić, who has dominated Serbian politics as prime minister or president since 2014, conceded on Sunday night — "It is clear that we have lost these elections, the Student List has won" — and tens of thousands of people poured into the streets of Belgrade and other cities in celebration. He had called the snap election in September after demonstrations, triggered by the collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad in 2024 that killed 16 people, swelled into a national movement against perceived corruption and creeping authoritarianism. The result echoes the defeat of Hungary's Viktor Orbán in April. The harder question now is what comes next: the winning list is a broad protest coalition rather than a conventional party, and even its own supporters admit few know how it will govern. "I thought it was unbelievable, I still do not believe it," said university administrator Marijana Radovanovic Pajovic. The students will have to convert a movement built on street protest into a government with policies, ministers and a programme — while a defeated but still large SNS bloc watches from opposition.

Latest · Africa · Sudan26 October 2026

RSF Drones Knock Out Five Power Stations in Sudan: Whole States in the East Go Dark, Insulin and Irrigation at Risk

Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces launched a coordinated drone attack on Monday against five electricity facilities, cutting power across large parts of the country's army-held east, a government official told AFP. The strikes hit two stations each in Al-Jazira and Gedaref states and one in White Nile state — the state power company named the Singa, Al-Hawata, Al-Fao, Mashkour and Al-Junaid substations — knocking out the south-eastern grid and, the company warned, putting the stability of the wider national grid at risk; guards at the Singa facility were injured. Electricity had been out since about 2 a.m. in Al-Jazira, with no word on when it would return. For residents the consequences are immediate: in Al-Fao, Babiker Youssef told AFP his diabetic son's insulin must be kept refrigerated and he feared it would spoil; a farmer in Al-Junaid said a station only recently repaired after an earlier strike — which had raised hopes irrigation pumps would finally run again — had been hit once more; a Gedaref grocer said he would have to sell off his chicken stock at a loss or lose it entirely. The UN has repeatedly warned that attacks on power stations threaten health and essential services for millions of people in what is already the world's largest hunger crisis, with nearly 20 million people facing extreme hunger; drone strikes alone killed more than 300 people and injured hundreds more in the first three weeks of October, according to a UN tally. The army and the RSF, former allies, have been at war since April 2023 in a conflict aid workers estimate has killed more than 200,000 people, shut most hospitals and left at least eight million children out of school.

Latest · Africa · Somalia27 October 2026

Dozens of Somali Soldiers Killed in a Major Battle With al Shabaab in the South, Soldiers Tell Reuters

Dozens of Somali government soldiers were killed in a major battle with al Shabaab militants in southern Somalia, two soldiers told Reuters, in one of the heaviest single clashes of the country's nearly two-decade insurgency. The fighting took place in the Jameeco area of Buurhakaba district, where, according to the defence ministry, militants attacked national army positions using vehicles packed with explosives. One soldier, who gave his name as Ahmed, said he had helped transport 40 dead soldiers and more than 100 wounded to hospitals in the capital, Mogadishu; another, Farah, said al Shabaab car bombs destroyed five military vehicles and estimated that dozens of soldiers were killed in the explosions. The defence ministry said government forces killed about 75 militants and wounded another 56, but its statement did not say how many soldiers were killed or wounded, and some army vehicles were damaged. Al Shabaab, which is linked to al Qaeda, has fought since the late 2000s to overthrow Somalia's internationally backed government and impose its own rule based on its strict interpretation of Sharia law, and it continues to control swathes of the countryside while mounting attacks on military and civilian targets. Somalia has had no strong central government since the fall of the autocratic ruler Mohamed Siad Barre in 1991.

Latest · Business & Technology · SpaceX26 October 2026

SpaceX Locks In $40 Billion of GPU Financing With Apollo — the Largest Deal of Its Kind — as Musk Warns Debt Markets Have Shut

SpaceX has secured a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion in financing for artificial intelligence chips and data-centre equipment, in what the company called the largest committed GPU financing to date, according to a regulatory filing disclosed on Monday. The structure is a lease rather than a conventional loan: an entity managed by Apollo Global Management will buy the chips and server hardware and lease them to SpaceX, which will repay about $40 billion in principal through quarterly instalments over roughly five years per batch, plus interest at a fixed rate yet to be set. The master lease is expected to close before the end of the year, with equipment — including Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips, for what Apollo called SpaceX's "terrestrial compute build-out" — arriving in tranches and leases starting in the first half of 2027. Chief executive Elon Musk, who announced the deal on Sunday, used it to warn that debt markets have "almost entirely shut off access to money for any company that doesn't have substantial revenue independent of AI data centers," adding that SpaceX qualified because it is investment grade with Starlink and launch revenue. CFO Bret Johnsen said the lease diversifies the company's funding and that its AI infrastructure investments are paying back in under a year. The scale reflects a business transformed since SpaceX absorbed xAI in February: its AI segment — the Grok chatbot, the Cursor coding tool and its cloud compute business — produced $7.6 billion of the company's $14.6 billion in third-quarter revenue, more than Starlink and rockets combined, while capital spending hit nearly $20 billion in the quarter and is planned at another $35–40 billion in the fourth quarter alone.
