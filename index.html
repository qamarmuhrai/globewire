Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya, Turkey — Longest COP on Record Ends Without a Deal23 November 2026

COP31 Climate Summit Collapses in Turkey as Countries Deadlock Over Fossil Fuels and Finance

The United Nations COP31 climate conference in Antalya ended in collapse on Sunday after more than two weeks of talks, the longest climate summit on record, when countries failed to agree an overarching pact. The proposed Antalya Pact broke down over whether the final text would name fossil fuels at all: the European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands, Norway and other vulnerable states refused to endorse a deal that omitted the main driver of warming, while Saudi Arabia, Russia and other oil-producing states blocked any reference to phasing fuels out and pushed carbon capture instead. About 130 countries had written to the presidency insisting that any agreement preserve the path set at COP28 in Dubai in 2023, when governments first agreed to transition away from fossil fuels. There was also no progress on money to help poorer countries shift their energy systems and cope with disasters. COP31 President Murat Kurum acknowledged there was no consensus to adopt the text, and negotiators and campaigners called the failure the biggest setback for climate diplomacy since Copenhagen in 2009, raising fresh questions about the UN’s consensus-based process.

Latest · Americas · Santa Clara — Board Reversal Lifts Shares More Than 31% in Premarket Trading23 November 2026

Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as Chief Executive, Sending US Stocks Sharply Higher

Nvidia said on Monday that it was restoring Jensen Huang as chief executive and a director with immediate effect, an abrupt reversal that followed pressure from President Donald Trump and a board intervention confirmed by the White House. The move caps days of turmoil after revelations about Huang’s personal conduct, including an alleged relationship with a woman reported to have links to Chinese intelligence, which had shaken confidence in the world’s most valuable chipmaker at the centre of the artificial intelligence boom. Investors reacted immediately: Nvidia shares jumped more than 31% in premarket trading and broader US markets rallied, with the S&P 500 recovering nearly half of the previous week’s AI-driven losses as traders bet that Huang’s return would stabilise strategy and supply talks. The Treasury Department separately signalled that no further restrictions were needed on Nvidia chip exports to China, adding to the sense of a coordinated effort in Washington to steady a company now treated as strategic infrastructure.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cikande, Banten — About 30 Workers Still Missing23 November 2026

Indonesia Confirms 24 Killed in Candy Factory Fire as Rescuers Search Unstable Wreckage

Indonesian authorities confirmed on Monday that 24 people died in a fire that tore through a confectionery factory in Cikande, Serang regency, over the weekend, while rescuers continued searching for about 30 workers still missing. The blaze broke out on Saturday evening at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia plant, part of United Family Food, when about 300 workers were inside; firefighters battled it for roughly 14 hours. Officials said 29 people were confirmed safe, five of them treated in hospital, and that 24 body bags had been recovered. The heat was so intense that visual identification is impossible, and police disaster teams are collecting DNA from relatives and from the missing workers’ belongings, a process expected to take two to three weeks. Preliminary findings point to a short circuit in an air-conditioning unit, and a presidential adviser on labour said safety compliance at the plant is now under review and the company’s chief executive will be summoned. Rescue teams warned that lingering hotspots and an increasingly unstable structure are slowing the search.

Latest · Americas · Washington — North Carolina Recount Decides 258th Seat23 November 2026

US Democrats Clinch 258 House Seats, Their Largest Majority Since 1992, and Control of the Senate Too

US House Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the 435-member chamber, their strongest result since 1992, after Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated 11-term Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina’s 5th district following a recount. The tally gives Democrats a gain of more than 40 seats in the 3 November midterms, surpassing their 2018 pickup, with four races still uncalled and Republicans on at least 173 seats. Democrats will also hold 56 Senate seats when the new Congress is sworn in on 3 January, giving them control of both chambers while a Republican president remains in the White House — a divided government that promises fights over spending, oversight and the administration’s agenda. Party leaders now face the opposite problem to defeat: voters delivered a sweeping mandate built heavily on affordability promises, and expectations to deliver quickly will be intense from the first weeks of the new session.

Latest · Europe · Madrid — Nearly a Decade in Self-Imposed Exile23 November 2026

Spanish Supreme Court Clears Last Legal Hurdle for Catalan Leader Puigdemont to Return From Exile

Spain’s Supreme Court has shelved the remaining criminal case against Catalan separatist leader Carles Puigdemont, removing the last legal obstacle to his return after nearly a decade of self-imposed exile. Puigdemont, a former president of Catalonia, left Spain after the region’s 2017 independence push and has lived abroad while Spanish courts pursued cases linked to that bid. Monday’s decision closes the final proceeding that risked his arrest on arrival, and it shifts the question from law to politics: when he returns, how Madrid manages his re-entry, and what his comeback means for Catalonia’s pro-independence movement and for Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s fragile parliamentary arithmetic, which has depended in part on Catalan parties. Supporters are likely to present a return as vindication, while opponents in Madrid have long argued that accountability for 2017 should not be traded away. No return date was announced in the initial reporting.

Latest · Middle East · Damascus — Demining Training at the Centre of the Proposal23 November 2026

United States Discusses Training Syrian Soldiers, in What Would Be a First Since Rebels Took Damascus

The United States is discussing a plan to train Syrian soldiers in demining, according to five people familiar with the proposal, in what would be the first American training mission with Syria’s military since former rebel fighters took power in Damascus in 2024. The multinational mission would build on the Damascus Coordination Center set up last year in Jordan to link Syrian authorities, US forces and regional allies. The idea still faces legal obstacles in Washington and scepticism inside parts of the Syrian military, the sources said. It follows a rapid thaw: Washington lifted broad economic sanctions in December 2025, removed Syria’s state sponsor of terrorism designation in August 2026, and eased arms-export restrictions in October so licences can be considered case by case. Syrian Defence Minister Murhaf Abu Qasra met the operation’s commander, Rear Admiral Liam Hulin, in Damascus on 12 November, according to state news agency SANA, a sign of how far contacts have moved from confrontation toward structured cooperation.

Latest · Africa · Kinshasa — Export Halt Starts 30 November23 November 2026

Congo Suspends Cobalt Exports Again to Fight Oversupply and Support Prices

The Democratic Republic of Congo will suspend cobalt exports from 30 November until further notice, its strategic minerals regulator said on Monday, as the world’s top producer tries to curb oversupply, lift weak prices and run down rising inventories. Cobalt is a key ingredient in many electric vehicle batteries and electronics, and Congo’s dominance of global supply means its export decisions ripple through automakers and battery makers worldwide. This is not Kinshasa’s first intervention: previous halts and quota systems have been used to assert more control over a sector long shaped by foreign mining firms and volatile prices. Traders will watch whether the suspension tightens supply enough to move prices, whether smuggling or stockpile releases blunt its effect, and how long the government can hold the line if mining revenues fall. The announcement gives no end date, leaving buyers to plan around an open-ended restriction from their most important source.

Latest · Europe · Prague and Budapest — Summit on 26–27 November to Debate 2028–2034 Plan23 November 2026

Czech and Hungarian Leaders Reject New EU Budget Proposal as ‘Absolutely Unacceptable’

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babis on Monday called the latest proposal for the European Union’s 2028–2034 budget absolutely unacceptable, joining Hungarian opposition ahead of a special leaders’ summit on 26–27 November. The plan, presented on Saturday by European Council President António Costa, would trim the seven-year budget to about €1.582 trillion, cutting €40 billion from the Irish presidency’s October draft and far more from the European Commission’s original proposal. Babis objected that too little goes directly to member states compared with central programmes, and repeated his view that funding for Ukraine should sit outside the budget; he has previously refused Czech participation in a loan for Kyiv. The proposal is being squeezed from both sides: Germany, the largest net contributor, says it is still too high, while France and the European Parliament’s chief budget negotiator have also objected. With net contributors and net beneficiaries far apart, Costa’s target of narrowing differences this week and reaching agreement in December looks difficult.

Latest · Global Affairs · Bridgetown, Barbados — About 150 Participants at Hybrid Summit23 November 2026

Guterres Urges Stronger AI Safety Rules at Barbados Summit as Risks Outpace Countries’ Capacity

UN Secretary-General António Guterres used the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety in Barbados on Monday to argue that far more must be done to stop artificial intelligence from causing harm, as the technology’s risks grow faster than many countries’ ability to test systems and respond. Co-hosted with Barbadian Prime Minister Mia Mottley, the hybrid meeting brought together about 150 participants, including ministers from Norway and Finland, industry leaders, heads of AI safety institutes and members of the UN’s independent scientific panel on AI. At its centre is Guterres’s proposal for a multilateral risk-management framework with independent oversight — explicitly not a treaty or regulation, his envoy Amandeep Singh Gill stressed, but a template countries can use to build national safety infrastructure suited to their own circumstances. The agenda also covers access, inclusion and linguistic diversity, amid fears in the developing world of being shut out of AI’s benefits in health, science, education and disaster forecasting. Military uses of AI are not on the table, and no negotiated outcome is planned; organisers expect calls to action and pledges instead.

Latest · Americas · Ottawa — Signing Expected Tuesday23 November 2026

Canada and Qatar Set to Sign Investment Protection Pact on Tuesday, Source Says

Canada and Qatar are set to sign a foreign investment promotion and protection agreement on Tuesday, a source familiar with the process said, in a step aimed at unlocking Qatari investment that Doha pledged in January but has yet to deliver. Such pacts set legal protections for investors on both sides — covering fair treatment, expropriation and dispute resolution — and are often the precondition Gulf sovereign funds and state-linked firms require before committing large sums abroad. For Ottawa, the deal fits a broader push to diversify capital sources and trade ties beyond the United States, courting Gulf money for energy, infrastructure and critical minerals. For Qatar, it deepens a North American foothold at a time when its investment diplomacy spans aviation, real estate and energy across several continents. The source gave no figure for the investment expected to follow, and the test will be whether signed protections translate into announced projects in the months after Tuesday’s ceremony.
