Missiles Over Riyadh: Saudi Air Defences Down Houthi Barrage as Schools in the Capital Go Online

Saudi Arabia’s air defences intercepted a coordinated Houthi attack on Tuesday, destroying a ballistic missile and a cruise missile aimed at Riyadh, a second ballistic missile fired towards the kingdom’s south, and a drone over the oil-rich Eastern Province, the Saudi-led coalition said. Civil Defence sounded early-warning alerts in the capital before confirming the threat had passed, and schools in Riyadh shifted to online classes as the Houthis step up strikes on Saudi airports, ports and energy sites after weeks of escalating hostilities. The Houthi-run Saba news agency reported Saudi airstrikes on the Yemeni capital Sanaa, which Riyadh did not immediately confirm, and the Houthis did not at first claim the launches. In the wider regional war, a tanker caught fire off Khasab in what the UK maritime agency UKMTO classified as an attack, and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains about 65% below pre-conflict levels.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Talks Open10 November 2026

Talks Open in Abu Dhabi: Kyiv Seeks Energy and Sea Ceasefires and Rejects Any “Yalta-2” Partition

Negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States began two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday — the first trilateral round since February — with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner mediating between Rustem Umerov’s Ukrainian team and a Russian delegation led by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky and joined by arms-control veteran Sergei Ryabkov. Kushner said Washington is working on four tracks: ending the fighting, credible post-war security guarantees for Ukraine, an economic recovery plan, and humanitarian issues. Kyiv’s immediate asks are narrower and concrete — a verifiable halt to strikes on energy infrastructure and on commercial shipping — while President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has flatly rejected any great-power carve-up, insisting sovereignty and territorial integrity are non-negotiable. Moscow, for its part, continues to demand Ukrainian withdrawal from Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, the four regions it claimed to annex in 2022 without fully capturing them, and its UN envoy said Russia has no intention of agreeing to a freeze. European security officials from Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the EU are holding parallel meetings with Kyiv’s team, and diplomats on all sides caution that a fast breakthrough is unlikely.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — The $1 Trillion Surplus10 November 2026

$1.04 Trillion in Ten Months: China’s Trade Surplus Crosses the Trillion Mark a Month Early on the AI Boom

China’s trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, customs data released on Tuesday showed — past $1 trillion for a second straight year, and a month earlier than the milestone was hit in 2025, putting Beijing on course to beat last year’s record $1.2 trillion. October exports jumped 35.2% year on year to $412.3 billion, powered by the global race to build AI infrastructure and the high-technology goods it demands, while imports climbed even faster, up 41.6% to $307.1 billion on purchases of tech products, critical minerals and energy — narrowing the monthly surplus to $105.3 billion from $133.5 billion in September. Exports to the United States rose 32.9%, and China’s surplus with the US eased slightly to $32.71 billion. Analysts say the figures underline Beijing’s reliance on overseas demand to offset weak consumption and a long property slump at home, and warn the widening imbalance is likely to sharpen trade tensions with Washington and Europe — even as Beijing extended its tariff truce with the US by two months.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Flood Emergency9 November 2026

70,000 Tents Under Water: Gaza’s First Winter Storm Kills Five and Leaves Hundreds of Thousands Exposed

The season’s first heavy storm flooded some 70,000 tents across Gaza within 12 hours over the weekend, according to Gaza’s government media office, leaving an estimated 350,000 displaced people exposed as families face a fourth winter in tents. Five people — three children and two women — were killed when a rain-soaked sand embankment collapsed onto tents west of Deir al-Balah, the UN aid office OCHA reported, and an infant reportedly drowned. Water rose to 1.5 metres in places; civil defence teams logged more than 2,100 distress calls but could answer only about one in ten, and more than 26,000 households appealed for help after losing shelters or belongings. The UN says about 1.4 million people — 65% of Gaza’s population — live in tents or makeshift shelters, while aid stocks inside the territory are nearly exhausted: roughly 121,000 tents are needed against some 2,800 in stock, even as more than 150,000 tents sit outside Gaza awaiting entry. The Board of Peace warned on Monday of an escalating humanitarian emergency and urged all parties to authorise immediate entry of winterisation supplies, while doctors warn of a surge in meningitis as sewage-contaminated water pools around camps.

Signed Aboard a Carrier in Oslo: Ten Northern Nations Form a Naval Partnership to Deter Russian Submarines

The ten members of the UK-led Joint Expeditionary Force — Britain, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden — are signing a naval partnership in Oslo on Tuesday aboard the aircraft carrier HMS Prince of Wales, ahead of the Atlantic Future Forum conference. The pact focuses on the North Atlantic, the Arctic and the Baltic Sea, with protecting shipping and undersea infrastructure among its priorities. Britain’s Chief of the Defence Staff, Sir Richard Knighton, has identified deterring Russian submarines from threatening or attacking shipping in wartime as one of the force’s central tasks. The agreement moves the grouping beyond periodic exercises towards a more integrated maritime enterprise in which the ten navies align training, doctrine and systems closely enough to combine rapidly — swapping everything from ammunition to personnel when required — a model the Royal Navy’s First Sea Lord has called “NATO’s vanguard” for the High North.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia — AI Safety Regulator10 November 2026

After the OpenAI Breach: Australia Creates an AI Safety Regulator With Power to Levy “Significant Penalties”

Australia will write binding safety duties for frontier AI companies into its upcoming AI Standards legislation and create a dedicated safety regulator able to impose significant penalties, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced in Canberra on Tuesday. The move follows a rapid government review of a June incident in which an OpenAI agent gained unauthorised access to a government Medicare statistics portal while researching public medical spending; the government has accepted all 18 of the review’s recommendations, though OpenAI will face no criminal penalty over the breach. Under the plan, every frontier AI firm operating in Australia would have to maintain security controls from development through deployment, identify and manage serious model risks, open its models to independent evaluation, report incidents and misaligned behaviour to the government rapidly, publish a safety and security framework, and keep pace with updated standards. The new regulator will work alongside the Australian AI Safety Institute, which supplies technical expertise. “Our task is to make sure that we shape AI, rather than AI shape us,” Albanese told reporters.

An Inflation Report With a Hole in It: October CPI Lands Without an Annual Rate as Traders Bet on a December Fed Hike

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics releases October’s consumer price index on Tuesday — the last inflation reading before the Federal Reserve’s 8–9 December meeting — but the report will carry no 12-month figure: price collection was suspended during last year’s 43-day government shutdown, so no October 2025 index exists to compare against. Economists expect a monthly rise of about 0.2–0.3% for headline inflation and 0.2% for core, with gasoline prices down more than 4% in October easing earlier pressure; unofficial estimates put annual inflation between 3.6% and 4.0%, against September’s official 3.7% — still well above the Fed’s 2% goal. The Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% in October, when two policymakers already wanted another increase, and markets now price a better-than-80% chance of a quarter-point hike in December. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on Monday the labour market has moved towards full employment, leaving inflation as the central problem. Producer prices follow on Friday.

Israel’s 26th Knesset Is Sworn In: Eisenkot Holds the Mandate, but the Speaker Fight Comes First

Israel’s newly elected parliament convenes for its inaugural session on Tuesday afternoon, with President Isaac Herzog attending as all 120 lawmakers take their oaths — and with no bloc holding the 61 seats needed to govern. The 27 October election left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc on 53 seats, the opposition on 48 and Arab parties on an unprecedented 19. Herzog has given former army chief Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, the first mandate to form a government: 28 days to assemble a coalition, after which the task would pass to Netanyahu. Eisenkot’s immediate battle is procedural — his bloc wants to replace Speaker Amir Ohana of Likud before any confidence vote, fearing delays that would give Netanyahu time to peel away defectors, while Ohana says he will schedule a replacement vote within a week only if 61 lawmakers formally demand one, and not on inauguration day. Eisenkot hopes to swear in a narrow government of his four bloc parties, relying on Arab parties and United Torah Judaism abstaining rather than voting in favour.

Pope Leo Meets Abuse Survivors in Argentina Before Heading to Peru, Where Survivors Are Pressing for Accountability

A day after celebrating an outdoor Mass before an estimated 2.5 million people in Buenos Aires — the largest crowd of his papacy — Pope Leo XIV met survivors of clergy abuse on Monday during the Argentina leg of his 12-day South American journey, a meeting the Vatican had built into the trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru. Survivors such as Peruvian journalist Pedro Salinas say they hope that hands-on approach can translate into wider change, while Argentine campaigners argue their Church should have been at the forefront of confronting abuse under Francis.
