Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Still Voting3 November 2026

Forecasters Lean Democratic in All Seven Key Senate Races, but the First Polls Have Not Even Closed Yet

America is deep into Election Day with nothing counted: the first polls close at 6pm Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky, and the last at 1am Eastern in Alaska’s Aleutian Islands. All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships are being decided, and early voting closed at a midterm record of more than 58 million ballots. The signals forecasters are trading on point one way: prediction market Kalshi had Democrats ahead in all seven closely watched Senate races — Iowa at 52%, Kansas 63%, Alaska, Ohio, Maine and Texas all around 70–72%, and Michigan at 79% — while Decision Desk HQ put the Democratic chance of taking the House at 84%, with 190 seats rated safe for the party. Republicans start from 218 House seats to the Democrats’ 214 and a 53–47 Senate edge, so Democrats need a net gain of four in each chamber. The day has not been glitch-free: officials in New York reported ballot-scanner problems in Westchester County, and the Justice Department’s roughly 1,000 federal monitors — its largest deployment ever — are spread across all 50 states. President Trump told supporters things were “going good” and urged them to vote as if his name were on the ballot; total cycle spending is projected near $12 billion, a midterm record. Officials warn the country may still go to bed without knowing who controls either chamber.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Oil Reverses3 November 2026

Up to $107 by Morning, Down to $102.97 by Evening: Oil Cannot Decide What the Qeshm Strike Means

Oil spent Tuesday whipsawing on the Strait of Hormuz. Brent spiked toward $107 in the morning after US officials confirmed a fighter jet had destroyed an Iranian missile launcher on Qeshm Island — retaliation for a Revolutionary Guard surface-to-air missile that downed an American drone east of the strait — then gave the gains back, trading 1.72% lower at $102.97 a barrel by early evening Gulf time, with US crude down about 1% at $95.47. The swing follows Monday’s plunge of more than 7%, the steepest since July, when Qatar-mediated talks raised hopes of a deal; no agreement exists, and a senior Iranian official told Reuters the sides remain far apart. Tehran says its seven-step proposal, now compressed, could reopen Hormuz within two days if Washington meets its conditions — an end to the war, unfrozen assets, lifted oil sanctions and an end to the naval blockade. Goldman Sachs has meanwhile raised its December Brent forecast to $105 from $90 and its 2027 average to $88, attributing roughly $27 a barrel of October’s rally to war-risk premium. The strait carried about a fifth of world oil and gas before the conflict began in late February, and the world has drawn down nearly a billion barrels from inventories since. Qatar said Tuesday it remains hopeful; markets are now watching whether anything escalates once America’s midterm vote is over.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Toll Rises to 333 November 2026

33 Dead at the Aid Centre, 50 Still Under the Rubble: Zelenskyy Takes the Case to Berlin as Europe Sets Its Own Table

The toll from Monday’s strike on the Komyshuvakha school-turned-aid-centre has risen again: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told reporters in Berlin on Tuesday that 33 people were killed, five were rescued, and more than 50 may still be trapped — and that after the first bombs, a second strike of nine more hit the shelter where people had gathered, a double-tap that killed two rescuers and four police officers among the dead. Standing beside him, Chancellor Friedrich Merz urged Moscow to engage seriously, and announced a leaders’ meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin on 19 November to agree support for Ukraine through 2027. The diplomatic calendar is filling around next week’s US-brokered Abu Dhabi talks on 10–11 November: the European Commission hosts the US, Ukraine, NATO, France, Germany, the UK and the World Bank in Brussels on Wednesday to discuss a “prosperity plan” and security guarantees. On the ground the war did not pause — overnight Russia fired 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles, knocking three of DTEK’s five thermal plants offline and leaving 130 settlements in Zhytomyr region without power; a Russian drone killed a 14-year-old in the Kyiv region, and four aerial bombs on Kramatorsk killed one person and injured five. Kyiv says it has resumed strikes on Russian oil refineries, with a major fire reported at Yaroslavl, and Ukraine’s general staff put Russian losses at 2,060 in the past day. Britain and France will meanwhile lead Exercise Koalicja in Poland from 9–22 November — 2,800 troops from 11 countries rehearsing a post-war deployment force for a peace that does not yet exist.

Latest · Europe · Aviation — Bomb Threats3 November 2026

Rome, Murcia, Sofia, Warsaw: A Wave of Bomb Threats Grounds Flights Across Europe — and Every Search Finds Nothing

A string of bomb threats disrupted passenger flights across at least five European countries on Tuesday, forcing diversions, emergency landings and full security sweeps — with no explosive device found anywhere. At Rome’s Fiumicino airport, two Ryanair flights, from Lisbon and from Dublin, were searched after landing; the Dublin flight was escorted in by the Italian Air Force. In Spain, Murcia’s airport activated its security protocol for a Ryanair flight from Manchester carrying 114 passengers and seven crew. Bulgaria took threats against three separate Sofia-bound services: a Ryanair flight from Dublin landed with 185 people aboard, a Wizz Air flight from Milan/Bergamo with 176, and a flight arriving from Israel made an emergency landing after a threat received by email — a day after another Wizz Air London–Sofia flight diverted to Budapest over a similar warning. Poland saw two incidents: a LOT flight from London Heathrow landed at Warsaw-Modlin instead of Chopin, and an SAS Copenhagen–Warsaw flight was checked on arrival at Chopin. Authorities have not linked the threats publicly, and the pattern stretches back at least to 18 October, when an Austrian Airlines Tel Aviv–Vienna flight diverted to Sofia. For passengers the cost is measured in hours of delay and evacuated terminals; for investigators, the question is whether one hoaxer or several are testing how much disruption a single message can buy.

Qatar and France issued a joint call on Tuesday for freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz to be restored “without constraints or fees”, for Israeli forces to withdraw from Gaza in line with the agreed arrangements, and for humanitarian aid to reach the enclave urgently, safely and without conditions. Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Nearly 1,000 Factories3 November 2026

300 More Factories Shut, and Dhaka Points at Hasina: Day Four Turns Bangladesh’s Wage Fight Political

Bangladesh’s garment unrest widened on Tuesday and acquired a political charge. The industry association BGMEA said fresh violence forced another 300 factories to close, taking the total affected since the protests began to nearly 1,000, and the government publicly accused deposed prime minister Sheikh Hasina of fuelling the labour unrest — a claim that moves the dispute beyond wages. On the streets the pattern held for a fourth day: paramilitary Border Guard units deployed with drones over Gazipur and Narayanganj, police fired tear gas and sound grenades in Ashulia after bricks were thrown, and workers again blocked highways including the Dhaka–Tangail road at Naojor. Police say 58 officers have been injured since Saturday, cases have been filed against thousands of unidentified protesters, and Dhaka police arrested 33 people in 24 hours who they link to the banned Awami League. The demand is unchanged and stark: 30,000 taka a month, about $245, against a minimum wage frozen at 12,500 taka since December 2023, in an economy running at about 8% inflation. Workers describe the arithmetic plainly — Al Jazeera spoke to sewing operator Laili Begum, who earns around 15,000 taka and says some days the family manages only puffed rice. The sector employs about four million people, half of them women, supplies brands including H&M, Zara and Gap, and earns more than 80% of Bangladesh’s export income — which is why a six-member wage board formed on 1 November has so far failed to empty the streets in peak order season.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal — “Miscommunication”3 November 2026

Outside Tower Coverage, Two Pilots Had Only Each Other’s Radios: First Clues in the Pheriche Rotor Collision

Nepal’s aviation authority offered its first explanation-shaped detail on Tuesday: the Pheriche helipad sits outside the coverage of Lukla airport’s control tower, so pilots approaching it must coordinate directly with each other — and preliminary reports point to miscommunication between the two crews. The collision happened at about 8:12am at roughly 4,371 metres, when a Fishtail Air AS350 (9N-AJR) descending from Gorakshep with two foreign passengers met an Air Dynasty AS350 (9N-AOD) arriving from Lukla on a cargo flight; video circulating online appears to show the Fishtail machine descending rapidly despite warnings from ground staff, and the Dynasty helicopter’s rotor blades struck its passenger-compartment windows while both were still two to three metres off the ground. Vasanth Kumar Balaramudu, 47, of Chennai, was killed. Injured were fellow passenger Shailesh Krishna Patel, a New Zealand citizen of Indian origin, and both pilots — Rabindra Prajapati of Fishtail Air and Prajwal KC of Air Dynasty — all airlifted to Nepal Mediciti Hospital in Kathmandu. The Civil Aviation Authority has opened a formal investigation and its director general stressed the cause is not yet established. The crash lands in peak trekking season on the Everest trail, where helicopters are ambulance, bus and freight service in one — and it renews scrutiny of an industry that lost six people in the July 2023 Manang Air crash in the same district.

Bukele Says the Remains Handed Over Were Not His Technicians — as the UN Distances Itself From His 2,000 Troops

The fate of Haiti’s four missing Salvadoran aviation technicians took another turn: President Nayib Bukele said remains handed over by an NGO, said to be the technicians, were not theirs — keeping alive El Salvador’s position that the men, ambushed on 18 October in gang-held Croix-des-Bouquets, may still be alive. That directly contradicts 400 Mawozo leader Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò Sanjou, who told the Associated Press the four are dead, without evidence, in his first interview with a major international outlet. Joseph’s letter to UN Secretary-General António Guterres — in which he says he “officially raised the white flag” on 20 August and asks for a UN delegation for dialogue — has now drawn a careful UN reply: spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric confirmed receipt but stressed it implies no endorsement, no recognition of its author and no UN position. Dujarric also drew a line around Bukele’s deployment of more than 2,000 Salvadoran troops, noting the vast majority do not operate under the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force and so fall outside the UN support office’s mandate and reporting. El Salvador says it is covering 90% of the mission’s operational costs and that the deployment is a rescue operation, not an invasion. Joseph, under UN sanctions since December 2023 with a $1 million US reward on his capture, says he fears reprisals; the UN counts more than 5,700 people killed or injured in Haiti between January and mid-September.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — AGOA Reply3 November 2026

Pretoria Fires Back: Washington “Introduced New Issues” Into Talks That Were Already Conceding Ground

South Africa has given its first formal answer to Washington’s decision to strip it — and Rwanda — of duty-free AGOA access from 1 January 2027, and the answer is an accusation: the trade department says Pretoria offered concessions beyond those it made in 2015, yet the United States kept raising matters that were never part of the negotiations running since 2025. In his letter of 28 October, President Trump gave 60 days’ notice, citing insufficient progress on corruption and on barriers to American trade and investment, and claiming South Africa’s cooperation with governments of concern threatens US security; the letter did not name the corruption cases or the relationships in question. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer confirmed on Monday that an annual review found both countries’ practices inconsistent with AGOA’s rules — for Rwanda, Washington also cited rule of law and political pluralism, weeks after sanctioning Kigali’s defence ministry over support for the M23 rebels in eastern Congo. The money at stake is shrinking but real: AGOA covered about $4 billion, 28% of South African exports to the US, in 2024, falling to roughly $1.7 billion, 10%, in 2025 — vehicles, jewellery, ferroalloys, fruit, nuts and spirits among them. Estimates cited in South African reporting put job losses near 3,000 in agriculture and manufacturing and close to 10,000 overall. Removal does not close the US market; it ends the preference, and exporters will pay standard tariffs instead. Pretoria says the wider relationship remains significant and the door to dialogue stays open.

Latest · Africa · Sudan — Drone Downed, Nile Pact3 November 2026

A “Hostile” Drone Falls Over Blue Nile State as Egypt and Sudan Re-Arm Their Nile Alliance Against Addis Ababa

Sudan’s army said on Tuesday its air defences shot down a “hostile” drone that crossed into southeastern Blue Nile state from Ethiopia — without identifying who flew it, where it was aimed, or what it carried. The interception is the latest move in an escalating paper-and-air war: on Sunday Khartoum filed formal complaints against Ethiopia at the UN Security Council and the African Union, accusing Addis Ababa of letting the Rapid Support Forces launch drone strikes from its territory, with a Sovereignty Council report documenting 70 attacks involving 187 one-way drones this year and nearly 500 civilians killed or injured; other Sudanese statements have put the incident count at 162 with over 400 civilians and 102 military personnel dead. Ethiopia denies everything and counter-accuses Sudan of backing Tigray-linked rebels. On Monday the dispute widened: Egypt and Sudan closed their third “2+2” ministerial meeting in Cairo by reaffirming their joint defence agreement, declaring their water security indivisible under the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement, condemning drone strikes on Sudanese power and water installations, and demanding Ethiopia return to prior-notification rules for operating the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. None of the drone claims — launch sites, operators or tolls — has been independently verified, but the alignment is hardening: a dam dispute, a civil war and a Red Sea rivalry are fusing into one regional confrontation, in a war the UN already calls the world’s worst humanitarian crisis.

Latest · Americas · Supreme Court — Preschools3 November 2026

Can a Catholic Preschool Take Public Money and Still Choose Its Families? The Supreme Court Spends Election Day Asking

While America voted, its Supreme Court heard arguments in St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, a case that could redraw the line between religious liberty and anti-discrimination law far beyond preschool. Colorado funds 15 hours a week of preschool for every four-year-old — about $6,000 a child — and lets families choose among public and hundreds of private providers, including religious ones, provided each agrees not to discriminate on eight grounds including sexual orientation and gender identity. Two Denver-area Catholic parishes, St. Mary in Littleton and St. Bernadette in Lakewood, want in without that condition: the Archdiocese of Denver, which oversees 34 preschools, admits children only from families who support Catholic teaching, and says being forced to choose between the money and the doctrine burdens its faith. Colorado answers that the parishes are asking for public funds to turn away four-year-olds and their families, and that its rule is neutral and generally applicable under the 1990 Smith precedent — a precedent the Court pointedly declined to revisit when it took the case. The Trump administration is backing the parishes; two lower courts, most recently the 10th Circuit in September 2025, backed Colorado. A core dispute is whether exempting secular preferences in the program — priority places for low-income and disabled children — while refusing a religious exemption is itself discrimination. A ruling is expected by June 2027, and after Carson v. Makin in 2022 barred states from excluding religious schools from funding programs altogether, this case decides the follow-up question: what strings may a state attach to the money?

Latest · Africa · Uganda — Leaving Somalia3 November 2026

After 19 Years, the First In Is Heading Out: Uganda Begins Its Long Withdrawal From Somalia

Uganda’s first soldiers are coming home from Somalia, opening a phased withdrawal of the African Union mission’s founding contingent that is due to finish by the end of 2027. Uganda was the first country to deploy, in 2007, and its troops were central to pushing al-Shabaab out of Mogadishu; army spokesperson Chris Magezi said the drawdown now lets Somalia take full responsibility for its own security. The exit follows talks between Presidents Yoweri Museveni and Hassan Sheikh Mohamud in which, by several accounts, Somalia asked for the Ugandan presence to be extended — a measure of how unfinished the handover feels, with al-Shabaab still able to strike and Somali forces still building the logistics, air support and financing that foreign contingents long provided. The withdrawal is one thread in a week of African security files moving at once: the African Union and UN security councils have set a joint position covering four African conflicts including a call for ceasefire, dialogue and aid access in South Sudan; South Sudanese media groups used press-freedom day appeals to demand an end to impunity for attacks on journalists; and Nigerian officials reported at least eight security personnel killed in an ambush in Zamfara State. For Somalia, the test of the next fourteen months is whether “full responsibility” is a capability or a deadline.
