Latest · Middle East · Israel — 67 Back Eisenkot5 November 2026

67 Endorsements: Eisenkot Clears the Threshold, Netanyahu Left on 46

Israel’s post-election arithmetic settled on Thursday night. After the Arab-led Joint List unanimously recommended Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot to President Isaac Herzog — joining Ra’am’s eight lawmakers, the Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu and Naftali Bennett’s Together — Eisenkot’s endorsements reached 67 in the 120-seat Knesset, six above the 61 needed for a majority. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s tally stayed at 46, down from his bloc’s 53 election seats, after the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism withheld any recommendation and made future support conditional on laws protecting Haredi draft exemptions. The Joint List said its move aimed to widen the gap, replace the Netanyahu–Ben-Gvir–Smotrich government and reduce the chance of a unity deal that keeps Netanyahu in power; senior figures in Eisenkot’s camp said nothing was promised in return, and a recommendation is not the same as joining a coalition. Herzog, who has until 11 November to assign the mandate, was due to meet Eisenkot and Netanyahu separately on Thursday night to close the consultations. Whoever is tasked will have 28 days, plus a possible 14-day extension, to form a government. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November, where the opposition also hopes to replace Speaker Amir Ohana. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister until a new government wins a confidence vote — or a fresh election is called.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — At the White House5 November 2026

“Humanity Over War”: Melania Trump Hosts Kyiv and Moscow Envoys Together

It happened. First Lady Melania Trump on Thursday hosted officials from Ukraine and Russia together at the White House — the first such joint appearance there — to mark the Oleshky operation, the six-day localised ceasefire she helped broker around the Russian-occupied Kherson-region town. Ukraine was represented by lawmaker Davyd Arakhamia and human-rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets; Russia by envoy Kirill Dmitriev and human-rights official Yana Lantratova. Mrs Trump said her communications with both sides had been “extensive, professional, detailed and highly cooperative”, noted that the two sides signed the underlying agreements on 23 October, and said the truce that followed from about 27 October allowed roughly 800 people to be evacuated and aid convoys to enter. “We have chosen humanity over war,” she said, urging the sides to build “something more enduring than a temporary ceasefire” and handing over personal letters for Presidents Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Vladimir Putin. Dmitriev’s presence was also the first in-person contact between Russian and US officials since Tuesday’s midterm elections. The gathering is a prelude, not a settlement: full US-mediated trilateral talks follow in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the first since February.

Latest · Sport · Portugal — The No. 7 Shirt Handed Back5 November 2026

146 Goals, 234 Caps, 23 Years: Cristiano Ronaldo Retires From International Football

Cristiano Ronaldo has closed the longest chapter in modern international football. The 41-year-old announced on Thursday, in an emotional video message, that he is retiring from the Portugal national team, saying the day had come to “hand back the number 7 shirt” and that Portugal would always be part of him. He leaves as the men’s game’s all-time international record holder on both counts — 146 goals in 234 appearances since his debut in August 2003 — a five-time Ballon d’Or winner who led Portugal to its first major title at Euro 2016 and to Nations League trophies in 2019 and 2025, and who this year became the first player to appear and score at six World Cups. The ending was not a stadium farewell. Ronaldo last played for Portugal on 24 September, in a 1–0 Nations League win over Wales, and his departure followed a public fallout with coach Jorge Jesus after he was left warming up unused against Norway and walked out of the training camp, drawing a 30-day suspension and a token fine. He is not retiring from football altogether: he continues with Saudi club Al-Nassr, where he has played since 2023, though he said this summer this season would probably be his last. His great rival Lionel Messi also ended his Argentina career this year, after the World Cup final. The Portuguese federation thanked him and promised a tribute.

Latest · Americas · Markets — The Rally Reverses5 November 2026

Nasdaq Down More Than 4%: Tech Slide Erases Wall Street’s Post-Midterm Glow

Wednesday’s celebration turned into Thursday’s retreat. About an hour into the New York session, all the main US indices were lower, led by technology: the Nasdaq Composite was down about 4.2 per cent, the Nasdaq 100 about 4.8 per cent, the S&P 500 about 2.7 per cent and the Dow Jones about 1.7 per cent — a sharp reversal from Wednesday, when the Dow had surged 1,569 points, its best day since April 2025, after Democrats swept both chambers of Congress. Traders described a rotation out of large technology and artificial-intelligence names into defensive sectors, on concern that a Democratic Congress could bring tighter regulation of the AI and data-centre build-out. Information technology was the S&P 500’s weakest sector, down more than 5 per cent in early trade, while consumer staples gained. Broadcom and Nvidia each fell around 7 per cent, Oracle close to 5 per cent, and Qualcomm about 9 per cent despite beating earnings expectations; Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX were also sharply lower. By mid-morning the Nasdaq was more than 10 per cent below its October record high, putting it in correction territory. Investors now turn to Friday’s October US employment report.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 43 Confirmed Dead5 November 2026

Komyshuvakha Toll Reaches 43, a Survivor Found Alive — and the EU Sends €4.36 Billion

The search at Komyshuvakha entered its fourth day with both grief and one relief. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Thursday that 43 people are now confirmed killed in Monday’s Russian bombing of a school in the Zaporizhzhia-region village that was serving as a shelter and humanitarian aid distribution point, while up to 33 people may still be under the rubble. One woman reported missing — Olha, 56 — was found alive and uninjured, and police located six other missing residents alive. Ukrainian officials say at least nine heavy aerial bombs, including three-tonne FAB-3000 glide bombs, hit the building just as civilians gathered for a publicly announced aid distribution, collapsing even its reinforced, EU-funded basement; two first responders were killed in a follow-up strike. The Council of Europe’s human-rights commissioner called it one of the deadliest single attacks on civilians of the war and said it appeared to violate fundamental humanitarian law; Russia’s defence ministry says it targeted Ukrainian military units. The same day, the European Union disbursed €4.36 billion to Ukraine — €3.7 billion in budget support and €658 million for defence — which Zelenskyy said would go to energy, air defence, drones and electronic warfare, alongside nearly €1 billion earmarked for winter energy support. More than 10 million Ukrainians are estimated to need humanitarian assistance.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 43% of Districts In5 November 2026

Count Passes 10 Million Ballots: ANC Near 32%, DA Near 27%, 66 Municipalities Complete

South Africa’s municipal count moved from a near-tie into a clearer, still provisional picture on Thursday afternoon. The Electoral Commission’s 15:59 file recorded 10,237 of 23,696 voting districts captured — about 43 per cent — covering 10,369,271 ballots, of which 10,197,309 were valid. On those figures the ANC held 3,266,301 votes, or 32.03 per cent, against the DA’s 2,756,221, or 27.03 per cent, with uMkhonto weSizwe third on 7.11 per cent; a results-centre snapshot at 41 per cent counted put the MK Party near 7 per cent and the EFF near 5.7 per cent. Sixty-six of 213 municipalities were complete, with 48 councils hung and 18 decided by a party majority — a reminder that national share does not itself award any mayoralty. The metro pattern held: the DA commanded Cape Town at about 60.9 per cent and led Johannesburg near 39.7 per cent against the ANC’s 17.2 and ActionSA’s 16.1, led Nelson Mandela Bay, while the ANC led Buffalo City near 40 per cent. Only 1,148 of 4,485 wards and 1,663 of 8,825 seats were complete, so shares can still move as slower-reporting areas come in. Official results are due on Sunday, 8 November, after which councils have 14 days to form.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — First Rise Since 20235 November 2026

Bank of England Raises Rates to 4% — and the Only Dissent Wanted More

The Bank of England tightened policy for the first time in more than three years on Thursday, lifting Bank Rate from 3.75 to 4.00 per cent by an 8–1 vote and warning that at least one more increase is likely if the US–Iran conflict continues. The single dissent underlined the mood: Catherine Mann voted not to hold, but to raise by half a point to 4.25 per cent, arguing the Bank had been slow to convince markets it had inflation in hand. The Bank now expects inflation — 3.5 per cent in the year to September — to peak at an average of about 4.8 per cent in the second quarter of next year, more than double its 2 per cent target, as Middle East energy costs pass into bills, before easing. It also revised growth up to 1.6 per cent this year and 1.3 per cent next, stronger than the government’s fiscal watchdog forecast last week. Governor Andrew Bailey said the move should not be read as the start of an inevitable long sequence, but that another rise may follow unless the conflict ebbs — bringing the Bank onto the path the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank have already taken this year. The decision is a blow for Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s cost-of-living pledges. The next rate decision falls on 17 December.

Latest · Europe · Eurozone — Twice the Target5 November 2026

Eurozone Inflation Hits 4% — Energy Up 20.8% Puts a December ECB Hike in Focus

Eurozone inflation reached 4.0 per cent in October, Eurostat’s flash estimate shows — up from 3.8 per cent in September, the highest since September 2023 and double the European Central Bank’s 2 per cent target. Energy did the damage, with prices 20.8 per cent higher than a year earlier, up from 18.9 per cent, as the Middle East conflict kept fuel costs elevated; services inflation edged up to 3.3 per cent and unprocessed food to 4.6 per cent. Crucially, core inflation — excluding energy and food — held steady at 2.5 per cent, suggesting the energy shock has not yet fed broadly into wages and underlying prices. The national spread complicates a single policy rate: Italy jumped to 5.6 per cent, Belgium reached 5.2 per cent, France rose to 3.8 per cent, while Germany eased to 2.8 per cent. The ECB held its key rate at 2.50 per cent on 29 October after raising in June and September; markets now put roughly an 85 per cent probability on a quarter-point rise at its 16–17 December meeting, and forecasters expect inflation to climb further toward about 4.5 per cent in January before falling back as last year’s spikes drop out of the comparison. Full October figures are due on 18 November.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — A $5.4 Billion Deal Blocked5 November 2026

China Blocks SoftBank’s $5.4 Billion ABB Robotics Takeover on Security Grounds

China has blocked one of the year’s biggest technology deals. ABB confirmed on Thursday that SoftBank had been told by China’s foreign-investment security review office — operating under the National Development and Reform Commission — that its $5.4 billion acquisition of ABB’s robotics business in China is prohibited. SoftBank had expected approval until it was informed last week, according to reports, and is now assessing whether it can seek approval again; both companies say they remain in close dialogue on the transaction, which was agreed in October 2025 as a centrepiece of Masayoshi Son’s push into physical AI. The stakes are concentrated in China, the unit’s biggest market, where more than 90 per cent of robots for Chinese customers are made locally, including at a mega-factory in Shanghai’s Pudong district. Reporting on the decision pointed to official concern that robotics data could be transferred overseas and used to train AI models, including those of OpenAI, in which SoftBank is a major investor; the review had also scrutinised supply chains and local manufacturing retention. It is the second major deal China has blocked this year, after Meta was ordered to unwind its $2-billion-plus purchase of AI start-up Manus. SoftBank shares fell more than 7 per cent in Tokyo; ABB’s slipped about 1.1 per cent. The deal still faces review in the United States and Europe.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Two Projectiles, No Claim5 November 2026

Tanker Struck by Two Projectiles North of Qatar as Iran Reports Fires in Hormuz

The Gulf’s shipping risk sharpened again on Thursday. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said a tanker was struck by two unidentified projectiles at 2320 GMT on Wednesday, about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal in Qatar; it did not name the vessel or its flag, no one has claimed responsibility, and the crew’s status, the damage and any environmental impact remain unconfirmed. Vessels were advised to transit with caution and report suspicious activity. Separately, Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz in overnight incidents, with one tanker said to have hit a naval mine while using a route Tehran describes as unauthorised. The incidents follow the 30 October hit on Qatar’s Nakilat LNG carrier Al Mayeda in the Gulf, in which no crew were injured. UKMTO warned last month that it is struggling to report the true number of attacks because most ships now refuse to report them. The flare-up comes while Qatar mediates between Washington and Tehran — both sides are reviewing mediators’ proposals — and while oil markets, with Brent near $93–$94, are pricing a lower, but far from absent, risk of renewed escalation.

Latest · Middle East · Türkiye–Azerbaijan — A Rare Crack5 November 2026

“No Obstacle Brotherhood Cannot Overcome”: Erdogan to Visit Baku on 8 November

President Recep Tayyip Erdogan moved on Thursday to repair a rare public strain between Türkiye and Azerbaijan, confirming he plans to travel to Baku on 8 November for Victory Day and saying there is no obstacle the two countries’ brotherhood cannot overcome. Speaking to reporters on his return flight from South Korea, Erdogan said he had already spoken by phone with President Ilham Aliyev and had sent both his intelligence chief and his foreign minister to Azerbaijan. The friction followed Baku’s September change to school uniform rules, restricting Islamic headscarves as incompatible with secular state education; criticism of the move by Turkish figures — including Erdogan adviser Oktay Saral, whom Erdogan dismissed on 25 October — drew accusations in Baku of meddling in domestic affairs, and Aliyev then skipped a Turkic states summit in Ankara last week despite a personal invitation. Erdogan framed the relationship as going far beyond ordinary diplomacy, rooted in shared history and culture, and said he hoped the visit would provide the opportunity to take the necessary steps, without elaborating. The alliance between Ankara and Baku sits at the centre of a fractured region, which is why even a dispute over school dress has drawn high-level shuttle diplomacy.

Latest · Americas · Vatican — 31,000 Kilometres From Friday5 November 2026

After Lung Surgery, Pope Leo Begins an 11-Day Latin America Tour on Friday

Pope Leo begins his first trip to Latin America as pontiff on Friday, an 11-day, roughly 31,000-kilometre journey from 6 to 17 November that the Vatican says is unchanged despite his lung-cancer surgery in mid-October, when surgeons removed a lobe of his right lung and doctors later judged the 71-year-old fit to travel. The itinerary covers ten cities: Montevideo, Paysandú and Florida in Uruguay over two days; Buenos Aires, Córdoba and Luján in Argentina — homeland of his predecessor Francis — over three; and then nearly a week in Peru, taking in Lima, Chiclayo, Cusco and Pucallpa. Peru is a homecoming: Leo arrived there as a missionary in 1985, later served as a bishop and took Peruvian citizenship three decades after arriving. He will celebrate outdoor Masses expected to draw millions, address governments in closely watched speeches, join youth vigils and reach remote Andean communities by helicopter, becoming only the second pope to visit Argentina and Uruguay after John Paul II in the late 1980s. The region remains the world’s most Catholic, holding roughly two-fifths of the world’s Catholics, but Catholic identity there is steadily shrinking even as belief in God remains strong — the pastoral challenge at the heart of the tour.
