Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Vice-President Resigns11 November 2026

Indonesia’s Vice-President Gibran Resigns Over His School Certificate, With a Replacement Vote Due Within 60 Days

Indonesia’s Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has resigned, an aide confirmed on Wednesday, after court rulings found that the schooling documents behind his candidacy did not meet election requirements. Aide Budi Arie Setiadi told Reuters that Gibran, 39, the son of former president Joko Widodo, handed his resignation letter to President Prabowo Subianto on Wednesday, and parliamentary sources said a formal letter to the upper house would follow on Thursday morning. The move came hours after 120 lawmakers from all eight parties filed a motion of opinion against him, the first formal step toward impeachment, ahead of a plenary vote set for Thursday. Senior law minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra said the upper house must now declare the post vacant, ask the president to nominate two candidates, and hold an election within 60 days. The Supreme Court has ordered the education ministry to withdraw the equivalence certificate recognising Gibran’s overseas schooling, and analysts say the vacancy will strain the alliance between Prabowo and the Widodo family that delivered their 2024 landslide.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Envoys to Moscow11 November 2026

After Abu Dhabi, US Envoys Head to Moscow Then Dublin as Kyiv Talks of a 1,500-a-Side Prisoner Swap

The diplomacy that produced a large prisoner-exchange agreement in Abu Dhabi now moves to the two capitals. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are due in Moscow on Thursday to meet President Vladimir Putin, and then in Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, with the exact swap numbers to be finalised on Thursday. Russian officials said the aim is to go past 1,000 prisoners on each side within two to three weeks, while Ukrainian officials have spoken of a figure as high as 1,500 a side. The two days of talks, the first direct Russia–Ukraine meeting in nine months, also made progress on post-war security guarantees and ceasefire-monitoring ideas, according to the US side. But Kushner was explicit that no settlement can be signed until the territorial dispute is resolved, and Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky restated Moscow’s demand for a Ukrainian withdrawal from the Donbas territory Kyiv still holds. The war is now in its fifth year.

Latest · Americas · US — Hegseth Impeachment Shelved11 November 2026

US House Votes 211–204 to Shelve the Hegseth Impeachment Bid, Leaving It for the New Congress in January

The US House of Representatives has blocked an immediate impeachment vote on Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, voting 211 to 204 on Tuesday to refer the articles to the Republican-led Judiciary Committee, where they are expected to sit without action for the rest of this Congress. The eight articles, introduced by Republican congressman Thomas Massie of Kentucky, accuse Hegseth of waging war on Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorisation and raise further allegations over maritime strikes, the seizure of Venezuela’s leader and retaliation against a senator. Massie was the only Republican to oppose the referral; more than a dozen members, including 11 Democrats, missed the vote. Hegseth, in his first Pentagon briefing in six months, called the effort a farce and sabotage, while President Donald Trump hailed the outcome on Truth Social as the exoneration of a great American patriot. Impeachment proceedings can be carried into the next Congress, so Democrats, who take control of the House in January, could revive the case.

Nvidia Loses About $430 Billion in Two Days as the CEO Spy-Affair Scandal Draws in the Board, the FBI and Congress

Nvidia has shed roughly $430 billion in market value across two trading sessions, with shares down about 9.5 percent since Friday’s close, after chief executive Jensen Huang acknowledged an affair with a businesswoman US intelligence is reported to regard as a Chinese spy. Reports on Tuesday alleged the relationship helped facilitate at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese firms, including restricted models reaching companies such as Alibaba and Tencent via Singapore-based partner Megaspeed; Reuters said it could not independently verify the allegations, and China’s foreign ministry has dismissed them as groundless. Nvidia’s independent directors have hired outside counsel to investigate, the FBI is examining whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation, and the company says its board has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. Three Republican House committee chairs have asked Huang to give a closed-door briefing on 18 November, the day after Nvidia reports quarterly earnings.

Latest · Europe · Portugal — Storm Charles11 November 2026

Storm Charles Shuts All of Portugal’s Schools and Grounds More Than 300 Flights, Then Passes With Less Damage Than Feared

Portugal effectively shut down on Wednesday as Storm Charles came in from the Atlantic: about 8,000 schools cancelled classes nationwide for the first time in living memory, non-essential public services closed, and more than 300 flights were cancelled across four international airports. The weather agency IPMA recorded gusts above 120 km/h, civil protection logged 538 incidents from midnight, and Prime Minister Luís Montenegro chaired a crisis unit with eight ministers. The storm had already hit Madeira on Tuesday with winds of about 130 km/h and flooding around Funchal, but its eye split in two over the Atlantic and it weakened before reaching the mainland through the Algarve, which alone remained on red alert by mid-afternoon. Streets in Lisbon were largely deserted after the government told people to stay home, and the system was forecast to move on into Spain.

Latest · Europe · Italy — School Bomb Hoax11 November 2026

One Hoax Email Reaches 6,300 Italian Schools and Forces Hundreds of Evacuations; Similar Threats Land in Britain and Ireland

Hundreds of Italian schools were evacuated on Wednesday after a single bomb-threat email was sent to about 6,300 institutions, in the latest of a series of disruptions European officials fear may form part of a hybrid-warfare campaign. Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara said at least 300 schools were affected and the number could rise, while Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, who convened ministers and intelligence chiefs, said checks had found no element suggesting a concrete danger. The message, seen by Reuters, was signed in the name of a purported group leader, threatened the prime minister, and claimed a device could be detonated remotely; no device has been found. Similar emails reached more than 300 schools in Northern Ireland, where police said the message originated outside the UK and was treated as a hoax, and schools in Scotland and Ireland also reported alerts. Defence Minister Guido Crosetto has described the wider pattern — which has also hit supermarkets, blood banks, air traffic and broadcasters — as an effort to generate panic, while noting copycats cannot be ruled out.

Poland’s President Leads a Vast Independence Day March in Warsaw Days After the Kraków Shooting Killed Six

Tens of thousands of right-wing supporters marched through Warsaw on Wednesday for Independence Day, in an annual rally held this year under the slogan that Poles are masters of their own land. President Karol Nawrocki walked at the front of a crowd that stretched for more than 3 kilometres; organisers claimed about half a million people, while city authorities put the turnout at about 250,000, and the march passed calmly. The atmosphere was charged by Saturday’s mass shooting in Kraków’s main square, where a gunman killed six people. Prime Minister Donald Tusk’s government initially suggested the attacker may have been motivated by far-right ideology, a suggestion that has sharpened friction between the government and the nationalist opposition ahead of the march, with Tusk urging restraint in the days before it.

Latest · Europe / Americas · UK — US Soldier Arrested10 November 2026

A Senior US Soldier Based at NATO’s Rapid Reaction Headquarters in Gloucestershire Is Held on Suspicion of Spying for Russia

British counter-terrorism police have arrested a 41-year-old senior US Army soldier near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia, in what is believed to be the first case of its kind involving an American serviceman in Britain. The Metropolitan Police said the man was detained on Tuesday under sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023, covering obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service, and confirmed the allegations relate to Russia. The BBC understands he was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, home to NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps. He has not been named or charged and can be held for up to 14 days without charge. Senior coordinator Vicki Evans said the arrest followed close collaboration between UK and US authorities, and FBI Director Kash Patel called the espionage allegations a matter of utmost concern for the United States and its allies. Police said the case is not linked to any other ongoing counter-terrorism investigation.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 and the Carbon Budget11 November 2026

In His Final Climate Summit Speech, Guterres Tells Leaders Fossil Fuels Must Go the Way of the Dinosaurs — as Fossil Emissions Edge Down 0.4%

UN Secretary-General António Guterres used his final climate summit address, at COP31 in Antalya on Wednesday, to attack the fossil-fuel lobby directly and to set hard numbers: emissions down about 6 percent a year, unabated coal cut by at least 75 percent by 2035 and almost phased out by 2040, oil and gas use down by a third, and renewable capacity tripled. He warned that current national plans deliver at best a third of the required pace and point above 2.5°C even if fully implemented. The speech landed a day after the Global Carbon Budget projected fossil carbon dioxide emissions of 38.2 billion tonnes in 2026, down 0.4 percent, with oil down 1.9 percent and gas down 0.3 percent but coal up 0.6 percent; total carbon dioxide including land use rose to 43.3 billion tonnes, leaving under four years of the remaining 1.5°C budget at current rates. Researchers said solar, wind and electric vehicles now meet two-thirds of energy-demand growth, and that the oil dip likely reflects the Iran-conflict price shock. The summit runs to 20 November, with Türkiye hosting and Australia’s Chris Bowen chairing negotiations.

Latest · Americas / Europe · US — Trump v BBC10 November 2026

A Florida Judge Lets Trump’s Defamation Claim Against the BBC Proceed, but Throws Out His Consumer-Law Count

A US federal judge has refused to dismiss President Donald Trump’s defamation lawsuit against the BBC over a Panorama documentary’s edit of his 6 January 2021 speech, allowing the case to move to its next stage. In a 40-page ruling issued on Tuesday, District Judge Jeffrey Kuntz held that Trump had plausibly alleged the edit was misleading, damaged his reputation, and was made with knowledge or reckless disregard, while stressing that whether he can prove the claims and any damages is for another day. The judge dismissed Trump’s second claim, under Florida’s deceptive trade-practices law, holding the documentary was not commercial speech. The suit seeks at least $5 billion on the defamation count; the BBC, which apologised for the edit in November 2025 but denies defamation, said it was surprised by the decision and will continue to fight the case, and must file its answer by 1 December. Trump, who called the ruling a huge victory, faces a court-ordered deadline of 11 December to sit for a deposition.

Latest · Middle East / Americas · Iran — Argentina Designations11 November 2026

Iran Designates Argentina’s Navy and Air Force as Terrorist Organisations in a Tit-for-Tat Over the Revolutionary Guards

Iran has designated Argentina’s navy and air force as terrorist organisations, its foreign ministry announced on Wednesday, describing the move as a reciprocal response under a 2019 Iranian law to Buenos Aires’ decision earlier this year to blacklist the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Argentina designated the IRGC in late March, citing its backing for Lebanon’s Hezbollah, following a push by the Trump administration for allies to do the same. Tehran’s statement named only the navy and air force, made no mention of Argentina’s army, and warned that responsibility for all consequences rests with the Argentine government. The designation is a legal and political countermeasure rather than a claim that Argentine forces attacked Iran, but it deepens a long-running dispute rooted in Argentina’s accusations over the 1994 bombing of a Jewish community centre in Buenos Aires.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Strikes Continue11 November 2026

Israeli Strikes Kill Three in Gaza City, Including Two Brothers in a Tent for Displaced Families

Israeli airstrikes killed three Palestinians in Gaza City on Wednesday, local health officials said. Medics reported that one man was killed when a strike hit the rooftop of a residential building, and that a second strike in the nearby Sheikh Radwan neighbourhood killed two brothers, Mahmoud and Jamil Al-Absi, in a tent housing displaced people. The Israeli military said it had struck militants in both areas but gave no further details. The deaths add to a toll that Gaza health officials put at more than 1,600 Palestinians killed since last year’s US-brokered ceasefire agreement, while Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed in the same period. The ceasefire was meant to lead toward reconstruction of the Strip, but little progress has been made, and the strikes underline how fragile the truce remains a year on.
