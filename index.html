Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 in Antalya14 November 2026

Britain, Norway and Canada Launch a $10 Billion Climate Fund for Emerging Markets at COP31

Three state-backed lenders used the Antalya summit to pool their firepower. British International Investment, Norway’s Norfund and FinDev Canada said on Friday they will work together to direct $10 billion into climate projects in emerging markets by 2030, sharing deal pipelines, coordinating due diligence and aligning how they report climate results under a new memorandum of understanding. The announcement was one of the clearest finance pledges of COP31’s first week, arriving as poorer countries press rich governments to turn earlier promises into money that actually moves. The hosts, Turkey, and Australia, which is leading the negotiations, have both stressed delivery of existing commitments over headline new targets.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — Fossil Fuels and Finance Gap14 November 2026

COP31 Ends Its First Week Split Over Fossil Fuels, as Island States Warn Climate Finance Is Falling Far Short

The talks in Antalya closed their first week with familiar lines hardening. Large producers and consumers resisted any text that would set firm new targets or name a shift away from oil, gas and coal, while European governments backed a Turkish proposal to expand electrification — cleaner cars, heating and equipment — as the centre of a deal. The United States skipped the summit for a second straight year. Small island leaders made the sharpest appeals: the Marshall Islands said defence budgets keep rising while the threat they call existential stays underfunded, and the Solomon Islands pledged $400,000 of its own money to the Green Climate Fund, a first for a small island state. Analysis cited at the summit put money mobilised in 2024 at $137 billion, with current trends leaving provision about $840 billion short of the $1.3 trillion goal, against a pledge to reach $300 billion a year by 2035. Australia is taking further submissions until Saturday night and aims to draft a political text early next week.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Suspected Drones Over RAF Lakenheath13 November 2026

US Air Force Responds to Suspected Unauthorised Drone Activity Over RAF Lakenheath in England

American personnel at RAF Lakenheath responded on Friday evening to what a US Air Force spokesperson described as suspected unauthorised drone activity over the base in eastern England. No personnel, aircraft or infrastructure were affected, the spokesperson said, and Britain’s Ministry of Defence was not immediately available for comment. The incident comes in a sensitive period for US bases in Britain: in recent weeks Washington has moved bombers that had been used to strike Iran out of the country and shifted large refuelling aircraft from Britain to Germany. The bombers had left RAF Fairford on security grounds after men approached that base in white vans at night, an episode that led to charges last month, with prosecutors alleging one man tried to assist Iranian intelligence. The Wall Street Journal has reported the refuelling move was linked to a threat from Iran.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Drone Evacuations Scale Up14 November 2026

Ukraine’s Giant Rescue Drones Go From a Seven-Day Gamble to a 12-Hour Routine in the Kill Zone

A Ukrainian unit that pioneered airlifting wounded soldiers by drone is now trying to make the tactic everyday practice. The 475th Separate Assault Regiment’s pilot known as KVN first guided a large unmanned hexacopter to a trapped comrade after seven days of preparation; two weeks later a similar rescue was assembled in 12 hours. The need is stark: inside the front-line kill zone, where strike drones watch for any movement, wounded fighters can be stranded for days, weeks or even months. In one mission the Ukrainian-built StarGate drone flew about 60 kilometres in total, half of it carrying a person, according to Kyiv’s defence ministry. Its maker, SkyFall, says the aircraft can hover and land autonomously even under electronic jamming, calls it Ukraine’s first purpose-built evacuation drone, and expects to build around 1,000 a month by the end of 2026 as other brigades ask for it.

Latest · Europe · Germany — Military Drones Fly Again13 November 2026

German Military Drones Grounded After a Cyberattack Are Flying Again, Maker Quantum Systems Says

Defence technology group Quantum Systems said on Friday that drones it supplies to the German military are back in the air after a precautionary grounding that followed a cyberattack last month. The company said the fleet is operating normally worldwide, including with the Bundeswehr, which it described as the only customer to have grounded the systems. The Financial Times had reported the grounding on Thursday and said it kept Quantum out of a recent exercise with the military. The German military offered no immediate comment. Quantum said an active attack by hostile actors was detected in October and contained, and noted it signed a deal last month to supply Ukraine with jet interceptors — a reminder of how closely European drone makers are now tied to the war.

Latest · Middle East · UAE — Fujairah Pile-Up Investigation14 November 2026

UAE Prosecutors Investigate Two Fujairah Crashes That Killed 14, as a Lorry Driver Is Held in Hospital Custody

The inquiry into one of the Emirates’ deadliest road disasters widened on Friday. Prosecutors are examining two crashes on Sheikh Khalifa Road in Fujairah on 12 November that together left 14 people dead and at least 30 injured in a pile-up involving 23 vehicles — 18 cars and five lorries — near a lane closed for maintenance, where a lorry overturned after hitting several vehicles and caught fire. Attorney General Dr Hamad Saif Al Shamsi ordered a team to inspect the site, gather tracking data and carry out brake inspections to establish the technical causes. The lorry driver in the second crash was questioned, is being held in hospital custody, and has had his passport and driving licence confiscated while the investigation continues. Fifteen of the injured have been discharged and 15 remain in treatment; the dead and injured include Emirati nationals and citizens of India, Oman, Brazil, Pakistan, Jordan and Egypt, with DNA testing being used to identify some victims. Among the Indian dead were 21-year-old twins from Kerala and their three-year-old nephew.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — UN Sanctions Renewed13 November 2026

Pakistan Demands an Immediate End to Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia as the Security Council Renews Yemen Sanctions

Pakistan used the Security Council’s Yemen vote to issue one of its strongest condemnations yet. Ambassador Syed Haider Shah said Islamabad condemns in the strongest terms the continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, demanded their immediate and unconditional end, and reaffirmed full solidarity with the kingdom’s leadership and people, along with support for Yemen’s sovereignty and unity and for UN-led peace efforts. The council adopted the resolution by consensus, extending asset freezes and travel bans on individuals threatening peace in Yemen until 14 November 2027 and renewing its panel of experts until 15 December 2027. The text also seeks to restrict Houthi access to dual-use components and precursor chemicals used to build drones and missiles. The vote followed Houthi claims of strikes on a pumping station on Saudi Arabia’s East–West pipeline, an oil facility at Yanbu and an Aramco building in Riyadh, claims Saudi defences have been intercepting missiles and drones against in recent weeks.

Latest · Americas · United States — Trump Approval at 32%14 November 2026

Trump Insists His Popularity Feels Higher as Polls Hold His Approval at 32% After the Midterm Losses

The president and the polls are telling different stories. In interviews this week Donald Trump suggested his popularity may be higher now than before the midterms, in which Republicans lost control of both chambers of Congress on 3 November. The numbers point the other way: a Reuters/Ipsos survey of 1,705 adults conducted 6–9 November put approval of his job performance at 32%, unchanged from two previous polls and just above the 31% career low recorded in mid-October, with disapproval at 65% in one reading. The weakness is concentrated on household costs — only 21% approved of his handling of the cost of living and 25% of the economy, compared with 38% on immigration. A separate Economist/YouGov post-election survey also showed approval in the low-to-mid 30s. On election day itself, nine in ten voters told pollsters the cost of living was an important factor in how they voted.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Exports Surge and Bengaluru Metro14 November 2026

India’s October Exports Jump 29.9% to $89.99 Billion as Bengaluru’s Pink Line Opens to Passengers

Two milestones landed together for India on Friday and Saturday. Government data released on Friday showed exports rose 29.9% in October to $89.99 billion, while the trade deficit narrowed to $20.09 billion. In Bengaluru, Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the elevated Pink Line of Namma Metro, built for about Rs 3,470 crore, and travelled on the new section from Tavarekere toward IIMB-Bilekahalli. The 7.5-kilometre stretch from Kalena Agrahara to Tavarekere, with six stations, opens for public service on 14 November and takes the city’s operational network to 103.6 kilometres — past the 100-kilometre mark and second only to Delhi in India. The metro operator plans five trains at first, four in service and one on standby, running about every eight minutes at peak times; the line’s 13.76-kilometre underground section toward Nagawara is due next year, with a state target of 170 kilometres by 2027. Modi also said steps taken over the past decade had helped India weather the energy crisis that followed the Middle East war.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Ola Fined Over Pre-Ride Tips13 November 2026

India’s Consumer Watchdog Fines Ola Rs 10 Lakh and Orders It to Scrap Pre-Ride Tip Prompts as a Dark Pattern

Paying extra before a cab is even confirmed does not get you a faster ride — and India’s regulator has now said suggesting otherwise is unlawful. The Central Consumer Protection Authority fined ANI Technologies, which runs Ola, Rs 10 lakh, the maximum for a first offence, and ordered it to immediately stop all advance tip prompts, pre-set amounts and messages that nudge riders to pay above the displayed fare before a journey is completed. In its 10 November order the authority called the feature a misleading advertisement, an unfair trade practice and a prohibited “confirm shaming” dark pattern. Ola’s own figures undercut the pitch: across Delhi, Bengaluru and Mumbai in early June, requests carrying an add-on were accepted far less often than those without one — about 32–38% against 62–68% in the Delhi data cited — while customers paid roughly Rs 56 lakh in such add-ons in a fortnight. Ola has 15 days to pay and file a compliance report; a similar Rs 10 lakh penalty was imposed on rival Rapido in September.

Hong Kong Developer New World Weighs a Rights Issue of Up to $3.2 Billion as Its Shares Are Halted

One of Hong Kong’s best-known property developers is preparing a large fundraising.
