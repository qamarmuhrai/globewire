Latest · Middle East · Yemen21 October 2026

Death Toll From Houthi Missile Strike on Yemen Training Camp Rises to 73 Recruits

The death toll from Monday's Houthi ballistic-missile strike on the al-Alam training camp in Yemen's southern Shabwa province has risen to 73, Governor Awad Ibn al-Wazir said, with more than 200 recruits wounded and some still missing. The missiles hit as the recruits — all aged between 18 and 20 and being trained for provincial security duties rather than combat — lined up in formation. It was the deadliest single attack since fighting escalated about three months ago, after government and Saudi-led coalition air strikes on Sanaa's airport in July shattered the 2022 truce. Yemen's information minister condemned the strike as a war crime, while the Houthis claimed the camp was run by Saudi officers. In a separate attack, Houthi fire on the besieged city of Taiz killed two teenage girls, and the UN has warned the city is at grave risk.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia20 October 2026

Britain to Fly RAF Typhoon Missions Defending Saudi Arabia Against Houthi Attacks

Britain will deploy Royal Air Force Typhoon fighter jets on defensive missions to help protect Saudi Arabia from escalating Houthi attacks, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, citing British officials familiar with the matter. The jets would defend Saudi cities and infrastructure against aerial attacks by the Iran-backed group, and the package also includes an Atlas A400M transport aircraft and military advisers. Britain's Ministry of Defence did not immediately respond to a request for comment. In September, London said it would provide additional defensive military assistance to Riyadh after renewed Houthi attacks, including a Voyager air-to-air refuelling aircraft — but that announcement did not include fighter jets. The move follows weeks of intensifying Houthi missile and drone strikes on the kingdom.

Source: Reuters / Bloomberg
Latest · Europe · Ukraine21 October 2026

Zelenskyy and US Hail 'Productive' Oslo Talks as Witkoff Heads to Meet Putin

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and the United States described talks in Oslo on Tuesday as "very productive," as Kyiv and Washington tried to draw a line under a bitter feud over the war with Russia. Zelenskyy thanked Washington for resuming US intelligence warnings of looming Russian strikes on civilian targets and energy plants, saying the early warning was vital for protecting civilians. US envoy Steve Witkoff is now due in Russia on Wednesday for talks with President Vladimir Putin, with Jared Kushner — who joined the Oslo discussions remotely — expected to take part. A US official said Ukraine brought new proposals and that the sides made meaningful progress toward a coordinated approach to engaging Moscow. President Trump, however, kept up the pressure from the White House, saying he wanted Zelenskyy to "make a deal" as Russia escalates strikes ahead of winter.

Rubio Warns a Peace Deal With Tehran Is 'Extremely Difficult' as Hormuz Attacks Continue

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio has told American media that reaching a peace deal with Tehran is "extremely difficult" while attacks around the Strait of Hormuz continue, Al Jazeera reported in its live coverage on Wednesday. The warning came as the fallout from the US–Israeli war on Iran spread across the region, with fighting intensifying in Yemen and Gulf states condemning Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. In Britain, police rearrested two men over a suspected plot against an RAF base used by US forces — a plot the prime minister said was "almost certainly" directed by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Tehran, for its part, has warned that any new US attack would bring destruction across the Middle East, and its foreign minister said neither Saudi Arabia nor the Houthis had answered an Iranian mediation offer.

Latest · Africa · Sudan21 October 2026

Drone Strike Near Abyei Market Kills at Least 150, Al Jazeera Reports

A drone strike near a market in Abyei, the disputed territory on the border between Sudan and South Sudan, has killed at least 150 people, Al Jazeera reported on Wednesday. The attack came as fighting between Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces intensified in recent days. Khartoum accuses the RSF of hiding in the disputed region near South Sudan. Abyei's status has been contested since South Sudan's independence, and the area — administered under a special arrangement while its final status remains unresolved — has repeatedly been caught up in violence linked to Sudan's civil war, which has displaced millions and deepened one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Source: Al Jazeera
Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea21 October 2026

North Korean Websites Hit by Intermittent Outages, Analysts Say

More than 40 official North Korean websites using the country's .kp domain — including those of the foreign ministry and the state news agency KCNA — were inaccessible or unstable on Tuesday, South Korea's Yonhap News Agency reported. Network monitoring service Telecom Observer detected nearly six hours of disruption in North Korea's Border Gateway Protocol routing infrastructure, between 1810 and 2350 GMT on Tuesday. Analysts observed further intermittent outages on Wednesday, although many sites, including KCNA and the Rodong Sinmun newspaper, were reachable again by 0043 GMT. The cause has not been publicly attributed. North Korea's tiny, tightly controlled internet presence has suffered disruptions before, including incidents that analysts linked to external attacks and internal failures.

Source: Reuters
Latest · Asia-Pacific · South China Sea21 October 2026

China Says It Tracked a US Navy Ship Sailing Near Disputed Sabina Shoal

China's military said on Tuesday it dispatched forces to "track and monitor" a US Navy vessel that sailed through disputed waters in the South China Sea. The People's Liberation Army said the USS Charleston, a littoral combat ship, had "illegally intruded" into waters adjacent to Xianbin Jiao — China's name for Sabina Shoal in the Spratly Islands — and accused the US Navy of infringing China's sovereignty. The Philippines rejected Beijing's account. "No single nation has the authority to require prior permission for lawful transit through international waters," Rear Admiral Roy Vincent Trinidad told reporters, warning China to stop "coercive and aggressive actions." China, Vietnam, the Philippines and Taiwan all claim parts of the Spratlys, and US forces regularly operate in the contested waters. US Pacific Command did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Source: Philstar / AFP

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi marked one year in office on Wednesday, facing difficult parliamentary battles over key policies and diplomatic and security challenges while public support for her Cabinet shows signs of weakening, Kyodo News reported. Japan also logged a trade deficit of 536.5 billion yen ($3.4 billion) in September, government data showed Wednesday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia21 October 2026

Australia's NextDC Shares Plunge 44% After Power Delays Force Revenue Downgrade

Shares in Australian data centre operator NextDC shed nearly half their value on Wednesday after the company sharply cut its revenue forecast and put future developments on hold, extending a burst of negative sentiment toward global AI stocks. NextDC said delays to electricity network upgrades by Jemena Electricity Networks at its M3 Melbourne site would defer about A$140 million ($98 million) in contracted customer revenue this financial year, with the next supply increase now not expected until 2028. The stock fell 44%, wiping about A$3.1 billion from its market value and making it the biggest loser on the ASX 200. The company now expects fiscal 2027 net revenue of A$475 million to A$500 million, down from A$615 million to A$640 million, and said it would shelve new development projects and land acquisitions while evaluating sales of selected sites. The rout follows a withdrawn data centre IPO in Australia last week, as investors question whether massive AI spending can justify soaring computing, land and power costs.

Indian Markets Poised to Rise as Jio Platforms Launches India's Record $3.2 Billion IPO

Indian shares were set to open higher on Wednesday as attention shifted to the launch of Jio Platforms' record initial public offering, with optimism over a possible US–India trade deal also supporting sentiment. GIFT Nifty futures pointed to a positive start after the Nifty 50 closed at 22,888.95 on Monday; markets were shut Tuesday for a local holiday. Gains could be capped, however, by crude oil — Brent futures rose more than 1% to $110 a barrel amid Red Sea shipping disruption fears, a negative for energy-importing India.
