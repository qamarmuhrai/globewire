Nasdaq Falls 5.5% Into Correction: The Midterm Rally Dies in a Day

Thursday’s closing bell confirmed the rout that had been building all session. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52 per cent, or 1,421 points, to 24,315 — more than 10 per cent below its October record and into technical correction — while the Nasdaq 100 dropped 6.36 per cent to 26,629.81, now 14.7 per cent under its 6 October record close. The S&P 500 lost 4.36 per cent to 7,353.73 and the Dow Jones fell 1,833 points, or 3.44 per cent, to 51,523.06, more than wiping out Wednesday’s 1,569-point midterm celebration. All three indices posted their worst day since April 2025. The fear gauge told the same story: the VIX jumped about 69 per cent to 28.09. Chip and data-centre names, the engine of the bull market, led the fall — SanDisk down 22.3 per cent, AMD 17.3 per cent, Nvidia 9.3 per cent, Broadcom more than 10 per cent and Qualcomm 9.5 per cent despite beating earnings — as investors priced in tighter Democratic oversight of the AI build-out. Tesla fell 10.8 per cent. Attention now turns to Friday’s October US jobs report.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Decision on Sunday6 November 2026

Herzog to Name His Pick on Sunday — Eisenkot Holds 67, Netanyahu 46

Israel’s week of presidential consultations closed on Thursday with the arithmetic unchanged and the decision deferred to the weekend. President Isaac Herzog finished his round of meetings with the incoming Knesset factions and announced he will task a lawmaker with forming the next government on Sunday, saying he intends to leave no stone unturned in the search for a stable government and will hold private talks with party leaders in the meantime. Gadi Eisenkot of Yashar holds the backing of 67 of the 120 lawmakers — his own party plus Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Democrats, Ra’am and the Joint List, after both Arab-majority parties endorsed him on Thursday — against 46 for caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from Likud, Shas, Religious Zionism, Zehut and Otzma Yehudit. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism recommended no one, leaving seven seats uncommitted. A recommendation is not a coalition promise, and Eisenkot’s hardest work — turning endorsements into 61 voting partners — starts only once the mandate lands; he would then have 28 days, extendable by 14. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November, and Eisenkot’s bloc is also preparing an early vote to replace Speaker Amir Ohana, who said he would schedule one within a week if 61 lawmakers formally ask.

Latest · Europe · Aviation — 25 Flights Hit6 November 2026

Bomb Hoaxes Sweep Europe’s Skies: Fighters Escort Airliners, No Device Found

A second wave of bomb threats in one week disrupted European aviation on Thursday, affecting at least 25 flights from Ireland to Bulgaria — and every alert checked so far has proved false. The most dramatic diversion was Aer Lingus flight EI402 from Dublin to Rome: warned by Italian authorities while over France, the Airbus A320 was escorted by a French military aircraft to the Évreux air base in Normandy, landing safely with 151 passengers and six crew, who were checked in groups while bomb-disposal teams searched the jet. Italian Eurofighters escorted a Finnair flight from Helsinki into Rome, Bulgarian officials reported three emergency landings involving Ryanair services, and a Wizz Air flight from Milan to Warsaw also changed course. Britain’s Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander called the incidents “malicious hoax threats”. Two of the Rome-bound flights reportedly received emails in the name of a self-styled anti-feminist group threatening to “turn the skies red”. EU Transport Commissioner Apostolos Tzitzikostas said it was too early to say whether the incidents — which followed a similar wave against about ten flights on Tuesday — are linked, and national investigations are under way.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Brussels Talks, Night of Drones6 November 2026

Reconstruction Talks in Brussels While 600 Drones Cross the Night Sky

Diplomacy and bombardment ran in parallel on Thursday. At NATO headquarters in Brussels, Ukrainian negotiator Rustem Umerov led talks with Western allies on postwar reconstruction, funding for 2027 and the security guarantees Kyiv wants once fighting ends — sessions set to continue for a second day, ahead of the US-mediated meeting with Russia in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November. Overnight, the air war intensified on both sides. Ukraine said its defences downed or suppressed 105 of 128 Russian attack drones, with strikes wounding four people in the Dnipropetrovsk and Kyiv regions and fires in the Bucha district outside the capital; Kharkiv’s governor counted more than 80 drones against the city and 17 settlements in a day, injuring six people including a child. Russia said it destroyed 496 Ukrainian drones over 13 regions, Crimea and the Black and Azov seas, and that a Ukrainian strike killed five people and wounded 13 in the occupied Luhansk region. Moscow also said its drones hit a Nova Poshta postal terminal outside Kharkiv, a power substation and a cargo ship, while Ukrainian drones set fires in an industrial zone at Ryazan, southeast of Moscow. In Komyshuvakha, the confirmed toll from Monday’s bombing of the aid-distribution school stands at 43, with the search of the rubble continuing.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 63% Counted, 69 Councils Hung6 November 2026

The Coalition Auction Opens: ANC Heads for Its Worst Result Since 1994

With about 63 per cent of voting districts counted by Thursday night, South Africa’s local elections had produced a map no single party can govern alone. Nationally the ANC stood at about 31.7 per cent — down from 45.6 per cent in 2021 and on course for its lowest share in any election since 1994 — with the DA near 25.3 per cent, the MK Party on about 8.5 per cent and the EFF near 6 per cent. Of the first 100 councils decided, 69 were hung: the ANC had won 21 outright, the DA six and the MK Party three, with 113 still to be calculated — a starting gun for days of bargaining in which small parties hold the keys to dozens of mayoralties. The metros underlined the shift. The DA led Johannesburg on about 40.6 per cent with the ANC and ActionSA scrapping for second, led Tshwane, and appeared set to be the only metro party to win an outright majority, in Cape Town. In KwaZulu-Natal the MK Party led provincially on about 33.9 per cent, ahead of the IFP and the ANC. More than 16.5 million valid votes had been counted across all ballots. Official results are due on Sunday, 8 November; councils then have 14 days to constitute and elect mayors.

Latest · Americas · United States — Divided Government6 November 2026

“We Don’t Work for Donald Trump”: Democrats Vow Oversight, Trump Reaches for the Veto

Washington began adjusting to divided government on Thursday, with the scale of Tuesday’s Democratic sweep still growing. Democrats hold at least a 54–44 Senate majority after flipping seats in deeply Republican states, and two uncalled races — Alaska and Mississippi — could yet lift them to 56; in the House they gained at least 30 seats. Hakeem Jeffries, on track to become the first Black Speaker, said the new majority would focus on affordability and accountability: “We don’t work for Donald Trump. We work for the American people.” The results were historic in places Republicans long owned: Texas elected its first Democratic senator since 1988 and ousted three-term Governor Greg Abbott, Kansas chose its first Democratic senator since the Great Depression, and Michigan’s Abdul El-Sayed becomes the Senate’s first Muslim member. President Trump, who accepted the outcome, spent his first post-election appearance showing reporters his under-construction White House ballroom — “For me, it’s relaxing” — while posting an image of his red campaign cap rebranded “VETO” and blaming candidates who were not himself. Senate Republican leader John Thune promised his caucus would not stand idly by. With investigations, nominee blockades and veto threats ahead, two years of deadlock look likely before the new Congress is even seated in January.

Latest · Americas · Vatican — The Tour Begins Today6 November 2026

Montevideo Rolls Out the Popemobile: Pope Leo Opens His Latin America Tour

Pope Leo arrives in Montevideo on Friday to begin the first Latin American journey of his pontificate, a 31,000-kilometre, 11-day tour undertaken barely three weeks after lung surgery in which doctors removed a lobe of his right lung and then cleared the 71-year-old to travel. The opening day is choreographed for a proudly secular host: a meeting with President Yamandú Orsi at the Estévez Palace, an address to authorities and diplomats at Plaza Independencia, the keys of the city at the Cabildo, and evening prayer with bishops and clergy at the Metropolitan Cathedral. On Saturday he celebrates Mass in Paysandú honouring Blessed Jacinto Vera, visits the poor neighbourhood of Casavalle, addresses the Catholic University and leads a youth vigil expected to draw some 60,000 people to the Estadio Centenario; on Sunday he says Mass in Florida at the shrine of Our Lady of the Thirty-Three, Uruguay’s patroness. The full programme — 36 speeches, 14 flights including two helicopter trips, nine public Masses — then moves to Argentina for three days, the first papal visit there in 39 years, and on to Peru, where Leo served as a missionary from 1985, later became a bishop and holds citizenship. Even his transport is local: the popemobile was built by Uruguayan manufacturer Nordex and handed to the nunciature this week.

156 Goals and Counting: Harry Kane Signs Bayern’s Biggest-Ever Deal to 2029

Harry Kane has ended any doubt about where his prime years will be spent. The England captain, who won the Ballon d’Or last week — the first Bundesliga player in 30 years, and the first Englishman in 25, to take the award — signed a two-year extension with Bayern Munich on Thursday, keeping him at the club until 30 June 2029. Since arriving from Tottenham Hotspur in 2023, the 33-year-old has scored 156 goals in 161 appearances, finished as the Bundesliga’s top scorer in every season, been named Germany’s Footballer of the Year for 2026 and collected two league titles, the German Cup and two Super Cups. Bayern did not disclose terms, but German media reported the most lucrative contract in the club’s history; one British report put it above £440,000 a week. “I feel right at home at FC Bayern,” Kane said, adding that the decision had effectively been made long ago and that a Champions League title before 2029 is the target he has set himself. Chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen called the extension “a commitment to FC Bayern and to a story that is far from over”. Kane will be 35 when the deal expires — and has 10 goals and eight assists already this season.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pakistan — $3 Billion Rolled Over6 November 2026

Saudi Arabia Extends $3 Billion Lifeline as Pakistan Hunts $7.3 Billion in Rollovers

Pakistan’s external-financing jigsaw gained a key piece this week. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb confirmed that Saudi Arabia has extended, for what he called a “reasonable period”, the $3 billion financing facility Riyadh provided in April — money that helped Islamabad repay $3.5 billion to the United Arab Emirates, was initially granted for three months and matured on 31 October. “You don’t need to worry as to for how long, given the kind of strategic relationship we have,” Aurangzeb told reporters at Parliament House. A separate Saudi $5 billion facility has already been extended to 2028. Officials say China and Kuwait have also given assurances covering a further $4.3 billion of rollovers — Beijing’s $4 billion deposit falls due in March 2027 — taking identified bilateral rollovers to $7.3 billion. The relief comes as a proposed $10 billion US liquidity-support facility, requested in July, remains uncertain following Aurangzeb’s Washington talks with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Ahead lie roughly $21.5 billion of gross external debt-service obligations in the next fiscal year; Islamabad aims to cut annual servicing to $3.5 billion, lift reserves from just over three months of imports toward four, and faces an IMF board review of its $7 billion programme — with a $1.2 billion disbursement at stake — in December.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — First Rise Since 20236 November 2026

Bank of England Raises Rates to 4% — and the Only Dissent Wanted More

The Bank of England tightened policy for the first time in more than three years on Thursday, lifting Bank Rate from 3.75 to 4.00 per cent by an 8–1 vote and warning that at least one more increase is likely if the US–Iran conflict continues. The single dissent underlined the mood: Catherine Mann voted not to hold, but to raise by half a point to 4.25 per cent, arguing the Bank had been slow to convince markets it had inflation in hand. The Bank now expects inflation — 3.5 per cent in the year to September — to peak at an average of about 4.8 per cent in the second quarter of next year, more than double its 2 per cent target, as Middle East energy costs pass into bills, before easing. It also revised growth up to 1.6 per cent this year and 1.3 per cent next, stronger than the government’s fiscal watchdog forecast last week. Governor Andrew Bailey said the move should not be read as the start of an inevitable long sequence, but that another rise may follow unless the conflict ebbs — bringing the Bank onto the path the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank have already taken this year. The decision is a blow for Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s cost-of-living pledges. The next rate decision falls on 17 December.

“No Obstacle Brotherhood Cannot Overcome”: Erdogan Heads to Baku on Sunday

President Recep Tayyip Erdogan moves this weekend to repair a rare public strain between Türkiye and Azerbaijan, travelling to Baku on Sunday, 8 November, for Victory Day. Speaking to reporters on his return flight from South Korea on Thursday, Erdogan said there is no obstacle the two countries’ brotherhood cannot overcome, that he has already spoken by phone with President Ilham Aliyev, and that both his intelligence chief and his foreign minister have been sent to Azerbaijan in recent days. The friction followed Baku’s September change to school uniform rules, restricting Islamic headscarves as incompatible with secular state education; criticism of the move by Turkish figures — including Erdogan adviser Oktay Saral, whom Erdogan dismissed on 25 October — drew accusations in Baku of meddling in domestic affairs, and Aliyev then skipped a Turkic states summit in Ankara last week despite a personal invitation. Erdogan framed the relationship as going far beyond ordinary diplomacy, rooted in shared history and culture, and said he hoped the visit would provide the opportunity to take the necessary steps, without elaborating. The alliance between Ankara and Baku sits at the centre of a fractured region, which is why even a dispute over school dress has drawn high-level shuttle diplomacy.
