Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — The Decision Is In5 November 2026

First Rise Since 2023: Bank of England Lifts Rates to 4% and Warns of 5% Inflation

The Bank of England has raised interest rates for the first time in more than three years, and signalled that at least one more increase is likely if the US–Iran conflict continues. The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8–1 on Thursday to lift Bank Rate by a quarter point, from 3.75 to 4.00 per cent — the first rise since August 2023, after six consecutive holds. The lone dissent was not for a hold but for a bigger move: Catherine Mann, who has argued the Bank was slow to show it had a grip on inflation, voted for a half-point jump to 4.25 per cent. In its new forecasts the Bank expects consumer inflation — already 3.5 per cent in the year to September, up from 3.1 per cent in August — to climb to around 5 per cent by the middle of next year as Middle East-driven energy costs feed through household and business bills, before easing back. Growth forecasts were nudged up to 1.6 per cent for this year and 1.3 per cent for next. Governor Andrew Bailey said the rise should not be read as the start of an inevitable long sequence, but that another increase may follow if the conflict remains unresolved. Sterling edged higher to about $1.3477 after the decision and gilt yields rose, with two-year yields up around two basis points to 4.76 per cent. The next decision falls on 17 December.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — The Sentence5 November 2026

Ten More Years: Joshua Wong Sentenced in His Second Security Case

The sentence is in. Hong Kong’s High Court on Thursday jailed Joshua Wong for ten years for conspiring to collude with foreign forces under the Beijing-imposed national security law — his second conviction under that law. Judge William Tam ruled the offence was of a “grave nature”, described Wong and exiled activist Nathan Law as ring-leaders of the conspiracy, and set a starting point of 13 years before cutting it to ten in recognition of Wong’s guilty plea in September. The new term runs concurrently with the roughly two months left on his existing four-year, eight-month sentence for subversion in the “Hong Kong 47” case, which means the 30-year-old — in custody almost continuously since November 2020 and previously expecting release in January — is now not due out until late 2036, when he will be 40. Prosecutors said that between July and November 2020 Wong and others asked foreign governments and bodies to impose sanctions or blockades on Hong Kong and China. Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International both condemned the sentence; Wong’s mitigation letter said the fresh charge, brought in June 2025, made him feel he was back at the beginning. He first rose to prominence as a teenage founder of the Scholarism student movement and a leader of the 2014 Umbrella Movement protests.

Latest · Americas · Markets — The Dow’s Best Day of 20265 November 2026

Up 1,569 Points: Wall Street Roars as the Midterms End and Oil Falls

Markets gave their verdict on the midterms on Wednesday, and it was emphatic. The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 1,569.40 points, or about 3 per cent, to close at 53,356.12 — its strongest session since April 2025 and, according to CFRA Research, its biggest post-midterm gain since 1982. The S&P 500 rose 1.6 per cent to 7,688.62, its best day in three months, and the Nasdaq Composite added 1.4 per cent to 25,736.51. Almost every sector climbed: consumer discretionary jumped about 4.3 per cent while energy fell about 4.8 per cent, tracking crude’s plunge below $95. Traders read the Democratic sweep of both chambers two ways at once — divided government limits how much of any agenda can pass, which markets historically like, and a Congress hostile to the Iran war raises the odds of a negotiated exit that would normalise oil supply. The rally had casualties: data-centre and crypto-mining names such as IREN and Hut 8 tumbled on fears of tighter AI rules, Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX both fell more than 3 per cent despite his heavy political spending, and Qualcomm dropped in extended trade on disappointing results. Ten-year Treasury yields eased to about 4.88 per cent.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — A Fourth Day Down5 November 2026

Brent at $93.82: Oil Falls Again, but Analysts See $110–$125 if War Resumes

Oil extended its slide on Thursday, falling for a fourth straight session as traders continued to price a lower chance of fresh escalation in the US-Israeli war on Iran after the Republican midterm defeat. Brent futures were down 80 cents, or 0.85 per cent, at $93.82 a barrel by 0426 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate fell $1.20, or 1.36 per cent, to $86.92 — a day after both benchmarks plunged more than 8 per cent to their lowest settlements since 31 August. The political logic is straightforward: President Trump has acknowledged the war and the fuel prices it caused hurt his party, Vice President JD Vance has said the same publicly, and Washington says it has no immediate plans for new strikes. But the pricing is fragile. Eurasia Group cut its odds of renewed US strikes only modestly, from 65 to 60 per cent, and actually lowered the chance of a deal from 20 to 10 per cent, warning that if escalation does come, Brent could snap back into a $110–$125 band. JPMorgan estimates the market was already in surplus by about 1.1 million barrels a day in September and 1.4 million in October, while Vortexa tracking shows Gulf exports averaged 17.1 million barrels a day in October — still only about three-quarters of pre-war levels.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — A Loyal Ally Walks Away5 November 2026

United Torah Judaism Refuses to Back Netanyahu — Eisenkot Leads 56 to 46

A party that stood behind Benjamin Netanyahu after the previous five elections broke with him on Thursday. United Torah Judaism, the seven-seat ultra-Orthodox party, told President Isaac Herzog it would recommend nobody to form Israel’s next government, after the spiritual leaders of both its factions — Lithuanian Degel HaTorah and Hasidic Agudat Yisrael — instructed their lawmakers to withhold an endorsement, with backing for any future administration explicitly conditional on laws protecting Haredi draft exemptions. The abstention reshapes the arithmetic of Herzog’s second day of consultations: Gadi Eisenkot’s expected endorsements stand at 56, after Ra’am chairman Mansour Abbas confirmed his party’s eight lawmakers for the Yashar leader in Thursday’s meeting — Abbas also said Ra’am backs Donald Trump’s Gaza peace plan and the full disarmament of Hamas, and criticised Netanyahu’s coalition over the hostages. Netanyahu’s tally sits at 46, built from the Religious Zionist Party, Shas, Zehut and Otzma Yehudit, with Likud itself closing the day’s rounds at 8:30 p.m. The Democrats recommended Eisenkot in the morning; Yashar and B’Yachad were still to be heard, and the Joint List had not declared. Neither bloc holds a majority on its own — Netanyahu’s won 53 seats in the 27 October election, Eisenkot’s 48 — and the president is expected to hand the mandate, worth 28 days plus a possible 14-day extension, to whoever leads the recommendations. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 11 a.m. at the White House5 November 2026

Enemies at One Table: Melania Trump Hosts Kyiv and Moscow Envoys Today

At 11 a.m. Washington time on Thursday — 9 p.m. in Pakistan — First Lady Melania Trump is due to stand in the White House with officials from two countries actively at war with each other. The gathering caps the Oleshky operation: a six-day localised ceasefire around the Russian-occupied Kherson-region town, which she helped broker, during which aid convoys got in and roughly 750 to 800 people were evacuated. Ukraine is represented by parliamentary faction leader David Arakhamia and human-rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets; Russia, according to TASS citing Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov, plans to send envoy Kirill Dmitriev and human-rights official Yana Lantratova, provided logistics allow. On Wednesday President Volodymyr Zelenskyy awarded the First Lady the Order of Princess Olga, First Class — Ukraine’s highest state honour for women — and Kyiv’s stated hope is that Oleshky becomes a template for evacuating civilians from other occupied areas. Peskov, for his part, praised her humanitarian work and said Moscow hoped it would continue. The White House meeting is explicitly a prelude: full US-mediated trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and Washington follow in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the first such round since February.

Latest · Americas · United States — The Morning After5 November 2026

Johnson Steps Aside, Democrats Vow to Rein In Trump — and Johnson’s Party Picks a Fight Over His Job

The midterm result is settling into a new Washington order. Democrats, projected to take at least 258 of the 435 House seats and 54 Senate seats against the Republicans’ 44 — with Alaska and Mississippi still to be called — say they will use control of both chambers to rein in President Donald Trump and focus on prices, setting up two years of likely deadlock; Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer has said the United States would end the conflict with Iran. On the Republican side, the leadership reckoning began Wednesday: House Speaker Mike Johnson told staff he will not seek to lead his party in the new Congress, though he keeps his Louisiana seat, re-elected for an eighth term. Two familiar rivals moved within hours — Majority Leader Steve Scalise and Judiciary Committee chairman Jim Jordan both launched bids to become minority leader, reviving the contest they fought after Kevin McCarthy’s ouster in 2023. Scalise, favoured by business and energy-sector conservatives, wrote that his first goal would be winning back the majority; Jordan is the hard right’s candidate. Candidates must declare by noon on Friday, and House Republicans vote on Tuesday, 10 November. The gavel itself is expected to pass to Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries when the new Congress elects its speaker on 3 January 2027.

Latest · Americas · United States — A First in American History5 November 2026

Georgia Makes History: Keisha Lance Bottoms Is the First Black Woman Elected Governor of a US State

Amid the midterm sweep, one result rewrote a two-century-old line in American politics. Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, won Georgia’s governorship on Tuesday with about 55.3 per cent of the vote against Republican billionaire health-care executive Rick Jackson — becoming the first Black woman ever elected governor of a US state, and Georgia’s first woman governor. The former Atlanta mayor, who served as a senior adviser to President Joe Biden and directed the White House Office of Public Engagement, flips the governor’s mansion back to Democrats after 23 years of Republican control; the outgoing governor, Brian Kemp, was term-limited. Bottoms has spoken of being the great-great-granddaughter of Shephard Peek, who was sold into slavery in Crawfordsville, Georgia, in 1826 and is believed to have become a member of Congress after the Civil War — she will now govern the state where he was enslaved. Her win came eight years after Stacey Abrams became the first Black woman nominated for governor by a major party, and she is one of five Black governor-elects from Tuesday’s elections. The same evening, Stacey Plaskett was elected governor of the US Virgin Islands, the first Black woman chosen to lead a US jurisdiction. Bottoms takes office in January 2027.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — “Americans Said No to War”5 November 2026

Tehran Claims the Midterms as Its Own Victory — and Warns the Next War Would Be Bigger

Iran is reading the Republican midterm defeat as a verdict on the war itself. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who leads Tehran’s negotiating team, posted that whether from conscience or the cost of living, “Americans said no to war, and we endorse this message” — and warned that if Washington ignores that message, Iran’s own “no” would come “with a nightmare”. In a later post he accused US leaders of “losing gambler energy”, unable to walk away from the table while America teeters on an “Israel-First cliff”. Supreme National Security Council chief Mohsen Rezaei claimed Iran had handed Donald Trump a strategic defeat on the battlefield and in Hormuz, and a political one in Washington. But the leadership is not counting on a policy change: Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei called it a grave delusion to expect partisan shifts in Washington to alter US hostility, or concessions to soften it. The warnings carry a military edge — Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani said a renewed conflict could spread beyond the Strait of Hormuz to the Bab al-Mandeb, and the IRGC claimed on Thursday that two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in separate overnight incidents in the strait and that it had seized two US Lucas-type drones electronically. One Iranian official summed up the posture: Tehran is preparing for renewed US and Israeli attacks, not for a deal. President Trump, for his part, has acknowledged the war and fuel prices hurt his party, while insisting he would make the same decision again; Qatari mediation to avert a return to fighting continues.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — The Count Passes a Quarter5 November 2026

Count Passes 25%: ANC Still in Front, DA Dominant in the Big Cities, Results Due Sunday

South Africa’s municipal count has moved well past the near-tie of the early morning. By about 10:40 on Thursday, 6,396 of 23,696 voting districts had reported — roughly 26 per cent — and the Electoral Commission’s dashboard put the ANC at 32.9 per cent of the national ward ballot with 1,777,681 votes, against the DA’s 28.62 per cent and 1,585,587 votes; a separate 10 a.m. snapshot had the ANC at 31.7 per cent and the DA at 29.2 per cent, with the MK Party third on about 5.7 per cent and the EFF on 5.2. The metro map is where the story sharpens: the DA leads Johannesburg on about 40.3 per cent against the ANC’s 16.8 and ActionSA’s 15.5, commands Ekurhuleni on 42.4 per cent, and holds Cape Town above 62 per cent, while in Tshwane ActionSA edged an early snapshot ahead of the ANC and the DA. In the Northern Cape, 14 of 26 councils had finished counting by Thursday morning — five with majorities, nine hung — and two municipalities, Renosterberg among them, had declared final results, Renosterberg returning an ANC majority with six of eleven seats. The CSIR’s projection model points to a final national picture near 32 per cent for the ANC and 26 per cent for the DA, though the commission stresses everything remains provisional. The rand strengthened about half a per cent to 16.3425 per dollar on the pro-business DA’s strong showing and the US midterm result. Official results will be announced on Sunday, 8 November, after which councils have 14 days to form.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — 21,468 Waiting to Get Out5 November 2026

One More Killed at a Displacement Tent — and 21,468 Sick and Wounded Still Waiting for Treatment Abroad

The killing continued on Thursday morning: an Israeli drone strike on a tent sheltering displaced people west of Al-Zawayda in central Gaza killed one man and wounded another, with further strikes and gunfire leaving one dead and four wounded across the Strip in the early hours, according to medical sources. Gaza’s health ministry puts the toll since the October 2025 ceasefire at 1,602 killed and 5,794 wounded, with 847 bodies recovered from rubble, and the overall toll since October 2023 at 74,618 dead and 175,920 injured. But the ministry’s sharper warning on Thursday concerned the living. Only 209 patients and wounded people were allowed to leave Gaza for treatment abroad during the whole of October — departures took place on just seven of 17 scheduled travel days — while 21,468 people remain on evacuation waiting lists, 6,430 of them classified as the highest priority. Just 3,056 patients have got out since February. The Euro-Med Human Rights Monitor adds that 1,134 Palestinian children who have undergone limb amputations are among those blocked from treatment abroad, facing lifelong complications. The UN human-rights office, meanwhile, continues to warn that Israeli forces are pushing Palestinians into ever-smaller areas of the Strip.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — No Sign of a Cyberattack5 November 2026

Docomo Narrows the Fault: Standalone 5G Down Nationwide, Emergency Calls Safe, Online Shop Still Suspended

NTT Docomo has narrowed down Thursday’s nationwide disruption — and separated it from the security scare that began before dawn. The carrier said the unstable voice calls, data and text messages are confined to its standalone 5G service, known as 5G SA, which has been unreliable since about 11:18 a.m.; its other 5G and 4G services, and the smaller providers that run on its network, are unaffected, emergency calls are working, and it has found no signs the glitch was caused by a cyberattack. Affected customers may be able to restore service by switching their phones to 4G, then restarting or toggling airplane mode. The cause remains under investigation. Separately, the online shop and My docomo portal suspended at about 1:05 a.m. — after the company concluded an outside party had likely gained unauthorised access to its servers — remain down with no restoration estimate, leaving customers unable to sign up for plans, check bills or change contracts. The double failure is the carrier’s second embarrassment in weeks: in mid-October a system malfunction stopped its Area Mail service delivering an emergency earthquake alert across eastern Japan, an alert the meteorological agency later retracted after overestimating the quake.
