Latest · Americas · US — Hegseth Impeachment Bid Blocked11 November 2026

US House Blocks the Bid to Impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth Over the Iran War, 211 to 204, With 11 Democrats Absent

The Republican-controlled US House of Representatives has voted 211 to 204 to send the resolution seeking Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s impeachment over his handling of the war on Iran to committee, a procedural move that effectively halts the effort. Eleven Democratic lawmakers missed the vote; Representative Jonathan Jackson attributed his absence to a traffic delay. Democrats say they will keep pressing for Hegseth’s removal: Senator Adam Schiff has said the party plans to use upcoming government funding negotiations to push for an end to the war, including by seeking to cut off money for military operations, and Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed publicly urged Hegseth to resign, calling his stewardship of the war a failure. Hegseth has dismissed the impeachment proceedings as a farce and a stunt. The vote took place against a tense security backdrop — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has reportedly moved to a military base following reported Iranian threats against his life.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Tanker Fire11 November 2026

Oil Tanker Catches Fire in the Strait of Hormuz After Striking a Mine, Iran’s Army Says, as Brent Rebounds to $92.53

An oil tanker caught fire in the eastern Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday after hitting a naval mine in a restricted area, Iran’s army said in a statement carried by state media. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre separately reported suspicious activity involving a tanker in the strait, with the crew’s status and any damage initially unclear. The incident came as diplomatic language hardened: Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi mocked US Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s remark that Iran would eventually have to “eat” and accept a deal, while Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei denied that any talks between Tehran and Washington are under way — a denial that helped oil recover ground after prices had fallen more than $3 a barrel on Tuesday when US officials played down further strikes. By early Wednesday, Brent crude futures were up 1.1 percent at $92.53 a barrel.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi, Day Two11 November 2026

Abu Dhabi Talks Resume on Day Two After First Day Ends Without a Deal; Prisoner Swap Still Unconfirmed

Ukraine, Russia and the United States return to the table in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday for the second day of their first trilateral round since February, after more than four hours of talks on Tuesday closed without an official result. Ukraine’s delegation, led by Rustem Umerov with Kyrylo Budanov among its members, says the first session covered humanitarian and de-escalation steps, the substance of long-term security guarantees and prisoner releases as a priority. Ukrainian media have reported a possible large prisoner exchange, but neither side has confirmed it and no timing has been given. The US team is led by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, Russia’s by Vladimir Medinsky. Before the trilateral session, Kyiv’s negotiators also met representatives of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission, who are consulting on the sidelines rather than sitting in the three-way room. Territory — above all Moscow’s demand over the Donbas — and whether a ceasefire is formally on the agenda remain disputed, and the round comes as winter approaches and Russian strikes on Ukraine’s energy grid have resumed.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — New Knesset Sworn In10 November 2026

Israel’s 26th Knesset Is Sworn In With No Government Formed, as Eisenkot Begins Coalition Talks Against a December Deadline

All 120 lawmakers elected on 27 October were sworn into Israel’s 26th Knesset on Tuesday, opening a parliament still without a government after the election stripped Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of his majority but left his rivals short of one too. President Isaac Herzog has tasked former general Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the centre-right Yashar party, with forming a government; Eisenkot was backed by 67 lawmakers against 46 for Netanyahu, yet his own bloc and its partners hold only 48 seats, while the Likud-led alliance holds 53. Eisenkot has until 6 December, with a possible two-week extension. His party moved quickly on procedure, filing a motion signed by a majority of members to replace Speaker Amir Ohana with Yashar’s Matan Kahana — a vote now set for 17 November — and submitting bills for a state commission of inquiry into the failures surrounding the 7 October 2023 attack and for prime-ministerial term limits. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister, and Yashar cancelled a planned Wednesday meeting with Likud after a swearing-in speech it condemned.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Vice-President’s Future in Doubt10 November 2026

Indonesia’s Vice-President Gibran Offers to Resign Over an Eligibility Dispute; Prabowo Urges Him to Fight the Court Rulings Instead

Indonesia’s Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo Subianto on Tuesday that he intends to step down, a presidential spokesperson confirmed, as lawmakers weigh an inquiry into whether he was eligible to stand in the 2024 election. The 39-year-old — the eldest son of former president Joko Widodo — met Prabowo for more than two hours. Spokesperson Prasetyo Hadi denied reports that Prabowo had rejected the offer but stopped short of saying it was accepted. Gibran later told reporters the president had advised him to fight to prove his credentials, and that he would appeal. The crisis follows rulings last month by the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court that the overseas education documents Gibran submitted as a candidate did not meet election requirements; the Constitutional Court said disqualification was for other institutions to decide. Some analysts see a parliamentary inquiry as a possible first step toward impeachment. The episode has also exposed a widening rift between Prabowo and the Widodo family, whose support helped deliver Prabowo’s landslide 2024 win.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Korea — Seongnam Bus Pileup11 November 2026

Commuter Bus Rams 32 Vehicles Waiting at a Traffic Light Near Seoul, Injuring 57 People in the Morning Rush Hour

A city bus ploughed into vehicles queued at a traffic signal in the Yatap-dong area of Bundang, Seongnam, just south of Seoul, at about 7:34 a.m. on Wednesday, striking four other buses, a cargo truck and 27 passenger cars over a stretch of roughly 100 to 150 metres before hitting a median barrier and stopping. Fire officials in Gyeonggi Province said 57 people were being treated for injuries, nine of them seriously hurt, though none in a life-threatening condition. Authorities sent 88 personnel and 33 vehicles to the scene and set up an emergency medical station. The driver, a man in his 60s, was taken to hospital, tested negative for alcohol and told investigators the brakes had failed; police say they will examine the vehicle’s recording data and CCTV footage, and check for possible drug use, as they establish the cause.

Trump Says the FBI Is Looking Into Nvidia Chief Jensen Huang’s Ties to an Alleged Chinese Spy — and That He Believes Huang Knew Nothing

President Donald Trump has confirmed that the FBI is examining Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based data-centre firm Megaspeed whom US intelligence officials believe worked for China’s Ministry of State Security. Trump told reporters he had spoken with Huang, who said he had “absolutely no idea” the woman was an alleged agent — an account Trump said he believes, adding that he expects Huang will be “in good shape.” Nvidia’s board announced on Sunday that it had opened an independent inquiry, overseen by outside counsel, into whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence operation, while saying it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. Shareholders have since filed a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose accusing the company of concealing the relationship and the risks it posed; Nvidia shares fell 4.3 percent on Monday. Beijing has rejected the spying allegations as groundless speculation and a smear, and incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries has said lawmakers need more information from the administration.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31, Antalya11 November 2026

At COP31, Iran Turns Its Pavilion Into a Memorial to a Bombed School as Leaders Arrive and Brazil Prepares a Fossil-Fuel Roadmap

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya has become a stage for the wars overshadowing it. Iran’s national pavilion displays photographs of schoolchildren killed in a US strike on an elementary school in Minab, alongside a mock classroom of strewn backpacks, desks and a chalkboard, and a virtual-reality exhibit; President Masoud Pezeshkian is due to address the leaders’ segment on Wednesday and says he will raise what he calls US and Israeli crimes against Iran’s environment. Ukraine’s pavilion, meanwhile, showcases renewable-energy projects built during Russia’s war, including model power plants meant to show why decentralised electricity matters for hospitals. Away from the pavilions, Brazil is set to launch a roadmap for transitioning away from fossil fuels, built around policy “levers” countries can use to shift from coal, oil and gas toward wind and solar, as delegates argue over energy prices driven up by the conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Winter Storm Aftermath8 November 2026

Gaza’s First Winter Storm Flooded Camps Across All Five Governorates, and the UN Says Shelter Stocks Inside the Strip Are Nearly Exhausted

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs says heavy rain and strong winds that battered Gaza from 6 November through the weekend flooded displacement camps in all five governorates, collapsing damaged buildings and overwhelming shelters, schools and clinics. At Al Berka Reservoir, west of Deir al-Balah, a sandy hill collapsed onto tents, killing five displaced people — three children and two women — and injuring another person, according to Gaza’s Civil Defense; an infant was also reported to have drowned at a displacement site. Aid partners logged appeals from more than 26,000 households whose shelters or belongings were damaged, and the Site Management Cluster says over 20,000 households across 222 sites urgently need shelter and household support. An estimated 1.4 million people — about 65 percent of Gaza’s population — live in tents or makeshift shelters. OCHA says stocks of tents, tarpaulins, blankets and clothes inside Gaza are virtually exhausted, while more than 150,000 tents, 300,000 tarpaulins and 500,000 blankets purchased by aid partners and donor states sit outside the territory awaiting approvals and funding to move.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi’s Air Eases, Slightly11 November 2026

Delhi’s Air Improves to an AQI of 299 — Still “Poor” — as an IITM Study Attributes 71% of Post-Diwali PM2.5 to Firecrackers

Delhi’s average Air Quality Index eased to 299 on Wednesday morning, down from 344 on Monday, according to the Central Pollution Control Board — an improvement that still leaves the capital’s air in the “poor” band, with many individual stations in “very poor” territory, Chhawla highest at 396 and the ITO station at 366. Stage III restrictions under the Graded Response Action Plan, in force since 6 November, remain in place. The numbers have sharpened a political argument over what drives the annual smog: an assessment by the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology estimates firecrackers contributed 71.1 percent of the city’s PM2.5 during the eight hours from 1 a.m. to 9 a.m. on 9 November, the morning after Diwali, while Punjab — which recorded 5,002 farm fires between 15 September and 10 November, 345 of them on Tuesday — pushes back against the focus on stubble burning. Forecasters expect continued winds to bring some further relief over the next two days.

Latest · Europe · Energy and Industry — Norway Pauses, Berlin Buys11 November 2026

Norway Pauses New Grid Capacity for Data Centres and Plans Licences, While Germany Agrees a €6.6 Billion Stake in Tank Maker KNDS

Two European governments moved this week to put strategic industries under tighter state control. In Norway, grid operator Statnett is temporarily pausing new allocations of electricity-grid capacity for data centres while the government designs a mandatory licensing regime that would make developers seek official permission, similar to energy and natural-resource projects. Officials say the aim is controlled growth: room for artificial-intelligence infrastructure, less room for energy-intensive cryptocurrency mining. In Germany, the government has agreed to buy a 50 percent stake in Franco-German tank manufacturer KNDS for €6.6 billion, banned Chinese-built drones and vehicles from military barracks over espionage concerns, and increased its loan to the Federal Employment Agency by €3 billion, taking total federal credit support to €8.2 billion for the coming fiscal year. The steps drew criticism from the VDMA engineering association, which accused the coalition of stalled economic reforms even as defence spending accelerates.

Latest · Middle East / Americas · Iran — Retaliation and Rhetoric11 November 2026

Iran Designates Argentina’s Navy and Air Force as “Terrorist Organisations” in Reply to the Blacklisting of Its Revolutionary Guards

Iran’s Foreign Ministry announced on Wednesday that it has designated Argentina’s navy and air force as terrorist organisations, retaliating for Buenos Aires’s decision in March to blacklist the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Tehran said Argentina had violated fundamental principles of international law and committed a hostile act against one of the pillars of Iran’s official armed forces, and that it was acting under a 2019 law requiring reciprocal measures against countries that follow the US designation of the IRGC. Argentina linked its listing to the Guards’ backing for Hezbollah, which it blames for the 1994 AMIA Jewish community centre bombing in Buenos Aires that killed 85 people. The exchange capped a day of pointed signalling from Tehran: Iran opened its first all-women military training camp, with dozens of volunteer “Janfadayan” drilling at Tehran’s Azadi Square amid fears of renewed US attacks, and its Foreign Ministry mocked France — which blames an IRGC cyber unit for October’s supermarket refrigeration sabotage — as a government that “cannot even manage refrigerators.” Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf accused Israel of working overtime to drag the US into an endless war.
