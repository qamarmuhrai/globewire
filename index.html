Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Kuwait and Bahrain Intercept Again18 November 2026

Explosions Over Kuwait Were Interceptions: The Gulf Exchange Widens Before Dawn

The confrontation around the Strait of Hormuz spread to two more Gulf states in the early hours of Wednesday. Kuwait’s General Staff said at about 01:39 Gulf time that its air defences were countering hostile drone attacks and that explosions heard across the country were interceptions; it named no origin, target, casualties or damage and did not blame Iran. Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, issued two fresh potential-threat alerts overnight and then announced the immediate danger had passed, while its Defence Force said it had repelled a number of Iranian air attacks on Wednesday after intercepting several more on Tuesday. Tuesday’s Bahrain attack — Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said drones targeted a US-operated radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base, the third such attack in five days — was intercepted without injuries or damage, according to Bahrain and US Central Command. The overnight activity followed the American precision strike on a Guards missile-launch site on Qeshm Island, and a third US carrier group led by USS Theodore Roosevelt is now in the region.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Xi Opens APEC18 November 2026

Remove the Barriers, Link the Bay Areas: Xi’s Keynote Sets Shenzhen’s Tone

President Xi Jinping opened the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen on Wednesday morning with the speech the week had been waiting for. Delivering a keynote titled “Forging Ahead with Resolve Toward a Bright Future for the Asia-Pacific” at the Qianhai International Convention Center, Xi called for peace and stability as the condition for shared prosperity, urged economies to remove trade and investment barriers, and said China would cut tariffs, widen market access and adjust rules for foreign manufacturers. He also backed a new world association for bay-area economic cooperation to link the world’s great bay regions and find new paths for Asia-Pacific cooperation. Xi now chairs the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting on Wednesday and Thursday under the theme of building an Asia-Pacific community to prosper together. South Korea’s President Lee Jae-myung arrived on Wednesday, and the week’s centre of gravity remains Thursday’s first-ever trilateral between Xi, US President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Latest · Americas · Brooklyn — 330 Months18 November 2026

330 Months for a Napkin Sketch and $5,000 Advances: The Merchant Plot Ends in Brooklyn

A federal court in Brooklyn has closed the 2024 assassination case. Asif Merchant, 48, a Pakistani businessman, was sentenced on Tuesday by US District Judge Eric Komitee to 330 months — 27½ years — for attempted terrorism transcending national boundaries and murder for hire, after a jury convicted him in March. Prosecutors said Merchant travelled to the United States to recruit killers for Iran’s Revolutionary Guards in retaliation for the 2020 killing of commander Qassem Soleimani, with Donald Trump the believed target and Joe Biden and Nikki Haley also named as possibilities; the men he paid $5,000 advances to were undercover FBI agents, alerted after he sketched the plan for an acquaintance. Merchant testified he joined unwillingly to protect family in Tehran, declined to speak at sentencing and showed little reaction; the judge called the crime exceptionally serious but rejected prosecutors’ bid for life, noting how early the scheme was stopped. He was arrested in Texas on 12 July 2024, a day before the unrelated Butler attempt on Trump’s life. Tehran has denied targeting US officials.

Revenue Doubles, the Door Stays Shut: Nvidia Reports Without Jensen Huang

Nvidia’s first earnings report since its founder left has produced two headlines at once. For the third quarter of fiscal 2027, ended 25 October, the chipmaker reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and ahead of the $109.1 billion analysts expected, with data-centre revenue up 110% to $107.6 billion and net income of $65.3 billion; it forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter, assuming no data-centre compute revenue from China, and shares rose about 5% in extended trading. On the same call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board unanimously accepted Jensen Huang’s 12 November resignation — announced after disclosures of personal behaviour inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct — and does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive, hours after Huang posted that he would be honoured to return. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, the longtime finance chief, said the product roadmap and customer commitments remain on track while search firm Heidrick & Struggles hunts for a permanent successor.

Business Forum to Mumbai, Eyes on the Sea: What the Manila Quad Actually Agreed

The Quad’s first leaders’ summit since Wilmington in September 2024 — and its first ever hosted in a non-Quad country — ended in Manila on Tuesday with five announced outcomes. Latest · Global Affairs · Oil — Below $93 Despite the Fire18 November 2026

Stocks Up 5.28 Million Barrels, Brent Down Anyway: Oil Refuses to Panic over Hormuz

Oil markets looked at a night of Gulf interceptions and a blocked strait — and eased. Brent crude slipped below $93 on Wednesday, trading around $92.85–$92.98 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate fell to about $85.40–$85.45, after American Petroleum Institute data showed US crude and fuel inventories rose last week, with crude stocks up 5.28 million barrels in the week ended 13 November. Traders weighed that supply cushion against continuing US–Iran attacks and disrupted Hormuz shipping, which limited the fall: both benchmarks had settled higher on Tuesday as the conflict intensified, an LPG tanker struck by a projectile off Oman on Monday had to be evacuated, and analysts noted that normal flows through Hormuz are still not possible under current conditions. Iran’s Mehr agency said the Qeshm strikes caused no casualties or damage to critical infrastructure. The balance, for now, is surplus against risk — with the risk repricing by the hour.

Arrested Near Gloucester, Handed to the US Army: The NATO Officer Espionage Case Moves

Britain has handed its most sensitive arrest of the month to Washington. A serving US Army officer arrested near Gloucester on 10 November on suspicion of offences under Sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023 — obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service, with Russia the state named by police — has been transferred to US military custody, the Metropolitan Police said on Tuesday, with the investigation passed to US partners under existing protocols. US officials have identified the officer as Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Tyner, 41, a senior human-resources officer at Imjin Barracks assigned to NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps headquarters; he joined the Army in 2008 and is expected to face charges. FBI Director Kash Patel called him a potential national security threat and credited work by the FBI’s Philadelphia and London offices with MI5, UK counter-terrorism police, UK defence counter-intelligence and US Army counterintelligence. The Kremlin has denied any connection. What information was allegedly accessed or passed has not been disclosed.

Latest · Europe · 8,000 Hoaxes — “Death Is Near”18 November 2026

6,300 Italian Schools in One Morning: Inside Europe’s Month of Hoax Bomb Threats

A Reuters investigation has put a number on a month of disruption: more than 8,000 hoax bomb threats sent to institutions and businesses across Europe, tallied from official disclosures, media reports and data from the cybersecurity firms Proofpoint and Mimecast. The pattern began on 19 October, when emails headed “Death is near” reached schools in Northern Ireland — about 300 received the same message, including St John the Baptist Primary in Belfast — Scotland and Bulgaria from at least four hijacked accounts. Early November brought threats against more than 50 flights at some 15 airlines, diverting a Wizz Air London–Sofia flight to Budapest, and the largest wave came on 11 November, when about 6,300 Italian schools were threatened under the name “Misoginistan,” alongside roughly 1,000 schools in Scotland and 300 in Northern Ireland, forcing nationwide evacuations. Later messages invoked the banned Russian group “Male State,” whose founder denies involvement. Reuters could not establish that all the emails share one source; France’s interior minister Laurent Nuñez said officials see a possibility of foreign interference “very clearly.”

Latest · Americas · Washington — Ninth Failure, First Majority18 November 2026

$4.9 Trillion in Assets, No Clean Opinion — but 14 of 25 Passed: The Pentagon Audit

The Pentagon has failed its financial audit for the ninth consecutive year — and, for the first time, most of it passed. An inspector-general report released on Monday gave the department as a whole another disclaimer of opinion, but chief financial officer Jules Hurst told Reuters that 14 of its 25 standalone reporting entities received clean opinions for fiscal 2026, up from 11, covering 56% of assets and 86% of liabilities across $4.9 trillion in assets and $5.2 trillion in liabilities; the Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund passed for the first time. The department, first audited in 2018, now plans to consolidate 25 opinions into eight and seek a full clean opinion in fiscal 2028, the year after Congress’s ten-year deadline falls due, with an independent accounting firm taking over from the inspector general. The result landed hours before Senate appropriators released a defence bill of nearly $1.1 trillion for fiscal 2027 — short of the $1.5 trillion the administration requested — with a government funding deadline of 11 December.

Latest · Africa · Eastern Congo — Two Tracks, One War18 November 2026

Washington for Rwanda, Doha for M23 — and Fighting in the Kivus Regardless

Two peace processes are running for eastern Congo, and the fighting is outrunning both. In North and South Kivu, the Congolese army continues to battle the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a coalition led by former president Joseph Kabila that was established in October and says it has succeeded the Congo River Alliance, after an early-November offensive in which monitors reported the capture of localities including Bitale and the centre of Bulambika on 8 November. The diplomacy is split by design: the Washington Accords of December 2025 address tensions between Kinshasa and Kigali, while the Doha process — whose framework was signed by the government, the Alliance and the Rwanda-backed M23 on 15 November 2025, with a road map agreed in August 2026 alongside Qatar, the United States, Togo, the African Union and Switzerland — is meant to settle the government’s conflict with the armed groups themselves. The African Union, after an October Peace and Security Council meeting, said implementation under both tracks remained limited. The human ledger behind the communiqués: about seven million displaced, 27.8 million facing food insecurity and nearly 1.4 million children acutely malnourished, with the UN mission MONUSCO weakened by funding cuts.

Latest · Europe · Kerch — Rail on One Track18 November 2026

An Arch in the Strait, 2,000 Cars Queued: Kerch on the Eve of the Shenzhen Trilateral

The Kerch Bridge remains the war’s most symbolic broken line two days before Shenzhen. Ukraine’s drone-and-missile strike brought down the main road arch of the 19-kilometre crossing linking Russia to annexed Crimea — video posted by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and verified by Reuters showed the arch fallen into the strait — in what Kyiv described as the seventh stage of a weeks-long campaign against Crimean air defences, and Moscow described as one of the largest Ukrainian drone barrages of the war, claiming 1,336 drones intercepted overnight and 2,049 over 24 hours. The Security Service said a metal arch and the road span beneath it collapsed; Russia’s Transport Ministry said rail traffic had resumed on one track while repairs continued on the other, Crimea’s governor Sergei Aksyonov confirmed a second day of attack and suspension, and about 2,000 vehicles were reported stranded in queues. Russia’s Investigative Committee has called the strike a terrorist act. Presidents Putin, Trump and Xi meet in Shenzhen on Thursday, with Ukraine’s place on — or off — that agenda still contested between the Kremlin and the White House.
