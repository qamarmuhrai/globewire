Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — Second Hit on the Tower in Less Than a Week24 November 2026

Houthi Attack on Riyadh’s Tallest Tower Injures 11 in the Financial District

Eleven people were injured on Tuesday when a projectile fired by Yemen’s Houthi movement struck a tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District, Saudi civil defence said. Witnesses told AFP the building hit was the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower, the capital’s tallest skyscraper and home to the kingdom’s sovereign wealth fund, which had already been targeted last Thursday in an attack that damaged several floors but caused no casualties. The injured were two Saudi citizens and nine foreign residents — four Egyptians, three Nepalis and two Indians. The Saudi-led coalition backing Yemen’s recognised government against the Houthis called the strike a cowardly terrorist attack and said investigators were examining the site. Yemen’s internationally recognised government separately welcomed new British sanctions on the Houthis and four senior officials.

Latest · Europe · Moscow — Refinery Output Down Sharply Since Late October24 November 2026

Putin Says Retaliation Is Inevitable After Ukrainian Attacks on Russian Refineries

President Vladimir Putin said on Tuesday that Russia would inevitably respond to continuing Ukrainian drone attacks on its oil refineries and asked his government to discuss stronger protection for fuel and energy sites and their staff. Speaking at a meeting on the fuel and energy sector, Putin said Kyiv had kept up the strikes despite a direct request from US President Donald Trump to stop targeting such facilities, and argued that the campaign, stepped up since early June, would not inflict critical damage on Russia. Estimates cited in live coverage put the fall in combined Russian refinery processing at about 45 percent since late October. Putin also said petrol prices had stabilised after an earlier peak and criticised European efforts to further restrict Russian energy exports, warning of consequences for Europe itself.

Latest · Europe · Zaporizhzhia — Infant Among the Wounded24 November 2026

Russian Dawn Strike on Zaporizhzhia Kills Two and Wounds More Than 40

A Russian air attack on the south-eastern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia early on Tuesday killed two people and wounded more than 40, regional officials said. The strike destroyed a private house and partially demolished a five-storey apartment building. Acting regional head Mykhailo Semikin said the wounded included four children — a baby under one year old and children aged four, six and 16 — and that one woman was in a serious condition with blast and head injuries. Ukrainian media later put the injured toll at 44 as rescuers searched the rubble for people who might still be trapped. The attack came a day after Kyiv confirmed its sea drones had sunk a Russian warship in the Libyan port of Benghazi, far from the front line, and hours before Norway’s defence minister urged Ukraine to respect stability in nearby areas amid allied unease over strikes in the Baltic.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza City — Meningitis Cases Hit 272 in October24 November 2026

Israeli Strike Kills Hamas Internal Security Chief in Gaza as Meningitis Alert Is Declared

An Israeli airstrike in western Gaza City on Tuesday killed Naeem Abu Nahel, the director of Hamas’s internal security apparatus, Hamas sources and Gaza’s interior ministry said. Abu Nahel, 50, a brigadier general who had led the service since September, was responsible for counter-espionage and for protecting senior officials and key facilities. Israel’s military and the Shin Bet said they had struck a senior Hamas figure, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu later confirmed Abu Nahel was the target. Medics said at least seven Palestinians were killed in strikes across the Strip on Tuesday, including police officers. At the same time, Gaza’s health ministry declared an epidemic alert after meningitis cases reached 272 in October — more than eight times the usual monthly level of 30 to 50 — with 15 deaths recorded this year. Hamas called the killing a war crime; 26 patients and 27 companions were evacuated through Rafah on Tuesday for treatment abroad.

Six Tankers Hit in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran Tensions Spread to Shipping

Six oil tankers have been hit in the Strait of Hormuz over the past 24 hours after striking mines, Iran’s army and the Revolutionary Guards navy reported, while the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre verified that one tanker was struck by an unknown projectile on Sunday evening and advised other vessels to transit with caution. Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority said it had added 26 tugboats to a non-compliance list for serving vessels south of the strait. In Washington, officials are investigating Iran as a possible culprit behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 ships carrying oil, gas and chemicals, the Wall Street Journal reported, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the United States would keep severing the financial lifelines Tehran uses for its military and proxies after fresh sanctions. The chief of staff of Iran’s armed forces warned that moving US bases would not put them out of missile range.

Latest · Americas · Washington — 16 Partners Covered by the Probe24 November 2026

Trump Administration Prepares Fresh Tariffs After Excess-Capacity Investigation

The Trump administration is expected to unveil the findings of a Section 301 investigation into excess industrial capacity as soon as Tuesday or Wednesday, paving the way for new import duties on a wide range of goods, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters. The probe, launched in March, covers 16 major trading partners — China, the European Union, India, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Switzerland, Norway, Singapore, Bangladesh and Cambodia. The new duties would stack on top of existing tariffs and would partly replace the global levies struck down by the Supreme Court in February. Washington singles out China as the largest source of excess capacity in steel, aluminium, electric vehicles, appliances and solar products, and is also targeting Chinese investment routed through South-East Asia to avoid duties. Rates and exemptions have not been announced, but officials intend to respect the ceilings agreed in recent trade deals, including a 20 percent second-term cap for China and 15 percent for the EU.

Latest · Africa · Dakar — Navy Rescues 974 People From Five Other Boats24 November 2026

Around 175 Migrants Unaccounted For After Contact Is Lost With Boat From Senegal

Around 175 people are unaccounted for after contact was lost with a Europe-bound wooden boat, known locally as a pirogue, that left the Yoff district of Dakar around 9–10 November, local officials and a human rights group said on Tuesday. Yoff’s mayor, Seydina Issa Laye, said about 175 people were on board; the group Action for Human Rights and Friendship put the missing at 172 and called for search-and-rescue efforts to be stepped up. No trace of the vessel has been reported in the two weeks since departure on the perilous Atlantic route toward Europe. Senegal’s navy said that on Monday alone it rescued 974 migrants travelling on five other pirogues, underlining how many people are still attempting the crossing despite the risks.

Latest · Europe · Stockholm — Vote Set for 30 November After 72 Days24 November 2026

Sweden’s Parliament to Vote on Whether Kristersson Stays as Prime Minister

Sweden’s parliament will vote on 30 November on whether Moderate Party leader Ulf Kristersson should remain prime minister, Speaker Andreas Norlén announced on Tuesday, in a bid to end a deadlock that has left the country without a permanent government since the September election. Kristersson’s right-wing bloc lost its majority in that vote but was asked to try to form a government after centre-left parties repeatedly failed to agree on sharing power. He wants to lead a coalition of the Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals and Centre, though Centre has not yet said how it will vote and no deal has been reached. Under Swedish rules, a nominee is defeated only if at least 175 of 349 lawmakers vote against; this will be the third prime ministerial vote, and if four votes fail a snap election must be called within three months. Norlén warned the risk of a fresh election remains significant after 72 days of talks.

Latest · Americas · Brasília — Real Up 2 Percent as Markets Welcome the Plan24 November 2026

Lula Approves a Second Fiscal Package After Congress Passes the First Spending Curbs

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has authorised his economic team to send Congress a second package of measures to curb mandatory spending, a source familiar with the matter told Reuters on Tuesday, to be presented as a proposed constitutional amendment. The move follows the lower house’s approval late on Monday, by 353 votes to 128, of a first government-backed package that caps annual spending growth at 2 percent, allows extra budget freezes when targets are at risk and trims tax breaks and credits. The bill, already passed by the Senate, is expected to improve public finances by up to 60 billion reais (about $10 billion) in 2027 and is central to the government’s goal of a primary surplus of 0.5 percent of GDP next year; Lula will now sign it into law. The real strengthened 2 percent against the dollar on Tuesday and the Bovespa index rose more than 1 percent, though investors still judge the measures insufficient to fix Brazil’s worsening debt outlook.

Latest · Americas · Mexico City — Annual Rate Rises to 3.96 Percent24 November 2026

Mexico’s Inflation Hits a Six-Month High While Core Prices Ease Unexpectedly

Mexico’s annual inflation accelerated in the first half of November to 3.96 percent, its highest level since early May and slightly above the 3.92 percent forecast in a Reuters poll, statistics agency INEGI said on Tuesday. The rate was up from 3.62 percent a month earlier. Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices and is closely watched by policymakers, moved in the opposite direction, easing to 3.68 percent year on year from 3.78 percent. The central bank targets inflation at 3 percent, plus or minus one percentage point, so headline inflation remains inside the tolerance band but close to its upper half. With the mixed picture, the bank is expected to leave interest rates unchanged at its next monetary policy meeting in December.
