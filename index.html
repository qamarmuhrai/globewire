Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash28 October 2026

At Least 46 Killed as Two High-Speed Trains Collide Near Toledo — Madrid–Andalusia Line Suspended

At least 46 people were killed and dozens injured on Wednesday morning when two high-speed trains collided near Urda in Spain's Toledo province, according to regional emergency services and Civil Protection reports cited by Spanish media. The crash happened at about 8:54 a.m. local time at kilometre point 118 of the Madrid–Andalusia high-speed line, between Los Yébenes and Urda. Initial reports say a Renfe AVE service that had left Madrid's Puerta de Atocha station at 8 a.m. for Seville struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train on the same line; two Iryo carriages derailed and fell down an embankment. The regional government said 544 passengers were aboard the two services combined. Rail operator Adif suspended high-speed traffic between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia, Renfe offered free changes and cancellations, and Castilla-La Mancha raised its Platecam emergency plan to level 2, deploying three medical helicopters, two mobile intensive care units and firefighters, with help requested from the Military Emergencies Unit. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez offered condolences and said emergency services were at work. Investigators have not yet established why the Iryo train had stopped or why the following train was able to approach it — Spain's second major rail disaster this year.

Latest · Middle East · Iran War Costs28 October 2026

US Spending on the Iran War Passes $47 Billion, Pentagon Figures Show — More Than 25% Above the July Estimate

The United States has spent more than $47 billion prosecuting its war with Iran, according to Pentagon data obtained by Reuters — over 25% higher than the Defence Department's estimate in July and $3.5 billion higher than in early September. The updated figure, shared with Congress and dated to 16 October, includes more than $29 billion to replace expended munitions and over $6 billion to replace lost equipment and aircraft, including many drones, plus nearly $12 billion in added operational costs; it excludes repairs to bases hit by Iranian attacks, which officials say could raise the total further. President Donald Trump launched the war jointly with Israel in February predicting a short conflict, but an April ceasefire allowed Iran to restore some defences, and Tehran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted global energy markets. The disclosure lands six days before the 3 November midterms, as Republicans wrestle with the political fallout of an unpopular war that has weighed on Trump's approval ratings. In Israel, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Wednesday that he saw a "unique historic opportunity" with Trump to eliminate what he called the existential threat from Iran.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election28 October 2026

Israel's Election Leaves Both Blocs Short of a Majority — Partial Count Puts Netanyahu's Coalition Ahead but Deadlock Holds

Israel's parliamentary election has produced no clear winner, with exit polls and partial results leaving Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc and the opposition led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot both well short of the 61 seats needed to govern the 120-seat Knesset. Two exit polls put the blocs tied at 51 seats each, while a third gave Netanyahu's side a narrow 52–49 edge; partial results with just over 55% of ballot boxes counted gave the Likud-led coalition 56 seats against the opposition's 49, according to figures cited by the Times of Israel. Likud remains the largest single party, at around 25–27 seats, down from 32 in 2022, while Arab-led parties surged to roughly 17–19 seats — the night's biggest shift, driven by anger in Arab communities over record murder rates. Netanyahu, 77, called for "a strong, right-wing, national government" and stays on as caretaker prime minister, while Eisenkot said voters had delivered a vote of no confidence and opposition leader Yair Lapid called the result a "crushing defeat" for Netanyahu's bloc. Final official results are not expected until later this week, and weeks of coalition bargaining — or another election — may follow.

Latest · Africa · Sudan War28 October 2026

More Than 100 Killed in a Drone Strike on a Busy Market in Nyala, Rights Group Says — MSF Treats Over 150 Wounded

More than 100 people were killed and 113 injured when a drone struck a crowded market in Nyala, capital of Sudan's South Darfur province, on Monday evening, according to Emergency Lawyers, a Sudanese rights group tracking the war. The group blamed the Sudanese military for the strike on the market in the RSF-controlled city and called for an independent investigation, saying the attack turned a civilian marketplace into a scene of mass killing; many of the dead and wounded were women and children. Doctors Without Borders (MSF) said 36 bodies arrived at Nyala Teaching Hospital and more than 150 injured people passed through its emergency room, some brought in on tuk-tuks and donkey carts, with shrapnel wounds and severe burns. The toll reported by the rights group and the RSF-aligned administration's health minister has not been independently verified. Drone strikes by both the army and the Rapid Support Forces have escalated sharply in recent weeks, hitting hospitals, schools, markets and displacement camps; the UN calls drones the deadliest threat to civilians in a war that has left about 34 million people — nearly two in three Sudanese — needing aid. On Tuesday a separate drone attack on a transformer station in Omdurman cut power across much of Khartoum for over 12 hours.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia28 October 2026

At Least 60 Orthodox Christians Killed in Two Attacks in Ethiopia's Oromia Region, Church Says

Two attacks in Ethiopia's Oromia region killed at least 60 Orthodox Christians in the past week, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said, urging authorities to protect civilians and bring those responsible to justice. The church's Holy Synod said 14 people were killed in Merti district on 20 October and a further 46 in Shirka district on 24 October, both in the Arsi Zone; more than 20 people were seriously wounded and over 20 homes were burned, in what it called "brutal and merciless massacres." Witnesses described attackers moving through villages, and one resident told the Associated Press he had travelled to the Shirka village of Tereta Chole to attend funerals and help load the wounded onto a truck. Oromia regional government spokesperson Hailu Adugna blamed the Oromo Liberation Army and its allies, accusing them of trying to provoke ethnic and religious conflict, but presented no evidence; a source linked to a rebel alliance blamed the regional government instead. The OLA, one of seven armed groups that formed an alliance in September aimed at removing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government, did not immediately comment and has previously denied targeting civilians.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cruise Ship Stranded28 October 2026

World-Cruise Residents Put Ashore in Cambodia as Iran-War Fuel Prices Sink the Ship's Business Model

Hundreds of people who live permanently aboard a round-the-world cruise ship have been forced to disembark in Cambodia after the operator said fuel price increases driven by the Iran war had made the voyage unaffordable. The Villa Vie Odyssey had been at sea for at least 755 days as one of the few ships selling long-term or permanent residences — inside cabins from $120,000 and villa suites up to $440,000. Owner Villa Vie Residences announced on Tuesday that it was suspending operations and putting passengers ashore while it arranges temporary accommodation, saying monthly fuel costs that once averaged about $1,800 per cabin had recently reached an estimated $6,000 to $8,000 in parts of Asia, a shortfall it could not pass on to residents. A provincial official in Preah Sihanouk said 203 passengers and 156 crew were aboard; some residents have already flown out, others are waiting in the port city of Sihanoukville, and the ship is due to refuel and leave Cambodia on Saturday. Videos posted by residents showed uncertainty and disappointment, though some said they would enjoy the extra time in Southeast Asia.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine Railways28 October 2026

Ukraine's Trains Keep Rolling Through 1,700 Russian Attacks This Year — Passengers Evacuate Again and Again

Russia has attacked Ukraine's railways more than 1,700 times this year, the state operator Ukrzaliznytsia says, striking stations, locomotives and passenger wagons as new jet-powered drones that are hard to shoot down hit everything from shopping centres and filling stations to power plants. For passengers, the disruption is now routine: Kyiv has set new rules for travellers during air-raid alerts, and on a recent Dnipro-to-Odesa journey — some 400 km — riders were evacuated eight times on the way out and once more on the return, each stop lasting roughly half an hour under a black October sky. "Every third trip, we end up evacuating once, twice or even three times," said Antonina Viter, a beauty salon owner who crosses the country by rail for work. "What can you do? How else do you get to your client?" Direct attacks remain a constant threat — a drone strike on the same line killed two train drivers on 13 August — and some stations in the east and south have closed indefinitely as fighting approaches. Russia says its targets are objects used by Ukraine's military.

Latest · Business · Auto Industry28 October 2026

Volkswagen Will Take China-Developed Technology Global — as Beijing Urges Germany and the EU to Stay "Rational" on Trade

Volkswagen announced on Wednesday that technology developed at its research centre in Hefei, China — its Compact Main Platform and China Electronic Architecture, built with local partners — will feature in next-generation models for markets across the Global South, starting in South America by the end of the decade. The move marks a shift from the German carmaker's earlier "in China, for China" strategy, as it tries to turn tie-ups with firms such as Xpeng and Horizon Robotics into commercial success and revive its China sales during the largest restructuring in its 89-year history. "China is a key innovation engine for the Volkswagen Group — and increasingly a product and tech hub for our global markets," chief executive Oliver Blume said. Volkswagen also plans to ship more China-built Volkswagen and Jetta cars to South America and Africa, and unveiled VEGA, a package with 850-volt fast charging and Level 3 autonomous-driving readiness due in 2027. The announcement came as China's foreign ministry responded to Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Singapore call for wider Europe-Asia market access, urging Germany and the EU to remain "objective and rational," reduce one-sided dependencies without decoupling, and settle differences through dialogue.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab28 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab Stalls Off the Andhra Coast With No Deaths Reported — Rescues in Flooded Kakinada as Landfall Drags On

Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab remained practically stationary off the Andhra Pradesh coast on Wednesday, its centre near 16.4°N, 82.0°E — about 30 km east of Narsapur and 70 km south-southwest of Kakinada — with the landfall process expected to continue for several more hours, the India Meteorological Department said. Maximum sustained winds reached 135 km/h with gusts to 150 km/h, and the northern spiral band pushed further inland, bringing the heaviest rain to Visakhapatnam, at more than 22 cm, followed by Kalingapatnam, Kakinada and Machilipatnam. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu said no loss of life had been reported, crediting officers who worked through the night; police rescued people stranded by flooding in Kakinada, the worst-hit district, and the chief minister ordered blocked roads cleared, power restored quickly and crops protected, with about 6,000 energy personnel deployed. Around 50,000 people were moved to safer areas in Andhra Pradesh and another 48,000 in neighbouring Odisha, and disaster teams continued clearing felled trees and debris, including in Yanam, where poles were down and houses damaged.
