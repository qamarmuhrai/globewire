Latest · Americas · Junín, Peru — Magnitude 6.821 November 2026

A 6.8 Quake Deep in the Andes Sends Lima Into the Streets — and, So Far, No Deaths

A powerful earthquake struck central Peru on Friday, shaking buildings in the capital and sending residents hurrying outdoors, though its depth appears to have spared the country the worst. The magnitude 6.8 tremor hit the Andean region of Junín at a depth of 129 kilometres, according to Peru’s Geophysical Institute, with the German Research Centre for Geosciences also recording the event. In Lima, people evacuated their homes as the shaking rolled through the city, and television footage showed lawmakers filing out of Congress. There were no immediate reports of casualties or significant damage, though regional authorities in Junín released images of houses affected near the epicentral area. Peru sits on the Pacific “Ring of Fire,” where deep-focus quakes like this one are often felt across enormous distances while doing less harm at the surface than shallower tremors of similar size. Officials continued assessing remote Andean communities on Saturday.

Latest · Global Affairs · New York — The AI Trade Unwinds20 November 2026

The AI Boom Meets Its First Real Correction: the S&P 500 Falls 3.7% in a Day

The stocks that powered Wall Street’s year led it off a cliff on Friday. A bruising sell-off in artificial-intelligence shares dragged the S&P 500 down 3.7% — 265 points to 6,910 — capping its worst week in more than 18 months and leaving the index more than 10% below its 6 October record, the threshold investors call a correction and the first since March 2025. The Nasdaq composite slumped 5.3% to 22,618, slipping into negative territory for the year, and the Dow Jones shed 950 points. Chipmakers bore the brunt: Nvidia, the emblem of the AI boom, fell 13.8% for its ninth loss in ten sessions; Micron dropped 13.9%, AMD 13.3% and Broadcom 12.1%, while the semiconductor index sank 10.7%. The pain spread to the boom’s wider orbit — Corning down 10.9%, Bloom Energy 15.3% — and Anthropic, a day after its stock-market debut, tumbled 9.1%. The rout went global overnight, with Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s Kospi each falling more than 5%, as investors increasingly question whether AI valuations and the industry’s vast spending can be justified.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Into Overtime21 November 2026

COP31 Runs Past Its Deadline: Business Pleads for Ambition, Panama Threatens to Block the Deal

The climate summit that was supposed to end on Friday is still arguing on Saturday. COP31 in Antalya has slipped into overtime after a slimmed-down draft deal stripped out every reference to fossil fuels — Monday’s language on limiting their use is gone — along with the proposed $120 billion-a-year adaptation finance target for developing countries. What the presidency’s text offers instead is its “35 by 35” electrification goal, raising electricity to 35% of global final energy use by 2035, a first quantitative target for carbon capture of at least 500 million tonnes of CO₂ by 2035 that producing states favour, and a call to at least triple adaptation finance without firm new money — against developing-country needs the text itself estimates at $5.1 to $6.8 trillion by 2030. The draft would also be the first COP decision to state plainly that warming is projected to overshoot the Paris 1.5°C limit. In a late intervention, 123 investors and business groups urged delegates to agree an ambitious deal to accelerate electrification. Panama has threatened to formally oppose the draft — a serious move, since COP decisions require consensus — and vulnerable countries and campaign groups have condemned the weakening. A final text is still awaited.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — The Reset, Signed in Substance20 November 2026

Food, Carbon and 100,000 Young People: Britain and the EU Agree the Post-Brexit Reset

Britain and the European Union concluded negotiations on Friday on the most substantial rebuilding of their relationship since Brexit, at a Brussels summit between Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Council President António Costa. Three agreements anchor the package. On food, checks and certificates on animals, plants and products such as sausages, cheese and seeds will be removed, easing trade in both directions; London estimates the change could add up to £5.1 billion a year to the UK economy, though it requires Britain to align with EU food-safety rules — the point Brexit supporters attack hardest. On climate, the two sides will link their emissions-trading systems from 2028, letting allowances work across both markets and exempting goods such as steel from each other’s carbon border levies; the EU’s border mechanism took effect in January and Britain’s follows on 1 January 2027. And on people, a youth mobility scheme will let up to 100,000 under-30s from each side live in the other’s territory for up to three years to work, study or travel. The food deal is due to take effect in March 2028.

Latest · Middle East · Sanaa / Hodeidah — Claims and Counter-Claims20 November 2026

Houthis Say Saudi Strikes Killed 41 in Yemen, and Claim Hits on a Saudi Power Plant and Port

Yemen’s war flared across the Red Sea coast on Friday, in claims neither side’s account could independently confirm. Houthi-run al-Masirah TV, citing health authorities in Sanaa, reported that airstrikes attributed to Saudi Arabia killed at least 41 people — among them 11 children and seven women — and wounded 28 more, including three children, over the previous 24 hours; it gave no locations for the strikes, and Saudi authorities offered no immediate comment. The Saudi-led coalition said its aircraft had struck the Red Sea port of Hodeidah to destroy Houthi capabilities, and that two ballistic missiles were intercepted over Jazan in the kingdom’s southwest. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea said the group had retaliated against what it called attacks on Yemeni ports and power infrastructure, claiming direct hits on the Al-Shuqaiq power plant and Jazan port. Those claims, too, remained unverified. The exchange marks a sharp escalation in a conflict that has repeatedly threatened Red Sea shipping and the fragile calm between Riyadh and the Iran-aligned movement.

Latest · Middle East · Washington / Tehran — “Not Acceptable to Me”20 November 2026

Iran Says It Still Wants Diplomacy. Trump Says Tehran’s Terms Are Not Ones He Can Accept

The two capitals talked past each other on Friday about ending a war that began when the United States and Israel attacked Iran on 28 February. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi told the state-owned Iran newspaper that Tehran still seeks “an agreement that would ensure there was no further war,” wants guarantees that Washington is serious before talks proceed further, and has discussed the shape of confidence-building measures with mediators; he restated a red line — Iran’s enriched uranium stays in the country — while suggesting he believed a deal remained possible. Hours later, at a White House news conference a day after his summit with the Chinese and Russian presidents, Donald Trump gave the American version: “They want to make a deal, but they don’t want to make a deal that’s acceptable to me.” He accused Tehran of decades of bad-faith negotiating and warned of a US response to any attempt at a radioactive “dirty bomb” attack on American soil. Qatari and Iranian officials met on Tuesday in the mediation track, even as US reporting suggested Washington and Israel were weighing renewed strikes — a reminder that the diplomacy and the threats are running in parallel.

Latest · Americas · Chiapas — North Again20 November 2026

300 More Migrants Cross Into Southern Mexico, a Week After Trump’s Tariff Threat

A new group of about 300 migrants crossed into southern Mexico early on Friday, entering over the Rodolfo Robles bridge at Suchiate, in Chiapas, around dawn and setting off toward Tapachula. The travellers — reported to come from Honduras, Guatemala, Nicaragua and Venezuela — joined a larger caravan of nearly 1,500 people that formed last week and has since split into at least two columns moving through the southern communities of Arriaga and Tonalá. The movement north comes a week after President Donald Trump threatened fresh tariffs on Mexico if it did not halt the flow, putting Mexico’s government between Washington’s pressure and the people on its roads. Caravans have long formed partly for safety in numbers on a route where migrants face extortion, robbery and worse when travelling alone or in small groups. Mexican authorities have not said whether this group will be allowed to continue north.

Latest · Africa · Bayelsa, Nigeria — A Funeral Journey20 November 2026

Four Mourners, Two of Them Children, Die as an Overcrowded Boat Capsizes in Nigeria

A passenger boat carrying people to a funeral overturned in Nigeria’s southern Bayelsa State on Friday, killing at least four people, two of them children. Police said the overcrowded vessel was travelling from Ayama to Ukubie when it developed a mechanical fault and capsized at about 11 a.m. near the river junction between the Oweikorogha and Otuan communities in the Southern Ijaw district. Twenty-four people were rescued: 19 were treated at a local clinic and five taken to hospital in the state capital, Yenagoa. Police spokesman Musa Mohammed said the boat’s operator, identified only as Omovie, fled the scene, while the boat’s manager was taken into custody for investigation; the search for further victims continued. Boat disasters are a recurring tragedy on Nigeria’s inland waterways, where overloading, poorly maintained vessels and weak enforcement of safety rules combine on routes that, for many riverine communities, are the only way to travel.

Latest · Global Affairs · Barcelona — “The Closest to Cure”20 November 2026

A Daily Pill Made Tumours Vanish in One in Twelve Patients With Advanced Pancreatic Cancer

Pancreatic cancer is called a silent killer because it is usually found late and resists treatment; that is what makes a small trial presented in Barcelona this week so striking. In a phase I/II study, 61 patients with recently diagnosed advanced pancreatic cancer that had already spread were given the daily pill daraxonrasib alongside chemotherapy. The drug halted tumour growth in 90.2% of them; in 60.7%, tumours shrank or disappeared altogether — and in five patients, roughly one in twelve, scans afterwards showed no detectable tumour at all. Dr Wungki Park of Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, who presented the results at the EORTC-NCI-AACR symposium on molecular targets and cancer therapy, said he had never seen so many tumours disappear in patients whose cancer had spread, calling it “the closest to cure.” Daraxonrasib works by blocking KRAS, a gene whose mutations drive most pancreatic cancers. The researchers themselves urge caution: this is an early trial, it is not yet known how long the benefit lasts, and larger studies must confirm the results before the treatment can be considered for routine use.

Latest · Global Affairs · Low Earth Orbit — Home by Saturday Evening21 November 2026

Dragon Is on Its Way Home: Thousands of Pounds of Science, Splashdown Off California Today

The 35th SpaceX commercial resupply mission to the International Space Station is coming home. The unpiloted Dragon cargo spacecraft undocked from the space-facing port of the station’s Harmony module at 12:05 p.m. Eastern on Friday and fired its thrusters to move safely away from the orbital complex, beginning a day-long descent. In its hold are thousands of pounds of cargo, including research samples from experiments conducted in orbit that scientists hope will advance future space exploration and improve life on Earth. Dragon is due to re-enter the atmosphere and splash down under parachutes off the California coast at about 4:55 p.m. Pacific on Saturday. NASA streamed the departure live but will not broadcast the splashdown itself, posting updates instead on its space-station blog as recovery teams move to retrieve the capsule and its cargo.
