Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Brent at $94.7126 November 2026

Oil Edges Higher as Tankers Hit Mines in Hormuz and Houthis Claim Refinery Strike

Crude prices extended gains in early Asian trade on Thursday as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was disrupted again. Brent futures stood at $94.71 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate at $84.03, after Brent jumped 3.5 percent and WTI rose 2 percent on Wednesday. Yemen’s Houthis said they had struck Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery — the kingdom’s largest, and a major export terminal — along with Riyadh’s financial district, a claim analysts said would lift the geopolitical risk premium. Separately, Iranian state media reported that two tankers struck naval mines and exploded in the strait on Wednesday, while the semi-official Fars agency cited an army statement that another tanker hit a mine in a restricted zone and caught fire. Flows through Hormuz had only recently recovered toward pre-war levels. OPEC+ ministers meet on 29 November to set 2027 output policy, and US crude stocks rose 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million in the week to 20 November.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pyongyang — Fourth Nuclear Site Tour26 November 2026

Kim Jong Un Tours Nuclear Plants and Calls for Tactical Weapons Across More Missions

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un inspected nuclear material production sites and a weapons research institute on Wednesday and urged that tactical nuclear weapons be deployed across a wider range of missions, state media KCNA reported on Thursday. Kim visited a newly built weapons-grade material base, a nuclear-power equipment plant and the Nuclear Weapons Institute to check progress on the nuclear strategy set at this year’s party congress; KCNA did not name the locations. He said the country was steadily expanding its capacity to produce nuclear material and stressed that using low-yield battlefield weapons in diverse areas was urgent, citing what he called the reality on the border with the South. Photos released by KCNA showed rows of centrifuges and several warhead designs. It was Kim’s fourth reported visit to a nuclear facility, after trips in September 2024, January 2025 and June this year, and it came days after he ignored a US offer to meet during President Donald Trump’s recent Asia trip.

Latest · Americas · Miami — No Injuries, FAA Investigating25 November 2026

FedEx Cargo Jet Overruns Runway at Miami Airport on Thanksgiving Eve

A FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 cargo plane ran off Runway 26R at Miami International Airport at about 8:40 p.m. on Wednesday, forcing the closure of all but one runway on one of the busiest travel nights of the year. The airport said there were no injuries; the two crew members were unhurt and there was no fire. The Federal Aviation Administration, which will investigate, said Flight 910 had arrived from Memphis and issued a ground stop for inbound flights. FedEx said the aircraft had an issue on landing and that it was coordinating with officials. The incident came almost three months after an Amazon cargo jet overshot a different Miami runway and struck a van, killing five people on the ground. Three of Miami’s runways converge at the west end, close to where both aircraft ended up.

Latest · Americas · Washington, DC — Second Injunction Issued25 November 2026

Second US Judge Blocks Trump’s Latest Birthright Citizenship Order

A second federal judge on Wednesday barred enforcement of President Donald Trump’s August executive order narrowing birthright citizenship. District Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington, DC, granted a preliminary injunction in a class action brought on behalf of children who would be denied citizenship under the order, barring the State Department, the Department of Homeland Security and the Social Security Administration from applying it to those children. Cooper wrote that the Supreme Court had already made clear the president cannot create new exceptions for children born in the United States and subject to its laws, noting the second order followed soon after the court’s June ruling in Trump v. Barbara, which found the first effort unconstitutional by 6–3. A Maryland judge, Deborah Boardman, blocked the same order in September as almost certainly unconstitutional, so the administration would now need to overturn both rulings before it could ever take effect.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Canberra — A$740 Million Over 10 Years25 November 2026

Australia and Solomon Islands Sign Treaty Barring Foreign Bases, Cementing Canberra as Primary Partner

Australia and the Solomon Islands signed a comprehensive treaty in Canberra on Wednesday that names Australia the archipelago’s primary security partner and records Honiara’s decision to allow no foreign military or security base on its soil. Prime Ministers Anthony Albanese and Matthew Wale signed the deal during a visit by Wale and 15 ministers. Canberra will provide A$740 million (about $490 million) over ten years — A$670 million in grants and A$70 million in concessional loans — for free education, health, policing, resources and climate resilience, with funding due to start in January. The treaty also covers support for the Royal Solomon Islands Police Force and border management, protection for sensitive infrastructure including airports, ports, energy and telecoms from militarisation and foreign interference, and a requirement to consult Australia before security or critical-infrastructure deals with countries outside the Pacific Islands Forum. Australian media reported the Solomons’ 2022 security pact with China had been scrapped; Wale declined to comment, citing a non-disclosure clause.

Latest · Europe · Warsaw — Zone Planned by Early 202725 November 2026

Poland Plans Border No-Fly Zone After Russian Missile Incursions

Poland is preparing a no-fly zone along its eastern border with Ukraine to help its military spot and engage aerial threats faster, Deputy Defence Minister Cezary Tomczyk said on Wednesday. The limited zone would sit from just over a dozen to several dozen kilometres from the border, and Tomczyk wants the regulation in force by late December or early January. He urged other countries on NATO’s eastern flank to consider similar zones, noting the Baltic states have followed Poland’s lead on related measures. The move follows repeated incursions during Russian attacks on Ukraine, including debris from three Russian cruise missiles falling on Polish territory on 20 November and further airspace violations on 23 and 24 November. The zone would support a new procedure letting Polish pilots act on radar data, allied intelligence, altitude and speed rather than waiting for visual identification. Tomczyk could not confirm reports that Warsaw had asked NATO to intercept Russian missiles over Ukraine, and said the alliance does not currently support that step.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — Four-Way Meeting Sought25 November 2026

Zelenskyy Says Ukraine Is Waiting for Washington to Set Date for Next Peace Talks

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Wednesday that Kyiv is waiting for the United States to set a date for the next round of peace talks with Russia, and that the meeting should for the first time be four-sided, with European representatives at the table alongside Ukraine, Russia and the US. Zelenskyy made the remarks after meeting Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna in Kyiv, saying Ukraine had done a great deal to bring Europe fully into the US-mediated track. The previous round, in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, agreed in principle on a large prisoner exchange but made no breakthrough on territory or security guarantees; US envoy Jared Kushner said afterwards that both sides had agreed the next round would include Europeans. The head of Zelenskyy’s office, Kyrylo Budanov, has suggested a four-party working meeting could happen before Christmas, while Moscow says no decision has been taken on European participation.

Latest · Global Affairs · Trade — Hearings Set for 25 January26 November 2026

Singapore Says It Is ‘Very Disappointed’ by Proposed US Tariffs of Up to 20 Percent

Singapore on Thursday rejected US findings that underpin proposed tariffs of up to 20 percent on its exports, in the sharpest response so far to Washington’s new Section 301 action against 14 economies. The US Trade Representative published its report on 25 November (US time) after an eight-month investigation, launched in March, into structural excess capacity in manufacturing. For Singapore, the proposed rate is net of existing Section 301 duties, with exemptions for some goods, and could fall to 10 percent if Singapore reaches a reciprocal trade agreement or otherwise resolves the issues raised. Singapore’s Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry said it disagrees with the findings and is very disappointed, arguing the probe ignores the structure of its open, trade-reliant economy and noting that under the US–Singapore Free Trade Agreement Singapore charges no tariffs on US goods while Washington committed not to tariff Singaporean imports. Comments are due by 5 January 2027, with hearings from 25 January. Headline proposed rates across the 14 partners range from 20 to 40 percent.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Technology — Flagship Models Slipping26 November 2026

US Chip Curbs and Distillation Crackdown Slow China’s Next AI Models, Sources Say

New US computing restrictions and tougher safeguards against model distillation are pushing back the next generation of Chinese artificial-intelligence models, people familiar with the matter say. Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, has fallen behind schedule on its flagship GLM-5.5 — a model of roughly 3 trillion parameters planned for release this month — partly because leading US labs have made it harder to train on their models’ outputs. The company released the smaller GLM-5.4, built on a 750-billion-parameter base, on Monday, but sources say the flagship could now slip to the first quarter of 2027 or later. Beijing-based Moonshot AI, which relies heavily on rented overseas computing, expects the gap between major releases could double from six months to a year or more if that access is cut, putting its Kimi K4 model at risk of delay until around mid-2027. A Commerce Department rule that took effect on 19 November targets overseas data-centre arrangements above a threshold of roughly 750 top-end Nvidia chips, closing a route Chinese firms had used despite years of export controls.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Researcher Names MiniMax26 November 2026

Researcher Says Private Data Sent to China’s MiniMax Was Accessed by Unknown Parties

Confidential material submitted to Chinese AI developer MiniMax was accessed by unidentified parties even after users asked for it not to be stored, a cybersecurity researcher has told Reuters. HD Moore, chief research officer at US firm Dragos, identified MiniMax as the company referred to in Dragos’s 19 November investigation into AI data security, which found that private web links embedded in requests sent to AI services were later opened by unauthorised parties. The identification of MiniMax, and related findings involving Russia, are being reported for the first time. MiniMax, which listed in Hong Kong earlier this year, builds multimodal models that handle text, images and other media, along with consumer apps. The findings raise fresh questions about how AI services handle links and attachments users paste into prompts, and whether deletion requests are honoured in practice.
