Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi, Day Two11 November 2026

Abu Dhabi Talks Resume on Day Two After First Day Ends Without a Deal; Prisoner Swap Still Unconfirmed

Ukraine, Russia and the United States return to the table in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday for the second day of their first trilateral round since February, after more than four hours of talks on Tuesday closed without an official result. Ukraine’s delegation, led by Rustem Umerov with Kyrylo Budanov among its members, says the first session covered humanitarian and de-escalation steps, the substance of long-term security guarantees and prisoner releases as a priority. Ukrainian media have reported a possible large prisoner exchange, but neither side has confirmed it and no timing has been given. The US team is led by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, Russia’s by Vladimir Medinsky. Before the trilateral session, Kyiv’s negotiators also met representatives of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission, who are consulting on the sidelines rather than sitting in the three-way room. Territory — above all Moscow’s demand over the Donbas — and whether a ceasefire is formally on the agenda remain disputed, and the round comes as winter approaches and Russian strikes on Ukraine’s energy grid have resumed.

Latest · Middle East · US — Iran War and Blockade10 November 2026

Trump Calls the Iran War “Effectively Over” Militarily — While the Naval Blockade and Iron Gate Stay in Place

President Donald Trump has said the US-Israel war on Iran is “effectively over” in military terms, after meeting Qatar’s prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, in Washington. In a surprise briefing he said Iran’s military had been heavily degraded and that another major bombing campaign was not needed “at this point,” while warning the US remained ready to resume strikes if circumstances changed. The pause does not lift US pressure: Trump said Gulf partners including Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Kuwait preferred to keep the current naval blockade, which US officials say has blocked more than $25 billion in Iranian crude exports, and to press ahead with Task Force Iron Gate, announced on 7 November to restrict sanctioned goods and weapons-related trade across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea. Tehran dismissed the remarks, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio has described Iran’s own terms for ending the conflict — including lifting the blockade and sanctions relief — as unrealistic, leaving the sides in what Iran’s president has called a state of neither war nor peace.

Latest · Americas · US Economy — October Inflation10 November 2026

US Prices Rise 0.4% in October, Hotter Than Expected, and the Debate at the Fed Turns to How Big a December Hike Will Be

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, down from 0.6% in September but above the roughly 0.3% forecasters expected, the Labor Department reported on Tuesday, with core prices excluding food and energy up 0.3% for a third straight month. No annual rate was published because data collection in October last year was suspended during a government shutdown; the Cleveland Fed estimated prices were up about 3.8% over the year. The report is the last inflation reading the Federal Reserve will see before its 8–9 December meeting. Traders now largely expect at least a quarter-point rise, which would take the target range to 4% to 4.25%. Governor Christopher Waller, speaking in London before the release, said a core reading of 0.3% or more should prompt consideration of a half-point move, and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said officials may need to move faster this winter. Food prices were among the largest drivers in October, rising 0.5%.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — Trade Surplus Passes $1 Trillion10 November 2026

China’s Trade Surplus Passes $1 Trillion by October, a Month Earlier Than Last Year, on Surging AI Hardware Exports

China’s cumulative trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, customs data showed on Tuesday — past $1 trillion for a second year running and a month earlier than in 2025. Exports rose 35.2% in October from a year earlier to $412.3 billion, while imports jumped 41.6% to $307.1 billion, the fastest import growth since 2021, leaving a monthly surplus of $105.3 billion, down from $133.5 billion in September. Officials and analysts point to global demand for artificial-intelligence hardware as a central driver: integrated-circuit exports more than doubled in value over January to October, and shipments of computers and parts, including servers, rose sharply. Exports to the United States rose 32.9% in October, and China’s ten-month exports already exceed its total for all of 2025. The figures land a week before an expected Trump–Xi meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, and economists say the scale of the surplus could renew friction with trading partners even as imports also surge.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Leaders’ Summit, Antalya11 November 2026

World Leaders Gather in Antalya for COP31’s High-Level Summit Under a First-Ever Shared Presidency

Heads of state and government are gathering in Antalya on Wednesday and Thursday for the World Leaders’ Summit of COP31, the UN climate conference running from 9 to 20 November at the Antalya Expo Center. The summit operates under an arrangement never used before in three decades of climate talks: Türkiye hosts and holds the presidency through Environment Minister Murat Kurum, while Australia’s climate minister Chris Bowen serves as President of Negotiations, a split agreed after rival hosting bids. Organisers bill this as an “implementation COP” — focused on delivering existing national plans rather than adding new headline pledges — with food security, climate finance, clean energy, resilient cities, oceans and methane among the stated priorities. Nearly 50 parties have still not filed updated national climate plans. An agriculture and food-system forum met on the sidelines on Tuesday, and negotiators are also aiming for an Antalya Ocean Declaration before the conference closes on 20 November.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Koreas — DMZ Mine Blast Row10 November 2026

North Korea Rejects the UN Chief’s Call for Dialogue and Accuses Him of Taking South Korea’s Side Over the DMZ Mine Blast

North Korea on Tuesday rejected UN Secretary-General António Guterres’s call to resume inter-Korean dialogue after a landmine explosion in the Demilitarised Zone, with Kim Yo-jong, sister of leader Kim Jong-un, calling his stance “prejudiced and irresponsible” in a statement carried by state media KCNA. Guterres had urged both Koreas to restore communication, including at military level, to prevent unintended incidents. A joint investigation by South Korea’s military and the UN Command concluded antipersonnel mines deliberately planted by the North south of the Military Demarcation Line caused the explosion. Pyongyang denies responsibility, says its troops were fortifying the border, and dismisses Seoul’s findings. South Korea raised the case with the UN Security Council on 22 October, asking for an apology, removal of the mines and steps to prevent a repeat.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Cancer Drug Prices Fall9 November 2026

Cancer Drug Prices Fall by Up to 96% in India as a 30% Cap on Trade Margins Takes Effect for 1,364 Formulations

Revised prices for 1,364 anti-cancer formulations took effect across India on 9 November after the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority capped trade margins at 30% of the maximum retail price for 137 anti-cancer medicines, including patented ones. The government estimates annual savings of about ₹3,540 crore for patients. By the latest official count, 85 companies had reported new prices covering 121 drugs; 679 brands fell by more than 50% and the steepest single cut was 96.45%. The medicines span breast, lung, prostate, ovarian and blood cancers, and include widely used drugs such as bevacizumab, abiraterone and palbociclib, some of whose brand prices fell from tens of thousands of rupees to a few thousand. Patient advocates note the cap covers drug prices only, not the wider cost of treatment, and that prices for about 200 brands were unchanged. Patients can check revised prices on the Pharma Sahi Daam app and the NPPA website.

Latest · Europe / Middle East · France — Iran Fridge Cyberattack10 November 2026

France Blames Iran’s Revolutionary Guards for a Fridge Cyberattack on 700 Supermarkets; Tehran Calls It “Baseless”

France has formally accused Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps of sabotaging supermarket refrigeration across the country last month, and Iran on Tuesday rejected the charge as baseless and hostile. Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called the 11–12 October operation an attack of unprecedented scale — the first large-scale cyberattack carried out purely for sabotage on French territory — in which hackers remotely triggered abnormal defrost cycles at about 700 stores, spoiling perishable food at a cost estimated in the tens of millions of euros. Paris says investigators established with certainty that the IRGC’s Shahid Kaveh cyber unit was responsible, citing Farsi use and IP addresses pointing to devices in Iran; the Paris prosecutor has opened an investigation into the unit’s head and two deputies. France summoned Iran’s ambassador and says it will seek retaliatory sanctions at European level. Similar attacks hit supermarkets in Sweden, Belgium and Ireland earlier this month, though Paris has not attributed that second wave.

G7 Foreign Ministers Meet in Paris After a Last-Minute Move From Marseille, With Ukraine, Iran and Navigation on the Agenda

Foreign ministers from the G7 — Britain, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and the United States, with the EU also taking part — met in Paris on Monday and Tuesday for their second ministerial under France’s 2026 presidency, joined by partners including Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea, Kenya, Brazil and Ukraine. The meeting had been scheduled for Marseille but was moved to the capital at short notice, with France citing logistical and operational reasons linked to the compressed timetable with the Paris Peace Forum; diplomats noted the shift followed six weeks of nationwide protests, including violent clashes in Marseille on 17 October. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio attended on his first trip abroad since the 3 November midterm elections. The French hosts set an agenda covering Ukraine, Iran, freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, the wider Middle East, Africa, the Indo-Pacific, China and Latin America, as allies test how closely they still align with Washington.
