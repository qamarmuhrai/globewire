Latest · Middle East · Iran — Trump Calls New Strikes Unlikely9 November 2026

“Highly Unlikely”: Trump Steps Back From New Iran Attacks — and Oil Slides

Oil prices fell at Monday’s open after President Donald Trump said he was highly unlikely to order a fresh wave of attacks on Iran while talks to end the conflict keep moving. Brent crude dropped about 0.6–0.9 per cent to around $97.50–$97.70 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate eased to about $90.51, extending a slide that saw Brent lose some 17 per cent last week — its steepest weekly fall since the start of the COVID pandemic. “It depends on whether or not they sign a very strong agreement,” Trump told CNN in a phone interview. “The answer is, highly unlikely it’s going to be necessary.” He had previously hinted that major attacks could resume after last week’s US midterm elections, but no large-scale operation has materialised, even as both sides reported shipping hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend. Iran has warned it would answer any new strikes by hitting US and Israeli targets. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Sunday the war, “not necessarily popular”, had been launched to stop Iran’s nuclear ambitions.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Aramco Tower Hit by Debris8 November 2026

Windows Blown Out in Riyadh: Houthi Barrage Damages the Aramco Tower

Saudi Arabia’s capital was targeted on Sunday in one of the most visible Houthi attacks of the war. The Energy Ministry said an administrative building belonging to state oil giant Saudi Aramco in the King Abdullah Financial District was subjected to an attempted attack, with falling shrapnel causing limited damage and no injuries or deaths. A Reuters witness saw windows blown out across several floors of the Aramco Tower, one of the tallest buildings in the financial district, and other witnesses described shattered glass and damage to the facade. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched towards Riyadh, and later said cruise missiles and a drone were also intercepted over the capital, the Eastern Province and the southern region; it said it had destroyed Houthi launchers in Saada and Sanaa. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed a series of missile and drone operations against Saudi military positions, Aramco facilities and Riyadh airport. Fighting between Riyadh and the Iran-aligned Houthis resumed in July, ending a UN-brokered truce that had largely held since 2022.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Storms Flood the Tent Camps8 November 2026

Four Children Among Five Killed as Floods Tear Through Gaza’s Shelters

The first heavy rains of the season have turned Gaza’s displacement camps into a disaster zone. Local health authorities said at least five people, including four children, were killed on Sunday when a sandbank collapsed onto tents in Deir el-Balah in central Gaza, and several injured people were pulled from the wreckage. The Hamas-run government media office said about 70,000 tents sheltering tens of thousands of displaced people were flooded on the first day of rain alone, with water rising 300–400mm in several camps and dozens of weakened walls and roofs collapsing. Civil Defence spokesman Mahmoud Bassal said his teams took more than 1,500 emergency calls overnight and were operating “without any equipment or capabilities”. UNICEF estimated on Sunday that more than 100,000 people had been forced to seek other shelter after over 19,000 family tents flooded, and doctors warned of a surge in meningitis and other infectious disease. Gaza City emergency official Mustafa Qazzaat told AFP the territory urgently needs 200,000 prefabricated homes, plus heavy machinery, electricity and heating; the UN says some 950,000 displaced people are living in flood-exposed areas.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens in Antalya9 November 2026

An “Implementation COP” Begins: 200 Delegations, Two Presidents, One Funding Gap

The COP31 climate summit opened on Monday at the Antalya Expo Center in Türkiye, running to 20 November across a Blue Zone for official negotiations and a Green Zone for the public, together covering about 1.1 million square metres. In a first for the 30-year history of the talks, the conference has two leaders: Türkiye’s environment minister Murat Kurum holds the presidency, while Australia’s climate and energy minister Chris Bowen presides over the negotiations — an arrangement settled after rival hosting bids at COP30 in Belém. Organisers bill this as an “implementation COP”, focused on turning past pledges into bankable projects rather than signing a new landmark treaty. A World Leaders’ Summit follows on 11–12 November. The talks open under the shadow of the UN Environment Programme’s 2026 Adaptation Gap Report, published on 4 November, which warns that finance to help vulnerable countries cope with floods, drought and extreme heat remains far below what is needed, and of an Emissions Gap assessment pointing to roughly 2.6–2.8°C of warming on current paths. Food security, electrification, oceans and methane are among the stated priorities; the United States, which has moved to leave the UN climate treaty, is the major economy absent.

Latest · Europe · Poland — A Nation in Mourning8 November 2026

The Trumpet Call Falls Silent: Poland Mourns Six Killed in Kraków’s Main Square

Poland began two days of national mourning on Sunday for the six people killed when a gunman opened fire in Kraków’s historic Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon. Police say the 24-year-old Polish attacker drove into the square at about 3 p.m. and shot randomly at crowds in Poland’s second city, a UNESCO-listed tourist centre, killing four women and two men — among them a British citizen — and wounding 15 to 17 people, including two police officers who confronted him and were praised by Prime Minister Donald Tusk as heroes who likely saved many lives. The suspect, shot by police, remains in critical condition. Investigators later found three more bodies at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 200 kilometres north of Kraków: regional police said they were his parents and his sister. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the evidence points to “very, very radical” far-right and ultranationalist views, and the National Prosecutor’s Office has opened a terrorism investigation, though opposition politicians have questioned the early attribution of motive. In a gesture not seen since 1939, the hourly trumpet call from St Mary’s tower on the square was suspended for 12 hours overnight in memory of the dead.

Latest · Africa · Egypt — Seven Dead in Giza Train Collision8 November 2026

Rear-End Collision at Al-Ayyat: Two Trains Crash South of Cairo

At least seven people were killed and more than 110 injured on Sunday when two passenger trains collided in the Al-Ayyat district of Giza, south of Cairo. Egypt’s railway authority said a Giza-to-Fayoum service ran into the rear of a Cairo-to-Aswan train that had made an unscheduled stop between Kafr Ammar and Al-Raqqa stations at about 1.30 p.m., derailing and overturning several carriages onto a countryside road. The Health Ministry put the injured at 112, with prosecutors giving a preliminary figure of 114; the dead included the driver of the Fayoum-bound train. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said he was following the accident “with great concern” and ordered an urgent investigation to hold accountable anyone found negligent. Al-Ayyat is grimly familiar ground: it was the site of Egypt’s worst-ever rail disaster, and the country’s railways — the oldest in Africa and the Middle East — have a long record of deadly crashes blamed on human error and signalling failures.

Latest · Americas · Argentina — The Pope’s First Speech8 November 2026

“The Economy Must Serve the Person”: Leo Takes His Message to Milei’s Argentina

Pope Leo XIV used his first public address in Argentina on Sunday to deliver a pointed, courteous challenge to President Javier Milei’s austerity programme. “The economy must serve the person, not the person the economy,” the 71-year-old pontiff told officials, diplomats and civic leaders at Buenos Aires’ Palacio Libertad, urging policies that reach “the unemployed, retirees and all those who, despite hard work, nonetheless struggle to meet their household’s needs”, and calling for particular concern for people affected by illness, dependency and disability — widely read as a reference to cuts in disability assistance. Milei, who welcomed the pope at Jorge Newbery airport with his sister Karina, later responded that debate in Argentina lacked intellectual honesty and defended his drive for fiscal balance as protecting tomorrow’s Argentines. His programme has cut annual inflation from over 200 per cent to roughly 32–33 per cent and produced a rare budget surplus, but real wages remain below their level when he took office and the poverty rate stood at 32.3 per cent in September. The visit, the first by a pope to Argentina since 1987, is the second leg of a 12-day, three-country South American tour; Leo will visit a prison and a disability care centre, celebrate open-air Masses, and travel on to Peru on Wednesday.

Nvidia Investigates Whether Its CEO Was the Target of a Foreign Spy Operation

Nvidia’s board announced on Sunday an independent investigation into whether founder and chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, after he acknowledged an extramarital relationship with a woman US officials concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. The inquiry will be run by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel. “While the board has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, the board’s inquiry will examine all available information,” the company said, adding that it has confidence in Huang, who will remain in post. The New York Times reported last month that Alice Huang — no relation — a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed, which bought some $4.8 billion of Nvidia equipment, had cultivated ties with Huang to glean information about the chipmaker’s technology; US authorities have also scrutinised Megaspeed over suspicions it helped route export-controlled AI chips to China. In an email to staff on Sunday, Huang admitted he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologised to his family and employees. China’s embassy in Washington has denied the spying allegations, President Trump called Huang “a fine person”, and Republican House lawmakers have asked him to testify before Congress on 18 November.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — 5.7 Quake in the Night8 November 2026

Shattered Windows, 22 Hurt: Central Japan Braces for a Week of Aftershocks

A strong earthquake shook central Japan at 1.04 a.m. on Sunday, injuring at least 22 people, shattering shop windows and disrupting rail services across a wide area. The Japan Meteorological Agency measured the quake at magnitude 5.7 — the US Geological Survey said 5.5 — with its centre about 40 kilometres beneath western Shizuoka Prefecture and shaking reaching upper 5 on Japan’s seven-level intensity scale in parts of neighbouring Aichi. The tremor was felt from Hamamatsu to the old capitals of Kyoto and Nara, and in Yamanashi, home to Mount Fuji, and Gifu. Most injuries were minor — falls, head knocks and a broken ankle in Tokoname — and there were no deaths and no tsunami warning. Agency official Ayataka Ebita warned residents to stay alert for further strong quakes over the coming week, especially the next two to three days, and to watch for mudslides and falling rocks where rain has loosened the ground.

Latest · Middle East · Iraq — Drone Strike Halts Khor Mor9 November 2026

One Drone, 2.8 Gigawatts Gone: Iraq’s Biggest Kurdish Gas Field Shuts Down

Production was suspended on Sunday at Khor Mor, one of the largest gas fields in Iraq’s Kurdistan region, after a single drone struck its LPG storage tank and started a fire, two sources at the region’s electricity ministry told Reuters. The shutdown cut about 2.8 gigawatts of power generation across the Kurdish region, which depends on Khor Mor gas for much of its electricity. No group immediately claimed responsibility for the strike, the latest in a series of attacks on energy infrastructure in the region that officials have often attributed to Iran-aligned militias. The halt came as oil markets were already on edge over the Iran war and the weekend’s attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz — and it underlined how exposed the region’s energy system remains even as diplomacy inches forward.
