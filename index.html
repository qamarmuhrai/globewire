Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Polls Closing4 November 2026

Polls Begin Closing on a Judgement Day for Trump — and the Count Could Run for Days

America’s midterm vote is ending the way election officials hoped and feared in equal measure: mostly smooth, occasionally chaotic, and nowhere near decided. More than 57 million people voted early — a midterm record pace — and Election Day itself brought scattered failures rather than systemic ones. Ballot scanners failed across Westchester County, New York, home to one of the country’s most watched House races, Republican Mike Lawler against Democrat Cait Conley; officials retracted an early explanation about paper thickness before blaming the malfunction on another cause, while the state attorney general monitored. Heavy rain and flash flooding — 10 to 20 inches in places over the preceding day — closed or disrupted polling sites in Florida and across the Southwest. Overhead hung the politics of suspicion: the Justice Department deployed roughly 1,000 monitors, its largest such operation ever, after a campaign season in which President Trump repeatedly alleged fraud without evidence; on Monday the Supreme Court rejected his administration’s bid to force California to set aside thousands of ballots flagged as possibly cast by noncitizens, and asked whether he would accept the results, Trump answered, “I will if they are honest.” The stakes are total: all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships. Republicans, who start from 218 House seats and a 53–47 Senate edge, spent 88.1% of their television advertising in states and districts Trump won in 2024 — a defensive map — and were braced to lose the House while fighting to hold the Senate. Forecasters leaned Democratic in both chambers, but the first polls closed only at 6pm Eastern in Indiana and Kentucky, the last close at 1am Eastern in Alaska, and officials warn control of either chamber may not be known for days.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — The Seven Conditions3 November 2026

Tehran Names Its Price: Hormuz Stays Shut Until Washington Meets All Seven Conditions

The diplomatic opening that knocked 7.6% off oil prices on Monday met its price tag on Tuesday. Mohsen Rezaee, the Supreme Leader’s representative on Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, posted that Washington’s response to Tehran’s demands “was not convincing” and that the Strait of Hormuz will reopen only when the United States “fully and explicitly” addresses all seven Iranian conditions — an end to the war, the naval blockade lifted, sanctions eased and frozen assets released among them. The hard line sits awkwardly beside President Masoud Pezeshkian’s statement a day earlier that Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei had granted his government greater authority to negotiate, and beside President Trump’s hints that a deal “could happen” — though when asked whether strikes on Iran would resume after the midterms, Trump shook his head and said “no.” Markets split the difference: Brent, which settled at $104.77 after Monday’s plunge, dipped toward $103 after the Qeshm Island strike before recovering to around $103.50 on Rezaee’s comments, with US crude near $96. Qatar and Pakistan are mediating, and Qatari officials say they remain hopeful. The longer game is darker: Goldman Sachs has lifted its December Brent forecast to $105 and warned prices could reach $140 a barrel in 2027 if Gulf production stays far below its 2025 average — a reminder that nine months into the war, the strait that once carried a fifth of the world’s oil and gas remains the world’s most consequential closed door.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Deadlock Certified4 November 2026

53 Seats, 48 Seats, 19 Kingmakers: Israel’s Deadlock Hardens as the Result Is Certified

Israel’s election results are due to be certified on Wednesday, and certification changes nothing about the arithmetic: Benjamin Netanyahu’s right-wing bloc holds 53 seats from the 27 October vote, Gadi Eisenkot’s opposition alliance holds 48, and the 61-seat majority in the 120-member Knesset runs through the 19 seats won by the two Arab-led lists — the Joint List and Ra’am — in their best-ever performance, powered by record Arab turnout in an election that drew 77.7% of all voters, the highest in two decades. Neither camp will say so plainly. Netanyahu has ruled out any reliance on Arab parties and his camp has promoted largely discounted claims that their vote was manipulated by foreign interference. The opposition leaders met on Tuesday and announced they were preparing to “act immediately” to replace his coalition “with a national emergency government” — without explaining how they reach 61 without either the Arab lists or the ultra-Orthodox parties that have historically backed Netanyahu in exchange for shielding their members from conscription. “Essentially, we’re both on track to a government, and nowhere near being able to form one,” pollster Mitchell Barak told Al Jazeera, and Chatham House analyst Yossi Mekelberg was blunter: “Netanyahu cannot form a coalition. It’s a numbers game, and he just doesn’t have the numbers.” President Isaac Herzog will consult the factions once the results are official and hand someone a mandate; Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister meanwhile. Hovering over the talks is Washington — analysts note the whole process could be knocked sideways by America’s midterm results, or by a renewed war with Iran.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Brussels Day4 November 2026

Brussels Sets the Table Before Abu Dhabi — While Rescuers Still Dig in Komyshuvakha

The diplomacy of the Ukraine war moves to Brussels on Wednesday: the European Commission hosts the United States, Ukraine, France, Germany, Britain, NATO and the World Bank — at national-security-adviser level — to work on security guarantees and a post-war “prosperity plan” for reconstruction, building on earlier talks in Miami and setting up next week’s US-mediated Ukraine–Russia meeting in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, in Berlin on Tuesday beside Chancellor Friedrich Merz, confirmed Ukrainian teams would attend, and set out Kyiv’s Abu Dhabi agenda: an energy ceasefire, unblocking the Black Sea, prisoner exchanges and preparing a leaders’ summit, alongside the guarantees and recovery package Washington wants to discuss. The war supplied its own counter-briefing. Rescue work continued at Komyshuvakha, where Russian bombs hit a school serving as an aid distribution point on Monday: the Zaporizhzhia regional administration put confirmed deaths at 31, with nearly 50 people still missing — Zelenskyy told reporters in Berlin the toll had reached 33 — after a second strike hit the shelter where survivors had gathered. In the south, fighting and shelling resumed around Kherson after a brief local ceasefire, negotiated to evacuate the occupied town of Oleshky, expired. And the conflict acquired a digital front line: Kyiv says it hacked Russia’s emergency alert system in retaliation for a 1 November Russian cyberattack that pushed fake nuclear-threat warnings to Ukrainian users — a claim Zelenskyy confirmed. Merz has called the Coalition of the Willing’s leaders to Berlin on 19 November to fix support for Ukraine through 2027; what Brussels agrees today decides what there is to support.

Latest · Africa · Ebola — 5,376 Dead3 November 2026

5,376 Dead and Still Spreading: Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Has Crossed Borders With No Vaccine to Stop It

The numbers now have a shape, and the shape is grim. The Africa CDC’s latest count puts deaths from the Ebola outbreak at 5,376 — 5,372 of them in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and two each in Kenya and Uganda — with some 10,444 people infected across the three countries and a fatality rate above 50%. Congo’s outbreak, declared in mid-May, is the country’s 17th and by far its largest, and the second-deadliest in Ebola’s recorded history, behind only the 2014–16 West African epidemic that killed more than 11,000. It is driven by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine and no specific treatment. Congolese health-ministry data show the virus concentrated in the conflict-torn east — Ituri and North Kivu account for 9,782 confirmed cases and 4,792 deaths between them — and present in 67 of the country’s 178 health zones across eight of its 26 provinces; about 2,800 people have recovered, and roughly 980 were in hospital or isolation in the latest national bulletin, which counted 45 deaths and 53 new infections in a single day. The spread beyond Congo is being managed case by case: two suspected cases in Nigeria have tested negative, but Saudi Arabia has suspended travel to and from Kenya and barred entry to anyone who has been in an affected country within the previous 21 days. Health officials warn the outbreak is killing faster than past epidemics, in a region where armed conflict, delayed detection and exhausted clinics did the virus’s early work for it.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — The Morning After4 November 2026

No Deaths, 613 Generators and a 48-Hour Deadline: Andhra Pradesh Counts Murasu’s Damage

Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu crossed the Andhra Pradesh coast near Ongole between 3:30 and 5:30 on Tuesday morning with sustained winds of 100–110 km/h gusting to 120 — and by the standards of Bay of Bengal landfalls, the state got off lightly. The disaster management authority reported no deaths or injuries as of Tuesday morning; hundreds of trees came down across Nellore, Prakasam and Bapatla districts, but damage to the power network was less severe than feared. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu has ordered restoration completed within 48 hours, backed by 613 generators and 185 fire-service teams clearing uprooted trees and waterlogged roads. The storm itself is spent: it weakened to a cyclonic storm by Tuesday afternoon as it moved north-northwest past Bapatla, was expected to fade to a deep depression by midnight and to a well-marked low-pressure area by Wednesday morning — though the India Meteorological Department warns moisture over coastal Andhra, eastern Rayalaseema and southern Telangana could wring heavy rain from its remains, and red alerts stayed in force for Krishna, Guntur, Bapatla, Palnadu, NTR and Prakasam districts. Kothapatnam recorded 74.7mm of rain in the landfall hours. Fishermen were told to stay ashore until Wednesday morning, and offshore operations remained suspended pending review — a cautious end to a storm that arrived at full strength and, unusually, left the casualty column empty.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Day Five4 November 2026

The Wage Board Has Not Met, the Owners Threaten “No Work, No Pay” — and Now the Brands Are Being Asked to Choose

Bangladesh’s garment protests roll toward a fifth day with every formal channel still untested. The six-member wage board announced on 1 November — chaired by Senior District and Sessions Judge Sheikh Ashfaqur Rahman, with workers’ representative Babul Akhter of the Garment and Industrial Workers Federation among its members — had yet to hold its first meeting as Tuesday’s clashes closed nearly 1,000 factories and the government accused deposed prime minister Sheikh Hasina of fuelling the unrest. The industry association BGMEA has threatened to invoke “no work, no pay” if stoppages continue, while Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed conceded simply that “some factories are closed and workers have taken to the streets.” Into that standoff stepped the buyers: on Tuesday the Clean Clothes Campaign urged every brand sourcing from Bangladesh — the world’s second-largest apparel exporter after China — to publicly back a minimum wage of 32,000 taka and an end to blacklisting and union-busting, going beyond even the workers’ headline demand of 30,000 taka (about $245) against the current 12,500 taka, frozen since December 2023 in an economy running near 8% inflation. In Dhaka’s Mirpur district, ruling-party supporters patrolled alongside the protests, a sign of how quickly a wage dispute is becoming a political contest. The arithmetic that started it has not changed: four million workers, more than 80% of national export earnings, and pay that sewing operators say leaves some families eating puffed rice on bad days.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Voting Day4 November 2026

A Public Holiday and a Turnout Problem: South Africa Chooses the Government Closest to Home

South Africans vote on Wednesday in local government elections that will decide who runs their municipalities — water, power, roads and refuse — for the next five years. The day is a public holiday; stations open from 7am to 9pm, and anyone in the queue by closing time is legally entitled to vote. Voters in the eight metropolitan councils receive two ballots, those elsewhere three, after two days of special voting for the infirm, disabled and others approved in advance. The Electoral Commission runs the poll under a cloud it did not create in 2021: turnout fell from 57.94% in 2016 to 45.87% five years later, and the commission has logged more than 700 complaints about election-related disinformation during this campaign. President Cyril Ramaphosa used his weekly newsletter to plead for participation and a peaceful poll, party leaders spent Tuesday in final get-out-the-vote rallies, and the local government association urged voters to weigh “integrity, competence and commitment” — language that lands heavily in towns where municipal failure is a daily experience. The vote also arrives in a bruising week for the country’s external position, days after Washington confirmed South Africa will lose duty-free AGOA access from January. Results will trickle in from tonight; the bigger question pollsters are watching is whether the long slide in participation finally bottoms out.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indian Ocean — The Azini Rescue3 November 2026

Commandos Out of the Sky, 3,000 Kilometres From Home: How India Took Back the Azini

It began with a warning: the Iranian fishing vessel Azini, seized by 12 armed Somali pirates on 29 October between Bandar Bayla and Hafun, could be turned into a “mothership” — a mobile base for raids across one of the world’s busiest shipping lanes, as the EU-backed Maritime Security Centre for the Indian Ocean cautioned. India’s answer unfolded over more than 48 hours and 3,000 kilometres from Mumbai: a mission-deployed warship and a maritime reconnaissance aircraft tracked the dhow, an Air Force C-17 executed a precision airdrop of marine commandos, and the commandos stormed and secured the vessel on Monday, freeing all 20 crew with, India’s defence ministry said, no casualties or injuries. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh publicly congratulated the Navy, and Iran’s embassy in New Delhi issued formal thanks for the rescue of its nationals. The accounts diverge at the edges — a Puntland maritime police officer had earlier counted 21 Iranian crew and two Somali security guards aboard, and told AP the pirates were disarmed, had their phones confiscated and were released, coming ashore at Bandar Bayla with three of the group nursing minor injuries; the discrepancy in the crew count has not been explained. The operation extends a pattern: in March 2024 the Indian Navy retook the merchant ship MV Ruen some 2,600 kilometres offshore and captured 35 pirates. As traffic returns to the western Indian Ocean, New Delhi is advertising itself as the region’s first responder.

Latest · Global Affairs · Sport — Maxwell Retires3 November 2026

The Big Show Closes: Glenn Maxwell Walks Away Without the Farewell He Hoped For

Glenn Maxwell announced his retirement from international cricket on Tuesday, ending one of the most watchable careers of the modern game — and doing so on a podcast rather than at the Melbourne Cricket Ground send-off he admitted he had quietly hoped for. The 38-year-old was overlooked again when Australia named its Twenty20 squad to face England on Monday, selectors preferring youth with an eye on the 2028 Olympics cycle, and he read the signal clearly: “A little bit sad and a little bit inevitable too,” he told The Final Word, adding that he needs to invest in his body for the tournaments he has left. The record is substantial: 286 internationals — 149 one-day matches, 130 T20s and seven Tests — more than 7,000 runs and over 130 wickets, two ODI World Cups (2015 and 2023), the winning runs in the 2021 T20 World Cup final, five T20 international centuries (a mark he shares only with Rohit Sharma), and a T20 strike rate of 154.42. His defining innings remains the 201 not out from 128 balls against Afghanistan at the 2023 World Cup, dragged from 91 for 7 while he could barely stand. Maxwell had already quit one-day internationals in June last year; he now also ends his first-class and List A career with Victoria, but will play on for the Melbourne Stars in the Big Bash League, with his contract extended through 2027–28. His last game for Australia was against Oman at the T20 World Cup in February — two wickets, not required to bat. Few farewells are that quiet; fewer careers were that loud.

Latest · Europe · Diplomacy — The Circuit4 November 2026

Bratislava Hosts the Visegrad Four, Brussels Hosts Everyone Else: Europe’s Other Wednesday

While the European Commission runs the Ukraine meeting in Brussels, the continent’s diplomatic circuit turns on two other tracks today. In Bratislava, Slovakia hosts the presidents of the Visegrad Four — Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Poland — for a two-day summit that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen will also attend, a gathering that puts the bloc’s internal divides over Ukraine, energy and EU budgets in one room the same week its members are being asked to underwrite Kyiv’s security. Across Brussels, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte receives North Macedonia’s President Gordana Siljanovska Davkova at alliance headquarters, with no media opportunity scheduled — a courtesy call that nonetheless underlines the alliance’s steady attention to the Western Balkans. The circuit extends east: Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s summit visit to Seoul with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung concludes today, and Timor-Leste’s President José Ramos-Horta arrives in the South Korean capital on Thursday for a two-day summit of his own. None of it will outrank an American election count or a Brussels guarantees paper. But the density of the calendar — guarantees in Brussels, presidents in Bratislava, summits in Seoul — is itself the story of a week in which the post-war architecture of two continents is being sketched at once.
