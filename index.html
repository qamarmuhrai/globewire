Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Toll Rises8 November 2026

Six Dead in the Square, Three More at Home: Kraków Learns the Full Scale Overnight

The toll from Saturday’s attack in Kraków has risen, and the investigation has widened far beyond the Main Market Square. Officials now put the dead in the square at six — four women and two men, among them a British national and three Polish citizens — with 16 people wounded, including two police officers and five foreign nationals from Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia and Britain. The suspect, a 25-year-old Polish man who drove onto the square at about 3 p.m. and fired at random with an automatic weapon while dressed in military-style clothing, was shot by police and is in hospital after surgery. Late on Saturday, Prime Minister Donald Tusk said searchers had found three further bodies — two women and a man — at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, roughly 200 kilometres away, with strong indications they were close family members. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the evidence points to a man drawn to far-right ideology who held very radical, ultra-nationalist views, and the defence minister described the shooting as an act of terror, while stressing the gunman was never a soldier. Kraków has declared mourning until 11 November, with national mourning on 8 and 9 November. It is described as Poland’s deadliest shooting since the fall of communism in 1989.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Earthquake8 November 2026

1:04 a.m., Upper 5 in Chita: Central Japan’s Night Quake Injures 22

Central Japan was jolted awake in the early hours of Sunday by an earthquake that the Japan Meteorological Agency measured at magnitude 5.7, centred in western Shizuoka Prefecture about 40 kilometres underground. The US Geological Survey put it slightly lower, at 5.5. The strongest shaking, an upper 5 on Japan’s seven-point intensity scale, was felt in Chita in Aichi Prefecture — the first tremor of that strength recorded in Aichi since March 1997 — with lower 5 shaking across parts of Aichi, Nagano and Gifu. At least 22 people were hurt in Aichi and Shizuoka as residents rushed from buildings; shop windows shattered and goods were scattered. The tremor was felt widely, from Hamamatsu to the old capitals of Kyoto and Nara and toward Yamanashi, home to Mount Fuji. No tsunami warning was issued. The agency has warned households in the hardest-shaken districts to stay alert through the coming week, especially the next two to three days, when another strong tremor is most likely, and to watch for rockfalls and landslides if rain follows.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Blasts and Blockade8 November 2026

Blasts on Qeshm, Tankers on Fire, a Freighter Disabled: Hormuz Has a Dangerous Night

The Strait of Hormuz produced three separate alarms in a matter of hours. Iranian state television reported explosions on Saturday evening on Qeshm Island and in the coastal cities of Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas; the semi-official Tasnim agency first said two missiles had hit Qeshm, then cited a source saying the blasts there were not missile strikes, and no authority has confirmed a cause or a damage toll. Early on Sunday, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said two oil tankers had caught fire after striking mines while using what it called an unauthorised route through the strait. In the Gulf of Oman, meanwhile, US Central Command said an American aircraft had fired precision munitions into the stern of the St Kitts-flagged M/V Huda on Saturday after its crew ignored repeated warnings and tried to leave the area following a call at an Iranian port; the crew had first been ordered out of the targeted spaces. CENTCOM also announced a new outfit, Task Force Iron Gate, to choke off Iran’s overland and Caspian supply routes, and put the value of crude exports blocked by its months-long naval cordon at more than $25 billion. Tehran has not formally explained the explosions, leaving shipping to navigate claims as much as water.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Mandate Day8 November 2026

Mandate Day in Jerusalem: Eisenkot Gets 28 Days and a Coalition That Barely Exists

Sunday is the formal handover: President Isaac Herzog is to task former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, 66, with forming Israel’s next government after an inconclusive 27 October election. Eisenkot, who leads the Yashar party, drew recommendations from factions holding 67 of the Knesset’s 120 seats, against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — the widest backing for a first nominee since 2015 — but recommendations are not a coalition. Likud remains the largest single party with 27 seats and Netanyahu’s religious-nationalist bloc holds 53; Eisenkot’s own Jewish-led grouping holds only 48, and the Arab-led parties whose endorsements lifted him over the line are partners he has so far refused to govern with. He now has 28 days, extendable by 14, to find 61 seats; failure hands the attempt to Netanyahu, and failure there points to another election in roughly 100 days. Eisenkot, who would be Israel’s first prime minister from the Mizrahi community, has attacked settler violence and ruled out Netanyahu’s far-right partners. His first parliamentary hurdle arrives on Tuesday, when the new Knesset is sworn in and chooses a speaker.

Latest · Middle East · Syria–Yemen — Deployment Dispute7 November 2026

3,000 Troops, Says Aden. “Unfounded,” Says Damascus: the Syria-to-Yemen Story Splits in Two

Two versions of the same deal are now on the record. Yemeni officials told AFP that Syria has agreed to send about 3,000 troops in a first phase — potentially more than 10,000 in total — to help Saudi Arabia fight the Houthis, with hundreds already positioned on both sides of the Saudi-Yemeni border and some running drone operations. The arrangement, they said, was settled during President Ahmed al-Sharaa’s visit to Riyadh this week, when the two governments signed cooperation agreements including a defence deal, and Syria weighed joining the mutual-defence pact Saudi Arabia struck in August with Turkey and Pakistan in Mecca. Damascus tells it differently: a diplomatic source cited by the state agency SANA called reports of troops sent “at this time” entirely unfounded, said Syria’s priorities were internal security and reconstruction, and described the Saudi agreement as covering advice, training and exchanges of expertise, with Saudi coordination visits to start next month. What is not disputed is the backdrop: Houthi attacks have intensified, Riyadh has been gathering partners — France and Britain both pledged air-defence help in October — and Yemen’s war has widened again since a four-year truce collapsed in the summer.

Latest · Americas · Colombia — Drone Attack7 November 2026

Explosives From the Sky at Midday: Drone Strike Kills Three Soldiers in Bolívar

Colombia’s army says drones rigged with explosives hit its base at Santa Rosa del Sur, in southern Bolívar, around midday on Saturday, killing three soldiers and seriously wounding at least eight, with wider injury counts still being consolidated. Video shared online showed smoke rising from the compound after three blasts as troops sheltered behind sandbags. The base is home to the 48th Jungle Battalion, in foothills below the San Lucas range where gold mining has long drawn competing armed groups — the Gulf Clan, the National Liberation Army (ELN) and dissident factions of the former FARC. The army has not named a culprit and no group has claimed the attack, though the defence ministry has pointed toward the ELN as retaliation for a leader’s capture, and the army called the strike a grave breach of international humanitarian law. President Abelardo de la Espriella promised the attackers would have nowhere to hide. The method is the message: cheap unmanned aircraft are now able to strike a fortified cantonment in daylight, a vulnerability Colombia’s forces are meeting across several fronts at once.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Bank Scandal7 November 2026

From Palace to House Arrest in a Week: Peña Faces the Prosecutors Over Ueno Bank

Paraguay’s fallen president is now a defendant. Santiago Peña, impeached by the Senate on Friday by 44 votes to none and placed under house arrest, was questioned in Asunción on Saturday and charged with breach of trust and administering public resources for personal gain in connection with the collapse of Ueno Bank; prosecutors are also asking for pretrial detention. Investigators allege that Peña, who held a stake in Ueno’s parent until March 2025, channelled more than $800 million in public money toward the digital lender, shielded it from supervision, and let the state pension fund place about $355 million there beyond legal limits. He denies the charges and frames the failure as a regulatory lapse. Regulators seized Ueno on 3 November, freezing out nearly three million customers — about 60 per cent of adults, many banking for the first time — and triggering days of protests; the central bank said Saturday that 1.2 million customers had already had insured deposits repaid in full or in part. Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in to serve until August 2028. Peña had tried to resign on Thursday, but lawmakers chose impeachment instead, which also removed his immunity.

Latest · Europe · France — Wage Protests5 November 2026

575,000 or 1.6 Million? France Argues Over the Count, Not the Anger

France’s cost-of-living revolt has outgrown the students who started it. Thursday’s nationwide strike day brought teachers, rail staff, hospital and postal workers and some energy employees onto the streets in 193 demonstrations, after weeks of school and university protests over crumbling buildings, staff shortages and squeezed public services. The Interior Ministry counted 575,000 marchers, including about 65,000 in Paris; the CGT union, a co-organiser, counted nearly 1.6 million, including 350,000 in the capital. By early evening the ministry reported 448 arrests, 53 injured police officers and 86 fires, with tear gas used in Paris and Lyon and clashes in several other cities after a mostly peaceful day; about 70,000 police had been deployed. The demands are concrete: the CGT wants the minimum wage up 10 per cent, the CFDT a net €100 a month more, alongside across-the-board raises and tax changes. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has called unions and employers to a “social conference” at Matignon on Monday — six months before elections to succeed President Emmanuel Macron, with the far right ahead in the polls and the 2027 budget still unpassed.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — Wong Sentenced5 November 2026

Due Out in January, Inside Until 2036: Joshua Wong Gets 10 Years for Foreign Collusion

Hong Kong’s best-known democracy activist will not be going home in January after all. Joshua Wong, 30, who has been in custody since November 2020, was sentenced by the High Court on Thursday to 10 years in prison after pleading guilty in September to conspiring with exiled activist Nathan Law and others to ask foreign governments to impose sanctions and blockades on Hong Kong and mainland China in 2020. Judge William Tam called the offence one of a “grave nature” under the Beijing-imposed national security law, set a starting point of 13 years and reduced it for the guilty plea; the new term will absorb the roughly two months left on Wong’s existing four-year, eight-month sentence from the landmark “Hong Kong 47” subversion case. On current terms he remains jailed until November 2036, when he turns 40. Police national-security chief Steve Li welcomed the sentence outside court; Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International condemned it as baseless and cruel. Wong first led student protests as a teenager and became the international face of the 2019 movement — a role the 2020 security law has since criminalised in its foreign-collusion provisions, which carry up to life imprisonment.

Latest · Europe · Cyber — Freezers Hacked2 November 2026

Freezers Set to 50°C: Europe’s Supermarkets Learn Their Fridges Are a Front Line

Staff arriving for the Monday shift on 2 November found chilled cabinets warm and freezers defrosting. Attackers had reached the control systems for supermarket refrigeration in seven countries — Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland — and in some stores pushed temperatures as high as 50°C, forcing shops to dump spoiled food. About 40 Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg were hit, alongside roughly 15 Carrefour outlets in Belgium, a handful of Spar shops, and chains including ICA and Coop in Sweden and SuperValu in Ireland; one Belgian store put its losses near €100,000. Belgian refrigeration firm Sabcobel said evaporators had been switched into defrost mode using what appears to be the same method as an October attack that struck hundreds of French stores, with losses then estimated in the tens of millions of euros. The equipment maker Danfoss confirmed its products were involved in both waves. No group has claimed responsibility, national cyber centres are investigating, and Britain’s National Cyber Security Centre says it is working with affected UK firms, including meat processors, while warning retailers that internet-reachable fridge controllers are now a proven target.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — No Winner Yet8 November 2026

Still Deadlocked on Sunday: New Zealand’s Election Now Belongs to the Special Votes

A day after polling, New Zealand still cannot name its next government. Saturday’s election left Christopher Luxon’s National and Chris Hipkins’ Labour both below 30 per cent, a few thousand votes apart in some tallies, after a campaign fought over a weak economy and frustration with both major parties. On preliminary results National has about 42 seats; adding ACT’s 13 and New Zealand First’s 12 gives the incumbent bloc roughly 67 seats in a parliament swollen by overhangs to about 130–131 — a working majority of one or two. Labour’s 35–36 seats, the Greens’ record 16 and Te Pāti Māori’s five fall well short, which is why the night’s real winner may be the centrist Opportunity Party: it cleared the 5 per cent threshold for the first time, at about 5.9 per cent and seven seats, and could hold the balance if the numbers move. They may well move: several hundred thousand special votes — cast abroad or outside a home electorate, and historically favourable to the left — will not be tallied before the official result on 27 November. Luxon governs as caretaker until then; in 2023, coalition talks alone took 41 days.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens Monday7 November 2026

Delegates Arrive Monday. Above Istanbul, a Landfill Is Venting 7.4 Tonnes an Hour

Turkey opens the COP31 summit in Antalya on Monday, 9 November, talking up methane cuts — just as satellite research lands on its own waste sector. UCLA researchers using Carbon Mapper’s Tanager-1 data from January 2025 to May 2026 counted 70 methane plumes at Turkey’s 10 highest-emitting waste sites. The largest, the Seymen landfill west of Istanbul, receives about 8,500 tonnes of city waste a day and averaged roughly 7.4 tonnes of methane an hour, close to the highest landfill rate recorded anywhere in 2025, even though a gas-capture plant has burned collected gas for power there since 2020. Climate TRACE places Turkey fifth worldwide for waste methane, behind China, the United States, Russia and Brazil, and World Bank figures show its waste methane up nearly 25 per cent in the decade to 2024, against 6.5 per cent globally. The environment ministry disputes the satellite method, arguing snapshots cannot prove annual totals; operator ISTAC says typical rates are 4 to 6 tonnes an hour and blames one 12-tonne reading for the average. Turkey has also not joined the Global Methane Pledge, which aims for a 30 per cent cut from 2020 levels by 2030. The summit runs to 20 November.
