Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi, Day One Ends10 November 2026

“Constructive and Quite Substantive”: Abu Dhabi’s First Day Ends After 4.5 Hours With a Major Prisoner Swap on the Table

The opening day of trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and the United States in Abu Dhabi closed on Tuesday after about four and a half hours, with President Volodymyr Zelensky calling the sessions constructive and substantive and naming prisoner releases as Kyiv’s top priority. Ukrainian negotiators first met American and European partners before sitting down with the US and Russian delegations to discuss humanitarian steps, de-escalation and the substance of post-war security guarantees — including, US officials indicated, ideas modelled in part on NATO-style commitments to deter a renewed invasion. Zelensky said his team now has clear instructions for Wednesday’s second day. No agreement was announced, and the core divide remains: Kyiv, backed by European partners, insists the fighting must end before a new European security architecture is settled, while Moscow still demands Ukrainian withdrawal from territory it claims and rejects a simple ceasefire. Talks resume on 11 November.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Rubio at the G7 in Paris10 November 2026

Rubio Tells the G7 There Is “No Need” for Major Combat Operations Against Iran Because the Economic Campaign Is Working

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio told G7 foreign ministers in Paris on Tuesday that Washington does not need to resume major combat operations against Iran because sanctions and the naval blockade are inflicting extraordinary, compounding economic pain — a third path, he argued, between renewed war and accepting Iranian demands diplomats described as unacceptable. His remarks closed two days of G7 talks a day after President Donald Trump said he would hold off on fresh strikes following appeals from regional governments, including talks at the White House with Qatar’s prime minister. In an unscheduled stop, that same Qatari leader, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani — a key intermediary with Tehran — then joined European and Saudi ministers on the sidelines in Paris. European allies pressed Rubio to avoid any new escalation, French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot warned of the risk of one, and all sides agreed the war should end quickly even as their positions on Iran, Ukraine and the International Criminal Court remained far apart.

Latest · Europe · EU Budget — Merz on Ukraine Funding10 November 2026

Merz Draws a Line in the EU Budget Fight: Every Member State Must Back Ukraine, No Opt-Outs

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz insisted on Tuesday that support for Kyiv cannot be weakened and that every European Union member state must contribute to funding Ukraine, in the latest exchange in a clash over the bloc’s next long-term budget from 2028. In a letter to European Council President António Costa seen by Reuters, Merz pointed to a projected €70 billion funding gap for Ukraine in 2027 and an expected EU contribution of €45 billion, arguing the burden cannot fall only on willing states after several opted out of backing a new loan for Kyiv last year. Merz wants the new budget to prioritise defence spending and competitiveness while keeping Ukraine support collective. Germany is both the EU’s largest contributor and Ukraine’s biggest backer, and the dispute — sharpened by criticism from Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico earlier on Tuesday — is becoming another test of Western resolve more than four years after Russia’s full-scale invasion. The EU agreed a €90 billion loan for Ukraine last December.

Latest · Americas · Argentina — Pope Leo in Córdoba10 November 2026

650,000 in Córdoba: Pope Leo Draws Another Huge Argentine Crowd a Day After 2.5 Million in Buenos Aires

Pope Leo drew an estimated 650,000 people to an outdoor Mass in Córdoba on Tuesday, the second vast turnout in two days on his 12-day South America tour, as pilgrims travelled from across Argentina for the event. On Monday some 2.5 million people joined his Mass in Buenos Aires — the largest gathering of his 18-month papacy — and Leo is the first pope to visit majority-Catholic Argentina since 1987. Travelling some 1,300 kilometres back and forth from the capital for two events in Córdoba, a city in the foothills of the Sierras Chicas with deep Jesuit ties, he is using the tour of Argentina, Uruguay and Peru, running to 17 November, to reinforce a region long seen as a Catholic stronghold but where evangelical churches have been gaining ground. In Argentina he has also directly challenged President Javier Milei’s austerity agenda, urging him on Sunday not to target the country’s poorest as social services are cut.

Latest · Americas · Pentagon — Hegseth Impeachment Push10 November 2026

Hegseth Calls the Move to Impeach Him a “Farce That Will Fail” at His First Pentagon Briefing Since May

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Tuesday dismissed efforts to impeach him over alleged abuse of power in the Iran war as a farce that would fail, speaking at his first Pentagon news conference since May. The briefing came a day after Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced eight articles of impeachment against him; Massie said the House would hold a first vote on Tuesday on a procedural motion to kill the resolution. Hegseth noted that past attempts to impeach President Donald Trump had failed and said he stands beside every one of the Iran operations. The challenge is highly unusual — a Republican lawmaker moving against a Republican administration’s defense chief — and it lands a week after Republicans lost control of both chambers of Congress in the 3 November midterms, losses Trump himself has acknowledged were partly driven by the unpopular Iran war.

Latest · Americas / Energy · Russian Diesel — Tanker Waits Off the US Coast10 November 2026

First Tanker of Russian Diesel Since the 2022 Ban Sits Off the US East Coast, Still Waiting for Orders

A tanker carrying the first cargo of Russian diesel bound for the United States since Washington banned Russian oil imports in 2022 has halted off the US east coast with no discharge port yet decided, shipping data showed on Tuesday. The Malta-flagged Agioi Fanendes loaded 40,000 metric tons of ultra-low-sulphur diesel at Russia’s Baltic port of Primorsk on 22 October and had been expected to deliver around 7 November, according to LSEG data, but its Greece-based manager Eastmed told Reuters on Tuesday that nothing had changed and the vessel was still awaiting orders. Other tankers that had signalled the US Atlantic Coast as their destination have since altered course. The shipment follows President Donald Trump’s surprise deal with Moscow last month to import Russian diesel to ease prices at the pump ahead of the midterms, under which he waived parts of a new US sanctions law and said Russia would export 300,000 tons immediately, followed by larger volumes in November and beyond.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — ANC’s Worst-Ever Result10 November 2026

From 47.5% to 32.5%: Final Results Confirm the ANC’s Steepest-Ever Fall in South Africa’s Municipal Elections

South Africa’s African National Congress has recorded its worst-ever electoral result in final municipal election figures, falling to 32.5% of the local vote — down 15 percentage points from 47.5% in 2021, the steepest drop of any party on the ballot — and winning no outright majority in any of the country’s eight major metropolitan areas. The Democratic Alliance rose three points to 22.9%, while much of the ANC’s lost ground went to parties that did not contest the last local elections: former President Jacob Zuma’s uMkhonto weSizwe party debuted at 9.9% and the SACP, standing separately from its old alliance partner, took 2.6%; ActionSA nearly tripled its share to 5.0%. About 15.9 million of 29.2 million registered voters cast ballots, turnout of roughly 54.5%, up from 46% in 2021. The 4 November vote, dominated by anger over crime, corruption and failing water and power supplies, leaves coalition bargaining to decide who runs South Africa’s big cities ahead of the 2029 general election.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — 66 Soldiers Sentenced to Death10 November 2026

Congo Military Court Sentences 66 Soldiers to Death for Cowardice and Mutiny After a 12-Day Trial in North Kivu

A military court in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo has sentenced 66 soldiers to death for cowardice in the face of the enemy, military mutiny and refusing to obey orders, following a 12-day trial in Walikale, North Kivu province. The Goma Garrison Military Court delivered the verdict late on Friday 6 November against members of the army’s 3415th Regiment, who were among 71 troops prosecuted over incidents at Ntonto after their arrest on 20 October in Ngora village; four defendants were acquitted, including the regiment’s intelligence captain, and a sergeant received 10 years in prison after the court accepted mitigating circumstances. Prosecutors had sought death sentences for 70 defendants. Army spokespeople said the case was meant to reinforce discipline among forces fighting armed groups in North and South Kivu, where the soldiers allegedly abandoned positions during clashes with the FRN rebel coalition, which includes M23. Congo lifted a 20-year moratorium on executions in March 2024, but no executions from the verdict have been confirmed and no date has been reported.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea — New Torpedo Test10 November 2026

North Korea Test-Fires a New High-Speed Torpedo and Says the Navy Will Field It From Early 2027

North Korea test-fired a new type of high-speed torpedo in the East Sea on Monday, state media reported on Tuesday, saying the weapon is scheduled to enter service with its navy from early 2027. The Korean Central News Agency said the Academy of Defence Sciences, the Missile Administration and the Torpedo Weapon Research Bureau had verified the system’s combat effectiveness, describing the launch as part of the defence institutes’ regular work to advance weapons technology. Pyongyang released no specifications, photographs or launch platform details, and South Korean officials said more analysis is needed. The test continues a rapid naval build-up: in recent weeks North Korea has also trialled attack drones, an intermediate-range hypersonic missile and, last month, an underwater precision-guided torpedo it says uses artificial intelligence to find and pursue targets, alongside new destroyers and expanded underwater strike capabilities at a time of heightened tension on the Korean peninsula.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31, Brazil’s Roadmap10 November 2026

At COP31, Brazil Hands Over a Roadmap for Leaving Fossil Fuels Behind — a Guide, Not a Mandate

Brazil formally presented its long-promised roadmap for transitioning away from fossil fuels on Tuesday, the Energy and Transport Day of the COP31 summit in Antalya, three years after countries agreed in Dubai to move away from coal, oil and gas without agreeing how. COP30 President André Corrêa do Lago stressed the document is voluntary — not a mandate and not part of the formal negotiations — while Brazil’s COP30 chief executive Ana Toni said it breaks the transition into smaller, actionable levers across three linked problems: demand for fossil fuels, their production and supply, and the economic consequences of leaving them. Measures highlighted include halting new fossil-fuel power plants and phasing out subsidies that keep fossil fuels artificially cheap. UN Secretary-General António Guterres received the roadmap and warned of the cost of continued dependence, and host Türkiye used the day to promote its 35-by-35 electrification pledge: raising electricity’s share of global energy use from just over 20% today to 35% by 2035.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — Import Expo Record10 November 2026

Shanghai’s Import Expo Closes With a Record $85.45 Billion in Intended Deals

The ninth China International Import Expo closed in Shanghai on Tuesday with one-year intended deals reaching a record $85.45 billion, up 2.3% on the previous edition, the CIIE Bureau announced. The six-day event — the world’s first national-level expo dedicated to imports, first held in 2018 — drew participants from 162 countries, regions and international organisations across 440,000 square metres, with 4,142 exhibitors in the business exhibition and 485 new products, technologies and services making their debut, itself a new high. Deputy director Xiao Lu said five core measures, including exhibitor numbers and floor space, peaked this year, and about 143 overseas companies and institutions have already booked roughly 100,000 square metres for the tenth edition next November. Beijing says it will keep hosting the expo at a high standard under its 15th Five-Year Plan for 2026–2030, presenting the fair as a gateway into the Chinese market at a moment of sharpening trade tensions.

Explosive Device Kills a Police Officer and Seriously Wounds Another Responding to a Shooting in Northern Spain

One Spanish police officer was killed and another seriously wounded on Tuesday when an explosive device detonated as they responded to a shooting in the northern city of Oviedo, Spain’s National Police said. Authorities said there is so far no indication the explosion was terrorism-related. Government spokesperson Elma Saiz conveyed the government’s condolences for the fallen officer, and Spanish news agency EFE reported that the suspect in the incident has a record of petty crime.
