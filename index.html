Latest · Middle East · Israel — Eisenkot Gets the Mandate8 November 2026

67 to 46, Now 28 Days to Govern: Eisenkot Is Asked to Replace Netanyahu

The waiting ended on Saturday evening. President Isaac Herzog told former army chief Gadi Eisenkot that he — not Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — would be given the first chance to form Israel’s next government, with the formal handover set for Sunday. The arithmetic behind the choice was stark: factions representing 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members recommended Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party, against 46 for Netanyahu, the strongest endorsement for any candidate since 2015. Eisenkot now has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to assemble at least 61 seats. That is where the clarity stops. His own Jewish-led bloc holds 48 seats; the other 19 recommendations came from the two Arab-led parties, the Joint List and Ra’am, whom he has ruled out of government unless his conditions are met, including recognition of Hamas as a terrorist organisation. He says he wants a broad “national emergency government” and is courting, among others, the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism, whose seven seats refused to back either man. Avigdor Liberman’s party is another obstacle to clear. If Eisenkot fails, the mandate passes to Netanyahu; if both fail, Israel faces another election within about 90 days, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker in the meantime. His first test comes quickly — the new Knesset is sworn in on Tuesday, 10 November, and is expected to elect a new speaker.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Shooting7 November 2026

Five Dead in the Main Square: a Gunman Opens Fire on a Busy Saturday in Kraków

Poland is in shock after a lone gunman killed five people and wounded around a dozen more in the historic centre of Kraków on Saturday afternoon. Police say the man drove a car onto the Main Market Square — one of Europe’s largest medieval squares and the city’s tourist heart — at about 3 p.m. and began firing at random passers-by, reportedly from the moving vehicle as it came from Sławkowska Street. Officers exchanged fire with him, shot and wounded him, and took him to hospital in custody; two police officers were among the injured. A police spokeswoman said the attacker acted alone and is Polish, and that he was wearing military-style clothing. No motive has been announced. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it a tragic shooting and said he was being kept informed by the interior minister while investigators establish the background and circumstances. President Karol Nawrocki also responded publicly as emergency crews worked at the scene and the city urged people to stay away from the square and nearby Dietla Street. Mass shootings of this kind are rare in Poland, which is part of why a crowded Saturday crowd, in a UNESCO-listed old town, has made the attack reverberate well beyond the city.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — President Charged7 November 2026

Impeached, Under House Arrest, Now Charged: Paraguay’s Bank Collapse Reaches Its Former President

Paraguay’s crisis moved from parliament to the prosecutor’s office on Saturday. Former president Santiago Peña, removed by the Senate on Friday in a unanimous 44–0 vote and placed under house arrest, was questioned in Asunción and charged with breach of trust and administration for personal gain over the collapse of Ueno Bank, with prosecutors also seeking pretrial detention. Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in and will serve until August 2028. The allegations are specific: that Peña, who held a stake in Ueno’s parent company until March 2025, used his influence to steer more than $800 million in public funds toward the fast-growing digital lender, shielded it from regulatory scrutiny, and allowed the state pension fund to deposit about $355 million there above legal limits. He denies wrongdoing and calls the failure a regulatory matter. The human scale explains the politics: regulators seized Ueno on 3 November after its solvency collapsed, leaving nearly three million customers — roughly 60 per cent of adults, many of them first-time account holders — unable to reach savings, wages and pensions, and sparking days of street protests. The central bank said on Saturday that 1.2 million customers had already received partial or full repayment of insured deposits. Peña had offered to resign on Thursday; lawmakers rejected the resignation and impeached him instead, a step that also stripped his immunity.

Latest · Americas · US Politics — Miller Takes Over Monday7 November 2026

Doubling Down After a Drubbing: Trump Replaces Wiles With Miller as Chief of Staff

President Donald Trump’s first big answer to a historic midterm defeat is a personnel change that points away from conciliation. Susie Wiles, the first woman to serve as White House chief of staff, submitted her resignation on Thursday evening and will move to Trump’s political operation; Stephen Miller, 41, the deputy chief of staff and architect of the administration’s hardline immigration and deportation policies, takes over on Monday, 9 November, as the 33rd chief of staff. Trump, who in mid-October insisted Wiles would stay “for a long time”, announced the switch on Friday, saying “Sometimes in life, you have to make moves” and that Miller “has been with me from the beginning. He gets it.” Reporting from Washington describes a president who grew angrier as coverage of Tuesday’s losses — Democrats taking both the House and the Senate — sank in, and who was urged by allies including figures around Steve Bannon to replace Wiles. Miller, privately nicknamed “the prime minister” by allies and critics alike for his reach across domestic policy, posted that serving as chief of staff was an honour beyond words. The signal for the next two years is hard to miss: with a hostile Democratic Congress arriving in January, early talk of a comprehensive immigration compromise looks unlikely, and confrontation looks like the plan.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — No Winner Yet8 November 2026

Sunday Morning, Still No Government: New Zealand’s One-Seat Election Waits on Special Votes

New Zealand woke on Sunday exactly where it went to bed — without knowing who will govern it. Saturday’s general election left Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s National Party and Chris Hipkins’ Labour both just below 30 per cent of the party vote, separated in some counts by only a few thousand votes, after a campaign dominated by a struggling economy and disillusionment with both big parties. On the preliminary tally, National holds about 42 seats; with its partners ACT on 13 and New Zealand First on 12, the governing bloc reaches about 67 seats in an enlarged parliament of roughly 130–131 members swollen by overhang seats — a majority of one or two. Labour, on 35–36 seats, plus the Greens on a best-ever 16 and Te Pāti Māori on five, sits well short. The night’s breakthrough belonged to the centrist Opportunity Party, which cleared the 5 per cent threshold for the first time with about 5.9 per cent and seven seats, a bloc that could become decisive. Nothing formal can move until the Electoral Commission declares the official result on 27 November, once several hundred thousand special votes — cast overseas or outside a home electorate, and historically leaning left — are counted. Luxon will run a caretaker government meanwhile. Recent history suggests patience: coalition talks took 41 days in 2023.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Talks Set for Abu Dhabi7 November 2026

53 Dead at an Aid Centre, 227 Drones in a Night: Ukraine’s Winter War Meets a Talking Date

The war’s two tracks — bombardment and diplomacy — are running side by side into next week. Search teams in Komyshuvakha, in Zaporizhzhia region, have raised the death toll from the 2 November strikes on a school being used as a humanitarian aid centre to 53, on the fifth day of work in the rubble. Overnight, Ukraine’s air force reported another large Russian drone package, with most of the drones destroyed or jammed and hits recorded at multiple locations; Kyiv region reported injuries and damage in the Boryspil, Bila Tserkva and Obukhiv districts, and the private utility DTEK said Russian forces had again struck energy sites in Dnipropetrovsk region that crews had only just repaired. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says $16 billion in agreed American aid remains undelivered, while Spain has added a new package worth about $489 million. Against that backdrop, US-led talks between Russia and Ukraine are scheduled for 10 and 11 November in Abu Dhabi, announced by special envoy Steve Witkoff, and Germany has argued Europe must have a central role in any peace process. Kyiv and its allies remain wary that Washington is drifting toward Moscow’s terms. The memory shaping expectations is fresh: the longest truce of the war, brokered to allow an evacuation mission from occupied Oleshky, expired on 1 November — and the Komyshuvakha strike followed within hours.

Latest · Middle East · Iran Diplomacy — Two-Day Bridge Plan7 November 2026

Day One, Lift the Blockade. Day Two, Reopen Hormuz: Qatar’s Bridge Deal Awaits Tehran’s Answer

Qatar’s mediators have put a deliberately small deal in front of two governments fighting a war now in its ninth month. Under the “bridge” proposal Tehran is reviewing, confidence-building steps would be compressed into two days: on day one the United States would lift its blockade of Iranian ports and release some frozen Iranian funds, and Iran would readmit International Atomic Energy Agency inspectors to nuclear sites bombed by the US and Israel; on day two Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz under a temporary arrangement worked out with Oman. Only then would broader talks begin, covering Iran’s stockpile of about 440.9 kilograms of uranium enriched up to 60 per cent, among other issues. Qatar’s prime minister says Washington remains willing to seek a solution after the midterms, and Doha reports real-time exchanges of proposals. The obstacle is sequencing: Tehran insists its wider ceasefire conditions come first — an end to hostilities on all fronts, full sanctions relief, unfreezing of assets, reparations, recognition of its sovereignty over Hormuz and a guarantee against future attack, in the five conditions set out by its armed forces chief, with other Iranian officials citing seven demands. The diplomacy is not happening in quiet: American forces carried out fresh limited strikes on an Iranian air-defence system on Friday, even as the paper moved between capitals.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — 70,000 Tents Flooded7 November 2026

70,000 Tents in 12 Hours: Gaza’s First Winter Storm Turns Camps Into Flood Zones

The first winter storm of the season has done in hours what aid agencies had warned about for months. Gaza’s Government Media Office says about 70,000 tents sheltering displaced families were flooded within 12 hours of the rain starting, with initial losses put at about $3 million; the civil defence service logged roughly 1,500 distress calls in 10 hours as water rose 30 to 40 centimetres in several shelter sites. In al-Mawasi, on the southern coast, where more than 900,000 displaced people are sheltering, rain fell from Friday night onward. Families described mattresses and clothes soaked beyond saving, and two people were injured when a wall of a war-weakened building collapsed in the north. Officials say they cannot cope: fuel shortages and damaged equipment limit pumping and rescue work, and the storm also threatened dozens of bomb-damaged buildings at risk of collapse. The underlying numbers frame the emergency — the UN says about 950,000 displaced people live in areas at moderate or high flood risk, some 294,250 families need shelter assistance, and available supplies cover only about 18 per cent of that need. Gaza’s emergency committee puts the requirement at about 200,000 weather-resistant tents plus heating supplies. Palestinian officials blame closed crossings and blocked shelter materials; winter, meanwhile, has only just begun.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens Monday7 November 2026

7.4 Tonnes an Hour From One Landfill: Turkey Hosts COP31 With a Methane Problem Overhead

Turkey opens the COP31 climate summit in Antalya on Monday, 9 November, with methane reduction as one of its stated priorities — and with new satellite evidence undercutting the pitch. Researchers at UCLA, using Carbon Mapper’s Tanager-1 satellite data from January 2025 to May 2026, identified 70 methane plumes from Turkey’s 10 highest-emitting waste sites. The worst, the Seymen landfill west of Istanbul, which takes about 8,500 tonnes of the city’s waste a day, averaged about 7.4 tonnes of methane an hour — just below the highest-emitting landfill recorded globally in 2025 — despite a gas-capture plant, opened in 2020, that burns collected gas for electricity. Climate TRACE ranks Turkey fifth in the world for methane from solid waste, behind China, the United States, Russia and Brazil, and World Bank data show its waste methane rose nearly 25 per cent in the decade to 2024, against a global rise of 6.5 per cent. Turkey’s environment ministry disputes the satellite figures, saying intermittent observations cannot establish annual emissions; the site operator, ISTAC, says typical rates are 4 to 6 tonnes an hour and blames a single 12-tonne reading for lifting the average. One more fact will follow delegates into the hall: Turkey has not joined the Global Methane Pledge, which seeks a 30 per cent cut from 2020 levels by 2030. The summit runs to 20 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Worst Nasdaq Day Since 20207 November 2026

Worst Day Since March 2020, Worst Week in 19 Months: What the AI Rout Is Really Pricing

The numbers from Wall Street’s week are now final, and they describe a political repricing more than an earnings story. On Thursday, 5 November, the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4 per cent — its worst day since March 2020 — the S&P 500 lost 4.4 per cent, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged about 12 per cent, erasing roughly $3 trillion of market value in a single session. Nvidia alone shed around $450 billion, falling 9.3 per cent; AMD dropped 17.3 per cent just two sessions after reporting record quarterly revenue of $13.4 billion, and Micron and SanDisk fell 17 and 22 per cent respectively. Friday’s rebound — AMD up about 11 per cent, Nvidia up 5.7 per cent — only softened the week: the S&P 500 closed down 2.7 per cent, the Nasdaq Composite down 3.4 per cent, its steepest weekly fall in 19 months, and the Dow down 0.9 per cent. The shock spread to Tokyo, where the Nikkei fell 5.96 per cent on Friday and 9.3 per cent for the week. The trigger investors cite is the midterm map: Democratic wins in both chambers, plus governorships in states such as Texas and Georgia where candidates campaigned on curbing the data-centre boom, have raised the odds of tighter rules on AI infrastructure. UBS strategists estimate nearly a third of the US computing power expected online in 2028 is now at risk. Whether that risk is real policy or expensive fear is the question the market opens with on Monday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India–EU — €93.5 Billion Programme6 November 2026

€93.5 Billion Door Opens: India Concludes Talks to Join Europe’s Horizon Research Programme

India and the European Union have finished negotiating New Delhi’s association with Horizon Europe, the bloc’s €93.5 billion flagship research and innovation programme for 2021–2027, the European Commission announced on Friday. The deal, expected to be signed formally in January and to take effect that month, would let Indian universities, research organisations and companies receive EU funding directly and lead collaborative projects under the programme’s largest component, Pillar II — “Global Challenges and European Industrial Competitiveness”. Its fields read like a shared priority list for both sides: health, digital technologies and artificial intelligence, industry, space, climate, energy and mobility, food and the bioeconomy. The negotiation was fast by Brussels standards — exploratory talks began in February 2026, formal negotiations in July, and conclusion in under four months — and it sits inside a wider five-year strategic agenda and a joint comprehensive agenda toward 2030, alongside a free-trade agreement the two sides aim to sign next month. The timing carries a caveat: joining in January 2027 puts India in the programme’s final year, so the practical prize is as much position — inside European consortia and infrastructure for whatever succeeds Horizon — as it is the remaining money. India’s financial contribution has not been announced.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Diwali Air Emergency8 November 2026

Diwali Day, Severe Air Forecast: Delhi Wakes as the World’s Most Polluted Major City

Delhi has reached Diwali itself with the forecast officials dreaded: air quality expected in the “severe” band through Sunday and Monday, 8 and 9 November. On Saturday the capital’s official average index stood at 369 — “very poor” on India’s Central Pollution Control Board scale, close to the severe threshold of 400 — while the Swiss monitor IQAir, using a different scale, ranked Delhi the world’s most polluted major city at 10:35 a.m. with a US-scale reading of 581, nearly double second-placed Lahore at 285. Of the city’s 59 monitoring stations, 16 recorded severe air and 29 very poor, with a peak of 452 near IIT Delhi. The ingredients are the familiar November stack, arriving together: firecracker emissions from the festival of lights, peak crop-residue burning in neighbouring states — estimated at roughly a sixth of the city’s fine-particle pollution in recent assessments — traffic fumes, and stagnant winter winds too weak to disperse any of it. Friday’s air was already reported as the worst pre-Diwali reading in eight years. The pattern is regional rather than local: Kolkata ranked third in the same IQAir snapshot at 198 and Mumbai tenth at 142. For Delhi’s residents, the practical advice from health experts is unchanged and unglamorous — limit outdoor exertion, especially for children, older people and anyone with heart or lung disease, and expect the worst of it the morning after the fireworks.
