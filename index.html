Latest · Americas · Markets — The Gridlock Rally4 November 2026

Dow Up 1,300 Points: Wall Street Decides a Divided Washington Is Good News

The market verdict on the midterms arrived a day after the voters’ one, and it was emphatic. The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped about 1,307 points, or 2.5 per cent — on course for its best day since April — while the S&P 500 rose 1.5 per cent and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.7 per cent. The move reversed an overnight slide in which Nasdaq futures had fallen as much as 2.4 per cent. Traders read the Democratic sweep two ways at once: certainty, because the result was decisive and accepted, and constraint, because a Congress held by the opposition limits how far President Trump’s tax, spending and tariff agenda can run. The volatility index sank 16 per cent, Treasury yields fell across the curve, the dollar slid against every major peer, and gold jumped as much as 3.1 per cent. Renewable-energy shares rallied on expectations of a friendlier Congress, while data-centre operators fell sharply on fears of tighter scrutiny of artificial-intelligence expansion. In Asia the reaction was rougher — Japan’s Nikkei dropped nearly 3 per cent and SoftBank plunged 8 per cent during counting — before European shares recovered to advance more than 1 per cent.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Results Certified4 November 2026

53, 48 and a Record 19: Israel Certifies Its Election — and No One Can Form a Government

Israel’s Central Elections Committee certified the results of the 27 October Knesset election on Wednesday and handed them to President Isaac Herzog, formally rejecting claims of fraud after reviewing 9,069 polling stations and finding no irregularities that compromised the vote or changed its outcome. The arithmetic leaves the country in deadlock: Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53 of 120 seats, the opposition alliance led by Gadi Eisenkot holds 48, and Arab-led parties — Ra’am and the Joint List — hold a record 19. Sixty-one are needed to govern. Netanyahu’s Likud remains the largest single party with 27 seats, ahead of Eisenkot’s Yashar on 21, but his outgoing 68-seat coalition has lost its majority. Committee chairman Justice Noam Sohlberg warned that casting doubt on the results poses “a real danger” to democracy; Likud and Shas abstained in the ratification vote and coalition representatives largely boycotted the handover ceremony. Herzog now begins two days of consultations before assigning the first mandate to form a government. The new Knesset is due to be sworn in on 10 November, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker in the meantime.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — The Wiretap Arrests4 November 2026

The President’s Own Chief of Staff, Arrested for Allegedly Tapping the President’s Phone

Indonesia’s government moved on Wednesday to contain the most serious internal-security scandal of President Prabowo Subianto’s term. His chief of staff, Listyo Sigit Prabowo — national police chief for five years before taking the palace job on 1 October, and no relation to the president — was sacked a day after his arrest alongside two other senior police generals. Investigators allege the three used the police Lawful Interception Management System between 2025 and 2026 to tap the phones of the president, cabinet ministers, politicians, journalists, business figures and civil-society leaders without court authorisation or any underlying case, then deleted log records in October to hide it. The case surfaced after an officer reported the activity on 30 October. A presidential spokesman said the acts “may already fall into a category that can be considered an act tending toward treason”; the senior legal-affairs minister, Yusril Ihza Mahendra, was more cautious, saying no motive and no evidence of treason had yet been established. The charges filed so far — illegal interception, unauthorised system access and destruction of electronic data — carry combined maximum sentences of up to 24 years. Thousands of troops were being mobilised in Jakarta, the navy said, to safeguard stability while the investigation proceeds.

Latest · Africa · Western Sahara — The Mission Ends1 November 2026

35 Years, No Referendum, No Mandate: The UN Leaves Western Sahara

The United Nations has begun liquidating MINURSO, its Western Sahara mission, after the Security Council failed to renew the mandate that expired at midnight on 31 October — closing a peacekeeping operation created in 1991 to monitor a ceasefire and organise a referendum on the territory’s future that was never held. The end came by veto and counter-veto: Russia blocked a US-backed draft, supported by 11 of the 15 Council members, that would have extended the mission while making Morocco’s autonomy-under-sovereignty plan the sole basis for talks and dropping “referendum” from the mission’s name; a Russian proposal for a straight one-year extension then failed to win the nine votes required. A smaller UN team is now overseeing the repatriation of equipment and disposal of assets from the headquarters in Laayoune and nine team sites strung along the sand berm. Secretary-General António Guterres warned of escalation risk — low-level hostilities resumed after the ceasefire broke down in 2020 — while insisting the Sahrawi people’s right to self-determination survives the mission. Morocco’s royal palace called the non-renewal a “significant development”; the Algeria-backed Polisario Front, which seeks independence, loses the only international presence on the ground. Diplomacy now rests solely with the Secretary-General’s personal envoy, Staffan de Mistura.

Latest · Global Affairs · The World Court — Five New Judges4 November 2026

A Record Caseload Meets a New Bench: UN Elects Five Judges to the International Court of Justice

The UN General Assembly and Security Council, voting separately but simultaneously in New York on Tuesday, elected five judges to nine-year terms on the International Court of Justice, the 15-member court in The Hague that settles disputes between states. The new bench, taking office on 6 February 2027, comprises Dapo Akande of the United Kingdom, François Alabrune of France, Mahmoud Daifallah Hmoud of Jordan, Rena Lee of Singapore and Phoebe Okowa of Kenya. Four won in the first round; Okowa — already serving a partial term after filling a 2025 vacancy — took the fifth seat in a third round, with 183 General Assembly votes. Lee, Singapore’s ambassador for international law, becomes the first Singaporean and the first Southeast Asian woman on the court, topping the Assembly poll with 151 of 191 votes. They arrive at an institution under unusual load: the court’s president told the Assembly last week that 24 contentious cases are pending, among them South Africa’s genocide case against Israel over the Gaza war, and the court announced it will rule on 19 November in The Gambia’s case accusing Myanmar of genocide against the Rohingya.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Human Cost of the Grid War4 November 2026

A 14-Year-Old in Khotiv, 33 Dead at an Aid Point: Ukraine Counts the Week’s Toll

Behind the nightly drone statistics, Ukrainian officials spent Wednesday attaching names and ages to the week’s attacks. In Khotiv, in Kyiv region’s Obukhiv district, a Russian drone struck a two-storey private house directly and burned it to the ground; rescuers found the body of a 14-year-old, born in 2012, under the rubble, and the child’s mother was injured. The strike was part of the mass barrage of 3 November — 289 drones alongside 17 ballistic and 59 cruise missiles by Ukraine’s count — in which three drones were also directed at a substation critical to the Khmelnytskyi nuclear power plant, prompting Kyiv to alert the International Atomic Energy Agency. Separately, the confirmed death toll from the 2 November strike on a humanitarian-aid distribution site in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, rose to 33, with dozens still reported missing; Latvia has called an emergency UN Security Council meeting over the attack. Overnight into Wednesday, drones again damaged an energy facility in Ternopil region and a strike on an enterprise in Dnipro injured two men. US-brokered talks involving Russia and Ukraine are scheduled to resume in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November; the US Air Force, meanwhile, is seeking contractors to mass-produce more than 1,000 extended-range ERAM cruise missiles for Ukraine.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Twelve Million Ballots by 5 p.m.4 November 2026

Counting Begins in South Africa: 12 Million Votes Cast Before the Final Four Hours

South Africa’s municipal elections moved from voting to counting on Wednesday night, with the Electoral Commission reporting 12 million ballots cast by 5 p.m. from a record register of 29.2 million, ahead of the 9 p.m. close — anyone still queueing at closing time was entitled to vote. More than 136,000 candidates contested 10,526 council seats across 257 municipalities. Gauteng led turnout with about three million votes, followed by KwaZulu-Natal with 2.5 million. The day was largely orderly, the commission said, with isolated disruptions — a Johannesburg station in Orange Grove stalled for an hour when ballot papers ran out, and some Soweto voters were redirected after their names were missing from the roll. The stakes are set by the polling: surveys put the ANC near 32 per cent nationally, the Democratic Alliance leading in Johannesburg and Tshwane without a majority in either, and former president Jacob Zuma’s MK party ahead in Durban’s eThekwini metro. Ballots are counted at each station in front of party agents before results slips are scanned and audited; the commission has seven days to declare the outcome but says it will not use all of them, with results expected by Sunday. Coalition negotiations in the big metros will follow.

No Agreement in the Qatar-Mediated Channel: Hormuz Stays Choked, Brent Eases to $103

The diplomatic track running parallel to the confrontation in the Gulf produced nothing on Wednesday: Qatar-mediated US–Iran contacts have yielded no agreement, with Tehran’s stated conditions — sanctions relief, an end to the US naval blockade and the release of frozen funds — still unmet. The shipping picture explains the urgency. Iran’s separate claim that four tankers were destroyed by naval mines remains unconfirmed by any flag state, insurer or Western command. Latest · Americas · US Midterms — The Morning-After Arithmetic4 November 2026

54 Seats, Five Governorships, One Pattern: How Deep the Democratic Wave Ran

With most races called, the shape of Tuesday’s result is now measurable. Democrats flipped seven Republican Senate seats — Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas — held every seat of their own, and stand at about 54 seats to 44 for Republicans, with Alaska’s ranked-choice count not due until 18 November and Mississippi still uncalled. In the House they passed the 218-seat threshold early and were at 222 called seats by Wednesday morning, winning districts President Trump carried by 20 points in 2024. The governorships tell the same story: five flips, in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas, where Gina Hinojosa’s defeat of three-term Governor Greg Abbott completed the party’s first Texas statewide wins since 1994. Democrats also broke Republican legislative supermajorities in states such as Indiana and Missouri. Both chambers changing hands in a single midterm has happened only three times since the Second World War. What Democrats do not have matters too: at 54 Senate seats they are well short of the 67 needed to remove a president or override vetoes on their own, which sets the terms of the next two years — investigations and blocked bills from Congress, veto threats and executive action from the White House.

Latest · Americas · Technology — The Data-Centre Sell-Off4 November 2026

The One Loser in the Rally: Data-Centre Stocks Fall as AI Politics Shifts

Wednesday’s broad rally had a conspicuous exception. While Amazon rose as much as 6.3 per cent and the solar sector’s main exchange-traded fund jumped about 9 per cent, shares of data-centre operators IREN, Hut 8, Applied Digital and Core Scientific fell between roughly 5 and 15 per cent, and Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX-linked holdings were also among the laggards. The logic is electoral geography: Democrats campaigned against the pace and power demands of artificial-intelligence build-out and won in the very states where that build-out is concentrated, including Texas and Georgia. Goldman Sachs economists estimated that Democratic victories at state and local level could bring short-term moratoria affecting about 45 per cent of planned US data-centre capacity expansion. The sector was already fragile — the Nasdaq 100 had entered correction territory on Monday amid doubts about AI capital spending — and investors now expect tougher regulatory scrutiny of the industry from the new Congress as well. Whether local pauses materialise is a state-by-state question, but the market has repriced the assumption that AI infrastructure expansion proceeds without political friction.

“Netanyahu Is Toxic”: The Aid Announcement Meets an Israel That Just Voted

President Trump’s election-day announcement that the United States will end its roughly $3.8 billion a year in military aid to Israel landed, on Wednesday, in an Israel preoccupied with its own certified results — and drew a sharp domestic reading. Opposition figures argued that Prime Minister Netanyahu’s standing in Washington has become a liability, with one senior critic branding him “toxic” and warning of isolation and a clash with the United States after the midterms; regional commentators made the mirror-image point, that a Democratic Congress changes the politics of US support for Israel regardless of who governs in Jerusalem. The factual position has not moved since Tuesday: the aid flows under a ten-year memorandum running to 2028, Israeli officials said they first heard of the plan from the broadcast, and no executive order, bill or budget request implementing a cutoff has been published. What has changed is the audience — a new US Congress that takes office in January will write the next foreign-aid bills, and a new Israeli government, whoever leads it, will have to negotiate with it.

‘No Need to Sit Face to Face’: Pyongyang Rules Out Talks, Then Fires Missiles Into the Sea

North Korea answered Washington twice on Wednesday — once in words, once in launches. Hours after Kim Yo Jong, the leader’s sister and a senior official, dismissed any return to dialogue, South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff reported several missiles fired from the Sondok area toward the sea off the east coast — the fourth launch event in a week, in a test cadence that has recently included cluster-warhead cruise and ballistic missiles and a salvo of around ten short-range ballistic missiles from Wonsan. Kim’s statement was aimed at a specific charge: the FBI’s attribution of the theft of more than $6 billion from the Binance cryptocurrency exchange to the North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor. She called the accusation a trite provocation and an extension of Washington’s hostile policy, said Pyongyang had “no need to sit face to face with such unreliable and hostile persons”, and made any future dialogue conditional on the US abandoning that policy. President Trump has spent two months hinting he wants to meet Kim Jong Un again, possibly around this month’s APEC summit. Wednesday’s sequence suggests Pyongyang intends to set the price of that meeting itself, and in public.
