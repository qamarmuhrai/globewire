Polls Open in Israel: Final Surveys Put Netanyahu's Bloc on 50 Seats, Eleven Short of Governing

Voting is under way across Israel in the first Knesset election since the 7 October 2023 attacks, with polling stations open from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. and exit polls due the moment they close. All three final television polls published on 23 October put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on 50 seats — eleven short of the 61 needed to govern in the 120-seat parliament — while the opposition ranged between 52 and 56 seats, itself short of a majority once Arab parties are counted separately. Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party led Likud in every final survey. On the eve of the vote Netanyahu claimed President Donald Trump would act again against Iran only if he remained in power, a claim Eisenkot dismissed as fear-mongering, and the White House has said the United States will not attack Iran before the 3 November midterms. Counting will run overnight, double-envelope ballots from soldiers and diplomats follow on 29 October, and official results are not expected until 4 November — after which the president gives one leader 42 days to try to form a government, with Netanyahu's caretaker administration staying in place in the meantime.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine27 October 2026

A Small Silence in Kherson: The Oleshky Ceasefire Takes Effect as Russia Halts Strikes to Let Aid In

Russia and Ukraine began a localised ceasefire early on Tuesday around the occupied town of Oleshky in southern Kherson, a rare pause agreed on 23 October with the involvement of US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo. Russia's defence ministry said shelling towards Kherson city stopped at 01:00 GMT to prepare for a humanitarian mission later this week, and Ukraine's human-rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets confirmed Kyiv was honouring the arrangement. Up to 2,000 people remain in Oleshky, down from 24,000 before the war, cut off by mines and drones and facing acute shortages of food, medicine and water after years of occupation and the flooding that followed the 2023 destruction of the Kakhovka Dam. The truce is due to run through Saturday, with doctors, supplies and water to go in before evacuation corridors open. It changes little elsewhere: a Russian missile attack on Kyiv on Monday killed at least one person and injured ten, including students in a dormitory, with further deaths reported in Kharkiv and Pavlohrad and fresh explosions heard in the capital on Tuesday morning. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow was open to more local humanitarian truces but would not slow its wider offensive, as technical talks involving Ukraine, Russia and the United States are planned for the first half of November in Abu Dhabi.

Syria Shuts Its Only Crossing With Jordan After Jordanian Jets Strike the South — and the Two Capitals Tell Opposite Stories

Syria closed the Nassib crossing on Tuesday, suspending all passenger and commercial traffic with Jordan, a day after Jordanian warplanes struck what Amman called militant hideouts in southern Syria in response to Friday's church funeral attack in Karak that killed nine people. Syria's Civil Defence said seven people were recovered from four sites in rural Daraa and Sweida — three men, a woman and three children — including a family of five killed in a tent in the village of Al-Tai, while some Syrian reports put the toll at eight. The diplomatic row is over coordination: Jordan's armed forces said the operation was carried out “in coordination with the Syrian government,” but Damascus said it had conveyed its “complete reservation” over strikes carried out without prior notice, announced an investigation, and stressed the need for field coordination in border areas. Jordan said it had linked suspects in the church attack — three of whom were arrested after a shootout in Karak on Monday — to terrorist groups operating across the border. Nassib, known as Jaber on the Jordanian side, is the two countries' only passenger and commercial crossing and a key overland trade route, and no reopening date has been given.

Latest · Middle East · Iran Diplomacy27 October 2026

“Very Good” Talks in Tehran: Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani Security Chiefs Meet Iran's Leaders to Stop the War Restarting

Senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks in Tehran on Monday aimed at preventing the eight-month Middle East war from reigniting, in the first joint visit since the three formed their Makkah defence alliance in August. Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin met President Masoud Pezeshkian, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and security chief Mohsen Rezaei. No concrete deal was announced — the sides agreed to continue discussions — but Rezaei told state television the talks were “very good,” saying a “new atmosphere” was emerging and that the four agreed regional security should be achieved by the region's own countries without foreign intervention, with Israel's actions in Lebanon, Syria, Gaza and the West Bank high on the agenda. Saudi Arabia said the visit sought open channels to “prevent the return of military escalation.” The meeting came as Washington, Tehran and Israel have all warned hostilities could resume, and as President Trump has repeated that the US may strike Iran again.

Eight Days Before the Midterms, Trump Puts a $1 Million Price on Voter-Fraud Tips That Lead to a Conviction

President Donald Trump announced on Monday that anyone who reports voter fraud — in past elections or the upcoming 2026 midterms — will be paid $1 million if the tip leads to an arrest and conviction. “Anybody reporting Voter Fraud in the past Elections, or the upcoming 2026 Midterm Election, will be paid $1,000,000 upon the arrest and conviction of those that created the Fraud!” he wrote on Truth Social before leaving for a rally in Corinth, Mississippi, repeating the offer to reporters under the wing of Air Force One. He did not say whether the money would come from public funds or his own resources, and the White House did not answer questions about how tips should be reported. The offer is the latest in a series of financial pledges ahead of the 3 November vote — including a 1% commission for whistleblowers who uncover fraud in government programmes and a promised $5,000 dividend if Republicans keep Congress — and it lands as polls show a difficult race for his party: a New York Times/Siena survey put his approval at 36% in October, with voters preferring Democratic congressional candidates by eleven points. Election officials and critics call the administration's wider push on voter data and mail ballots federal overreach; the administration says stricter measures are needed for public confidence. Trump also called Brazil's election, won on Sunday by Lula, “rigged,” pointing to a two-hour voting extension he described as strange.

Oil Eases, but Barely: Brent Slips to $115.15 as Tehran Diplomacy Meets a Worst Month for Hormuz Shipping Attacks

Oil prices edged lower early on Tuesday as the Tehran talks between Iran and the Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani delegations briefly eased fears of renewed fighting, but Brent crude stayed close to six-month highs. Brent futures were down 66 cents, or 0.57%, at $115.15 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate fell 44 cents to $101.32 — small moves after Brent touched $117 on Monday and surged more than $117 last week following Iranian attacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz. The losses were limited by fresh Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, which traders say leave little hope of immediate stability. October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in Hormuz and nearby waters since tracking began in 2017, according to data compiled by a Washington-based think tank — a striking fact given the strait carried about a fifth of the world's energy supplies before the conflict erupted in February.

Pakistan Raises Rates to 12% as Energy Prices and Wheat Disruptions Push Inflation Back Above 10%

The State Bank of Pakistan raised its policy rate by 50 basis points to 12% on Monday, effective 27 October, its first increase since June, with nine of ten Monetary Policy Committee members voting in favour. The bank said the intensifying Middle East conflict, persistently high global energy prices and domestic wheat supply disruptions had worsened the inflation outlook since its September meeting, even though growth and external-account forecasts were broadly unchanged. Headline inflation reached 10.3% year-on-year in September, above expectations, driven by energy and wheat prices, while core inflation stayed elevated at about 8.4%. The committee said the rise was needed to anchor expectations, contain second-round effects from the supply shock and return inflation to its 5–7% target over the medium term. Governor Jameel Ahmad put expected growth at 3.5–4.5% for the fiscal year, with exports projected at $32.5 billion, imports at $71 billion and workers' remittances at $45 billion, while foreign-exchange reserves held around $21.5 billion — about three months of import cover — and a staff-level agreement with the IMF was reached.

“Unprecedented Scale, Speed and Sophistication”: Australia's Cyber Agency Says AI Has Redefined the Threat

Australia's signals intelligence agency warned on Tuesday that artificial intelligence poses an “unprecedented” threat as foreign governments and criminals use it to hack networks and critical infrastructure. In its annual cyber threat report, the Australian Signals Directorate said AI “has been the defining force in the cyber threat landscape over the past year, allowing malicious actors to find vulnerabilities faster and exploit them at unprecedented scale, speed and sophistication,” and noted new risks in recent weeks from the unintended actions of AI agents and model testing. The numbers behind the warning are steep: the directorate issued more than 4,400 warnings about potentially malicious activity in the past year, up 80% on the previous twelve months, and responded to over 1,500 incidents. State-sponsored actors linked to China, Russia and North Korea have targeted Australian government networks, critical infrastructure, businesses and individuals alike, mainly to steal sensitive information. Defence Minister Richard Marles urged Australians to update and harden their systems, saying AI “is here and it's real” — while the agency itself used AI tools to help compile the report, part of a global rush in recent days to fix a critical flaw in a widely used programming language first found using a powerful OpenAI model.

Cyclone Arnab Heads for India's Andhra Coast — and Its Remnants Could Soak Nepal From Thursday

Nepal is bracing for moderate to heavy rain and Himalayan snowfall later this week as westerly winds combine with Cyclone Arnab, a storm that formed over the Bay of Bengal in the early hours of Monday — the first cyclonic storm of 2026 in the North Indian Ocean. India's meteorological department expects Arnab to strengthen into a severe and possibly very severe cyclonic storm before crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast on Wednesday, with one forecast putting winds near 125 km/h at landfall and a red alert issued for six Andhra districts. Nepal's forecasters expect cloud to build over Koshi, Bagmati, Gandaki and Lumbini provinces from Monday night, with rain and snow chances rising from Wednesday and the heaviest falls on Thursday and Friday — some places possibly very heavy. One Nepali forecast identified 29 districts at risk, with parts of Bagmati potentially receiving more than 150 mm of rain over four days. Trekkers, travellers and farmers have been advised to follow bulletins closely, as the system arrives barely two weeks after the monsoon withdrew on 14 October, over sea temperatures experts note are unusually warm for the season.

Latest · Americas · Health26 October 2026

Two Months, No Cull: Bird Flu Keeps Spreading on a Utah Mink Farm While Officials Say They Have Few Options

An H5N1 bird flu outbreak at a Utah mink farm west of Provo has been spreading since August without being brought under control, prompting public-health experts to warn that a mammal farm is exactly where the virus gets chances to adapt. The US Department of Agriculture confirmed H5N1 in captive mink from samples collected on 7 August, and owner Chad Fenn has said the virus is still spreading, albeit more slowly, through a herd of thousands raised for fur, with state records pointing to expected losses around 80%. A ProPublica investigation published on 23 October found farmworkers were not being routinely monitored, despite CDC guidance recommending ten days of symptom monitoring after last exposure. Scientists are watching mink closely because their airways carry receptors similar to humans'; a preliminary analysis of ten genomes posted on 22 October found all belonged to the B3.13 lineage circulating in US dairy herds, plus a mutation also seen in Spain's 2022 mink outbreak, though no new alarm-bell mammal-adapting changes were identified. The USDA says it has no species-specific authority or dedicated funding to vaccinate, cull or compensate mink, and Utah's state veterinarian has acknowledged there are “not a lot of options” beyond waiting. Officials still rate the risk to the general public as low, and no human-to-human spread has been identified in the United States.
