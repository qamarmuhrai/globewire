Latest · Asia · Pakistan-Afghanistan Border28 October 2026

Pakistan Says Afghan Fire Killed One Person in Waziristan — Troops Responding "Heavily" After Homes and a Market Were Hit

One person was killed and five others were wounded after fire from the Afghan side struck civilian houses and a market in Pakistan's Waziristan area near the shared border, a senior Pakistani district official said on Wednesday. The Deputy Commissioner of South Waziristan Lower described the shooting on Tuesday as unprovoked and said Pakistani forces were responding "effectively and heavily." Taliban spokesperson Zabihullah Mujahid did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The exchange comes only days after Kabul said its differences with Islamabad could be settled through diplomacy. The two neighbours, once close allies, have grown increasingly hostile over militancy: Pakistan accuses the Afghan authorities of sheltering fighters who launch cross-border attacks, while the Taliban government rejects the charge and calls militancy an internal Pakistani problem. Fighting between the two sides has already killed hundreds of people this year.

Trump Says the Iran War Can End "Whenever I Want It To" — as Gulf States Are Warned of a Possible Short, Intense Campaign After the Midterms

US President Donald Trump told a campaign rally in Lubbock, Texas, that the war with Iran could end "very quick" but only on his terms, saying Tehran must state clearly that it will never obtain a nuclear weapon. The White House declined to comment on specific plans but said all options remain on the table to prevent an Iranian nuclear weapon. In Moscow, Qatar's emir and Iran's president also discussed the war, the blocked Strait of Hormuz and energy supplies on the sidelines of a gas forum.

Latest · Americas · Brazil Election27 October 2026

Flávio Bolsonaro Reaches the United States After Losing Brazil's Runoff — Defying a Court Order to Surrender His Passports

Defeated Brazilian presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro, the son of jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, has left Brazil for the United States despite a Supreme Court order barring him from travelling and requiring him to hand over his passports, according to US officials and multiple media reports. A senior State Department official said Washington would issue visas to Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry. Bolsonaro, 45, lost Sunday's runoff to incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who took 51.79% of the vote, refused to concede, and is under investigation for alleged corruption and money laundering — a probe in which he denies wrongdoing and which will cost him his parliamentary immunity after the defeat. A police source said his Brazilian and diplomatic passports had been cancelled and that there was no formal record of his departure, though he has also held Italian nationality since 2023. His campaign manager said he had received only a brief text message and had no news of his whereabouts. Lula called the departure "shameful," saying: "Brazil is their second nation, the U.S. is their first." The move is expected to deepen tensions between Brasília and Washington, after Lula accused the Trump administration of meddling in the election.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations28 October 2026

UN Votes for the 34th Time on Ending the US Embargo on Cuba — After a Shouting Match Halted the Assembly Debate

The UN General Assembly votes on Wednesday on Cuba's annual resolution calling for an end to the decades-old US economic embargo, a non-binding measure Havana has brought every year since 1992 and which has always passed by a wide margin — last year by 165 votes to 7, with 12 abstentions. This year's debate turned confrontational on Tuesday when US Ambassador Mike Waltz criticised Cuba's government from the podium and Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez walked toward him demanding a point of order, calling him a liar. Waltz replied that the UN was "not your communist dictatorship" and told the Cuban delegation to step aside, prompting the Assembly president to suspend the session briefly until order was restored. Cuba says Washington has mounted an unprecedented campaign — including an oil blockade and pressure through its embassies — to push countries toward voting no or abstaining, while eleven former Caribbean leaders signed an open letter urging all nations to back the resolution, calling the embargo a breach of international law and humanitarian principles. Havana estimates the embargo cost its economy about $8.08 billion between March 2025 and February 2026 alone. President Trump has also recently raised the possibility of US warships stopping in Cuba on their way back from the Iran war.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab28 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab's Landfall Grinds On Along the Andhra Coast: Visakhapatnam Past 22 cm of Rain as NDRF Crews Clear Felled Trees in Yanam

The landfall of very severe cyclonic storm Arnab was still continuing along the Andhra Pradesh coast on Wednesday, with the storm's centre hugging the shoreline west of the Bay of Bengal — near 16.4°N, 82.0°E, about 30 km east of Narsapur and 70 km south-southwest of Kakinada — the India Meteorological Department said. The northern spiral band has pushed further inland and the crossing, between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam close to Yanam, is expected to take several more hours, with maximum sustained winds up to 135 km/h and gusts to 150 km/h. Rainfall so far has been heaviest at Visakhapatnam, at more than 22 cm, followed by Kalingapatnam above 18 cm, Kakinada above 15 cm and Machilipatnam above 13 cm. National Disaster Response Force teams are clearing uprooted trees and debris in Yanam, where strong winds have brought down electricity poles, damaged houses and blocked movement. The slow-moving system follows the evacuation of more than 180,000 people in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha reported earlier in the day, with hundreds of schools serving as relief shelters and no deaths reported so far.

Latest · Business & Technology · AI Chips28 October 2026

Malaysia Seizes $12.1 Million Worth of AI Chips at Kuala Lumpur Airport — Servers Falsely Declared as Ordinary Electronics

Malaysian customs officers have seized advanced artificial intelligence chips worth about 49.5 million ringgit ($12.11 million) at Kuala Lumpur International Airport this month, as authorities move against a syndicate suspected of smuggling high-performance semiconductors through the country's main gateway. Airport customs director Aspalila Ag Tuah told reporters that officers inspecting the free trade zone on 15 October found 30 server units containing the chips, shipped from one Asian country for re-export to another without the required trade permit. The servers had been falsely declared as ordinary electronic components, she said, apparently so the onward shipment would face no restrictions, and investigators believe the same network was behind a similar attempt foiled in June. A Malaysian company that facilitated the shipment has been called in to assist the investigation. Malaysia tightened controls last year on the movement of US-origin advanced AI chips, after Washington pressed it to stop the hardware flowing onward to China.

Merz Pitches Closer Europe-Asia Ties in Singapore and Warns on China — as the EU Unveils a Climate Resilience Plan After €200 Billion in Damage

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz used a speech in Singapore on Tuesday to argue that Europe and Asia will be stronger if they coordinate on trade and security, warning that both regions face pressure from great powers — above all China, whose surging, increasingly high-end exports risk hollowing out European industry. "Your security has a direct bearing on ours," Merz said, adding that China bears a particular responsibility for order and stability in the Indo-Pacific and that "wise China policy begins at home" with a united, competitive Europe. The trip, which continues to Japan and South Korea, follows a new consensus between Merz and French President Emmanuel Macron on tougher EU trade tools, with the bloc preparing safeguard measures that could curb Chinese imports in sectors such as chemicals, furniture and machinery. In Brussels, the European Commission separately presented a climate resilience framework on Tuesday, citing roughly €200 billion in economic losses between 2022 and 2025 and warning that Europe is warming at twice the global rate. In Germany, meanwhile, chemicals group BASF raised its 2026 core profit outlook for a second time, to €7.4–7.8 billion, as Hormuz-related supply disruption lifted demand for European producers.

Indonesia Deploys Hundreds of Security Personnel in Jakarta Ahead of Student Protests Over Fires and Spending

Hundreds of police and military personnel, backed by armoured vehicles, took up positions across central Jakarta on Wednesday ahead of planned student protests against several of President Prabowo Subianto's policies and his government's handling of forest fires in Sumatra and Borneo. Students from the University of Indonesia said demonstrations under the hashtag #IndonesiaAware would be held at several locations in the capital. Their demands include declaring the wildfires a national disaster and halting spending on populist programmes such as the president's free meals scheme. Protests have also taken place in haze-affected areas, including in Central Kalimantan last week. The show of force comes two years into Prabowo's presidency, during which his approval ratings have fallen steadily in surveys by pollster SMRC. Indonesia's last major wave of protests was in August, when hundreds of people were detained; it was not yet clear how many students would turn out on Wednesday.

Latest · Americas · US Strikes at Sea28 October 2026

US Military Says Five Killed in Latest Strike on a Vessel in the Eastern Pacific — Toll From the Boat Campaign Passes 230

The US military said five people were killed on Tuesday in a strike on a vessel in the Eastern Pacific, the latest in a campaign of attacks that Washington says targets drug traffickers but which human rights experts condemn as extrajudicial killings. US Southern Command said on X that it had carried out a "lethal, kinetic strike" on a fast boat travelling along established trafficking routes, citing confirmed intelligence that the vessel was actively involved in drug trafficking, without providing further detail, and described those killed as "narco-terrorists." The Trump administration maintains that the boats it has struck since September 2025 were carrying narcotics; the strikes in the Caribbean and the Eastern Pacific have now killed more than 230 people in total. Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International and independent UN experts have called the attacks unlawful, while the American Civil Liberties Union has dismissed the administration's claims against those targeted as unsubstantiated.
