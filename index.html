Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Breakthrough11 November 2026

Abu Dhabi Talks End With Agreement on a Large-Scale Prisoner Exchange, and US Envoys Head to Moscow on Thursday

Two days of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi closed on Wednesday with Russia and Ukraine agreeing to a large-scale prisoner exchange, according to US envoy Jared Kushner, who said the numbers would be finalised on Thursday and described the deal as the biggest humanitarian release yet discussed. Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky confirmed the agreement. Kushner said the sides also made progress on how to deconflict forces and how a future ceasefire could be monitored, and announced that he and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet President Vladimir Putin. Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky called the round constructive and substantive, while Russia’s Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said it was too early for a full assessment. The optimism came with a reminder that the war continues: Ukraine’s prime minister said Russian strikes hit six large electricity substations across four central regions on Wednesday, and core disputes over territory and long-term security guarantees remain unresolved.

Latest · Americas · US — Hegseth Impeachment Blocked11 November 2026

Trump Calls the Blocked Hegseth Impeachment an “Exoneration” as Iran Accuses Washington of Diplomacy Through Threats

The Republican-led US House voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday to refer eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, effectively ending the effort in the current Congress. The articles, introduced by Republican Representative Thomas Massie and backed by Democrats, accused Hegseth of unlawfully directing operations in Iran, Yemen and Venezuela, including the capture of Venezuela’s president in January, without congressional authorisation. President Donald Trump praised the outcome on social media as an exoneration and gave Hegseth top marks, while a Reuters/Ipsos poll found 71 percent of US adults want the incoming Democratic-majority Congress, which takes office in January, to legislate an end to the Iran war. In Tehran, President Masoud Pezeshkian accused Washington of conducting diplomacy through threats, and Iran wrote to the UN and the IAEA protesting a US offer of up to $20 million for information on its nuclear programme, which Tehran called repugnant and dangerous for its scientists.

Tanker Burns in Hormuz After a Mine Strike, a Second Vessel Is Hit in the Gulf, and Insurers Put Gulf War Losses Near $790 Billion by 2030

Iran’s army said on Wednesday that an oil tanker struck a naval mine in a restricted area of the eastern Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, the latest incident in a waterway Tehran has restricted since the war began in late February, insisting ships use routes it authorises while the United States maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports and escorts tankers through. Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Leaders’ Summit11 November 2026

“The Climate Crisis Is Outrunning Us”: Guterres Opens the COP31 Leaders’ Summit as Turkey Pushes a 35% Electrification Goal

Heads of state and government from more than 100 countries began two days of speeches at the COP31 leaders’ summit in Antalya on Wednesday, with UN Secretary-General António Guterres warning that the slow global response has put the world on a path to mutually assured destruction. A UN report last month found the world on course to breach the 1.5°C warming limit set under the Paris Agreement. Host Turkey is pressing for electrification to anchor the final deal, proposing that electricity rise to 35 percent of global energy use by 2035, up from just over a fifth today, to cut emissions from transport, factories and buildings. Wednesday’s speakers include Brazil’s President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, Chinese Vice President Han Zheng and Marshall Islands President Hilda Heine. The United States, the largest historical emitter, is absent for a second straight year after President Trump withdrew from climate cooperation in 2025. The conference runs until 20 November.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — Ebola Outbreak11 November 2026

Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Passes 11,042 Cases and 5,387 Deaths, Now the Second-Largest and Second-Deadliest on Record

Government data released on Tuesday put the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s Ebola outbreak at 11,042 confirmed cases and at least 5,387 deaths, making it the country’s deadliest outbreak and the second-largest ever recorded, behind only the 2014–2016 West African epidemic. The outbreak, declared in mid-May and caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, has spread to eight of Congo’s 26 provinces, concentrated in Ituri and North Kivu, and cases have also been reported in Kenya and Nigeria in the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak to date. There is no approved vaccine or specific treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, many deaths have occurred outside treatment centres, and health authorities say armed conflict, poor roads, a strained health system and community mistrust are hampering the response. Congolese scientists have sequenced more than 1,500 viral genomes in under six months, and experimental vaccine candidates are being studied in a WHO-supported trial that began enrolling in Bunia in October.

Latest · Europe · NATO — Espionage Arrest in Britain10 November 2026

US Army Officer Assigned to a NATO Headquarters in England Is Arrested on Suspicion of Spying for Russia; the Kremlin Denies Any Link

British counterterrorism police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier near Gloucester on Tuesday on suspicion of offences under Sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023, covering protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service, with police confirming the allegations relate to Russia. The unnamed soldier, described by US officials as a field-grade officer entrusted with sensitive information, was serving with NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps at Imjin Barracks, the alliance’s British-led rapid-deployment headquarters. The Metropolitan Police said the arrest was made working closely with US authorities, the US military and the FBI, whose director called it an important arrest, and that the case is not linked to other counterterrorism investigations. The soldier is in custody at a London police station and officers have searched addresses in Gloucestershire. No charge had been announced by Wednesday, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia has no connection to the case whatsoever.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Impeachment Move11 November 2026

120 Indonesian Lawmakers Formally Seek an Opinion Against Vice-President Gibran, With a Plenary Vote Set for Thursday

The crisis over Indonesia’s Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka escalated on Wednesday when 120 members of the House of Representatives formally submitted a proposal to use the chamber’s right to express an opinion on his position, with a plenary session scheduled for Thursday, 12 November. The move follows Gibran’s disclosure on Tuesday that he had told President Prabowo Subianto, in a meeting lasting about two and a half hours, that he intended to resign after the Supreme Court voided the certificate recognising his overseas schooling as equivalent to an Indonesian secondary qualification and the Constitutional Court found no evidence of the diploma he used to register as a candidate. The government clarified that no formal resignation letter has been submitted and that Gibran remains in office and on duty. Analysts say a vacancy would trigger a contest among coalition parties for the vice-presidency and deepen the rift between Prabowo and Gibran’s father, former president Joko Widodo, whose backing helped deliver the 2024 landslide.

Bus Rams 32 Vehicles at a Traffic Light Near Seoul: Nine Seriously Hurt and 48 With Minor Injuries, Driver Cites Brake Failure

A city bus crashed into vehicles waiting at a traffic light in Bundang, Seongnam, south of Seoul, at about 7:34 a.m. on Wednesday, involving 32 vehicles in total — four other buses, a cargo truck and 27 passenger cars — before hitting a median barrier. The National Fire Agency said nine people were seriously injured and 48 suffered minor injuries, for a total of 57 casualties, none reported as life-threatening. Emergency crews set up a treatment station at the scene in the Yatap-dong area. The driver, a man in his 60s, was taken to hospital, tested negative for alcohol and told investigators the brakes had failed. Police say they will establish the cause using the bus’s recording data and CCTV footage from the junction, and are also checking other possible factors including drug use.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi Air11 November 2026

Delhi’s Air Eases to an AQI of 299 but Stays “Poor,” With Younger Pupils Shifted to Hybrid Classes and Stage III Curbs Still in Force

Delhi’s average Air Quality Index improved to 299 on Wednesday morning, down from 307 on Tuesday and 376 on Monday, according to the Central Pollution Control Board, but the capital’s air remains in the “poor” band and many monitoring stations still read “very poor.” Stage III restrictions under the Graded Response Action Plan, in force since 6 November, remain in place, and younger school pupils have been shifted to hybrid classes as a precaution. The post-Diwali debate over causes continues: an Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology assessment attributed 71.1 percent of the city’s PM2.5 in the early hours of 9 November to firecrackers, while Punjab, which logged 5,002 farm fires between 15 September and 10 November, contests the emphasis on stubble burning. Forecasters expect winds to bring some further relief over the next two days, though no return to clean air is in sight.
