Latest · Americas · Brazil26 October 2026

Lula Wins a Fourth Term: Brazil's President Beats Flávio Bolsonaro in a Runoff Comeback No One Had Managed Since 1989

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won Brazil's presidential runoff, defeating Senator Flávio Bolsonaro to secure a fourth term in office, the Superior Electoral Court confirmed on Sunday night. With 99.77% of polling sections counted, Lula took 51.76% of valid votes — 64,078,503 ballots — against 48.24%, or 59,728,246, for Bolsonaro, a margin of more than 4.3 million votes. The result makes Lula, who turns 81 this week, the first candidate since Brazil restored direct presidential elections in 1989 to win the presidency after finishing second in the first round: Bolsonaro, the 45-year-old eldest son of jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, had led the October 4 vote by 47.03% to 45.16%. Voting on Sunday was extended by two hours by a Supreme Court justice after transport problems and severe weather disrupted access to polling stations — a move Bolsonaro's camp could still challenge in court. The campaign was dominated by the cost of living, crime and corruption allegations touching both families. Lula's new term begins on January 5, 2027, and his victory halts a run of far-right wins across Latin America by candidates aligned with US President Donald Trump, a frequent target of Lula's criticism.

“Don't Test Us”: Netanyahu Warns Iran May Attack to Disrupt Tuesday's Election, as Trump Poses a Deal-or-Destruction Choice

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has warned that Iran and its allies may launch attacks to disrupt Israel's general election on Tuesday, telling Tehran it would face “disproportionate blows” in response. Standing at an Arrow anti-missile battery at Palmachim airbase on Sunday, Netanyahu said Israel had “intelligence signs that our enemies, Iran and its proxies, could challenge us with attacks in order to disrupt the elections,” and that the military was prepared for both defence and offence. Hours earlier he had posted an AI-generated image of a mushroom cloud over Tel Aviv — a post that, according to Axios reporting, alarmed White House officials who feared it could provoke a miscalculation in Tehran. President Donald Trump told Fox News there would be no strike on Iran that night, while framing Washington's choice starkly: “Do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off? That's the only question.” Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, replied that a resumption of the war — now in its ninth month — would not change Tehran's policies: “They have already done so twice and have seen the results.” Netanyahu's rivals accuse him of fearmongering to salvage a campaign the final polls showed him losing; his own military chief, Eyal Zamir, was reported last week to see no operational need to strike Iran before the vote.

Machado Comes Home: Venezuela's Opposition Leader Ends Ten Months in Exile, to Crowds — and American Credit-Claiming

María Corina Machado, Venezuela's best-known opposition leader and Nobel Peace Prize laureate, returned home on Sunday after nearly ten months abroad, walking into a church in Barquisimeto, in Lara state, to applause, tears and chants of “María! María!” before joining a caravan of supporters heading for Caracas. Her arrival ends at least seven failed attempts to return since June. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio moved quickly to claim the moment for Washington, writing that the conditions for her safe return were “the direct result of the US supported reconciliation talks,” which he said had already freed hundreds of political prisoners and brought hundreds of exiles home — and which offer Venezuela its best chance in thirty years of “not just a free and fair election but an enduring democracy.” The politics are delicate: Venezuela has been run by interim president Delcy Rodríguez, a longtime ally of Nicolás Maduro, since US forces ousted Maduro in January, and the US-backed talks have proceeded with a rival opposition faction while Machado's movement was shut out; the latest round ended last week with agreements to review judicial reforms but no date for a presidential election. Asked about her return, President Donald Trump said he knew nothing about it, then added: “She's a very nice woman. She gave me her Nobel Prize. So how can I say anything else? And I hope she's safe.”

Four Killed in Dnipropetrovsk as Russia and Ukraine Trade Another Night of Strikes Ahead of Possible Talks

Russian strikes killed four people and injured 22, including an 11-year-old girl, in Ukraine's Dnipropetrovsk region on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, in another night of attacks that damaged nine regions ahead of possible new US-mediated negotiations between Kyiv and Moscow. A drone hit a high-rise residential building in Vinnytsia, injuring six, and in Kharkiv Russian forces struck a home-improvement hypermarket and then, Zelenskyy said, carried out a “double-tap” strike on the emergency crews who arrived, injuring first responders. Overnight targets included thermal power plants, electrical substations, coal mines, grain storage and shopping centres; by Zelenskyy's count, Russia has launched around 1,600 guided aerial bombs, 48 missiles and nearly 2,300 drones at Ukraine in the past week alone. Russia also reported casualties: authorities in the border region of Belgorod said Ukrainian attacks killed three people and injured 14, damaging dozens of apartment blocks. In Moscow, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov argued that Europe should be included in any peace talks, saying Washington had been unable to pressure Kyiv into accepting a deal.

Egypt's Parliament Votes Today on Military Support for Saudi Arabia — While Cairo Pushes Yemen Diplomacy

Egypt's parliament votes today on whether to authorise the country's armed forces to support Saudi Arabia against the Iran-aligned Houthis, a decision that could draw one of the region's largest militaries directly into the widening conflict. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi called an urgent session to approve the deployment; after a closed sitting on Sunday, lawmakers postponed the vote until Monday for further deliberation. Cairo has been careful to define the mission's limits: Sisi has told Riyadh — in a letter hand-delivered last week by Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty and intelligence chief Hassan Rashad to Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman — that Egyptian support would be confined to defending Saudi territory and borders, not attacking Yemen. At the same time, Egypt is publicly pressing the diplomatic track. Abdelatty said Cairo wants to “explore every opportunity” for a political settlement, including a full Houthi withdrawal from territory the group seized during its September advance near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the Red Sea chokepoint guarding the approaches to Egypt's own Suez Canal.

Thousands Fill Prague's Old Town Square to Defend Public Media From a Funding Overhaul They Call a Takeover

Several thousand people rallied in Prague's Old Town Square on Sunday against government plans to abolish the licence fees that fund Czech public television and radio and pay for them instead from the state budget — a change protesters say would put the broadcasters at the mercy of politicians. The lower house of parliament voted on Saturday to fast-track the bill in a rare overnight sitting, and demonstrators chanted “4:36” — the hour of the vote — alongside “We've had enough.” The reform, backed by Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's coalition, which includes two far-right parties, would cut the broadcasters' budgets by around 15%; it still needs upper-house approval, and the opposition has pledged a constitutional-court challenge. The European Broadcasting Union, the European Federation of Journalists, Reporters Without Borders and the International Press Institute have all criticised the plan. Among the protesters was 94-year-old Milan Kotal, who recalled that Czech television had twice been taken over by the state in his lifetime — “once by the Nazis during the war and then by the Communists until 1989.” Babiš insists funding would be guaranteed by law as a mandatory expenditure and that “no one wants to interfere with programming or news coverage.” A larger demonstration is planned for November 15, and public-media staff are set for a warning strike on Monday.

Evacuations Widen as Cyclone Arnab Strengthens in the Bay of Bengal, Two Days From a “Very Severe” Landfall

The storm that will become Cyclone Arnab intensified into a deep depression over the Bay of Bengal on Sunday and is expected to be named a cyclonic storm on Monday morning, the India Meteorological Department says, before strengthening to a severe storm on Tuesday and a very severe one ahead of landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Wednesday forenoon, with winds of 115–125 km/h gusting to 140 km/h. Odisha, which expects the heaviest rain rather than the strongest winds, has deployed 60 state disaster-response teams and 17 national teams, with 225 fire-service teams on high alert, cancelled leave for officials in critical posts and sent eight ministers to vulnerable districts under a stated zero-casualty objective. A red warning for extremely heavy rainfall covers five southern districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri — where schools and colleges will stay closed on October 28 and 29, and fishermen have been told to stay ashore. Andhra Pradesh has put its health department on alert, ordering uninterrupted emergency medical services in government hospitals from October 27 to 30.

A Magnitude 5.9 Tremor Rattles Tibet Again, Three Days After the Quake That Killed 16 and Drove Out 12,000

A shallow magnitude 5.9 earthquake struck Tibet's Nagqu prefecture on Sunday evening, the European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre said, at a depth of about 9 km — three days after a magnitude 7.0 quake in the same high-altitude region killed at least 16 people. China's earthquake agency put the new tremor at 5.7 in Baqing County, with a second quake of 5.6 following about a minute later near Zadoi County in neighbouring Qinghai; no casualties from Sunday's shaking had been reported. Thursday's 7.0 quake, the strongest to hit China since January 2024, collapsed at least 339 homes and 20 livestock sheds, injured 76 people and forced the evacuation of 12,489 residents around Baqên County; the zone has since recorded more than 800 aftershocks. Beijing has deployed some 1,600 rescue personnel, along with satellites and drones, but relief work is hampered by altitude — the epicentre sits at roughly 4,800 metres — rockfall-blocked roads and snow in the forecast. The Tibetan Plateau, where the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates collide, is among the most seismically active regions on Earth.

Nigeria's Ebola Watchlist Grows to 53 Contacts as Abuja Races to Ring-Fence West Africa's First Case of the Outbreak

Nigeria has widened its hunt for possible Ebola cases, tracing nine more contacts of the country's only confirmed patient and bringing the watchlist to 53 people, the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention said on Sunday. The first 44 contacts were health workers at the Abuja facility where the patient was treated; the nine new names add more medical staff plus members of her family and household. The patient, a 42-year-old woman married to a Swiss embassy employee, had travelled in Uganda before returning to Nigeria on October 3; she fell ill days later and was confirmed on October 20 with the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola — West Africa's first case of the outbreak devastating the Democratic Republic of Congo, and a strain for which there is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment. A suspected case in Rivers State has tested negative, though officials say two further suspected cases are being assessed. Screening has been stepped up for international arrivals, but reporters observed only generic health forms at the land border with Benin and no screening on domestic flights. The World Health Organization warns Africa now faces a “complex” outbreak, with the virus present in the continent's west, east and centre.

Latest · Global Affairs · Health24 October 2026

1.5 Billion Children Now Live With Dangerous Heat: UN Agencies Launch the First Scorecard for Tracking What It Does to Them

An estimated 1.5 billion children worldwide are exposed to heatwaves that are growing more frequent, longer and more severe, the World Health Organization says, as it and three other UN agencies launched the first standard set of indicators for tracking what extreme heat does to pregnant women, newborns and children. The framework, developed with the World Meteorological Organization, UNICEF and the UN Population Fund, asks countries to monitor preterm births, stillbirths, low birthweight, pregnancy hypertension, child deaths and heat-related clinic visits alongside temperature data — and even school attendance, as a measure of how heat disrupts children's lives. The evidence behind it is stark: heat exposure is associated with a 26% increase in the odds of preterm birth, and babies under one year old experienced 389% more heatwave days in 2024 than in the 1986–2005 baseline. “As our world continues to heat up, it creates new risks for health, and older people, pregnant women, newborns and children are among those least able to escape them,” said Bruce Gordon, acting director of the WHO's environment and climate department. “Our health systems need the data to recognize this reality and take action.”
