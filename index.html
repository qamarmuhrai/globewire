Iran Warns It Will Strike Back Across “Nine Countries” as US Embassies Issue Security Alerts

An adviser to Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has warned that Tehran will hit “nine countries” if the United States again strikes its military bases or petrochemical sites, as the war entered its eighth month with fighting spreading across the region. Several US embassies in the Middle East have sent identical security alerts to American citizens, citing the threat of attack from Iran or the Iran-backed Houthis. Iran’s foreign minister said Washington started the war “without Iran doing anything wrong” and still cannot find a way out, while President Donald Trump insists the US will “soon finish the job.” Diplomacy is also moving: Pakistan’s army chief Asim Munir is due in Tehran on Tuesday, Russia’s foreign minister is also expected in Iran, and Egypt’s foreign minister has urged his Iranian counterpart to work for de-escalation. Oil prices slipped on Tuesday as investors weighed a Trump executive order waiving some sanctions and tariffs on Russian energy exports.

US Defense Secretary Hegseth Meets Netanyahu in Unannounced Israel Visit, a Week Before the Election

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Israel on Monday and met Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Axios reported, exactly one week before Israel’s Knesset election — the country’s first vote since the Hamas attack of October 7, 2023, which plunged the region into war in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. The Pentagon and Israel’s embassy in Washington did not immediately comment. The visit comes as Washington and Jerusalem discuss the possibility of resuming the war against Iran, and a day after President Trump endorsed Netanyahu for a “tremendous” election win. Vice President JD Vance pointedly declined to do the same, saying he endorses American candidates and is “not getting involved in Israeli elections.”

Latest · Global Affairs · Ukraine19 October 2026

Zelenskyy Accuses Washington of Deliberately Holding Up Interceptor Missiles Ukraine Has Already Paid For

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told US senators on Monday that the Pentagon is delaying delivery of interceptor missiles already paid for by European countries under the Prioritised Ukraine Requirements List (PURL) programme, saying the shipment is still awaiting Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s signature. “The missiles are ready, and they could already be in Ukraine,” he said. “But unfortunately, there is still no approval.” He added that several European countries are ready to hand over Patriot interceptors from their own stockpiles but also need US authorisation, and suggested a political decision had been taken to slow support for Kyiv. Ukraine also says it received no warning before powerful Russian missile strikes — a claim Washington denies, insisting intelligence-sharing has not changed. President Trump, who has blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries for high fuel prices ahead of next month’s midterms, responded that it was “time for somebody else to negotiate on behalf of Ukraine.”

President Donald Trump said on Monday he is prepared to meet Russia’s Vladimir Putin and China’s Xi Jinping in China next month, saying any summit would centre on nuclear weapons and denuclearisation but could also take up the Ukraine war. Latest · Europe · France19 October 2026

France Orders Partial Curfews After a Weekend of Riots, and Macron Refuses His Interior Minister’s Resignation

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has ordered regional authorities to impose “partial curfews” in selected urban areas after two days of violent unrest in which town halls were set on fire and hundreds of businesses looted. At least 2,400 people have been arrested and nearly 1,000 police officers injured. President Emmanuel Macron, who voiced “anger” and “shame” over the rioting, criticised security “failures” but refused the resignation of Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez, saying continued leadership of law enforcement was essential; he ordered Nuñez to deliver full transparency on the planning and intelligence failures and to put new policing methods in place. The protests — called by unions, students and activists seeking to revive the yellow vest movement — drew an estimated 550,000 marchers on Saturday by official count, with organisers claiming 800,000, amid anger over living costs and poor conditions in schools.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan20 October 2026

Japan Launches the First Mission to Bring Back Samples from a Moon of Mars

Japan’s space agency JAXA launched its Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft before dawn on Tuesday, sending it toward Phobos, the larger of Mars’s two moons, on the first mission ever intended to collect material from the Martian system and return it to Earth. The H3 rocket lifted off from the Tanegashima Space Center at 4:41 a.m. local time — the launcher’s first interplanetary flight, after a failed debut in 2023 followed by a run of mostly successful launches. MMX is due to reach Mars in 2027, spend about three years studying Phobos and Deimos and land on Phobos twice to gather at least 10 grams of surface material, with the sample capsule set to land in Australia in 2031. Scientists hope the samples will settle a century-old question — whether the moons are captured asteroids or debris from a colossal impact on Mars — and shed light on how water and organic material were spread through the early Solar System.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China20 October 2026

China Resumes Buying US Soybeans as Shipments Bought Under the Trade Truce Reach Its Ports

China imported 360,878 metric tons of soybeans from the United States in September, customs data showed on Tuesday — up from zero in the same month a year earlier, when trade tensions prompted Chinese buyers to hold off on the US harvest until after an October summit between the two countries’ leaders. The cargoes arriving now were bought under the deal that ended last year’s trade war; Beijing has since purchased around 12 million tons of US soybeans, still well below pre-trade-war levels, and traders say China has covered more than 60% of its 25-million-ton buying target for the 2026/27 season. Brazil remains the dominant supplier: its shipments to China dipped 2.4% to 10.7 million tons in September but rose 3.4% over January–September to 65.84 million tons, while US shipments in the same nine months fell 34% to 11.06 million tons. China’s total soybean imports in September were 11.88 million tons, down 7.7% year on year.

Gunmen Kill More Than 40 Civilians in Attacks on Camps in Northeastern Mali

More than 40 civilians were killed in weekend attacks by armed men on camps in Mali’s northeastern Menaka region, security and community sources told AFP on Monday. The Menaka region, near the border with Niger, has been contested for years by jihadist groups linked to Islamic State and al-Qaeda, and civilians in remote encampments have repeatedly borne the brunt of the violence.

Latest · Africa · South Africa19 October 2026

Ramaphosa Tells South Africa to Prepare for Losing Its Duty-Free Access to the US Market

President Cyril Ramaphosa said on Monday that South Africa should “prepare” for the possibility of being removed from AGOA, Washington’s duty-free trade programme for Africa, as relations between the two countries continue to fray. The African Growth and Opportunity Act gives eligible sub-Saharan African countries preferential access to the US market and has been renewed until the end of 2028, but South Africa’s place in it is now in doubt. Losing the preference would expose South African exporters — from carmakers to citrus and wine producers — to full US tariffs in one of their most important markets.

Christiane Amanpour to Leave CNN Next June, Ending a 43-Year Career at the Network

Christiane Amanpour, CNN’s chief international anchor and one of the most recognisable faces in broadcast news, announced on Monday that she will leave the network next June after 43 years. “After many months of thought and a few sleepless nights, I have decided to separate from CNN, my partner of 43 fantastic years,” the 68-year-old said, adding that she looks forward to new projects and will “always be rooting for CNN” — signing off with her maxim: “always be truthful, not neutral.” Amanpour has hosted her eponymous nightly interview programme on CNN International since 2009; from January 2027 it will move to a weekly slot until it ends in May, and she will keep reporting and travelling for the network until her departure. CNN chairman Mark Thompson called her “one of the most trusted and beloved journalists anywhere” and said the network would take time to celebrate her before she leaves.
