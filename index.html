Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Trump Lands18 November 2026

A Hug on the Tarmac: All Three Leaders Are Now in Shenzhen

The cast for Thursday’s unprecedented summit is complete. President Xi Jinping personally greeted Donald Trump at Shenzhen’s Bao’an airport on Wednesday, the two men shaking hands and embracing on a red carpet — a return of the welcome Trump gave Xi at Joint Base Andrews in September — before a welcome banquet brought Trump, Xi and Vladimir Putin together in one frame. Earlier, Xi and Putin held their fifth meeting of the year, covering trade, energy and finance, with Ukraine and North Korea in a smaller session; Xi said Beijing and Moscow should lead the push toward a multipolar world. Both had used their APEC speeches to attack Western policy, Xi against protectionism and Putin against sanctions he called failed. Thursday’s trilateral, set for 14:30 local time, is expected to range over Ukraine, the Iran war, trade, artificial intelligence and nuclear arms — the last US–Russia cap expired in February — though a senior US official has said no agreement is expected, and Moscow says a full Trump–Putin bilateral is unlikely. Kyiv and Europe, not in the room, fear decisions taken over their heads.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — GCC Condemns Iran18 November 2026

One Voice From the Gulf: Condemnation, a Third Carrier, and Falling Oil

The diplomatic front closed ranks on Wednesday even as the military one widened. The Gulf Cooperation Council’s secretary-general, Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi, condemned Iran’s attacks on Kuwait in the strongest terms and held Tehran responsible for the escalation, with Qatar calling the strikes a dangerous escalation and the UAE condemning the drone attack outright; Kuwait itself called the assault on its sovereignty flagrant, and Bahrain branded Iran’s attacks criminal. Iran’s army chief, Major-General Amir Hatami, said his forces were closely monitoring enemy movements as a third US aircraft carrier arrived in the region — the Theodore Roosevelt sailing alongside the George Washington in footage released by the US military. Tehran has hardened its line since US midterms returned Democratic majorities in both houses, and analysts describe a loop of attack and counterattack around a partly blocked Strait of Hormuz. In a sign of how markets are reading it, oil prices fell despite the overnight strikes and the ADNOC vessel attack that left one crew member missing and 11 injured.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — New Operation18 November 2026

Blocks 660, 661 and 666: Israel Opens a “Concentrated Operation” in Central Gaza

Israel’s military announced on Wednesday that it had begun a concentrated operation in three areas of central Gaza west of the yellow line — the demarcation set under the October 2025 ceasefire — saying it aimed to eliminate a threat to its forces, closing the Salah al-Din road through blocks 660, 661 and 666. The announcement capped a day of continued killing under the truce: medical sources said drone strikes killed Palestinians in Deir al-Balah and in Gaza City’s Sheikh Ajlin neighbourhood, an 11-year-old boy died of wounds from Tuesday’s strike in the north, and forces demolished homes east of the Maghazi camp with explosives dropped by quadcopter while shelling continued east of Khan Younis. Gaza’s Health Ministry puts the toll since the ceasefire took effect at 1,650 killed and 5,975 wounded; Palestinian officials say Israel’s controlled zone has crept from 53% of the enclave to about 63%, squeezing civilians into ever smaller areas.

Latest · Europe · Odesa — Four Killed18 November 2026

Three Waves Before Noon: Odesa’s Historic Centre Takes the Hits

Russia struck the centre of Odesa three times on Wednesday morning, killing four people and injuring nine in the Black Sea port’s historic heart. Regional governor Oleh Kiper and city military chief Serhiy Lysak updated the toll through the morning as successive waves damaged residential buildings, an educational institution, cafés and shops, set a car ablaze and destroyed a three-storey administrative building; emergency crews, including a psychologist photographed comforting a child, worked the scenes. The attacks extend a punishing pattern for the oblast: a crowded market in Chornomorsk was hit on 11 November, killing at least four, and strikes on 7, 9 and 13 November also killed civilians, with a large-scale regional barrage as recently as 15 November. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 141 drones overnight nationwide, of which defences neutralised 126. Odesa, intensively targeted since the summer tourist season, remains both a lifeline port and a front-line city.

€45 Billion Proposed, 113 Entities Sanctioned: Europe Answers the Bridge War

As the armies trade bridges, Europe is moving money and lists. The European Commission has proposed a new €45 billion loan covering two-thirds of Ukraine’s projected 2027 funding gap — Kyiv’s public-sector deficit may reach 17% of GDP that year — while EU foreign ministers have listed 113 more Russian entities, including nearly 50 banks and shadow-fleet counterparties, raising Moscow’s cost of settling payments abroad. Portugal has pledged a further €50 million for weapons and Canada $120 million in military aid, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte told a 17 November contact-group meeting in Brussels that support must flow at the highest possible pace so Ukraine negotiates from strength. On the ground, Russia’s strikes on Kyiv’s Northern and Southern Dnipro bridges — which disrupted heating and water — answered Ukraine’s destruction of the Kerch road bridge to Crimea; Moscow says it downed 1,441 drones in one overnight barrage. Latest · Africa · Sudan — 2,230 Verified Dead18 November 2026

Three of Every Four Killed by Drone: The UN Counts Sudan’s New Way of War

Drones have become the defining weapon of Sudan’s civil war, and the UN has now priced that shift in lives. A Human Rights Office report released in Geneva on Tuesday verified 2,230 civilian deaths between January and October 2026 — more than 1,630 of them, nearly three-quarters, by drone strike; in the first half of the year alone, drones caused over 80% of the 1,390 confirmed killings. The strikes hit markets, hospitals and power stations far beyond the front lines, with Kordofan accounting for over two-thirds of documented casualties and Darfur most of the rest. The report also documents reprisals against alleged collaborators and sexual violence used as a weapon. High Commissioner Volker Türk called the parties’ disregard for the laws of war brazen and said the violations may amount to war crimes, stressing that verified deaths are a floor, not the true toll, in a war between the army and the Rapid Support Forces now in its fourth year.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Trials Approved18 November 2026

60 New Cases a Day, and a First Hope: Two Ebola Vaccines Reach Trial

Congo’s Ebola epidemic is still accelerating, but the response finally has a candidate weapon. National figures to 16 November put the outbreak at 11,376 recorded cases and 5,568 deaths — 60 new infections and 36 deaths in a single day — across eight of the country’s 26 provinces, the largest Ebola outbreak in Congo’s history and the second-largest ever recorded. It is driven by the Bundibugyo strain, which has no licensed vaccine or treatment, and hampered by insecurity: the WHO counts some 80 attacks on health sites and workers, and Africa CDC chief Jean Kaseya says the outbreak remains out of control, with much pledged funding undisbursed or untracked. Against that, the WHO says two experimental vaccines built specifically against Bundibugyo have been cleared for phase 2 of the Tumani trial, with testing expected to begin in Bunia around 26–27 November — Bunia being an early hotspot where cases are now falling. Kenya, by contrast, discharged its last patient on 11 November and has begun its 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free.

Latest · Europe · Canary Islands — 11,037 in 15 Days18 November 2026

An Island Smaller Than Its Arrivals: El Hierro and Spain’s Election Reckoning

For the first time in nearly a month, no migrant boat reached the Canary Islands on Tuesday — a one-day lull after 11,037 people arrived between 1 and 15 November alone. The Atlantic route from West and North Africa has surged since late October: a record 1,580 people were rescued in a single Saturday, and tiny El Hierro, population about 12,000, has taken some 17,000 arrivals this year, straining reception centres at the port of La Restinga. Most arrivals set out from Senegal, Mali, Gambia, Guinea and Mauritania; irregular entries across Spain total 44,238 this year, up 39.4%. Madrid has declared a migration emergency, released €15.18 million plus €1 million for Red Cross health support, and met regional president Fernando Clavijo for a second time in a week as he demands healthcare reinforcements. With a general election on 29 November, the surge — arriving after a year in which Canary arrivals had fallen sharply — has become central campaign fodder.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bay of Bengal — Landfall Friday18 November 2026

Third Cyclone in 21 Days: Akvan Turns Toward Odisha

Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan changed its aim on Wednesday, and Odisha is now in the frame. The India Meteorological Department’s afternoon bulletin moved the expected landfall — around noon on Friday 20 November, with winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110 — to the stretch between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, raising a red cyclone warning for the north Andhra, Yanam and south Odisha coasts; the morning forecast had pointed further south with stronger winds. The storm, the third to form in the Bay of Bengal in 21 days after Arnab and Murasu battered Andhra Pradesh, sat about 770 km south of Puri and accelerating. Odisha has restricted beach tourism, identified 141 landslide-prone sites, begun moving people from vulnerable areas and issued red warnings of extremely heavy rain for six districts, with storm surge expected to flood low-lying coastal districts on both sides of the state line.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Delhi — Deal Done, Not Signed18 November 2026

“More or Less Finalised”: The India–US Deal Waits on Washington’s Tariff Blueprint

India and the United States have finished negotiating and still cannot sign. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said on Wednesday that every aspect of the bilateral trade agreement has been more or less finalised and talks will conclude soon — but the February framework, under which Washington would apply an 18% reciprocal tariff to covered Indian goods, was built on executive tariffs the US Supreme Court has since struck down as unauthorised under emergency-powers law. Until Washington formalises a replacement tariff architecture, including outcomes under Section 301, partner countries cannot sign, he said. The delay has already stretched for months: Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal described the pact in October as in fine-tuning, with zero tariffs envisaged for generic pharmaceuticals, gems and diamonds and aircraft parts, Indian cuts on US industrial and farm goods, and an Indian intention to buy $500 billion of US energy, aircraft, metals, technology and coal over five years. Exporters, meanwhile, trade under the interim stack of duties actually in force.

Latest · Americas · Washington — The Plan Now Due18 November 2026

After the Label, the Homework: America Owes PAHO a Measles Plan Within Weeks

The day after losing its measles-free status, the United States acquired an assignment. PAHO Director Jarbas Barbosa, announcing on Tuesday that endemic transmission has been re-established in the US, Mexico and Bolivia, said countries that lose elimination status must submit a corrective plan — Washington’s is expected within weeks — and then prove 12 consecutive months without sustained transmission to win it back, a process experts say will take years. The CDC count behind the decision stands at 5,412 confirmed cases in 45 states plus the capital as of 12 November, with more than 590 hospitalisations; about 13 measles deaths have been recorded across the last two years against three in the previous 25. CDC Director Erica Schwartz repeated that the designation is not what protects Americans — vaccination does — and cited $100 million invested in state and local health departments, while Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. blamed Covid-era shutdowns. The American Medical Association called the loss a deeply troubling and entirely preventable setback; one Johns Hopkins expert called it a defeat for public health.

Two Dissenters Wanted a Hike in October: Tonight’s Minutes Test the Story

At 19:00 GMT, markets get the document they have traded against all week. The minutes of the Federal Reserve’s 27–28 October meeting will show how the 10–2 vote to hold rates at 3.75%–4.00% was argued — and how isolated Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack were in wanting an immediate quarter-point rise. Since that meeting, hotter inflation data and hawkish speeches, including New York Fed President John Williams saying another rise will likely be needed, have pushed the dollar index to about 103.65, near a 19-month high, and spot gold down to roughly $3,785–3,796 an ounce, its lowest since September 2025 and more than 30% below January’s record near $5,595. Futures price about a 96% chance of a 25-basis-point December hike to 4.00%–4.25%, with a small chance of 50 points. One caution hangs over the release: the minutes describe a meeting held before October’s inflation surprise, so traders will weigh the record against everything learned since.
