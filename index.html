Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Third Tanker Reported Ablaze25 November 2026

Three Tankers Ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz After Striking Naval Mines, Iran Says

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy said two oil tankers struck naval mines early on Wednesday, exploded and caught fire while using what Tehran called an unauthorised route through the Strait of Hormuz, and Iranian media later reported a third tanker ablaze after hitting a mine in a restricted zone of the strait. Tehran warned vessels to coordinate their passage with Iranian forces and said new surveillance systems had been deployed to tighten control of the waterway. The incidents follow a Monday report by the British maritime monitor UKMTO that three ships had been struck by unidentified projectiles in the strait, and come as attacks on shipping there continue at a near-daily pace.

Latest · Global Affairs · Washington — China Faces the Highest Rates25 November 2026

US Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners Over Excess Factory Capacity

The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners, the outcome of an eight-month Section 301 investigation into alleged excess industrial capacity among 16 partners. A senior US official said China would face the highest rates: a proposed 20 percent excess-capacity duty on Chinese goods, on top of the roughly 25 percent tariffs imposed during President Donald Trump’s first term. The list also includes close allies such as the European Union, Japan and South Korea, alongside India, Vietnam, Thailand and others. The duties now face a public comment period and a hearing before they can take effect.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Tiangong Station — Docked in Under Four Hours25 November 2026

Pakistani Astronaut Becomes First Foreigner to Board China’s Tiangong Station

China’s Shenzhou-24 spacecraft docked with the Tiangong space station on Wednesday, about three and a half hours after lifting off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre on a Long March-2F rocket. Aboard was Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistani pilot flying as payload specialist — the first Pakistani in orbit and the first foreign astronaut to board Tiangong. Ali, selected under a China–Pakistan cooperation agreement, will spend roughly a week aboard conducting experiments alongside commander Tang Hongbo and flight engineer Wu Yi before returning to Earth.

Nigeria Seizes Methamphetamine Worth $227 Million and Arrests Five Mexican Nationals

Nigeria’s anti-drug agency said on Wednesday that it had seized a consignment of methamphetamine valued at $227 million in the southeastern state of Enugu and arrested five Mexican nationals in connection with it. The agency gave no further detail in its initial announcement, but the involvement of foreign nationals underlines how West Africa has become a significant transit and production corridor in the global synthetic-drugs trade, drawing enforcement attention well beyond the region.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Beijing — Fourth Member Probed Since 202525 November 2026

China’s Politburo Shrinks Further as Graft Probe Reaches Shanghai Party Chief

China’s anti-corruption watchdog has opened an investigation into Chen Jining, Shanghai’s party chief and a member of the ruling Communist Party’s Politburo, making him the fourth member of the elite body to face a graft inquiry since 2025. The announcement came days after Chen’s last public appearance on a corporate visit in Shanghai, and follows the earlier falls of He Weidong, Zhang Youxia and Ma Xingrui. The widening campaign has steadily thinned the 24-member Politburo formed in 2022.

Latest · Americas · Cartagena — One Captor Killed in Pre-Dawn Raid25 November 2026

Colombian Forces Storm Cartagena Supermarket and Free All Remaining Hostages

Colombian security forces stormed an Éxito supermarket in the Caribbean city of Cartagena early on Wednesday, ending a day-long siege and rescuing all the hostages still inside. President Abelardo de la Espriella said one captor was killed in the joint police and military operation. The gunmen had seized roughly 30 people after fleeing a failed attempt to rob an armoured cash vehicle at a nearby mall; ten hostages, including several elderly women, had been released during the standoff on Tuesday, and the city’s mayor said those rescued were safe and would undergo medical checks.

Sahel Insurgents Take Their War to the Skies With Cheap Kamikaze Drones

Armed groups across West Africa’s Sahel are reshaping their fight against government forces with first-person-view drones costing only a few hundred dollars, Reuters reporting shows. Footage from Mali shows separatists and an al Qaeda affiliate using the craft against the army and its Russian paramilitary backers, applying battlecraft refined in Ukraine’s war to rob governments of the air superiority they once held with jets and helicopters. Mali and Niger have accused Kyiv of aiding the rebels; Ukraine’s foreign ministry has firmly rejected claims that it supports illegal armed groups in the region.

Qatar’s Grand Prix Goes Ahead as War Squeezes Gulf Economies and US Pledges Stall

Qatar’s Formula One Grand Prix will go ahead in Doha this weekend after months of doubt caused by the regional war, though the usual celebrity turnout is uncertain and the city’s streets have instead filled with Saudi visitors on school break. The race lands as the conflict squeezes Gulf economies that had pledged trillions of dollars of investment in the United States: the IMF projects the six Gulf economies will shrink 4.8 percent in 2026, with Qatar contracting by 15.6 percent. India Says ‘Cockroach’ Movement Leader Was Told Not to Use His Passport

India’s foreign ministry said on Wednesday that passport authorities had asked Saurav Das, a leader of the ‘cockroach’ protest movement, not to use his passport until he clarifies information about his place of birth.
