Latest · Middle East · Iran — Tehran's Two-Day Ultimatum9 November 2026

“Rain Down Hell”: Iran Gives Washington Two Days to End the War on All Fronts

Iran’s foreign minister Abbas Araghchi has put a stark choice to the White House: accept Tehran’s proposal to end hostilities on every front within two days, or face a renewed military onslaught. “Our 2-day proposal stands: meet the requirements, including end to war on all fronts, and you will see results. Or choose war, and see our alert and battle-hardened Warriors rain down hell,” he wrote on X on Sunday, adding that American voters had made their choice in the midterms and it was now Washington’s turn. Speaking to state television, Araghchi said mediation had stalled since the Republican losses, with regional envoys pausing their visits. The two-day plan is a compressed version of a seven-day framework first floated in September to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. President Donald Trump, for his part, told CNN on Sunday that fresh US strikes were “highly unlikely” if Tehran signed a strong agreement — a contrast in tone that leaves Qatar-led mediation, and a planned Trump meeting with Qatar’s prime minister on Monday, as the live channel.

Latest · Americas · United States — Trump on 60 Minutes9 November 2026

“It Might Be Too Tough”: Trump Rethinks Deportations and Predicts a Reshaped Ukraine

In his first major interview since the Republican midterm defeat, President Donald Trump told CBS’s 60 Minutes that his mass-deportation drive may have gone too far. “I think it was too tough… I think it might be too tough,” he said, distinguishing between removing people convicted of serious crimes and deporting long-settled workers in sectors such as hospitality and landscaping, and saying he would be willing to work with Democrats on a deal for some who have lived in the US for 10 or 15 years. He also blamed the Iran war and higher petrol prices for the losses. On Ukraine, Trump said the war would end soon because of his involvement and that Kyiv would likely emerge with “a slightly differently shaped” country — remarks that land on the eve of Tuesday’s US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi. The softening words sit uneasily alongside personnel: on Friday Trump promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the crackdown, to chief of staff, and the Department of Homeland Security said enforcement was not slowing, with officials reportedly told to aim for about 2,700 arrests a day.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Gunman Dies9 November 2026

Kraków Gunman Dies in Hospital as Poland's Terrorism Inquiry Continues

The 24-year-old who killed six people in Kraków’s Main Market Square on Saturday has died in hospital, Lesser Poland police announced early on Monday. The man, from Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, had been in critical condition since officers shot him within about three minutes of the attack beginning — a response officials credit with preventing more deaths — and had undergone several operations. Six people, four women and two men including a 32-year-old British man, were killed, and 16 people including another Briton and two police officers were taken to hospital. Police say the suspect, who wore military-style clothing and a bulletproof vest and fired at random from a black car, is also suspected of killing his parents and his 21-year-old sister, whose bodies were found at the family home. The government says he may have been inspired by far-right ideology, and the National Prosecutor’s Office has opened a terrorism investigation that will continue despite his death. Poland is in two days of national mourning.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — Hottest Years and COP319 November 2026

2026 on Track to Be Among the Four Hottest Years Ever Recorded, UN Warns on COP31's Opening Day

This year is set to rank among the four hottest on record, the World Meteorological Organization said on Monday, as delegates gathered in Antalya, Türkiye, for the COP31 climate talks running to 20 November. The WMO’s State of the Climate report put the global average temperature for January to September at 1.47°C above the pre-industrial level, with a powerful El Niño expected to push it higher, and found that each of the past 12 years ranks among the 12 warmest since records began around 1850. The EU’s Copernicus service has already said 2026 is all but certain to finish near the top of the table. The findings frame a summit billed by its hosts as an “implementation COP” — focused on delivering past pledges on adaptation finance, food security, electrification, oceans and methane rather than negotiating a new treaty — with a World Leaders’ Summit on 11–12 November and the United States, now leaving the UN climate treaty, the notable absentee.

Latest · Middle East · Energy — The Glut After the War9 November 2026

Price War in the Gulf: Hormuz Reopens and the Oil Market Tips Toward Oversupply

Middle East producers are cutting prices to win buyers as tanker flows through the Strait of Hormuz recover sharply, tipping a market shaped all year by war toward a glut. Brent crude fell more than 17 per cent last week to around $97 a barrel — its steepest weekly drop since the 2020 pandemic — after the US midterm results raised expectations of de-escalation. Crude exports through Hormuz averaged 10.3 million barrels a day in October, up from virtually zero in the war’s first month, using US Navy-protected convoys and excluding Iranian shipments; total regional exports reached 16.6 million barrels a day, just shy of the pre-war 17 million, according to Kpler, while global production hit 85 million barrels a day, above the five-year average. Saudi Arabia has cut the December price of its flagship Arab Light for Asian buyers by $2 a barrel, to a $6.50 discount — the lowest since May 2020 — and Oman’s spot premium has collapsed from $34 in mid-September to under $2. JPMorgan estimates war has cut global consumption by about 3.3 million barrels a day.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — ANC's Worst-Ever Result8 November 2026

“Catastrophic”: Mandela's ANC Falls to 30.8 Per Cent in Municipal Elections

South Africa’s African National Congress has recorded its worst election result since the end of apartheid, taking 30.76–30.81 per cent of the vote in last Wednesday’s municipal elections, the Electoral Commission confirmed on Sunday — down from 45.6 per cent in 2021 and less than half the 64.9 per cent it won in 2006. The party won no outright majority in any of the eight metros; its steepest collapse was in eThekwini, from 61 per cent in 2011 to 16.5 per cent now, and it holds only 53 of 213 councils outright, with more than half of councils hung. The Democratic Alliance came second on about 24.6 per cent, followed by Jacob Zuma’s MK party on 9.8 per cent and the EFF on 6.2 per cent; turnout was 52 per cent. Voters cited water shortages, power cuts and failing services, alongside unemployment above 34 per cent. ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula called the outcome “catastrophic”. Parties now have 14 days to negotiate coalitions, with Johannesburg — where the DA led the ANC by about 35 to 20 per cent — the biggest prize.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Zaporizhzhia Back on the Grid8 November 2026

Power Restored at Zaporizhzhia After Its 27th Blackout of the War

External power has been restored to Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant, Europe’s largest, after it spent most of Sunday on emergency diesel generators. Ukraine’s operator Energoatom said the plant’s last working line, the 330 kV Ferosplavna-1, disconnected at 02:36 Kyiv time and was reconnected at 17:41, returning the site to the Ukrainian grid; the main Dniprovska line has been out since October with combat damage. The International Atomic Energy Agency said the cause and location of the fault were still unknown and counted this as the 27th complete loss of off-site power during the conflict — Energoatom counts 16 blackouts this year alone — calling the safety situation unstable. The plant’s six reactors, totalling 6,000 megawatts, are all shut down and generate nothing, but they still need steady power to cool fuel; diesel reserves cover roughly two weeks of limited generator running. Energoatom warned the risk will persist while the plant remains under Russian occupation.

Latest · Middle East · Iraq — Khor Mor Blackout Deepens9 November 2026

One Drone Leaves 70 Per Cent of Iraqi Kurdistan Without Power

Fresh reporting on Sunday night’s drone strike on the Khor Mor gas field underlines the scale of the blackout now gripping northern Iraq. An explosive-laden drone hit the field’s gas storage facility in Sulaimaniyah governorate, starting a fire and forcing a full suspension of production. Kurdistan electricity ministry spokesman Omid Ahmed said between 2,500 and 2,800 megawatts of generation were knocked off the grid, cutting supply by 70 to 80 per cent and leaving more than 70 per cent of the Kurdish region without power, including Erbil, Sulaimaniyah, Bazian, Chamchamal and Duhok, which rely on Khor Mor gas for their power stations. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi said an initial assessment found no casualties. No group has claimed responsibility, and it is not yet clear whether the attack is linked to the wider US–Israel war on Iran; Khor Mor, the region’s primary energy lifeline, has been hit repeatedly by drones and rockets in recent years. Al Jazeera’s Sanad agency verified footage of smoke and flames rising from the site.

China Keeps Its 24 Per Cent US Tariff Suspended for Two More Months

China will extend its suspension of an additional 24 per cent tariff on imports from the United States for a further two months, its finance ministry announced on Monday. The move prolongs a truce measure in the long-running trade dispute between the world’s two largest economies, holding the higher levy in abeyance rather than removing it — a signal of continued, if fragile, stabilisation in trade ties while the underlying tariff structure stays intact.

37,000 Jobs a Year: Google Unveils a $15 Billion Build-Out in Malaysia

Google has set out a US$15 billion investment in Malaysia for 2027 and 2028, centred on new energy-efficient data-centre campuses in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan alongside its first Malaysian campus at Elmina Business Park, which is now operational. Announced in the presence of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, the programme builds on a $2 billion commitment from 2024 and will supply the computing capacity for Malaysia’s upcoming Google Cloud region and for domestic AI and digital services. The package also promises more than 1 gigawatt of new clean-energy capacity for the national grid with Tenaga Nasional, $160 million in shared water infrastructure on the Linggi River, and training to upskill about 800,000 Malaysians by 2031, including Data Centre Institutes at polytechnics in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan from 2027.

“R2” Captured in Zapopan Shoot-Out: Mexico Detains Jalisco Cartel Cell Leader and 19 Others

Mexican authorities say they have captured Rafael “N”, alias “R2”, an alleged leader of the “Los Rs” faction of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel (CJNG), in an operation in Zapopan, Jalisco, aided by intelligence shared by the US Drug Enforcement Administration. He faces an aggravated-homicide warrant in Mexico and an extradition warrant to the US, where the DEA says he is wanted on federal drug-trafficking and firearms charges. The CJNG was designated a foreign terrorist organisation by Washington in 2025.

Latest · Africa · Sport — Nigeria's Sprint Double in Dakar8 November 2026

First Country Ever to Sweep Both: Nigeria Takes the Men's and Women's 100m at the Youth Olympics

Nigeria made Youth Olympic history in Dakar on Sunday, becoming the first country to win both 100-metre titles at the same edition of the Games. In the women’s final at the Complexe Iba Mar Diop, 17-year-old team captain Rosemary Chigozie Nwankwo won in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds — the +2.4 m/s tailwind puts the time outside record eligibility — ahead of the UAE’s Arwa Ali (11.41) and Qatar’s Dana Salem (11.48), becoming Nigeria’s third women’s champion after Josephine Omaka in 2010 and Rosemary Chukwuma in 2018. Fifteen-year-old Gift Bright then completed the double in the men’s final, running a personal best of 10.28 seconds with a legal +1.7 m/s wind to beat China’s Zhang Hangyu (10.57) and Poland’s Szymon Dolega (10.61) — the first Nigerian man ever to win the title. The two golds are Nigeria’s first medals of Dakar 2026, the first Olympic event staged on African soil, which runs to 13 November with about 2,700 athletes aged 14 to 17.
