Latest · Europe · Benghazi, Libya — Seven Sea Drones Used23 November 2026

Ukraine Says Its Sea Drones Sank Russian Corvette Merkury Off Libya and Hit a Weapons Cargo Ship

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that Ukrainian forces sank the Russian corvette Merkury near the Libyan coast using seven domestically built naval drones, in one of Kyiv’s longest-range operations of the war, carried out roughly 2,400 kilometres from the front line. Zelenskyy said the strike at the Benghazi naval base also destroyed a Russian Ka-27 combat helicopter and hit a second vessel, the dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said Moscow used to move weapons. The Merkury was among Russia’s newest warships, commissioned in 2023, a year after the full-scale invasion began. Libyan officials said the corvette was severely damaged by an explosion and fire on Sunday. The claim follows a separate drone strike on Saturday against the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Baltic, which caught fire, drifted into Finnish waters and had to be towed away, an incident the Kremlin on Monday denounced as terrorist activity while urging Baltic states to press Kyiv to stop such attacks. Analysts say the Mediterranean has now become an external theatre of the war, as Ukraine steps up strikes on Russian naval assets, energy sites and sanctions-evading shadow-fleet vessels while Russia intensifies its own air campaign at home.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — Emergency North Atlantic Council Session23 November 2026

NATO Meets in Emergency Session After Two Russian Missiles and a Drone Fell Inside Poland

NATO’s North Atlantic Council met in emergency session in Brussels on Monday after two Russian cruise missiles and a jet-powered drone crossed into Polish airspace during a large Russian attack on western Ukraine on 20 November and came down in south-eastern Poland. Polish Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said Warsaw would present its assessment of how the objects crossed the border, seek a clear show of allied solidarity, and propose concrete steps to strengthen air defence on the alliance’s eastern flank. Polish prosecutors confirmed that one object recovered near Nowy Lipowiec was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile produced in late 2026; the other missile fell near Rudnik nad Sanem and drone fragments were found at Mołodycz. No one in Poland was injured, though staff at Rzeszów–Jasionka airport were evacuated during the alert and Polish F-16s and Swedish Gripens stayed airborne through the night. Secretary General Mark Rutte, who visited Warsaw on Saturday, called the incursion dangerous and pledged that NATO would defend every inch of Polish territory. Poland has not invoked Article 4 consultations and is instead seeking extra assets under Operation Eastern Sentry, the eastern defence mission set up in September 2025 after an earlier drone incursion.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Dhaka — Planned December Return Fuels Unrest23 November 2026

Bangladesh Vows to Arrest Sheikh Hasina If She Returns From India, and Presses for Her Extradition

Bangladesh will arrest and jail former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina if she returns from India, a senior minister said, as talk of a December homecoming fuels political tension and a renewed wave of street violence. State Minister for Foreign Affairs Shama Obaed Islam told Reuters that Dhaka is instead pursuing Hasina’s extradition under its existing treaty with India and expects New Delhi to comply. Hasina has lived in India since fleeing Dhaka in August 2024, when mass protests demanding her resignation swept the country and toppled her government. Her Awami League announced a week of rallies backing her return this month, culminating in a nationwide lockdown call on 19 November. Police have recorded dozens of crude bomb blasts and arson attacks on vehicles in at least 28 districts over the past ten days, injuring several officers and civilians, according to local media. Dhaka is also stepping up pressure on neighbouring Myanmar over the return of Rohingya refugees, after the UN’s highest court ruling added legal weight to its case; about 1.2 million Rohingya remain in Bangladesh after repeated repatriation efforts failed.

Latest · Middle East · Knesset, Jerusalem — Fresh Round Set for Monday Evening23 November 2026

Likud and Yashar Return to the Table as Pressure Mounts on Eisenkot to Abandon Unity Talks

Negotiating teams from Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud and Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar were due to meet again at the Knesset on Monday evening, a day after four hours of talks in Herzliya ended with little movement on who should lead the next government or who should sit in it. Sunday’s session largely avoided the premiership question, made some progress on an inquiry into the 7 October attack and on judicial overhaul laws, and recorded broad agreement only on recognising Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people and opposing a Palestinian state; Yashar’s demand to exclude far-right minister Itamar Ben-Gvir was rejected. Pressure is now building inside Eisenkot’s own camp: Democrats leader Yair Golan urged him on Monday to end what he called deceitful negotiations and pursue a minority government instead, while Likud officials claimed Yashar never truly wanted unity and is building a case for a minority coalition backed by Arab parties. Eisenkot, a former army chief, holds the mandate to form a government until 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension, but his 48-seat bloc remains split over relying on ultra-Orthodox parties, Arab parties, or Likud.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Agency Staff Member Killed23 November 2026

Israeli Strike Kills UNRWA Worker in Gaza, the Agency and Local Health Officials Say

An Israeli airstrike killed a Gaza man employed by UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, in the Gaza Strip on Monday, the agency and local health officials said. The death adds to a toll that has made Gaza exceptionally dangerous for humanitarian staff throughout the war, with UN agencies repeatedly warning that aid workers, distribution points and convoys remain exposed despite coordination arrangements meant to protect them. UNRWA is the largest relief provider in the enclave, running shelters, clinics and food distribution for a population largely displaced by fighting, and it has lost large numbers of staff since the war began. The strike came as humanitarian groups continue to press for safer access and more consistent deliveries of food, medicine and fuel, and as diplomatic efforts over Gaza’s future run in parallel with Israel’s own inconclusive coalition politics. No further operational detail about the strike was given in the initial reports, and the account rests on statements from the agency and health officials in Gaza.

Zambia Opposition Leader Charged With Plotting to Overthrow the Government

Zambian opposition leader Brian Mundubile and more than a dozen associates have been charged with attempting to overthrow the government and committed to the High Court to stand trial for treason, according to a charge sheet read in court on Monday. Prosecutors allege that Mundubile, an associate named Zulu and 12 others plotted to seize power between April and August 2026. The case lands in a charged political climate after President Hakainde Hichilema won re-election amid fraud allegations from the opposition, which he has denied. Treason is among the most serious charges in Zambian law, and committing the case to the High Court means the accused will face a full trial rather than a preliminary disposal in a lower court. The arrests and charges are likely to sharpen debate about political space in Zambia, where the government insists it is enforcing the law against a genuine plot and opposition figures are expected to argue that the prosecution is an attempt to sideline a leading rival after a disputed election.

Latest · Europe · Freiburg — About 450 Schools Threatened by Email23 November 2026

Hundreds of German Schools Receive Bomb Threats in a Single Morning, Bild Reports

About 450 schools across Germany received bomb threats by email on Monday morning, the newspaper Bild reported, citing police in the south-western city of Freiburg. The scale of the wave forced schools and local police forces across multiple states to assess each message, check buildings and decide whether to evacuate, in a pattern that has become familiar in Germany after repeated mass-threat campaigns aimed at schools and public institutions. Such emailed threats are often hoaxes, but each one must be treated as credible until cleared, tying up emergency resources and disrupting lessons for tens of thousands of pupils. Authorities did not immediately report any devices found. The incident also spread concern beyond Germany, with similar school threats reported in Denmark fuelling questions about whether a coordinated scare campaign is moving across European countries. Police investigations will focus on the origin and wording of the emails to establish whether the German and Danish cases are linked and who is responsible.

Latest · Global Affairs · Yerevan — Draft Published Monday23 November 2026

Armenia Publishes Draft New Constitution in a Key Step Toward Peace With Azerbaijan

Armenia published a draft proposal for a new constitution on Monday, a development officials present as an important step toward signing a formal peace agreement with Azerbaijan, its neighbour and longtime adversary. Constitutional change has been one of the most sensitive elements of the peace process, because Azerbaijan has long objected to references in Armenia’s founding documents that it reads as laying claim to Azerbaijani territory, while any rewrite in Yerevan is politically delicate and likely to require a public referendum. Publishing a draft moves the issue from diplomatic talking point to a concrete domestic debate, in which the government must persuade voters that revising the basic law serves peace rather than conceding under pressure. The two South Caucasus states fought wars over Nagorno-Karabakh before Azerbaijan retook the region, and they have since edged toward a settlement through draft treaty texts, border talks and outside mediation. A constitution that both sides accept as compatible with a treaty would remove one of the last structural obstacles to a signature, though the referendum timetable and opposition reaction in Armenia will decide how fast that can happen.

India Announces $1.62 Billion Economic Package for Flood-Hit Nepal After Modi–Shah Talks

India will give Nepal an economic package worth 240 billion Nepalese rupees, about $1.62 billion, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced on Monday after talks in New Delhi with his Nepali counterpart Balendra Shah. The support is aimed at a neighbour still recovering from damaging floods, and it underlines how quickly Shah’s government in Kathmandu is being courted and tested on the regional stage, with infrastructure, energy and disaster recovery all competing for funds. For India, assistance to Nepal is both humanitarian and strategic: the two countries share an open border, deep power and trade links, and a history in which delays in aid delivery can quickly become political arguments in Kathmandu about dependence on either India or China. The package gives Shah tangible backing early in his dealings with New Delhi, while its real value will depend on how fast projects are approved and money is disbursed, and on whether flood defences and reconstruction feature alongside headline support. Latest · Global Affairs · Mercury Orbit — Thruster Burn Cut Short21 November 2026

BepiColombo Begins Orbiting Mercury After Eight Years, Though a Software Glitch Shortens a Thruster Burn

After an eight-year journey through the inner solar system, the European–Japanese spacecraft BepiColombo has begun orbiting Mercury, the smallest and innermost planet, becoming only the second mission ever to do so after NASA’s Messenger. The European Space Agency confirmed the orbit insertion, which took place on Saturday, but said on Sunday that a thruster burn during the manoeuvre was shorter than planned because of a software issue. The spacecraft is safe, ESA said, and teams at the European Space Operations Centre are working to understand what happened, with a further update promised on Monday. BepiColombo, launched in 2018 as a joint ESA and Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency mission, is a stacked pair of orbiters — ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter and JAXA’s Mio magnetospheric orbiter — due to separate on 9–10 December before further burns settle them into their science orbits by March. Routine science operations are scheduled to begin in April 2027, studying Mercury’s surface, interior and magnetic environment in conditions where solar radiation is ten times stronger than at Earth.
