Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — The Banquet18 November 2026

A Handshake Under the Host’s Gaze: Trump and Putin Meet at Xi’s Table

The eve of the summit produced its defining image. At Wednesday night’s welcome banquet at the Shenzhen International Exchange Center, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin shook hands as Xi Jinping looked on — their first meeting in more than a year — hours after Xi had broken protocol to greet Trump on the tarmac at Bao’an airport, the first airport welcome he has given any visiting leader and a return of Trump’s gesture at Joint Base Andrews in September. In his banquet speech around 9:20 p.m., Xi called on APEC leaders to work together, having earlier pledged to stand against trade protectionism; Putin, who landed overnight and held his fifth meeting of the year with Xi during the day, again branded Western sanctions a failure and said he expects “big benefit” from Thursday’s talks. Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said work on a joint statement for the trilateral is proceeding intensively. The three men meet Thursday at 14:30 local time with Ukraine, the Iran war, trade, artificial intelligence and nuclear arms on the table — a lineup already drawing comparisons with Yalta, and fears in Kyiv and Europe of decisions taken over their heads.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Escalation Risk18 November 2026

“Inherently Unstable”: Analysts Warn the US–Iran Standoff Could Tip Back Into War

Nine months into the war, the pattern is set — and, analysts warn, inherently unstable. Iran’s pre-dawn drones at Kuwait’s Ali Al Salem base, the first attack on Kuwait since September, answered a US strike on a Revolutionary Guard missile site on Qeshm Island, which itself answered an Iranian hit on a US radar site in Bahrain; Bahrain intercepted Iranian attacks for the third time in a week, the Houthis sent five drones at Saudi pipeline pumping stations near Riyadh, and a Saudi-led coalition destroyed a cruise missile aimed at the capital. Al Jazeera’s analysis notes both sides seem reluctant to return to all-out conflict, yet each exchange narrows the space for restraint, with Israel liable to rejoin the strikes. At sea, the US blockade has stopped Iran loading crude since late August, Tehran insists the Strait of Hormuz stays closed to unauthorised transit, and ADNOC is still missing one crew member from Tuesday’s vessel attack, with 11 injured; the Panama-flagged gas tanker Tara Gas also caught fire in the strait. Qatari mediation has stalled, and the Gulf Cooperation Council has condemned Tehran in one voice.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Collapse Warning18 November 2026

“A Ceasefire That Delivers Nothing Visible Cannot Be Sustained”

The body charged with making Gaza’s peace work has told the UN Security Council it is failing. The US-backed Board of Peace, created under President Trump’s 20-point plan, delivered its first annual assessment on Tuesday, faulting both Israel and Hamas for falling far short of the October 2025 ceasefire: continued Israeli strikes, Hamas military activity, and a disarmament-and-withdrawal road map effectively stalled since it began. The 12-page report singles out Israeli delays in approving the civilian bodies meant to govern Gaza — a foot-dragging it says effectively keeps Hamas in power — and restrictions on winter shelter equipment, warning that civilians face another winter in tents and rubble and that if the truce collapses there will be “no road map to return to.” Hamas, which agreed a disarmament plan in July that Israel rejected, dismissed the report as disinformation. The warning landed the same day Israel opened a new concentrated operation in central Gaza; Gaza’s Health Ministry counts 1,650 killed since the ceasefire took effect. The board presents its findings formally to the Council at the end of November.

Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — 66 to 5218 November 2026

The Gavel Changes Hands: Eisenkot’s Bloc Takes the Knesset Speakership

Israel’s parliament has a new speaker, and with him a new centre of power. After six hours of filibustering by Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc, the Knesset voted 66–52 late on Tuesday to elect Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party, defeating Likud’s Ofir Katz and replacing Amir Ohana, who had held the post since 2022. Kahana — a former Sayeret Matkal soldier and air force colonel who served as religious services minister in 2021–22 — was backed by Eisenkot’s four-party bloc plus the Arab-majority Ra’am and Joint List; together they hold 67 of 120 seats after last month’s election stripped Netanyahu of his majority. The speakership controls the legislative agenda, and the bloc is expected to move as early as Wednesday to repeal key elements of the judicial overhaul. Eisenkot, who holds the president’s mandate and up to five more weeks to form a government, said the Knesset would again be “the house of the people, of all the people”; Kahana pledged to be a speaker for everyone, “irrespective of race, religion or gender.”

Latest · Europe · Odesa — Toll Rises to Five18 November 2026

“Brutal and Deliberate”: Zelenskyy Says Rescuers Were Targeted in Odesa

The count from Wednesday’s strikes across Ukraine rose to at least five dead by nightfall. Four people were killed and 13 wounded in Odesa, where three Russian strikes hit the UNESCO-protected historic centre — blowing out hundreds of windows in at least seven buildings on Yevropeiska Street, setting a car ablaze and damaging homes, cafés, shops and a school — and a fifth person, a woman, was killed by a drone in Kushuhum in the Zaporizhzhia region, where a man was also injured and families with children were ordered to evacuate. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the Odesa attack “brutal and deliberate,” saying follow-up strikes hit the impact site after rescuers and police had arrived. The barrage came a day after Ukraine’s strike on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea killed two and collapsed part of the span, and on the eve of Zelenskyy’s summit with allies in Berlin. Ukraine’s Energy Ministry says Russia has launched more than 1,000 attacks on energy infrastructure since September; overnight, air defences downed 126 of 141 drones launched nationwide.

Latest · Europe · Siberia — An-12 Crash18 November 2026

Nineteen Dead in the Taiga: Russia’s Ageing An-12 Fleet Loses Another Aircraft

A Russian military transport plane has crashed in western Siberia, killing everyone on board. The Antonov An-12 came down on Tuesday near the village of Yagunovo, outside Kemerovo some 3,000 km east of Moscow, during a scheduled flight; the Defence Ministry said all 19 people aboard, crew included, died in the unpopulated area. Governor Ilya Seredyuk posted from the scene as emergency crews extinguished the fire. Preliminary findings point to a technical malfunction, an Aerospace Forces commission is working at the site, and the Investigative Committee has opened a criminal case over possible flight-safety violations — early reports had put the toll at 17 before the ministry settled on 19. The An-12 is a Soviet-built four-engine turboprop in service since the late 1950s and still widely used to move weapons and personnel; one report said the aircraft was flying from Novosibirsk toward Kansk in Krasnoyarsk Krai. The victims have not been publicly identified.

Schools Shut, Leave Cancelled: The Coast Empties Itself Before Akvan

By Wednesday night, India’s east coast was in full pre-landfall mode. Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan — the third Bay of Bengal storm in 21 days — is now expected to cross between Machilipatnam and Puri around noon on Friday 20 November at 90–100 km/h, gusting to 110, its forecast track nudged north through the day as it accelerated to about 9 km/h. Odisha placed eight districts on high alert, cancelled officials’ leave, postponed a state recruitment exam and ordered schools and Anganwadi centres shut in seven districts from 19 to 21 November, with 131 fire-service teams deployed, the BASUDHA 1916 helpline live and Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi chairing an evening review at the State Emergency Operations Centre. Andhra Pradesh is under a red alert for extremely heavy rain on Thursday and Friday, high waves already breaking at Visakhapatnam’s RK Beach. The IMD has trimmed its intensity call from very severe to severe — small comfort for the low-lying districts in the storm’s path.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Papua New Guinea — Magnitude 6.018 November 2026

Ten Kilometres Down: A Shallow Quake Shakes Papua New Guinea

A magnitude 6.0 earthquake struck Papua New Guinea early on Wednesday, the German Research Centre for Geosciences (GFZ) reported, at a shallow depth of just 10 km — the kind of depth that produces strong shaking near the epicentre. GFZ placed the tremor at 03:42 GMT, at roughly 5.82° south and 146.65° east. By Wednesday night there were still no confirmed reports of casualties or major damage and no tsunami warning had been issued, though communications with remote areas of the country are slow and the first GFZ readings are preliminary: no USGS figure had been published, and agencies routinely revise magnitude, depth and location in the hours after a quake. Papua New Guinea sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is no stranger to such events — a magnitude 6.4 quake hit its Eastern New Guinea region on 20 September — but each shallow tremor near populated areas is assessed anxiously until local authorities report in.

Latest · Global Affairs · Nvidia — $115.8 Billion Quarter18 November 2026

The First Quarter Without Huang: Nvidia Doubles Revenue Anyway

Nvidia’s first earnings report since founder Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November was a study in continuity. Revenue for the quarter ended 25 October surged 103% year on year to $115.84 billion, beating consensus near $108–109 billion; non-GAAP earnings of $2.68 a share topped the $2.47–2.50 expected, and net income climbed 105% to $65.3 billion. Data Centre sales rose 110% to $107.6 billion — 93% of the total — with the new Vera Rubin platform contributing nearly $20 billion in its first quarter of shipments, the company’s fastest product ramp ever. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, the long-serving finance chief who led the call, guided the current quarter to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, and raised the fiscal 2028 growth forecast to 75%. Shares rose more than 3% after hours. Two cautions sit inside the triumph: the guide assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China, and gross margin is expected to narrow to 71–72%; chairman Harvey Jones also told investors the board does not intend to reinstate Huang.

Robbed of $6.3 Billion, Binance Plans to Expand Anyway

The largest crypto theft ever recorded has not slowed the world’s largest crypto exchange. Latest · Africa · Sudan — The Market Strikes18 November 2026

Thirty-Nine Strikes on Markets: Inside the UN’s Sudan Drone Count

The UN’s Sudan report, absorbed across the region on Wednesday, is as much an inventory as an indictment. Of 2,230 verified civilian deaths from January to October 2026, more than 1,630 were caused by drones — over 80% of the toll in the first half of the year. In those six months alone, investigators documented at least 39 drone strikes on markets, 20 on civilian vehicles, 17 on fuel tankers and depots and 16 on health facilities; one strike series on markets in the disputed Abyei region killed at least 80 people. Between 20 October and 4 November, 29 strikes attributed to the Rapid Support Forces hit electricity and fuel sites across 11 states, part of a pattern of attacks on the water, power and fuel systems civilians need to survive. Kordofan accounts for more than two-thirds of documented casualties. High Commissioner Volker Türk called the parties’ disregard for the laws of war brazen and said the violations may amount to war crimes; the UN stresses the verified figure is a floor, not the true toll, in the war between the army and the RSF now in its fourth year.

Futures price roughly a 96% chance of a 25-basis-point December hike, with a small chance of 50. The standing caution applies with force: the minutes describe a meeting held before October’s inflation surprise, so traders will weigh the paper against everything learned since.
