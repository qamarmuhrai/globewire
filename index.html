Latest · Europe · France24 October 2026

More Than 400,000 March Across France as Police Fire Tear Gas and 64 Are Arrested in Paris

More than 100,000 people marched in Paris on Saturday — 110,000 by the police count, and about 410,000 nationwide according to the Interior Ministry — in protests over school funding and the rising cost of living that have become a major test for President Emmanuel Macron in the final months of his term. The Paris march was confined to the south-eastern edge of the city, away from the centre hit by looting a week earlier, and began peacefully before clashes near the endpoint at Porte de Vincennes, where police fired tear gas and used water cannon. Tear gas was also used in Lyon, Clermont-Ferrand, Strasbourg and Marseille, and water cannon in Nice and Nantes. The Paris prosecutor said 64 people were arrested, 40 of them held in custody, including six minors, mostly over suspected weapons offences or preparing violence. The movement began over staff shortages at a high school and now draws students demanding more university places and workers demanding higher wages. Organisers have called another march for October 31, while the far-right National Rally urged voters to turn their anger into votes ahead of May's presidential election.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan24 October 2026

Seven Killed at a Church Funeral in Jordan as King Abdullah Vows an 'Iron Fist' Response

Gunmen opened fire for several minutes during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hammoud in Jordan's Karak governorate on Friday, killing seven people and wounding 34, the head of the Public Security Directorate said, with 27 of the wounded still in hospital. State television aired footage showing two men with pistols and a third with a rifle approaching the church, where between 150 and 200 people were gathered; a witness said the attackers arrived by vehicle and fired continuously for about five minutes before fleeing. Interior Minister Mazin Al Farrayeh described the assailants as extremists, but said it was too early to link them to any known group, and no one has claimed responsibility. King Abdullah II visited Karak and a local hospital on Friday evening and posted that the attack would be met with the harshest retaliation. The US Embassy in Amman said security had been stepped up at malls, embassies and other sites across Jordan, where such attacks on the Christian minority are rare, and a manhunt for the gunmen continued on Saturday.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War24 October 2026

Russia and Ukraine Agree a Local Ceasefire for Oleshky and Plan New Peace Talks in November

Ukraine and Russia said on Friday they had agreed a ceasefire in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in the southern Kherson region, brokered through US First Lady Melania Trump, to allow aid deliveries and evacuations through humanitarian corridors. Both sides said the truce would take effect on October 27, although the White House statement had given October 29. Oleshky, across the Dnipro from Ukrainian-held Kherson, had about 24,000 people before the war and now has roughly 1,700 to 2,000, mostly elderly residents short of food, medicine and clean water. The Kremlin praised Melania Trump's contribution, and she said the town was on the brink of disaster. President Volodymyr Zelensky said negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States had agreed to technical-level meetings in the first half of November in Abu Dhabi, with a US official saying the talks could instead be in Switzerland, and Zelensky said he hoped a leaders' meeting would follow. Big obstacles remain: Russia still demands all of the eastern Donbas, including land it does not hold, while Kyiv refuses to give up territory it controls.

Latest · Europe · France24 October 2026

Questions Remain Over Motive After Trappes Shooting That Macron Called a Racist Terrorist Attack

Investigators were still examining the motives behind Friday's shooting in Trappes and La Verrière, west of Paris, in which a gunman firing in bursts from a car killed a 26-year-old man and seriously wounded five others, including a 12-year-old girl. Visiting Trappes on Friday evening, President Emmanuel Macron called it a racist terrorist attack and a turning point, saying the victims were targeted because of their skin colour or perceived religion. The anti-terrorism prosecutor has opened an investigation into premeditated murder linked to a terrorist enterprise, with the alleged racial or religious motive as an aggravating factor. The suspect, identified only as Kevin T., born in 1987, was arrested within about 20 minutes; police found a Sten-type submachine gun in his car, and reports said his apartment had been booby-trapped with gas cylinders. Trappes mayor Ali Rabeh described the attack as probably Islamophobic. Political leaders across the spectrum condemned the shooting, while prosecutors said the suspect's exact intentions were not yet established.

Latest · Africa · Health24 October 2026

Kenya Records a Second Ebola Death as Congo Begins a Vaccine Trial in Bunia

Kenya's health ministry said a 65-year-old woman, a close relative of the country's first Ebola victim, had become its second death in its first-ever outbreak, bringing confirmed cases to nine, with seven patients still being treated at two isolation facilities. Officials said all cases belong to a single cluster, with no evidence of community transmission, and that surveillance and contact tracing had been stepped up. The outbreak is driven by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine. In the Democratic Republic of Congo, where the outbreak began in May and has grown into the country's worst ever — more than 9,700 cases and over 4,600 deaths, according to the WHO — the first participants in a trial called Tumaini, meaning hope in Swahili, were vaccinated in Bunia, Ituri province. The trial, backed by the WHO and partners, will test whether the Ervebo vaccine, approved for the Zaire strain, offers cross-protection against Bundibugyo, and may later include candidate vaccines from Oxford and Moderna. The WHO says the outbreak has now been reported in four African countries.

Kim Jong Un Crosses a Newly Opened Bridge Into China as Beijing and Pyongyang Warm Trade Ties

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un visited the Chinese border city of Dandong on Saturday, crossing the New Yalu River Bridge, China's Xinhua news agency reported — a symbolic trip highlighting a push by Beijing and Pyongyang to expand trade. Kim was accompanied by Wang Huning, China's fourth-highest-ranked official, who conveyed greetings from President Xi Jinping, and Chinese television showed Kim walking on the bridge past people waving flags. The three-kilometre bridge, completed years ago but only officially opened this month after long delays, is expected to ease a major logistics bottleneck and help move heavy equipment, raw materials and energy products into North Korea. China accounts for most of North Korea's official trade and remains its main economic and diplomatic backer. The visit follows two summits between Kim and Xi in the past 13 months, even as Pyongyang's deepening military ties with Russia since 2022 had raised questions about whether it was becoming less reliant on Beijing. South Korean officials said Kim was expected to return home the same day.

Latest · Americas · Weather24 October 2026

Hurricane Vance Strengthens Towards Category 3 Off Mexico's Pacific Coast

Hurricane Vance rapidly strengthened in the eastern Pacific, reaching Category 2 with maximum sustained winds of about 105 mph (170 kph) on Friday and forecast to briefly become a major Category 3 storm, the US National Hurricane Center said. The storm was located roughly 425 miles (685 kilometres) south-southwest of Acapulco, Mexico, moving slowly north-west and expected to continue that motion through the weekend, approaching the Puerto Vallarta area by Monday. Its outer bands were already bringing gale-force winds and rough seas into the Gulf of Tehuantepec, and Mexican authorities warned of heavy rain, high waves and dangerous rip currents along the coast from Guerrero towards Colima. Vance is the season's latest in an unusually intense run of Pacific storms — it would be the seventh major hurricane in a row, after Nolo, Odalys, Polo, Rachel, Simon and Trudy — in a season forecasters link to strong El Niño conditions. It is also the first V-named eastern Pacific storm since 2018.

Latest · Europe · Germany24 October 2026

Fire Guts Hitler's Eagle's Nest Retreat in the Bavarian Alps; Suspect Detained

A fire engulfed the Kehlsteinhaus, known as the Eagle's Nest, Adolf Hitler's Nazi-era mountain retreat above Berchtesgaden in Bavaria, on Friday night, German police said on Saturday, and a suspect has been taken into temporary custody. Around 200 firefighters were sent to the remote site at 1,834 metres (6,000 feet) after an alarm sounded at about 9:50 p.m.; they found the building completely ablaze, with flames still burning in the early hours as snow fell, and no one inside. Police said the altitude and access made firefighting difficult, and Bild reported the historic building was so badly damaged it might not be saved. Investigators suspect arson, though police gave no motive and said it was still to be determined whether the detained person, reported to be a motorcyclist stopped nearby, was connected to the fire. Bavaria's interior minister said the monument, built in 1938 and now a tourist site, would be rebuilt, with damage estimated in the millions of euros.

Ras Al Khaimah Closes All Its Beaches as the UAE Works to Contain an Offshore Oil Spill

Authorities in Ras Al Khaimah said on Saturday that all beaches in the northern emirate would be closed until further notice, a day after an offshore oil leak prompted closures in neighbouring Umm Al Quwain and Sharjah. The UAE's Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said efforts were under way to contain oil leaking from a tanker offshore and that it was carrying out an environmental assessment, urging fishermen and other sea users to avoid affected areas. All beaches around Umm Al Quwain were closed until technical teams finish their assessments, and Sharjah temporarily closed Al Hamriyah Beach as a precaution. Residents in parts of Sharjah, Ajman and Umm Al Quwain reported unusual odours, which the ministry said posed no health risks. Ras Al Khaimah, known for its beaches and resorts, is also home to Wynn Resorts' planned casino resort on Al Marjan Island, due to open in early 2027. Since the start of the US-Israeli war with Iran, attacks on commercial vessels in Gulf shipping lanes have heightened concerns about the conflict's environmental fallout.

Latest · Americas · United States24 October 2026

Trump Says He Ordered a Lawsuit to Block Alaska Senate Certification If the Republican Loses

President Donald Trump said on Friday that he told the Justice Department to sue to preemptively block Alaska from certifying its US Senate result if the Republican incumbent loses by a narrow margin — telling reporters, 'I'm the one that said do it.' The lawsuit, filed on Thursday in federal court in Anchorage, argues that voters could be confused because another candidate on the ballot, retired schoolteacher Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., has a name almost identical to incumbent Senator Dan S. Sullivan, who faces Democrat Mary Peltola in a close race that could decide control of the Senate. The department wants a judge to stop certification of any result that may have been affected by confusion, pending an investigation into whether votes for one Sullivan were meant for the other. The Justice Department said it was protecting the integrity of the election. Critics, including Democratic and some Republican election officials, called the move federal overreach, and Peltola called it desperate and corrupt. Alaska's Supreme Court had earlier rejected efforts to remove the second Sullivan from the ballot.
