Latest · Global Affairs · G20 Atlanta31 October 2026

G20 Ministers End the Atlanta Meeting With No Joint Communique — Only a US Chair's Statement

The G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta closes on Saturday without agreement on the central security questions before it, diplomats say, and the two days of talks will be summed up in a chairman's statement issued by host Secretary of State Marco Rubio rather than a negotiated joint communique. The split was visible from the start: Rubio cancelled the traditional group photograph on Friday after European ministers objected to appearing alongside Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and threatened to boycott the picture. Participants describe the deepest divides as running over Russia's war in Ukraine, the US-Israeli war against Iran, American tariffs, and China's conduct in the South China Sea — compounded by Washington's decision not to invite G20 member South Africa. Poland's Radosław Sikorski, attending as a guest for the first time, said he was unsurprised, noting that with wars under way and belligerents seated at the same table, a common line is hard to reach. Diplomats say ministers nonetheless agreed to try to make December's leaders' summit, hosted by President Trump in Florida, a success, with Saturday's remaining sessions focused on supply chains and critical minerals.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China Economy31 October 2026

China's Factories Slip Back Into Contraction as the October PMI Falls to 49.3

China's manufacturing sector contracted in October at its fastest pace in three months, according to the official purchasing managers' survey released on Saturday. The headline index dropped to 49.3 from 50.1 in September, below the 50 mark that separates growth from contraction and short of the 50.0 economists polled by Reuters had expected. It is the third contraction in four months. Both new orders and new export orders stayed below 50, and production and purchasing activity also slipped into contraction after expanding the month before; analysts link the export softness partly to Christmas-season orders having been pulled forward into September. Services and construction weakened too, with the non-manufacturing index easing to 49.7 from 50.2. Bright spots remain in high-end industry — output and orders for railway, shipbuilding and aerospace equipment and for computers and electronics all read at or above 53 — but smaller manufacturers lagged badly. Analyst Zhang Liqun of the China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing called weak demand the central problem and urged faster policy support, including expanded public investment.

Latest · Americas · Markets — AI Selloff31 October 2026

Intel Plunges Nearly 29% as Wall Street Turns Cold on the Cost of the AI Buildout

US stocks closed sharply lower on Friday as disappointing results from Amazon and Intel revived doubts about whether Big Tech's enormous spending on artificial-intelligence infrastructure will pay off. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00 — its steepest one-day drop since early June — while the S&P 500 lost 1.82% to 7,580.07 and the Dow Jones shed 572 points, or 1.09%, to 51,770.31. Intel was the day's casualty: its shares plunged about 28.6%, wiping out more than $140 billion in market value, after the chipmaker warned it would burn cash this year amid a weak PC market, manufacturing bottlenecks and the cost of expanding production; it also blamed an AI-driven memory-chip shortage for pushing up its costs. The rout spread across semiconductors, with AMD down about 10.9%, Nvidia off 4.2% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 7.2%. Amazon reported a sharp third-quarter cash outflow and signalled still-higher capital spending, and Alphabet extended losses after its own miss a day earlier. For October as a whole, the Nasdaq fell 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz and Mediation31 October 2026

Qatari Mediators in Tehran as Iran Halts Tankers in Hormuz and Washington Weighs Its Next Move

A Qatari delegation arrived in Tehran on Friday for talks with senior Iranian officials, state media reported, as mediators try to restart negotiations with Washington amid fears the Middle East war could flare again. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they had halted vessels attempting the strait on what they called unauthorised routes: two tankers were stopped south of Hormuz after ignoring radio warnings, and a third supertanker carrying two million barrels of oil caught fire after striking a naval mine. The strait normally carries about a fifth of global oil consumption, and Tehran has restricted passage since the war began on 28 February with US-Israeli strikes that killed then-supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. President Trump has said publicly he believes the war will end soon, but reporting on Friday indicated his administration has warned Gulf allies to prepare for a possible short, intense round of renewed strikes after Tuesday's midterm elections, with Iran expected to retaliate against regional partners. On the sidelines in Atlanta, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio also discussed the Iran negotiations with Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan.

Latest · Europe · Black Sea Grain31 October 2026

Poland's Sikorski Urges a Rapid Black Sea Ceasefire So Grain Can Move Again

Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski used his guest appearance at the G20 in Atlanta to call for a quick ceasefire in the Black Sea that would let grain ships sail safely from both Ukrainian and Russian ports. He argued the urgency is humanitarian — buyers in the Global South depend on those cargoes — and insisted Ukraine must be part of any arrangement to end Russia's invasion, now in its fifth year. Sikorski also said Moscow should have to accept a fair end to the war before it is allowed to sell energy into Europe again, formerly its most lucrative market. The visit carried a second agenda: the United States, this year's G20 president, has proposed making Poland a full member of the group, and Sikorski said he had canvassed support in bilateral meetings in Atlanta. He noted that Britain, which chairs the G20 next year, has already invited Prime Minister Donald Tusk to the 2027 leaders' summit in Manchester. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov criticised Poland's invitation as counterproductive.

Protest Saturday in Europe: 70,000 Police Deployed in France, Youth Rallies in More Than 30 German Cities

France is meeting Saturday's nationwide protests with a police deployment of some 70,000 officers, determined to avoid a repeat of the riots and looting two weeks ago that damaged shops and public buildings across the country. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez has promised a tough response, and the US Embassy has advised travellers to steer clear of demonstration areas. The movement, fuelled by anger over living costs, strained public services and President Emmanuel Macron's minority government, accelerated in late September as 2027 budget talks opened against public debt nearing 120% of output; figures on Friday showed the economy already contracted in the third quarter, and the Chamber of Commerce puts retailer losses above €1 billion. The unrest has boosted far-right leader Marine Le Pen in polling ahead of April's presidential election. The same youth-led wave reaches Germany on Saturday, with demonstrations planned in more than 30 cities against compulsory military service and for greater investment in education.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Immigration31 October 2026

Britain's Settlement Overhaul Nears Its Final Form — With a Likely Reprieve for Care Workers Already in the UK

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood is expected to lay her final decision on settlement rules for legal migrants before Parliament in the coming week, and reporting on Saturday suggests the plan will be softened for one group at its centre. Mahmood had proposed stretching the wait for indefinite leave to remain from five years to ten for most workers, and to fifteen for some lower-paid migrants — a change driven by concern at how many people became eligible for permanent residence after a period of very high net migration under the previous government. The proposal drew resistance inside the government, including from Housing Secretary Angela Rayner, who before rejoining the cabinet called applying new rules to people who arrived in good faith "un-British." The BBC understands that social care workers already in Britain are now likely to be exempted from the longer wait, while most other workers would still face the ten-year route. The public consultation drew some 200,000 responses, and the Home Office says it will not detail the final decisions until they reach Parliament.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia — Courts30 October 2026

Australian Appeal Court Removes Any Chance of Release for Mushroom Murderer Erin Patterson

Victoria's Court of Appeal on Friday dismissed Erin Patterson's challenge to her convictions and went further than the original sentence, ordering that she spend the rest of her life in prison with no possibility of parole. Patterson, 52, was convicted last year of murdering three relatives of her estranged husband — his parents Don and Gail Patterson and his aunt Heather Wilkinson — by serving them beef wellington laced with death cap mushrooms at a July 2023 lunch in Leongatha, and of the attempted murder of Heather's husband Ian, who survived after weeks in hospital. She had been serving life with a 33-year non-parole period; prosecutors argued that was too lenient, and the three appeal judges agreed, describing the offending as extraordinarily cold and calculated, carried out through an elaborate series of deceptions that included a fabricated cancer diagnosis used to explain her children's absence. Patterson has always said the poisoning was accidental. The ruling makes her only the second woman in Australia sentenced to life without parole; she can still seek leave to appeal to the High Court, her final avenue.

India Marks National Unity Day as Modi Releases the 24th PM-KISAN Payment and a Rafale Lands on INS Vikrant

India observed Rashtriya Ekta Diwas — National Unity Day — on Saturday, the birth anniversary of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, with 'Run for Unity' events nationwide and the main ceremony at Ekta Nagar in Gujarat, home to the Statue of Unity. Final Rally Weekend: Trump Clashes With Protesters in Florida as Record Early Voting Rolls On

With three days left before Tuesday's midterm elections, President Donald Trump held his final in-person rally in Florida, an event briefly interrupted by protesters whom he ordered removed to cheers from the crowd, telling them to "shut up" as security escorted them out. The rally caps a campaign closing stretch in which control of Congress for the last two years of Trump's presidency is at stake. Voting is already well advanced: the University of Florida's Election Lab counted 47.9 million early ballots by midday Friday, past the previous midterm record of 45.9 million from 2022, with turnout running far ahead of the last cycle in battleground states and in Texas, where more than six million people had voted early by Wednesday. In states that register voters by party, Democrats accounted for about 44% of early ballots to Republicans' 31%, though analysts stress that registration says nothing about how those ballots were cast, and tens of millions more votes are expected on election day itself.
