Latest · Middle East · Jordan24 October 2026

Gunmen Kill Seven Mourners at a Church Funeral in Jordan, and the King Vows Retaliation

At least seven people were killed and 34 wounded on Friday when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hamoud, in Jordan's southern Karak governorate — a rare attack on the kingdom's Christian minority. Security camera footage aired by state broadcaster Al-Mamlaka showed three attackers firing from a pavement before running towards the church entrance, where between 150 and 200 people had gathered. Jordan's public security chief, Major General Abdallah al-Maaytah, said most of the wounded were still in hospital and the hunt for the attackers was continuing. Interior Minister Mazin Al Farrayeh blamed extremists but said it was too early to say whether they were linked to any known group, and no one has claimed responsibility. King Abdullah II travelled to Karak on Friday evening, visited the wounded and promised the attack would be met with the harshest retaliation. UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the shooting as especially heinous.

Latest · Europe · France24 October 2026

Macron Calls the Paris Suburb Shooting a 'Racist Terror Attack' as a Suspect Is Held

A gunman firing from a car killed one person and wounded five others, including a 12-year-old girl, in about 15 minutes of shooting across Trappes and La Verrière, two towns roughly 30 kilometres west of Paris, on Friday. The victims were hit at a petrol station, a medical centre and a children's playground; three of the wounded have life-threatening injuries, and the girl was reported to have been shot eight times. President Emmanuel Macron described the attack as a planned racist act of terrorism, saying the victims were chosen for the colour of their skin or their perceived religion, and called it a turning point for France. Police arrested a 39-year-old suspect, identified only as Kévin T., within 20 minutes; investigators found a submachine gun and tear gas in his car and a booby-trapped apartment rigged with gas cylinders and a timer. The national anti-terrorism prosecutor has taken over the case, and a member of the suspect's family is also in custody. The shooting lands amid heightened tension after violent nationwide protests on October 17, with more demonstrations expected this weekend.

Latest · Africa · Health24 October 2026

Kenya Records a Second Ebola Death as the First Vaccine Trial Opens in Congo

Kenya's health ministry confirmed a second Ebola death late on Friday — a 65-year-old woman who was a close relative and direct contact of the country's first patient, a man who had travelled from Congo and died in Nairobi on October 5. She had been in quarantine when symptoms appeared on October 13. Kenya now has nine confirmed cases, with seven patients in isolation, and officials stress that every case sits inside one traced cluster, with no sign of community spread; 135 contacts of the first patient were quarantined. On the same day, the World Health Organization announced that the first participants in the Tumaini trial — Swahili for "hope" — had been vaccinated in Bunia, the capital of Congo's Ituri province and the epicentre of the outbreak. The trial will test whether Ervebo, a vaccine licensed against the Zaire strain, offers any cross-protection against the Bundibugyo strain driving this epidemic, for which no vaccine or specific treatment is approved; two Bundibugyo-specific candidates, from Oxford and Moderna, are expected to join the trial. Congo's outbreak, declared in May, is the country's worst ever, with more than 9,700 cases and over 4,600 deaths, and cases have now been reported in four African countries.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine24 October 2026

Russia and Ukraine Agree a Local Ceasefire for Starving Oleshky, Brokered by Melania Trump

Russia and Ukraine have agreed to halt fighting in the Russian-occupied southern city of Oleshky from October 29, in a localised ceasefire the White House credits US first lady Melania Trump with brokering. The pause is meant to let humanitarian aid into the city and open a designated safe passage for civilians who want to leave; only some 1,700 to 2,000 residents remain, most of them elderly people short of food, medicine and clean water after more than four years of occupation. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed Moscow's agreement, praised the first lady's role and said the two militaries were still working out the implementation dates, with the evacuation itself expected to take several days. Ukraine's chief negotiator, David Arakhamia, said the deal followed a long process involving Melania Trump and Pope Leo XIV. Oleshky sits across the Dnipro River from Ukrainian-held Kherson and has been held by Russian forces since February 2022.

Saudi Arabia Intercepts a Houthi Missile Aimed at Riyadh as Britain Sends Jets to Help Defend the Kingdom

The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile heading towards Riyadh and a drone over the kingdom's eastern region, the latest exchange in a Yemen war that reignited in September after a years-long truce collapsed. Britain announced this week that RAF Typhoon jets will fly defensive counter-air missions to protect Saudi cities and critical infrastructure — intercepting missiles and drones but carrying out no strikes inside Yemen — alongside a transport aircraft, military advisers and extended air-to-air refuelling support. Riyadh's international airport has been targeted some 20 times since the start of the month, Defence Secretary Wes Streeting noted in condemning the attacks. The human cost inside Yemen keeps climbing: the reported death toll from Monday's Houthi missile strike on the al-Alam training camp for government forces in Shabwa province has risen to 73, with more than 200 wounded and 14 people still missing.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India24 October 2026

'Declared Dead': Indian Voters Fight to Get Back on Electoral Rolls After 130 Million Names Are Struck Off

A sweeping revision of India's electoral rolls has removed around 130 million names — including some 28 million people recorded as dead — and left living voters scrambling to prove they exist. Among them is Kamta Prasad, a 53-year-old Delhi tailor who has been shuttling between government offices, giving up his daily wage of 250 rupees, to show officials he is not dead; he is one of 4.2 million voters struck off in Delhi alone under the draft rolls published on August 31. The Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision requires voters to link themselves, or their parents or grandparents, to the rolls of 2002–03, a burden critics say falls hardest on women, minorities and the poor, many of whom have no documents. The deletions have triggered a political storm and near-daily protests in New Delhi and other cities, with opposition parties accusing election chief Gyanesh Kumar of shaping the exercise to benefit Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party — a charge both the commission and the BJP deny. The commission says the revision is lawful and meant to clean up duplicates and ineligible entries.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China24 October 2026

Tibet Quake: Rescuers Finish Three Rounds of Village-by-Village Searches as Relief Pours In

Rescue teams combing the remote, high-altitude Nagqu area of Tibet have completed three rounds of household-by-household searches after Thursday night's magnitude 7.0 earthquake, and officials say no one is reported missing in Baqen County, where the quake struck hardest. The death toll stands at 16, with 76 people injured, 339 homes collapsed and more than 12,000 residents moved to safety. More than 5,000 people from three counties joined the relief effort, and supplies have reached the region, including about 2,000 prefabricated housing units, some 3,400 tents, 2,000 folding beds and winter clothing — vital at altitudes near 4,800 metres, where cold and thin air complicate the work. Officials warned that homes left cracked or leaning by the main shock could still collapse in aftershocks — more than 400 have been recorded — even as power and communications are gradually restored. The quake, the strongest to hit China since January 2024, struck at 10:33 p.m. on Thursday with its epicentre about 190 kilometres east-northeast of Nagqu city.

Latest · Europe · Spain24 October 2026

Four Dead and More Than 2,000 Evacuated as Storm Adam Floods Eastern Spain

Torrential rain from Storm Adam killed four people and forced more than 2,000 residents from their homes along Spain's Mediterranean coast this week, in flooding that revived memories of the catastrophe that killed more than 230 people in the Valencia region two years ago. In places, as much as 700 litres of rain per square metre fell within 24 hours; the town of Pego recorded 632 millimetres in 36 hours. Two people died in the coastal town of Oliva — a 79-year-old woman found in her flooded home and a man who suffered a cardiac arrest at a campsite — a third died on the coast at Sagunto, and a fourth fell into a swollen stream in Catalonia. Emergency crews carried out more than 1,000 rescues, airlifting some residents from rooftops, and at one point around 800,000 people were ordered to stay indoors; classes were suspended in more than 40 towns across Valencia and Alicante, and flooding also disrupted the Balearic islands of Mallorca and Ibiza.

Latest · Americas · Canada24 October 2026

Alberta Votes by a Landslide to Stay in Canada, Handing Carney a Unity Victory

Voters in Alberta have overwhelmingly chosen to remain part of Canada, rejecting a proposal to start down the road towards separation in the oil-rich province's first-ever referendum on the question. With more than 1.8 million votes counted, about 76 percent backed staying, against 24 percent who wanted the province to begin the legal process that could lead to a binding independence vote; official results are due on October 29. The non-binding referendum was called by Premier Danielle Smith under pressure from the separatist wing of her governing United Conservative Party, even though she herself opposes independence. Prime Minister Mark Carney, who grew up in Alberta, said voters had made a clear choice: "They chose unity, to build together, and to take care of each other." The result is a boost for Carney's national-unity push amid a trade war and annexation threats from US President Donald Trump. Separatist leaders conceded the numbers but vowed their campaign for an independent Alberta will continue.

Washington Weighs a Weekend Strike on Iran as a Tanker Is Hit Near the Strait of Hormuz and Oil Tops $114

A US envoy has said an American strike on Iran is possible as soon as this weekend, even as President Donald Trump insists he remains open to a deal — the latest turn in a confrontation that has pushed oil prices to around $114 a barrel. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint for a fifth of the world's oil, is under mounting strain: the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a tanker struck by a projectile near Oman, and October has seen the highest number of attacks on commercial shipping in the strait since the war began, with at least 28 confirmed. European and Middle Eastern foreign ministers met in Istanbul to discuss the strait's security, while Iran's military chief warned that US warships will not be allowed back into the Gulf and Tehran vowed retaliation for any attack. In Israel, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — who has vowed to topple Iran's government — faces elections on Tuesday after two major bombing campaigns against Iran in the past year.
