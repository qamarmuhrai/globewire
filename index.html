Iran Says US Drone Downed Near Hormuz as Three More Tankers Catch Fire After Hitting Mines

The confrontation around the Strait of Hormuz sharpened overnight. Iran’s military said its forces had brought down a US-operated drone over the Persian Gulf east of the strait, while the Revolutionary Guard reported, via Fars news agency, that three more tankers had suffered severe explosions and fires after striking naval mines in the waterway in recent hours. The claims came as Qatar’s prime minister met the US secretary of state in Washington, with Iran high on the agenda; Doha warned that the Middle East cannot withstand another escalation between Washington and Tehran. In the US Senate, Republican lawmakers were reported to be weighing Democratic demands to cut funding for the Iran war, and Washington announced fresh Iran-related sanctions against at least eight people and 22 trading entities registered in Iran, the UAE, Turkiye and Hong Kong. The UN refugee agency put the number displaced by fighting in Yemen at more than 360,000, and at least four Palestinians, including an UNRWA employee, were reported killed by Israeli attacks in Gaza during the day.

Latest · Africa · Benghazi, Libya — Seven Sea Drones, 2,400 km From the Front Line24 November 2026

Ukraine Says Its Sea Drones Sank a Russian Warship in a Libyan Port and Hit a Weapons Cargo Ship

Ukraine says it sank a Russian naval corvette and struck a cargo vessel off Libya’s coast on Sunday, in one of the war’s most distant operations. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said seven Ukrainian-made naval drones attacked the corvette Merkury at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya, destroying a Ka-27 helicopter on board, and also hit the dry cargo ship MV Angara, which Kyiv says Moscow used to move weapons. Ukraine’s SBU security service said the drones were launched from a mother ship in the region and released footage of the attack. Libyan officials gave a more cautious account, saying at least four drones were intercepted, two hit the corvette, and about ten people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, were wounded; eastern Libya’s authorities condemned the strike and the UN mission in Libya called for an independent investigation. The attack followed a drone strike a day earlier on another Russian cargo ship, the Sparta II, in the Gulf of Finland, and brings the number of Russian vessels reported targeted this month to at least four.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Dhaka — Planned December Return Fuels Protests and Violence24 November 2026

Bangladesh Vows to Arrest Former Prime Minister Hasina on Sight and Presses India to Extradite Her

Bangladesh will arrest and jail former prime minister Sheikh Hasina the moment she sets foot in the country, a senior minister has said, as her planned return in December stirs protests and renewed street violence. State Minister for Foreign Affairs Shama Obaed Islam told Reuters that Dhaka is instead pressing India, where Hasina has lived since fleeing during the 2024 uprising that ended her rule, to hand her over under the existing extradition treaty. Hasina faces a death sentence at home and her Awami League party is banned; her requests for a new passport and identity card ahead of the return were refused. Her supporters have held a week of rallies culminating in a nationwide lockdown on 19 November, and the minister said there had been no official talks with New Delhi about flying her back, while insisting Bangladesh wants balanced ties with India that respect its dignity and interests. India said in April it was examining the extradition request and did not immediately comment on the latest remarks.

Latest · Americas · New York — Nasdaq Up 6.5%, S&P 500 Up 4.7% at the Close24 November 2026

Wall Street Posts a Historic Rally as Nvidia’s Returning Chief Lifts Chip Stocks and Ends a Losing Streak

Monday, 23 November is likely to be remembered as one of the most extraordinary days in US market history. Eleven days after Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia’s chief executive under a cloud of scandal, his reinstatement triggered a surge of more than 30% in Nvidia shares and a broad rebound: the Nasdaq closed up about 6.5%, the S&P 500 about 4.7% and the Dow about 3%, snapping Wall Street’s longest losing streak since April 2024, with the chip index up roughly 12% and technology stocks leading nine rising sectors. Dell gained about 20% and Micron about 18%. Reporting through the day added political colour: sources said President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance personally intervened over the weekend, convinced Huang uniquely protects American open-weight AI models, while some US lawmakers criticised the decision to restore him. The rebound also took pressure off authorities in China, Taiwan and South Korea, which had moved over the weekend to support sliding markets before the recovery made those steps less urgent.

Latest · Global Affairs · Austin and San Jose — Largest Cybersecurity Deal Ever Announced24 November 2026

CrowdStrike Agrees to Buy Zscaler for About $43.9 Billion in an All-Stock Cybersecurity Mega-Deal

CrowdStrike said on Monday it would acquire fellow cybersecurity firm Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion, the largest deal the industry has seen and one that eclipses Alphabet’s 2025 purchase of Wiz. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share they hold, valuing Zscaler at about $260.41 a share — a premium of roughly 34% to its closing price on 20 November — and leaving CrowdStrike holders with about 84% of the combined company. The pairing joins CrowdStrike’s endpoint protection business with Zscaler’s zero-trust network security, and the companies say it expands their addressable market by more than $185 billion by 2029, with combined annual recurring revenue expected above $10 billion. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry and affiliates, holding about 34% of the company, have agreed to support the deal. It still needs regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028, even as CrowdStrike shares fell about 9% on the announcement despite record quarterly results.

US Congressman Jimmy Gomez to Resign After Ethics Panel Finds Sexual Misconduct With a Staffer

Democratic Representative Jimmy Gomez of California announced on Monday that he will resign from Congress effective 1 December, within an hour of the House Ethics Committee publishing a report that found substantial evidence he made unwelcome sexual advances and nonconsensual sexual contact with a congressional aide. The committee concluded his conduct caused the staffer significant and ongoing distress, contributed to her leaving House employment, and was worsened by his failure to recognise the power imbalance between members and staff. Gomez, 51, strongly disputed the report, saying key parts of his testimony were omitted and that he was given no chance to respond, while also taking responsibility for personal mistakes outside his marriage. He had already lost his re-election bid earlier this month to challenger Angela Gonzales-Torres, so his term was ending within weeks; the committee recommended no specific sanction given the short time left but published its findings to put the conduct on record. He is the third member to resign this year in the face of sexual misconduct allegations.

Zelenskyy Urges Four-Way Peace Talks With Russia, Europe and the US ‘As Soon as Possible’

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy used a video address to NATO lawmakers gathered in Berlin on Monday to press for four-party negotiations — Ukraine, Europe, the United States and Russia — to begin as soon as possible, saying Kyiv is ready literally tomorrow. Partners hope to convene the meeting before Christmas, he said, but Ukraine wants it sooner, and he insisted the Western side must first agree a clear joint agenda so the talks produce lasting security rather than a pause before renewed aggression. In his framing, Ukraine, Europe and America would sit on one side and Russia on the other, making the negotiation in effect one between the West and Moscow about Europe’s future security architecture. He also repeated his call for a path to full NATO membership rather than guarantees alone. The Kremlin’s spokesman said new trilateral contacts would happen soon but gave no date, leaving the four-way format — and Europe’s seat at the table, which Kyiv says Washington has now accepted — still unconfirmed.

Latest · Europe · Kharkiv and Kyiv — 153 Drones Launched Overnight, 134 Downed24 November 2026

Russian Attacks Kill Four and Injure 19 Across Ukraine as Drones Hit Homes, Schools and Railways

Russian attacks killed four people and injured 19 across Ukraine over the past day, regional authorities reported on Monday, in a wave that mixed a mass overnight drone launch with strikes on railways and power networks. Ukraine’s air force said Russia sent 153 drones overnight, including jet-powered Shahed types and decoys, and that air defences downed or suppressed 134 of them, though impacts were recorded at 11 locations. In Kharkiv region, the city and 20 settlements were hit and nine people were injured, including three children; a drone struck a high-rise in the Shevchenkivskyi district and a children’s facility was damaged. A 15-year-old boy was killed in Sumy region, and kindergartens and schools were reported damaged in Zhytomyr, Dnipropetrovsk and Kyiv. Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said consequences were recorded in 12 regions and the capital, with railway stations struck in Dnipro and Zhytomyr region, a locomotive depot hit in Kharkiv, and the death toll from the earlier strike on a residential building in the Vyshhorod district rising to 11.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pyongyang — Row Over Sanctions on a Children’s Camp24 November 2026

North Korea Says It May Shut the Swiss Diplomatic Office in Pyongyang Over New Sanctions

North Korea has condemned Switzerland for imposing sanctions connected to a children’s camp in the North and its ties with Russia, and says it will consider closing the Swiss diplomatic office in Pyongyang, state media KCNA reported. The office is one of the few Western presences in the capital and has historically played an outsized role as a neutral channel and a base for humanitarian work, so even the threat of closure carries weight beyond the immediate dispute. The row underlines how Pyongyang’s deepening military and political alignment with Moscow since the invasion of Ukraine keeps pulling third countries into friction they previously avoided: measures aimed at Russia-linked activity now land directly on North Korea’s relationships in Europe. No deadline or final decision was announced, and Bern had not publicly responded in the initial reporting, leaving open whether the warning is leverage ahead of quiet talks or the start of a genuine expulsion of one of the last Western missions in the country.

Latest · Americas · Iowa — Nearly 10,000 Pounds of Degraded Explosive Chemical24 November 2026

Trump Orders the Army to Pause Detonation of a Toxic Lead Azide Stockpile in Iowa Pending a Court Ruling

President Donald Trump said on Monday he had ordered the US Army to pause the planned detonation of a deteriorating stockpile of lead azide in Iowa until a federal court can rule on the matter. The Army had intended to destroy 68 barrels — nearly 10,000 pounds, or about 4,500 kilograms — of degraded lead azide, a highly sensitive explosive chemical used in military munitions, at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant. A court issued a temporary stay halting the detonation last Thursday, and Trump’s post on Truth Social said the operation would remain paused while judges decide. The standoff captures a familiar tension in munitions disposal: degraded primary explosives become more dangerous the longer they sit, which argues for prompt destruction, while nearby communities and litigants want fuller review of the blast’s environmental and health effects before it goes ahead. No new date for the detonation was given.
