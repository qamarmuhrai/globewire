Latest · Europe · France20 October 2026

France Extends Localised Night Curfews for a Second Night After Cost-of-Living Riots

France kept partial night curfews in place for a second evening on Tuesday after weekend protests over rising prices turned into some of the country's worst street violence in years. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said local prefects would decide where restrictions were needed, in consultation with mayors. In Paris the curfew covered Place de la République and Les Halles from 11 p.m., with gatherings banned from 8 p.m.; Lille, Nice, Lyon and Bordeaux applied curbs focused in places on minors. About half a million people marched on Saturday at the peak of four weeks of unrest that began with school students. Authorities said more than 2,500 people had been detained, nearly 1,000 security personnel were injured, and at least 3,000 shops were damaged. Lecornu said he was determined to restore order and did not rule out a state of emergency if needed, while organisers called for further protests on Saturday.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza20 October 2026

Israeli Strikes and Gunfire Kill at Least Four Palestinians in Gaza, Health Officials Say

At least four Palestinians were killed in separate Israeli attacks across Gaza on Tuesday, according to health officials. An air strike on a residential flat in Gaza City killed Akram Yassin and his wife Dina Abu Baker. In the south, a 17-year-old was shot near Qizan an-Najjar in Khan Younis, and another person was killed by gunfire in Zeitoun, east of Gaza City, while artillery fire was also reported south of Khan Younis. Al Jazeera's correspondent in Deir el-Balah described a surge of overnight attacks concentrated on Gaza City, with drones and fighter jets active over central areas and a flat hit without warning. Relatives at the funeral said they were not experiencing a ceasefire in practice. Gaza health authorities put the overall toll since October 2023 at at least 74,480 killed, with the UN estimating about 82 percent of buildings damaged.

Source: Al Jazeera
Latest · Asia-Pacific · India–US20 October 2026

Rubio Meets Jaishankar in Mumbai as US Pushes India on Energy and Defence Buying

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio began a three-day visit to India on Tuesday, holding talks in Mumbai with External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar on energy security, defence, trade and emerging technology. It was Rubio's second trip to India in five months, arriving amid friction over US tariffs, immigration policy and Russian oil purchases. Rubio described India as a refining superpower and said he would visit Reliance's Jamnagar refinery, arguing that understanding refining capacity required speaking directly to the company. He also laid a wreath at the Taj Mahal Palace memorial for victims of the 26/11 attacks, including six US citizens. Jaishankar, Rubio and UAE Foreign Minister Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan also met in a trilateral format to discuss regional energy security and shared economic priorities, with West Asia described by Jaishankar as a matter of deep concern.

Latest · Middle East · Iran20 October 2026

Iran's President Pezeshkian Calls for Peace Without Surrender in Al Jazeera Interview

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has set out Tehran's position in an exclusive Al Jazeera interview recorded at the United Nations in New York, titled "Peace, Not Surrender." He portrayed Iran as the victim of what he called terrorism, citing assassinations of officials, scientists and schoolchildren in US and Israeli attacks, and said heavier US sanctions had seriously harmed the economy. Pezeshkian argued that a series of broken agreements had destroyed trust in Washington, defended Iran's retaliatory strikes on US bases in the Gulf as a response, and said regional security should be managed by regional actors rather than outside powers. He also discussed tensions around the Strait of Hormuz, the wars in Gaza and Yemen, Syria's new government, and ties with Russia and China, while appealing for Muslim unity anchored by Saudi Arabia.

Qatar Says Only Negotiations Can Ease Regional Tensions and Reopen the Strait of Hormuz

Qatar has said diplomacy remains the only path to lowering regional tensions, as mediation efforts continue over the Strait of Hormuz. Mediator Ibrahim Bin-Sultan Al Hashmi said a military solution was not possible and that negotiations were essential to restoring stability and bringing down energy prices. The comments came as separate reporting described Pakistan and Qatar jointly promoting a compromise between Washington and Tehran, and as foreign ministers gathered in Mumbai to discuss West Asia and energy security. Qatar's position underlines the central role Gulf mediators have taken as shipping through Hormuz — a chokepoint for global oil and gas — remains disrupted and as both the United States and Iran keep their core demands unchanged.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan20 October 2026

Tokyo Residents Plan Japan's First Civil Lawsuit to Stop a Major Data Centre Project

Residents in Akishima, western Tokyo, said on Tuesday they will file a civil lawsuit this year against GLP Japan to halt a large logistics and data centre complex — which they described as Japan's first such case against a data centre operator. The planned development includes three logistics buildings and eight data centre buildings. Opponents say the project would threaten local wildlife, strain water supplies, add pollution and sharply raise electricity use. The group said years of campaigning had not changed the developer's plans. The dispute highlights growing friction in Japan between national efforts to expand digital infrastructure and neighbourhood concerns about the environmental and social costs of very large data facilities.

Source: Reuters
Latest · Asia-Pacific · Vietnam20 October 2026

Maersk Arm and Hateco to Study $3.5 Billion Rail Freight Network in Northern Vietnam

APM Terminals, the ports business of Denmark's A.P. Moller-Maersk, and Vietnam's Hateco Group have signed a non-binding agreement to study building and operating railway freight terminals in northern Vietnam, the Vietnamese government said on Tuesday. The signing took place during a visit to Denmark by Vietnam's leader To Lam. The study will cover a terminal in Hai Phong with cargo handling and storage, plus three additional sites, intended to link industrial parks, logistics hubs and manufacturing centres to major gateways as one integrated system. Preliminary investment was put at up to $3.5 billion. Officials said the network could relieve pressure on roads, improve freight efficiency and widen options for import, export and cross-border trade. The two companies opened a $400 million deep-water container terminal in Hai Phong in 2025.

A woman has died after being attacked by a shark while swimming near KuGompo City in South Africa's Eastern Cape province on Tuesday, public broadcaster SABC reported. Latest · Americas · United States20 October 2026

Small Businesses Sue to Block Trump's August Tariffs on Imports From Canada

A group of small businesses filed a new lawsuit on Tuesday seeking to block President Donald Trump's August tariffs on Canadian imports, arguing the duties are another attempt to claim an unrestricted presidential tariff power that courts have already rejected. The case adds to ongoing legal challenges over the administration's use of emergency and trade authorities to impose broad levies. It lands two weeks before the midterm elections, as separate polling shows voter concern about prices and trade weighing on the administration. The outcome could affect importers who rely on Canadian supply chains, though previous tariff litigation has moved slowly through federal courts.

Source: Reuters
Latest · Americas · Brazil20 October 2026

Brazil's Flavio Bolsonaro Pledges to Restore Events Tax Break Ahead of Sunday Runoff

Senator Flavio Bolsonaro said on Tuesday he would restore Perse, the tax relief programme for Brazil's events sector, if he wins Sunday's presidential runoff, courting an industry hit hard by the pandemic. Perse was created in 2021 to support events, tourism and related businesses and was later narrowed. Bolsonaro finished unexpectedly ahead of President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva in the first round earlier this month, and recent polls show him holding a narrow lead. His campaign has promised broad tax cuts alongside a faster fiscal adjustment — pledges economists note sit in tension, because lower taxes would also cut government revenue. The runoff will decide whether Brazil shifts course after Lula's term.
