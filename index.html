Latest · Asia-Pacific · Markets — Nikkei −5.96%, Bear Market6 November 2026

Tokyo’s Third-Worst Day Ever: Nikkei Sheds 3,643 Points and Falls 20% from Its Peak

Japan’s Nikkei 225 closed on Friday at 57,434.77, down 3,643.13 points or 5.96 per cent — the third-largest single-day point fall in its history, behind only the August 2024 yen-carry crash and the 1987 Black Monday aftermath. The index lost more than 4,300 points intraday, closed below 60,000 for the first time in about six months, and is now down 20.6 per cent from its all-time high of 72,366 on 25 June, the textbook threshold for a bear market. It was the fifth straight losing session, a cumulative drop of 7,224 points. The broader Topix fell 5.90 per cent to 3,707.99, and 98 per cent of Prime Market stocks declined. The trigger was the same one that hit Seoul in the morning: Wall Street’s Thursday rout — Nasdaq down 5.52 per cent, the semiconductor index down 12.24 per cent — and investor fear that the new Democratic majority in Washington will tighten rules on AI data centres, starving the build-out that has powered chip and infrastructure shares. SoftBank Group fell 10.83 per cent, Kioxia 8.72 per cent, and Fujikura at one point plunged 20 per cent. The 10-year Japanese government bond yield fell 0.12 points to 2.635 per cent on safe-haven buying, and analysts warned the slump itself acts like financial tightening, complicating the Bank of Japan’s next move.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Senate Trial, Day Two6 November 2026

Peña in the Dock: Senate Resumes at 10 a.m., Prosecutors Open Three Criminal Cases

Paraguay’s impeachment trial moved into its decisive day on Friday. The Senate, which opened proceedings at 7:45 p.m. on Thursday after the Chamber of Deputies voted 78–0 to impeach, resumed at 10 a.m. with President Santiago Peña expected to present his defence in person — he has promised to argue “with arguments and evidence, with respect for the Constitution”. The timetable envisages evidence in the afternoon, closing arguments and a recorded vote, across up to five sessions aimed at finishing Friday. In parallel, Attorney General Emiliano Rolón Fernández has opened three criminal investigations covering the supervision and resolution of Ueno Bank, the placement of public funds in the lender, and the conduct of current and former officials, including Peña himself, though he has not been charged. A judge has ordered six Ueno executives into preventive detention, including founder Miguel Vázquez and bank president Juan Manuel Gustale, with two more under house arrest; 27 officials and associates face charges including breach of trust and attempted fraud. The central bank began deposit-guarantee payments on Friday to a first group of customers, capped at 10 million guaraníes — about $1,565 at the day’s rate. Opposition senators argue resignation must not be allowed to stand because it would preserve Peña’s claim to a senator-for-life seat and its legal protections; removal by the Senate would strip that path. If he is removed, Vice President Pedro Alliana succeeds him.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Verdict at Southwark6 November 2026

Russell Brand Convicted of Rape and Three Sexual Assaults; Judge Warns of a Long Sentence

A jury at London’s Southwark Crown Court convicted comedian and actor Russell Brand, 51, on Thursday of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, after about eight and a half hours of deliberation at the end of a four-week trial. He was acquitted of two further rape counts and one count of attempted indecent assault. The offences he was convicted of involved four women between 2004 and 2009; the prosecution said the rape victim was a fan he met after a 2009 stand-up show at the Hammersmith Apollo. Brand had pleaded not guilty to all seven charges, which spanned allegations from 1999 to 2009 involving six women. Mr Justice Joel Bennathan told him a custodial sentence of some length is inevitable, refused bail on flight-risk grounds and remanded him in custody. Sentencing is set for Monday, 9 November, at the same court; rape carries a maximum of life imprisonment in England and Wales. The Metropolitan Police say a separate investigation into allegations by two other women remains open, with no charges yet. The verdict has also returned attention to how the BBC and Channel 4 handled complaints during Brand’s broadcasting years.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Diet Votes amid the Rout6 November 2026

While Markets Crashed, the Diet Cut the Food Tax: Lower House Passes Takaichi’s Flagship Bill

On the same day its stock market recorded a historic fall, Japan’s House of Representatives passed the government’s flagship cost-of-living bill in a fast-tracked plenary vote on Friday. The measure cuts the consumption tax on food and beverages — reported at from 8 per cent to 1 per cent for two years — and introduces an income-linked cash benefit programme. It is the top priority of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s government in the extraordinary Diet session running to 12 December, and it cleared the lower chamber on the strength of the ruling coalition’s supermajority there. Its path now hardens: the coalition lacks a majority in the upper house, where the bill’s fate is uncertain. The government also unveiled an emergency cybersecurity package on Friday responding to a recent spate of personal-data breaches from unauthorised access, amid rising public concern. Separately, Japan and the US military agreed to increase the frequency and expand the areas of joint patrols in Okinawa following the alleged robbery and murder of a local woman by a US Marine, Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi said. And the Imperial Household Agency announced that Kuniaki Kuni, a cousin of former Emperor Akihito, died of pneumonia on Friday aged 97.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 79% Counted6 November 2026

Count Passes 79%: ANC Stuck at 31.8%, DA Sweeps Gauteng Metros, Official Result Due Sunday

South Africa’s municipal count advanced to 79 per cent of voting districts by Friday evening, with 21.69 million valid votes tallied and 4,212 of 8,825 council seats declared — and the historic shape of the result unchanged. The Electoral Commission’s live figures put the ANC on 31.8 per cent and 1,885 seats, the Democratic Alliance on 24.2 per cent, the MK Party on 9.3 per cent and the IFP on 4.9 per cent. BusinessTech reported the DA sweeping through Gauteng’s metros as counting neared completion in Buffalo City, where the ANC held about 42 per cent with 98.4 per cent counted. The ANC’s fall from 45.6 per cent in 2021 remains its worst national showing since 1994, and hung councils still dominate: most of the country’s municipalities will be decided by coalition talks rather than outright majorities, with Jacob Zuma’s MK Party entrenched as KwaZulu-Natal’s leading force and the ANC reduced to third place in that province. The commission is expected to announce the official outcome and seat allocations over the weekend — Sunday, 8 November, is the stated target, with a legal deadline of 11 November. The rand traded around 16.52 to the dollar on Friday, little moved as investors waited for the final numbers.

Latest · Americas · Vatican — 13½ Hours over the Atlantic6 November 2026

Pope Leo over the Atlantic: Montevideo Landing at 4:30 p.m., First Papal Visit since 1988

Pope Leo XIV’s chartered ITA Airways aircraft was mid-Atlantic on Friday afternoon, on a 13½-hour, roughly 6,900-mile flight from Rome to Montevideo that opened the longest journey of his pontificate — 12 days, ten cities and nearly 40 addresses in Spanish across Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, ending 17 November. Landing at Carrasco International Airport is scheduled for 4:30 p.m. local time, followed by a 5:30 p.m. welcome ceremony at Plaza Independencia, meetings with President Yamandú Orsi, authorities and diplomats, and Vespers at the Metropolitan Cathedral. Uruguayan police have warned of traffic restrictions around the airport and the delegation routes. The trip comes just three weeks after the 71-year-old pope’s 13 October lung-cancer surgery; the Vatican describes its medical precautions as routine for a man who has undergone an operation, not a permanent condition, and Leo has completed the required convalescence. Uruguay — Latin America’s most secular country, where Christians are a minority — has not received a pope since John Paul II in 1988. Ahead: Saturday’s Mass in Paysandú and youth vigil at the Estadio Centenario, Sunday’s Mass in Florida, then Argentina’s first papal visit in 39 years and the longest leg in Peru, where Leo spent more than two decades as a missionary and bishop.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations — Eighth Straw Poll6 November 2026

Grynspan Widens Her Lead for UN Chief: 12 Encourage, 3 Discourage — but the Veto Question Stays Hidden

Costa Rica’s Rebeca Grynspan strengthened her hold on the race to succeed António Guterres in the Security Council’s eighth straw poll on Thursday, winning 12 “encourage” votes against three “discourage” and no “no opinion” — her best result yet, up from 11–4 in the seventh round. Grynspan, a former vice president who heads the UN trade body UNCTAD, has now led seven of the eight polls. Argentina’s Rafael Grossi, the IAEA director general, stayed second at 9–4–2, followed by Guyana’s former foreign minister Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett at 8–5–2 and the UAE’s Lana Nusseibeh at 7–6–2. Türkiye’s late entrant Feridun Sinirlioğlu, the OSCE secretary general nominated on 1 November, debuted in sixth place at 3–8–4; he addresses the General Assembly in an informal dialogue on Friday and meets council members on 9 November. The catch is in the ballot design: Thursday’s vote used undifferentiated ballots, so nobody outside the room knows whether any of Grynspan’s three discourages came from a veto-holding permanent member — in the one colour-coded poll last month, two did. A candidate needs nine votes and no veto in the formal vote before the General Assembly appoints. Guterres’s term ends on 31 December, and Russia has restated that the next secretary-general should come from Latin America and the Caribbean — the region whose turn diplomats say it is, and home to the top three candidates.

Latest · Americas · Grenada — 14 of 15 Seats6 November 2026

A 14–1 Landslide in Grenada: Dickon Mitchell Turns Nine Seats into a Near-Sweep

Grenada’s Prime Minister Dickon Mitchell has secured a second term with one of the most one-sided results in the island’s history. His National Democratic Congress won 14 of the 15 seats in the House of Representatives in Thursday’s general election, up from the nine with which it formed a government in 2022, and took 37,369 votes — 61 per cent of valid ballots — on turnout of about 63.3 per cent. The opposition New National Party, led by Emmalin Pierre, was reduced to a single seat, St George North West, held by Adrian Joseph, with 22,567 votes or 36.84 per cent; the Democratic People’s Movement finished third on 1.94 per cent. The Parliamentary Elections Office figures remain provisional pending official certification, with 279 of 282 polling divisions reported on election night. The campaign closed amid a legal fight over diaspora voting: on 4 November, High Court Justice Agnes Actie dismissed a challenge and ruled Mitchell had acted constitutionally in dissolving Parliament. Mitchell, who has pressed Britain for a slavery apology and reparations, was congratulated on election night by Guyana’s President Irfaan Ali, among other regional leaders.

Latest · Europe · Sweden — Schengen Checks to May 20276 November 2026

Sweden Extends Border Controls Again: Six More Months from 12 November, Citing Russian Hybrid Threats

Sweden will reintroduce controls at its internal Schengen borders for a further six months, from 12 November 2026 to 11 May 2027, the government announced on Thursday — the day after its security service Säpo warned that the risk of Russian sabotage in Sweden has increased. Stockholm’s stated assessment is that Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine has created a serious threat to public order and internal security, with hybrid threats and hostile actors moving within the passport-free Schengen area; Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer pointed to a heightened sabotage threat against Sweden and other member states. The European Commission’s notification list records the new period as covering all internal borders — land, air and sea — plus the land border with Denmark, with reasons also including organised crime and the instrumentalisation of migration. Police will decide where and how checks are carried out; controls do not mean passport inspection at every crossing. Sweden has maintained such controls almost continuously since November 2015, renewing them in six-month steps, and it is not alone: France, Germany, Denmark, Norway and others are running similar temporary regimes into 2027, a standing exception to Schengen’s free-movement principle that EU law allows only as a last resort, limited in time and proportionate to the threat.

The War That Cost the Midterms: Trump Weighs What to Do Next on Iran

The hardest question left by Tuesday’s midterm rout is not personnel but war. President Trump, taking stock of a defeat that cost Republicans both chambers, must now decide the future of the eight-month-old war against Iran that voters plainly punished — in his first post-election comments on Wednesday he largely agreed with Vice President JD Vance’s public verdict that the war was a catastrophe for the party: “The reason we lost is the war.” The options are all costly: press on and own an unpopular conflict with a hostile Congress, seek an exit that critics will call surrender, or escalate toward the wider target set Tehran has threatened to answer by widening its own — a scenario European analysts warn could put Europe in the line of fire. Latest · Middle East · Israel — Decision on Sunday6 November 2026

Herzog to Name His Pick on Sunday — Eisenkot Holds 67, Netanyahu 46

Israel’s week of presidential consultations closed on Thursday with the arithmetic unchanged and the decision deferred to the weekend. President Isaac Herzog finished his round of meetings with the incoming Knesset factions and announced he will task a lawmaker with forming the next government on Sunday, saying he intends to leave no stone unturned in the search for a stable government and will hold private talks with party leaders in the meantime. Gadi Eisenkot of Yashar holds the backing of 67 of the 120 lawmakers — his own party plus Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Democrats, Ra’am and the Joint List, after both Arab-majority parties endorsed him on Thursday — against 46 for caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from Likud, Shas, Religious Zionism, Zehut and Otzma Yehudit. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism recommended no one, leaving seven seats uncommitted. A recommendation is not a coalition promise, and Eisenkot’s hardest work — turning endorsements into 61 voting partners — starts only once the mandate lands; he would then have 28 days, extendable by 14. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November, and Eisenkot’s bloc is also preparing an early vote to replace Speaker Amir Ohana, who said he would schedule one within a week if 61 lawmakers formally ask.
