Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz31 October 2026

Trump Says the Iran War Will Be 'Wrapped Up Soon' as More Tankers Are Hit in Hormuz

President Donald Trump has said he believes the war with Iran will end soon, telling a Kansas City radio station, "It's going to get wrapped up soon," while insisting any settlement must guarantee Tehran never obtains a nuclear weapon. He gave no timetable or mechanism, and separately told reporters Iran "keeps talking" and Washington has time — remarks that came as concern grew that US strikes could resume after the 3 November midterms. On the water, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said four tankers had caught fire after striking mines on what Tehran calls unauthorised routes through the Strait of Hormuz, while Oman said its navy rescued 25 crew from a Panama-flagged merchant ship set ablaze off Musandam. US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper countered that he had flown over the strait and seen dozens of commercial vessels transiting freely, that Iran's navy is incapacitated and that the main lanes have been cleared of mines; Washington also says its blockade has halted Iranian oil exports while Gulf partners have shipped more than 1.5 billion barrels since the summer. A Qatari delegation has returned to Tehran with updated proposals, and Iranian sources say Tehran has received a US letter on ending the war, though no deal has been announced.

Latest · Global Affairs · G20 Atlanta30 October 2026

At the G20, Britain's Foreign Secretary Tells Lavrov to His Face: 'We All Know You Are the Aggressor'

The second day of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta laid bare how far apart the bloc remains. Host Marco Rubio urged ministers to cooperate on stability, energy and food security, trade and freedom of navigation, warning that interconnected crises threaten the global economic order. But the invitation to Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov dominated the room: Britain's Ed Miliband addressed him directly, saying Russian drones had killed 15 people only the day before and that everyone knew Russia was the aggressor. Lavrov replied that Moscow's position is unchanged and that it cannot accept Ukraine as a military threat to its lawful security interests. Canada's minister missed the opening session, the traditional family photo was dropped after Europeans refused to be pictured with Lavrov, and diplomats expect the meeting to close with only a US chair's summary rather than a joint declaration. Attention now turns to Saturday's sessions on supply chains and critical minerals — including Rubio's Pax Silica push to counter China's dominance in rare earths — a Quad meeting on the sidelines, and the leaders' summit in Miami on 14–16 December, which Vladimir Putin may or may not attend.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Air Defence30 October 2026

US Approves a $2.1 Billion Sale of 500 Patriot Missiles to Ukraine — But Delivery Is Years Away

The US State Department has approved the sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptors to Ukraine for an estimated $2.1 billion, one of the largest defensive-weapons steps since the second Trump administration began. The PAC-3 MSE is among the few systems able to shoot down incoming ballistic missiles, and Lockheed Martin is the principal contractor. Kyiv is not being given the missiles: it will pay using contributions from Canada, Germany and Norway channelled through the US JUMPSTART programme, plus national funds financed by the EU's Ukraine Support Loan. The catch is timing. The interceptors have yet to be manufactured, and people briefed on the decision say delivery is not expected until a couple of years into the 2030s. President Zelenskyy, who had waited almost a month for Washington's sign-off while Russia stepped up strikes on energy and civilian infrastructure, appealed publicly for a different sequence — hand over interceptors from partners' stocks this winter, when the need is acute, and replenish those arsenals later from the newly contracted production.

Latest · Americas · Canada — EU Alliance30 October 2026

Canada and the EU Launch an 'Alliance for the Future' in Montreal, With a Ukraine Loan at Its Centre

Canada and the European Union closed a two-day summit in Montreal on Friday by launching what they call an Alliance for the Future, with more than twenty measures spanning trade, digital rules, critical minerals, artificial intelligence, energy and security. Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Council President António Costa; the sides signed a digital trade agreement and a pact allowing the European Investment Bank to back Canadian critical-mineral projects. Carney also announced an initial €500 million Canadian contribution to the EU's €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan for 2026–27 — a mechanism that would also let Kyiv buy equipment from Canadian defence firms with EU financing, as Ukraine faces a budget shortfall of at least €70 billion next year even after €45 billion already set to be disbursed. Carney described the talks as a step toward Canada becoming the EU's first "associate member," but the term has no legal basis, is contested by some EU states, and was left out of the final joint statement; leaders set an eight-month negotiation window, with terms targeted for mid-2027.

Zelenskyy Meets Erdoğan in Istanbul, With Trilateral Talks Eyed in Abu Dhabi Next Week

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is in Istanbul on Saturday for talks with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, part of a renewed diplomatic push after more than four and a half years of war. In his nightly address Zelenskyy called the Turkey discussions "important," said Kyiv is pursuing every available diplomatic opening, and expressed hope that November will bring movement toward formats long under discussion. He has said he wants a trilateral meeting of Ukrainian, Russian and US negotiators in Abu Dhabi between 6 and 10 November, with Europe involved in any settlement talks. Türkiye, which has hosted several rounds between Ukrainian and Russian officials since 2022, has repeatedly offered to facilitate a leaders-level meeting. The diplomacy runs in parallel with the fighting and with Poland's call in Atlanta — see the G20 reports in this edition — for a rapid Black Sea ceasefire so grain from both Ukraine and Russia can move safely to buyers in the Global South.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election30 October 2026

Israel's Final Count Leaves Netanyahu's Bloc on 53 Seats — Eight Short — and Arab Parties Holding the Balance

Final results from Tuesday's Israeli election confirm that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc has lost its parliamentary majority, setting up weeks of bargaining in a fragmented Knesset. Netanyahu's right-wing and religious allies won 53 of 120 seats, with his Likud the largest single party on 27. The opposition grouping led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot took 48, while Arab-led parties — the Joint List with 11 and Ra'am with eight — secured a record 19 between them. No camp reaches the 61 needed to govern alone. On paper, either side could get there only with Arab-party support, yet leaders on both sides have called reliance on those parties unthinkable or have pledged against it, which is why analysts expect difficult, possibly prolonged negotiations. Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving prime minister, remains in office in the interim and has claimed his bloc holds a clear majority among Zionist voters; his party has also made unsubstantiated claims that the vote was stolen. Official certification is expected on 4 November, after which President Isaac Herzog will consult party leaders, and the new Knesset is due to be sworn in on 10 November.

Latest · Europe · France and Germany Protests31 October 2026

France Deploys 70,000 Police for Halloween Protests as Germany's Gen Z Movement Expects 160,000 on the Streets

France is bracing for a fresh wave of unrest on Saturday, deploying some 70,000 police after riots and looting two weeks ago caught security forces off guard and left shops and public buildings damaged across the country. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez has vowed there will be no repeat, and the US Embassy has advised travellers to avoid demonstration areas. The protests, driven by anger over living costs, public services and President Emmanuel Macron's minority government, gathered pace in late September as 2027 budget talks began against public debt approaching 120% of output; new data on Friday showed the economy already shrinking in the third quarter, and the Chamber of Commerce puts retailer losses above €1 billion. Across the Rhine, the youth-led wave reaches Germany the same day: the campaign group Gen Z regelt expects more than 160,000 people at rallies in 34 cities including Berlin, Frankfurt and Munich, opposing compulsory military service and demanding more investment in education. German authorities expect the gatherings to be largely peaceful but warn extremist groups may try to exploit them; the national school-student body has distanced itself and plans its own protest day on 20 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · Crypto Heist31 October 2026

After the Record $6.3 Billion Binance Heist, Rival Exchanges Scramble to Lock Down Their Own Vaults

Cryptocurrency exchanges are tightening defences and calling for industry-wide information-sharing after a record $6.3 billion theft from Binance, the world's largest exchange, exposed how vulnerable the sector remains as it moves deeper into mainstream finance. Binance halted withdrawals after Thursday's attack, which US blockchain firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis say unfolded in about 20 minutes and bears the hallmarks of previous operations blamed on North Korea; Pyongyang routinely denies such involvement and did not respond to requests for comment. Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said the complex attack may have begun with a compromised software engineer's laptop, giving the thieves access to withdrawal-processing systems, and the exchange says company funds will cover customer losses. Coinbase says it has adjusted how it stores keys offline, stablecoin issuer Tether has frozen nearly $1.57 billion linked to the theft and Circle a further $800 million. Bitcoin fell more than 4% and ether more than 5% on Thursday before partly recovering on Friday. Executives are now pressing for a coordinated hack-detection system, arguing that Trump's regulators' push for deeper integration of digital assets raises the stakes of the next breach.

China is willing to import more high-quality German products, Premier Li Qiang told visiting German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier on Friday, as Beijing and Berlin seek to shore up trade ties amid rising protectionism between Europe and China. Latest · Global Affairs · Biodiversity COP1730 October 2026

Nearly 200 Nations Agree in Yerevan to Try to Get the 2030 Nature Goals Back on Track

Nearly 200 nations closed the COP17 biodiversity summit in Yerevan, Armenia, on Friday by adopting hard-fought agreements meant to revive the world's 2022 pledge to halt the destruction of nature by 2030. Delegates cheered as COP17 president Hambardzum Matevosyan gaveled the texts through in overtime after two weeks of tense negotiation; the final documents were not immediately published. The need was not in dispute: ahead of the summit the UN warned most objectives are falling short, including protecting 30% of land and oceans, tackling pollution and delivering conservation finance to poorer countries, with current commitments on course to protect only about 26% of land and 12% of sea. A draft text called on countries to accelerate efforts and tackle the root drivers of nature loss. Money remained the sharpest fault line — the 2022 framework calls for at least $200 billion a year by 2030, while recent flows are estimated at well under half that — and talks also struggled over reforming the Cali Fund, which is meant to share profits from companies using genetic data from nature. Ecuador will host the next summit in 2028.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms30 October 2026

Record 47.9 Million Americans Have Already Voted — and a Judge Bars Threats Against Election Officials

Early voting in the US midterm elections has already broken records: the University of Florida's Election Lab counted 47,922,669 early ballots by midday on Friday, passing the previous midterm record of 45.9 million set in 2022, with turnout surging in battleground states whose Senate races could decide control of Congress for the final two years of Donald Trump's presidency. Texas alone recorded more than six million early votes by Wednesday, with in-person turnout about 50% ahead of 2022; in states that track party registration, Democrats made up about 44% of early voters to Republicans' 31%, and Georgia's in-person early voting broke its state midterm record, with women casting 56% of ballots there. Analysts caution that registration does not reveal how anyone voted and that tens of millions more will vote on Tuesday, 3 November. Separately on Friday, US District Judge Trina Thompson ordered the Justice Department to stop threatening to prosecute state and local election officials who refuse administration demands based on its list of alleged non-citizen voters, and barred it from ordering officials to remove voters, refuse ballots or segregate ballots on that basis. The department had no immediate comment.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations30 October 2026

Costa Rica's Grynspan Widens Her Lead in the Race to Succeed Guterres as UN Secretary-General

Former Costa Rican vice-president Rebeca Grynspan has extended her lead in the contest to become the United Nations' tenth secretary-general, according to two people close to the process. In the Security Council's seventh informal straw poll on Friday, Grynspan drew 11 votes in favour and four against — her strongest showing yet — as the 15-member council tests support for candidates to succeed António Guterres, who finishes his second five-year term this year. Friday's ballot was the first since UAE diplomat Lana Nusseibeh entered the race; she received seven votes in favour, six against and two marked no opinion. A formal recommendation requires at least nine positive votes and no "discourage" votes from any of the five permanent members — Britain, China, France, Russia and the United States — and Friday's ballots were not coded in a way that reveals how the permanent members voted. Whoever wins will inherit an organisation strained by multiple wars, cuts in American contributions and Washington's withdrawal from dozens of UN agencies, alongside questions about the body's political relevance.
