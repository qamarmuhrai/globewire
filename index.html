Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Xi in the Chair19 November 2026

The Bilaterals Are Done: Xi Chairs the First Three-Way Table

The sequence in Shenzhen has reached its centrepiece. Chinese state media report that Xi Jinping will chair the unprecedented trilateral session with Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit, after Trump’s bilateral and working lunch with Xi ended and a separate Trump–Putin conversation of roughly 40 minutes covered the war in Ukraine, according to the Kremlin, without any announced measures. Trump called the gathering a “win” before it began, pointing to the airport welcome Xi gave him on Wednesday, and said he wants denuclearisation on the table; Beijing frames the agenda more broadly as world peace and stability in relations. Analysts remain sceptical of substance: no fixed agenda has been published, no joint statement is expected, and the three leaders arrive with different priorities across Ukraine, Iran, trade, technology and nuclear arms — New START having expired in February, leaving the two largest arsenals uncapped. Fresh Russian strikes on Odesa and Sumy and Ukrainian drone attacks on Kursk on Thursday underlined how far the battlefield is from the negotiating room. Japanese reporting says Trump and Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi plan a phone call immediately after the trilateral.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Dhaka — Lockdown Day19 November 2026

Yunus’s Ancestral Home Torched as Bangladesh Shuts Down

Bangladesh’s political crisis turned violent on the day of a nationwide shutdown. Police said two members of the ruling party were killed and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus in Chattogram, about 300 km southeast of Dhaka, was set ablaze late on Wednesday — flammable material apparently thrown into an unused ground-floor room while family members on the upper floors escaped unharmed. The banned Awami League of exiled former prime minister Sheikh Hasina called Thursday’s lockdown as the culmination of a week of protests against the government’s refusal to issue her a new passport and identity card for a planned return in December. Crude-bomb explosions were reported in the capital before dawn, and the bank Yunus founded, Grameen Bank, said at least 37 of its branches and offices have been targeted in arson attacks or attempted fires since 10 November. To contain the unrest, 97 platoons of Border Guard Bangladesh deployed across seven districts, including Gopalganj and Faridpur. The shutdown falls days before the first anniversary of the verdict that sentenced Hasina, 79, to death in absentia for ordering the deadly 2024 crackdown on the student uprising that drove her from power.

Crew Safe After Engine-Room Hit: The Tanker Strike, Clarified

One detail from the overnight Hormuz attack has firmed up: nobody was hurt. 80,000 Evacuated, 74 Rescue Teams Out: Odisha Braces for Akvan

The evacuation is now the story on India’s east coast. Odisha officials said more than 80,000 people have been moved to safety, 74 rescue teams deployed and schools closed across exposed districts as Cyclone Akvan accelerates over the Bay of Bengal, its forward speed reaching about 12 km/h in the India Meteorological Department’s Thursday morning bulletin. The forecast track has narrowed: a severe cyclonic storm crossing between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, close to east of Gopalpur, around noon on Friday 20 November, with sustained winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110 — strong, but no longer expected to reach the “very severe” category projected earlier. Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Final Week at COP3119 November 2026

Ministers Take the Pen at COP31, With a Phase-Out Fight Unresolved

The climate talks in Antalya have entered the phase where drafts go to ministers. The COP31 presidency’s first cover text — 18 pages and 144 paragraphs, drawn from submissions by some 160 countries — proposes accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, including an immediate transition away from them in energy systems, a tripling of adaptation finance for developing countries to at least $120 billion a year by 2035, and raising electricity’s share of global final energy demand to 35% by the same year. It also acknowledges that warming is projected to overshoot the Paris 1.5°C threshold and urges bringing temperatures back below it as early as possible. None of it is agreed: the presidency itself stresses the text reflects the breadth of positions, not consensus, and Australia’s Chris Bowen, who presides over the negotiations under a split presidency with Türkiye, has said some clauses have already been rejected. With nearly 200 parties needing consensus before Friday’s scheduled close on 20 November, the fossil-fuel language and the finance figure are the two fights most likely to decide whether Antalya delivers.

Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — Reading Due19 November 2026

At the Peace Palace, a Seven-Year Wait Nears Its Answer

The World Court’s Rohingya judgment is due to be read later on Thursday, and the legal question at its heart is narrow and enormous at once: whether Myanmar, as a state, acted with intent to destroy the Rohingya in whole or in part during the 2017 military campaign in Rakhine State that drove more than 700,000 people into Bangladesh. The Gambia filed the case in November 2019 with the backing of the 57-member Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, alleging breaches of the 1948 Genocide Convention; Myanmar denies genocide and describes the operation as counter-terrorism after insurgent attacks. In eight decades the court has never found a state responsible for genocide, and this is only its third merits judgment in a genocide case. The ruling, to be read by President Yuji Iwasawa, is final with no appeal; enforcement would fall to the UN Security Council. Whatever the bench decides will be studied far beyond Cox’s Bazar, where about 1.2 million Rohingya remain in the world’s largest refugee settlement — not least by the lawyers arguing the separate genocide case now before the same court.

$81.3 Billion Raised, Grey Market at $98: Anthropic’s First Day

Anthropic’s debut is bigger than first reported, and the market is already marking it up. Final filings show the Claude maker enlarged its offering to 980 million shares at $83 — 956 million sold by the company and the rest by existing holders — raising about $81.3 billion, surpassing SpaceX’s $75 billion June listing to become the largest IPO on record, with a further $26 billion in concurrent private placements from strategic investors taking the total deal above $107 billion. The sale was reported nearly seven times oversubscribed. Ahead of the first Nasdaq trades under the ticker ANT on Thursday, over-the-counter and pre-market pricing hovered near $98 a share, a premium of more than 18% over the offer price. The disclosures behind the valuation remain stark: revenue of $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $2.5 billion a year earlier, with third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a 22-bank syndicate; the offering closes on Friday 20 November, and underwriters hold an option on 147 million further shares.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Kumamoto — 2:22 a.m.19 November 2026

Magnitude 5.7 at 2:22 a.m.: Kumamoto Is Shaken Again

Southwestern Japan was woken by a strong aftershock in a prefecture still recovering from disaster. A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck the Amakusa-Ashikita area of Kumamoto Prefecture at 2:22 a.m. on Thursday at a depth of about 10 km, registering lower 5 on Japan’s seven-point intensity scale in Minamata and injuring two people, authorities said. No tsunami warning was issued. The Japan Meteorological Agency urged residents to stay alert for about a week for another quake of similar strength, and noted the tremor is believed to be part of the seismic sequence that began with the magnitude 7.1 earthquake of 28 July, which hit the maximum intensity of 7 and killed more than 30 people. Minamata and Kuma issued evacuation orders, and JR Kyushu suspended eight shinkansen services between Hakata and Kagoshima-Chuo, affecting around 5,600 passengers. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, in China for the APEC summit, posted that she had been briefed and had instructed agencies to assess the situation and keep the public informed. It was the first quake in the prefecture to reach intensity lower 5 or above since 1 August.

Latest · Europe · Kerch Strait — Third Strike in a Week19 November 2026

Hit Again After Midnight: The Crimean Bridge Takes a Third Attack in Seven Days

The bridge Vladimir Putin opened in 2018 as the symbol of Russia’s hold on Crimea has been struck for the third time this week. Crimea’s Russian-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said traffic over the 19-kilometre crossing was temporarily suspended after an attack just after midnight on Thursday, with emergency services at the scene and damage still being assessed; rail traffic was later reported resumed. The new strike follows Tuesday’s far heavier blow, when Ukrainian missiles and drones destroyed the bridge’s central road section and brought its main road arch down into the Kerch Strait, in what Ukraine’s SBU security service described as a seven-phase operation prepared over weeks. The timing is part of the message: the latest attack landed hours before Putin’s meetings with Trump and Xi in Shenzhen. The bridge is both a principal supply route for Russian forces and a flagship of the annexation Moscow proclaimed in 2014. Russia has vowed to rebuild, but specialists cited by Ukrainian media put full repairs at a year or more — a span that would leave Crimea’s logistics dependent on ferries and the land corridor through occupied southern Ukraine.
