Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Supertanker Sunk, Tehran Says16 November 2026

A Supertanker With Two Million Barrels of Oil Has Sunk in Hormuz, Iran Says — and a Second Tanker Burned Beside It

The war at sea took its sharpest turn yet on Sunday. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers travelling what they called an unauthorised route through the southern Strait of Hormuz struck naval mines within hours of each other, exploding and catching fire — and that one of them, a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude, sank in the southern waters of the Gulf. Iran’s navy later added a third incident, saying another tanker hit a mine and caught fire east of the strait after violating maritime restrictions, and warned vessels that enter the waterway without coordinating with Iranian forces risk the same fate. Tehran has declared the strait closed since July, when it shut the corridor again after US strikes resumed, and the Guards warned fishing boats to keep clear of the wreckage and tanker operators to stay out of what they described as a prohibited mine zone. None of the claims has been independently confirmed: the Guards named neither vessel nor flag, and the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said only that it was aware of a report of an attack on a Gulf tanker, that the crew was safe, and that the damage and environmental impact were still unknown. The strait normally carries a substantial share of the world’s seaborne oil and liquefied natural gas, which is why even an unverified sinking moves markets and insurers. Separately, Iran’s foreign minister is travelling to Pakistan and Oman this week as regional mediation efforts continue.

Latest · Middle East · Naval Build-Up — Third Carrier Arrives16 November 2026

Three US Carriers in One Theatre: The Roosevelt Joins the Iran Blockade as Tehran Says It Is “Still in a State of War”

The United States now has three aircraft carrier strike groups in the Middle East at once. The USS Theodore Roosevelt group entered US Central Command’s area of operations early on Sunday, US officials said, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington groups already operating in the Arabian Sea — part of a deployment of more than 20 Navy vessels enforcing the blockade of Iranian ports imposed in April after nuclear talks collapsed. Three carriers in a single theatre is a rare concentration; analysts note the Navy usually assembles that much air power ahead of major combat operations, though it remains unclear whether the Roosevelt is reinforcing the blockade or rotating in to relieve one of the carriers already on station, and no new large-scale strike campaign has been announced. The timing is pointed: the arrival came hours before President Trump was due to leave Washington for Asia and Thursday’s trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, where Iran is expected to feature. In Tehran, Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Iran was preparing for three scenarios — diplomacy, economic blockade or military aggression — and that the country remained in a state of war. The conflict, which began with US and Israeli strikes on 28 February, is now in its ninth month.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Donbas for NATO?15 November 2026

“What If Ukraine Joins NATO — Would It Withdraw From Donbas?” Washington Floats the Trade Kyiv Has Always Refused

A new question has entered the peace talks, and it cuts to the centre of the war. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told reporters that US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner asked, during the latest round of US-mediated talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi, whether Ukraine would withdraw from the parts of Donbas it still holds if it were admitted to NATO — the first time, he said, that territorial withdrawal and alliance membership had been linked in the negotiations. Zelenskyy’s answer was a refusal with a narrow door left open: Kyiv will not voluntarily surrender territory, and neutrality is off the table because it would require changing the constitution — but if a comprehensive package were confirmed, with credible security guarantees, a strong and well-funded Ukrainian army and reconstruction money, he would put the question to a national referendum. The obstacles are formidable on every side. Moscow has called Ukrainian NATO membership a red line since before the 2022 invasion and demands Donbas as a condition of peace; NATO admission needs the unanimous consent of all 32 members; and a referendum cannot legally be held while martial law, extended for another 90 days from 31 October, remains in force. The Abu Dhabi round ended without a breakthrough, though the sides agreed in principle to a prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people. Zelenskyy also accused SpaceX of misrepresenting Ukraine’s access to its Starshield military satellite system, whose speed limits he blames for interceptor drones losing contact with Russian attack drones.

“Due Process, Not Mob Justice”: Musk and Silicon Valley’s Elite Rally for Jensen Huang as Nvidia Reports Earnings Without Him

Nvidia reports its quarterly earnings on Tuesday in circumstances nobody imagined a month ago: without Jensen Huang, the co-founder who ran the chipmaker for 33 years until his resignation last Thursday. Huang stepped down as chief executive, president and board member after admitting an extramarital affair and after reporting linked him to a Singapore-based executive whom US intelligence suspects of working for Chinese intelligence; a second reported relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive who reported directly to him, preceded the board’s final loss of confidence. Huang, who still holds about 3.6 percent of the company, faces a possible congressional subpoena over the intelligence allegations. Tuesday’s numbers will be read as a verdict on whether the AI boom’s central company can steady itself.

Latest · Europe · Migration — The Rwanda Return Hub16 November 2026

Five EU Countries, One Hub in Rwanda: Europe’s First Deportation Centre Outside the Bloc Is Unveiled in Vienna

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands are set to announce on Monday, at a migration conference in Vienna, a deal to establish a migrant return hub in Rwanda — the first deportation facility located outside the European Union. The five have negotiated with potential hosts for months and kept the location under wraps; Rwanda confirmed in August that it had begun preliminary discussions with several European states and has sent a delegation to Vienna for the announcement. Diplomatic sources describe a pilot scheme: roughly 100 places at first, with each country sending around 20 people, and a target of opening in 2027. The legal basis is the EU’s new Returns Regulation, which speeds up deportations and expressly allows member states to run return hubs in third countries. The design differs in one crucial respect from Britain’s abandoned Rwanda scheme, which the UK Supreme Court ruled unlawful in 2023: the European hub would take only people who have exhausted every legal avenue to remain in the EU, not asylum seekers whose claims are still being processed. Rights groups have raised concerns about the model, which is the flagship of a broader European push, running since 2024, to raise deportation numbers and curb irregular migration.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — Pullback at Bab el-Mandeb15 November 2026

The Red Sea’s Southern Gate Is Changing Hands Again: Government Forces Abandon Bab el-Mandeb to Defend Aden

Yemen’s internationally recognised government has given up the coastline it spent six weeks fighting to hold. Its Southern Armed Forces announced a withdrawal from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area — the strait where the Red Sea meets the Gulf of Aden — to concentrate on defending Aden, the government’s temporary capital, against an advance by the Iran-aligned Houthis, who seized the Red Sea coast there in September. The pullback ends a six-week counteroffensive and hands the Houthis a stronger position on one of the world’s most sensitive shipping chokepoints, through which traffic between Europe and Asia must pass; shipping already faces prolonged detours around Africa. The weekend also brought the air war back to Saudi Arabia: the Saudi-led coalition reported intercepting Houthi drones over the Mecca region, and Riyadh said it had intercepted ballistic missiles aimed at the capital — the same days that saw Iran strike at Bahrain and tankers burn in Hormuz. Taken together, the three chokepoints of Gulf energy — Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb and the Red Sea — are all under simultaneous pressure for the first time in this war. Brent crude resumed trading after the weekend at about $96 a barrel, and jet fuel prices, though down from last month’s peak near $180, remain more than $60 above their pre-war level.

No Inspectors at the Damaged Sites Until the War Is Over: Iran Draws a Line for the UN’s Nuclear Watchdog

Iran will not allow UN nuclear inspectors into its damaged facilities until the war has ended and Washington meets its conditions, a senior Iranian official said on Monday — closing the door, for now, on international verification of what remains of the programme that US and Israeli strikes have targeted since February. The Department of Homeland Security, meanwhile, says the US is fully prepared to counter Iranian assassination plots: Secretary Markwayne Mullin has said intelligence suggests Tehran could target Trump and cabinet officials with explosives-laden drones. Iran is expected to be a central topic when Trump meets Xi and Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, even as Tehran insists the strait stays shut and warns that any further American aggression will draw a harsher response.

Latest · Technology · Privacy — Italy’s Record Fine for Meta14 November 2026

€805 Million for Looking at Your Camera Roll: Italy Hands Meta Its Regulator’s Largest-Ever Penalty

Italy’s data protection authority, the Garante, has fined Meta €805 million — about $880 million — the largest penalty it has ever imposed on a technology company, over a Facebook feature most users never knew was running. Since 2023, the Facebook app on Android has offered “Get suggestions from your gallery while browsing Facebook,” surfacing photos and videos from the phone’s own gallery as ideas for posts. The regulator found that through the feature Meta accessed Italian users’ photographs, videos and related metadata without obtaining their specific consent and without properly informing them, in breach of the Italian law implementing the EU’s ePrivacy directive. Around 20 million Android users in Italy may have been affected; the authority said the fine — roughly 0.4 percent of Meta’s $201 billion global turnover in 2025 — reflected the number of people affected, the duration of the breach, which it said is still ongoing for some users, and the company’s lack of cooperation. Meta has 60 days to introduce a separate, specific consent request, make clear that the feature reads the phone’s gallery and how to switch it off, and set it to disabled by default. The company says it will appeal.

Latest · Technology · Microsoft — The 30-Hour Outage Bill16 November 2026

Thirty Hours Dark, Fifty Percent Back — If You File: Microsoft 365 Customers Face a 31 December Deadline

The outage is over; the paperwork is not. Microsoft’s incident record shows its Microsoft 365 failure ran from 10:05 GMT on 12 November to 16:10 GMT on 13 November — about 30 hours in which Exchange Online failed first and then dragged down every service that depends on it, including Teams calendars, SharePoint and Copilot, across more than 70 countries, with some 130,000 outage reports in the United States alone. Microsoft says the cause was a latent fault in systems handling Exchange connections, exposed when network-maintenance traffic shifts followed an earlier capacity upgrade; it extended the email timeout window to 48 hours to protect delayed messages and has promised a post-incident review, which had not been published as of the weekend. For business customers, the practical question is money. The service-level agreement promises 99.9 percent monthly uptime — about 43 minutes of permitted downtime — and a full 30-hour loss for every user in a tenant works out to roughly 95.8 percent uptime for November, which falls in the 50 percent credit tier, short of the 100 percent band that needs 36 hours. But credits are never automatic: each organisation must file its own claim, citing incident references EX1550543 and MO1550821, and Microsoft must receive it by 31 December 2026. Personal and family subscribers have no uptime promise to claim against at all.

Latest · Americas · Aviation — The Bill for the Meltdown16 November 2026

Flights Back, Questions Open: Washington Asks for Billions to Rebuild Air Traffic Control as Democrats Ask Where the Last Money Went

America’s skies were returning to normal on Monday after the worst disruption since 9/11 — more than 17,000 cancellations across three days, triggered when a newly installed router, fitted near El Dorado, Arkansas during a copper-to-fibre upgrade by contractor L3Harris, flooded the air-traffic network and destabilised 17 of the FAA’s 22 en-route centres. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy had predicted a return to normal schedules by Monday, and airlines spent the weekend repositioning aircraft and crews ahead of the Thanksgiving travel rush. The argument has now moved from the failure to the funding. The Trump administration is asking Congress for billions of dollars to overhaul the FAA’s ageing systems, while Democrats are questioning how earlier modernisation money was spent — a fight with a hard deadline underneath it: roughly 30 percent of the system’s copper wiring still awaits replacement, and full conversion to fibre is not targeted until September 2027. The suspect router remains under test at an L3Harris facility with FAA and FBI personnel present; investigators have found no evidence of a cyberattack, and whether the unit was faulty or wrongly installed is still unresolved. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford has apologised for Friday’s four-hour-plus nationwide ground stop, the longest since the September 11 attacks.
