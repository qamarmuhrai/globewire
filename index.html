Latest · Europe · France20 October 2026

France Extends Night Curfews After a Weekend of Riots, Looting and Thousands of Arrests

French authorities are extending targeted night curfews after a calm first night under the restrictions, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said on Tuesday, following a weekend of cost-of-living protests that tipped into some of the country’s worst street violence in years. Partial curfews were imposed on Monday in parts of Paris — around Place de la République and Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., with gatherings banned from 8 p.m. — and in Lyon, Nice, Lille and other towns, some applying only to minors. Officials say about 2,500 people are in custody, 149 have been jailed awaiting trial, and more than 3,000 shops were damaged. President Emmanuel Macron rejected Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez’s offer to resign and asked him to stay on to maintain the chain of command. New protests are expected on Saturday, and unions have called strikes for 5 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · Ukraine19 October 2026

Trump Says Someone Other Than Zelenskyy Should Negotiate an End to the Ukraine War

US President Donald Trump said on Monday that it may be time for someone other than Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to negotiate on Kyiv’s behalf to end the war with Russia. Speaking to reporters at the White House before a campaign trip to Kansas, Trump said Zelenskyy “should have had this war settled a long time ago” and argued Ukraine was losing its male population. He also described Russia as having been “fine” on Ukraine and as “not doing much,” remarks that came despite continued Russian attacks. Trump added that the United States continues to share intelligence with Ukraine and that he would consider letting Ukrainians stay in the US under temporary protected status until the war ends. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are meanwhile joining talks in Oslo on Tuesday with Zelenskyy and the leaders of Norway and Finland.

US Defence Secretary Hegseth Meets Netanyahu in an Unannounced Visit to Israel

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth met Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu during an unannounced visit to Israel on Monday, Axios reported, citing two sources familiar with the meeting. Britain Warns Firms That Bidding to Build the E1 Settlement Could Bring Sanctions

British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has warned companies and individuals not to bid for construction tenders linked to the E1 settlement project in the occupied West Bank, saying new UK powers allow sanctions against those involved in building illegal settlements. “We urge businesses and individuals not to proceed with bids for E1 construction tenders,” Miliband said as the bidding deadline fell on Monday, warning of legal and reputational consequences, including involvement in serious breaches of international law. London says E1 — thousands of homes between Jerusalem and Ma’ale Adumim — would cut through Palestinian territory, separate the West Bank from East Jerusalem and undermine a two-state solution. The government also says it will legislate to ban imports of goods from illegal settlements and act against services that finance or facilitate them, and it condemned escalating settler violence, including recent attacks on European officials and a US journalist.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan20 October 2026

Japan Launches the World’s First Mission to Bring Back Samples From a Moon of Mars

Japan has launched its Martian Moons eXploration spacecraft, MMX, on an H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center at 4:41 a.m. local time on Tuesday, starting the world’s first mission to collect material from a Martian moon and return it to Earth. The probe separated successfully and entered its planned orbit, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency said. MMX is due to reach Mars in August 2027, study Phobos — the larger of Mars’s two moons — from orbit, deploy a small Franco-German rover, and land to collect at least 10 grams of material from below the surface. The sample is planned to return to Earth in 2031, landing in Australia’s Woomera area. Scientists hope it will show whether Phobos and Deimos are captured asteroids or debris from a giant impact on Mars, and shed light on water and organics in the early Solar System.

Latest · Africa · Mali20 October 2026

More Than 40 Civilians Killed as Gunmen on Motorcycles Storm Camps in North-Eastern Mali

More than 40 civilians were killed over the weekend when armed men arriving on motorcycles attacked camps in Mali’s north-eastern Ménaka region, security and community sources told AFP. The raids, around Tinabow village and the Tagadayat area about 50 kilometres from Ménaka town, mainly targeted members of the Daoussahak community; its executive bureau blamed the Islamic State group, put the toll in Tagadayat alone at 48, condemned the killings as a massacre and appealed for emergency aid for people displaced to Ménaka. A security source in Gao linked the attacks to retaliation for recent military operations and for attacks by the Movement for the Salvation of Azawad, an armed group drawn from the Daoussahak community and aligned with Bamako’s junta. Witnesses described attackers shooting indiscriminately and setting homes on fire. Mali has faced a deep security crisis since 2012 involving jihadist groups, separatists and criminal gangs.

Latest · Africa · South Africa19 October 2026

Ramaphosa Tells South Africa to Prepare for Possible Loss of US Duty-Free Trade Access

South African President Cyril Ramaphosa said on Monday the country must prepare for possible exclusion from AGOA, the US programme giving eligible African nations duty-free access to the American market for thousands of products, as relations with Washington continue to sour. AGOA has been renewed by Congress until the end of 2028, but Ramaphosa noted the pact still needs President Donald Trump’s signature and that some US officials have suggested South Africa — the scheme’s largest beneficiary — should be removed. “I need, as president, to prepare us if it ever happens,” he said, warning the impact on jobs would be “devastating.” He said he and Deputy President Paul Mashatile have been travelling to explore alternative markets, citing a recent deal under which China introduced zero tariffs for qualifying South African goods in May.

Lavrov in Tehran as Iran and Russia Talk Up Their Closest-Ever Strategic Partnership

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov held talks in Tehran on Monday, meeting Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in his first visit since the outbreak of the current conflict. Latest · Europe · Football19 October 2026

Ronaldo Given a 30-Day Portugal Ban and a €255 Fine After Walking Out of Camp

Cristiano Ronaldo has been suspended for 30 days and fined €255 ($286) by the Portuguese Football Federation for leaving the national team camp without permission after a dispute with head coach Jorge Jesus over his playing time. The federation’s disciplinary council found the 41-year-old captain deliberately committed an offence under its regulations when he left Portugal’s base in Copenhagen on 30 September, a day before the Nations League match against Denmark, having been an unused substitute against Norway. Ronaldo later apologised, admitted the facts and said he would accept punishment; the federation said his remorse and clean record helped keep the sanction at the minimum range. Because the ban runs from 1 October, he has already served 19 days and should be available for Portugal’s next matches in November. Federation president Pedro Proença said everyone at the FPF wants Ronaldo back once the matter is resolved.
