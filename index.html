Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya, Türkiye — First No-Deal COP Since 200923 November 2026

COP31 Ends Without a Deal as Blame Falls on Oil States and Pacific Nations Voice Despair

The United Nations climate summit in Antalya has closed without the overarching political agreement that hosts Türkiye and Australia had pushed for, after nearly two days of overtime talks. Negotiators said the European Union, small island states and other climate-vulnerable countries refused to sign a final text that made no mention of fossil fuels, while Saudi Arabia and other oil producers resisted any language on moving away from oil, gas and coal and pressed instead for carbon capture. Ireland’s climate minister, speaking for the EU, said the meeting had been hijacked by oil states, and several delegations complained that Saudi officials had unusually close access to the presidency while others struggled to get meetings. It was the first COP since Copenhagen in 2009 to finish with no cover decision, and it came just after the UN’s environment body confirmed the world is on course to pass the 1.5-degree threshold scientists link to sharply worse extreme weather. Pacific island nations, facing rising seas, expressed deep disappointment, and in Australia opposition politicians called for the resignation of Energy Minister Chris Bowen, who presided over the negotiations. China’s delegation also voiced disappointment at the lack of consensus, while India said it had engaged constructively throughout. Some diplomats argued that refusing a weak text was better than accepting one, and pointed to smaller forums on fossil-fuel transition, methane and clean grids as places where work will continue.

Latest · Europe · Dymer, Kyiv Region — Toll Climbs Through Monday23 November 2026

Russian Strikes Kill at Least 15 Around Kyiv as Rescuers Pull Bodies From a Collapsed Block of Flats

Rescue teams in Ukraine spent Monday searching the rubble of a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, about 45 kilometres north of Kyiv, after an overnight Russian strike partly collapsed the block and killed at least 11 people there. Regional governor Tymur Tkachenko said the toll in Dymer rose through the day as more bodies were recovered, and the emergency service said the dead included a teenager and a woman in her forties. A child was pulled out alive on Sunday evening, while searchers had earlier been looking for 11 people, including two children, who could not be reached. Elsewhere in the region, a strike on a supermarket in Vyshneve killed three people and wounded 15, including three children, according to the town’s mayor, and a strike on a private home in Bila Tserkva killed one more person and set the building ablaze, bringing the regional toll to at least 15. Russia also hit a power substation in the Dnipropetrovsk region. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy used the attacks to press Washington again to release Patriot interceptor missiles, saying Russia had launched roughly 2,700 attack drones, nearly 1,460 guided bombs and 165 missiles across 20 regions in the past week alone. Moscow has stepped up nightly aerial barrages as Ukraine heads into another winter, in the fifth year of the full-scale invasion.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cikande, Banten — About 30 People Still Missing23 November 2026

Indonesia Confirms 24 Dead in Candy Factory Fire as DNA Identification Begins and the Search Goes On

Indonesian authorities confirmed on Monday that 24 people died in a fire that tore through a confectionery factory near Jakarta at the weekend, while rescuers continued to pick through an unstable, still-smouldering structure for about 30 workers who remain unaccounted for. The blaze broke out on Saturday evening in one building of the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in Cikande, in Banten province, about an hour’s drive from the capital. The plant belongs to United Family Food, and its chief executive said roughly 300 people were on site for the Saturday shift. Presidential labour adviser Said Iqbal told reporters that safety compliance at the plant is now being assessed and that he intends to summon the company’s leadership; early indications point to a short circuit in an air-conditioner as a possible cause, though the investigation is continuing. Banten’s search and rescue chief said 29 people were confirmed safe, five of them in hospital, and that crews had removed 24 body bags. Police said the fire was so intense that visual identification is impossible, and that DNA samples from relatives will be needed — a process that could take two to three weeks. The company said it has opened a hotline for families seeking news of missing relatives.

Latest · Americas · Washington — 258th Seat Won in North Carolina22 November 2026

Democrats Reach 258 House Seats, Their Largest Majority Since 1992, With Four Races Still Uncalled

Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the US House of Representatives, the largest majority either party has held in the chamber in more than three decades. The milestone came on Sunday when Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx in North Carolina’s 5th district after a recount, unseating a congresswoman who had served 11 terms. The result caps a sweeping midterm performance on 3 November in which Democrats also took control of the Senate, and it leaves Republicans with at least 173 seats. Four House contests are still outstanding — one each in Minnesota and Tennessee and two in Louisiana that will go to a December run-off — so the final margin could grow further. By the Associated Press count, Democrats have gained more than 40 seats compared with the outgoing Congress, surpassing the 40-seat pickup they achieved in President Donald Trump’s first midterm in 2018. The new Congress, the 120th, is due to be sworn in on 3 January, setting up divided government with a Republican president facing Democratic majorities in both chambers and immediate pressure on the incoming majority to deliver on the affordability agenda it campaigned on.

Latest · Americas · Middletown, Iowa — Blast Now Set for Wednesday23 November 2026

US Army Shifts Iowa Lead Azide Detonation to Wednesday as Court Fight Over Emergency Powers Continues

The US Army now plans to carry out a controlled detonation of degraded explosive material at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant on Wednesday, after moving the date several times amid public alarm and a legal challenge. At issue are 68 barrels holding roughly 9,700 pounds of lead azide — a compound used to trigger munitions — that technicians found deteriorating in storage bunkers in March after more than two decades in place. The Army says the material has become extremely sensitive to friction and movement, and that falling temperatures raise the risk of an uncontrolled explosion, which is why it argues the stockpile must be destroyed in place. A federal appeals court paused the Environmental Protection Agency’s emergency permit last week after the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club’s Iowa chapter sued, arguing the public had too little notice and that safer alternatives were not properly considered. In a filing late Saturday, the Justice Department said the Army can proceed under a separate emergency exception in environmental law even without the permit. The Army said Sunday evening the extra time would allow for the best weather and for all mitigation measures to be in place, including wetting the ground above the bunkers to suppress lead-bearing dust. Iowa’s governor has activated the state emergency operations centre, Illinois officials are warning residents downwind, and flights around Middletown are restricted through Wednesday.

Latest · Middle East · Herzliya — Deadline of 6 December Approaches22 November 2026

Netanyahu and Eisenkot Parties End a Second Round of Unity Talks Without a Deal

A second round of coalition talks between caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud and Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party has ended without agreement, leaving Israel still without a path to a new government weeks after an inconclusive election. Delegations met for four hours at a hotel in Herzliya on Sunday and said afterwards only that they had set out their positions in detail and would meet again, without naming a date. Reporting in Israel pointed to two sticking points: who would serve as prime minister, and whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would sit in the next government. Likud is said to favour a rotation of the premiership with Netanyahu going first and to want Ben-Gvir included; Yashar insists Eisenkot should lead for a full term and that Ben-Gvir be excluded, while Avigdor Lieberman has ruled out joining any government that includes Netanyahu at all. Eisenkot, a former army chief, holds the mandate to form a government until 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension. His bloc controls 48 of the Knesset’s 120 seats, well short of the 61 needed, and is itself divided over whether to seek support from ultra-Orthodox parties, Arab parties, or Likud. If unity talks fail, Yashar says it will put a minority government to a confidence vote before the deadline expires.

Latest · Africa · Khartoum — UN Building Damaged With Staff Inside22 November 2026

Drone Strikes Kill Four Around Khartoum and Damage a UN Building as Saudi Arabia and Egypt Condemn the Attacks

Drone attacks blamed on Sudan’s paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed four people in and around Khartoum early on Sunday and damaged a United Nations building while staff were inside it, according to local reports and UN officials. Explosions were heard across the capital from about 2 a.m. to 4:30 a.m. as army air defences tried to intercept drones said to be aimed at government buildings and the airport. Witnesses said one strike completely destroyed a house, and another hit a home in Jebel Aulia, about 45 kilometres south of the capital. The UN humanitarian coordinator in Sudan, Denise Brown, confirmed the damage to UN premises in a civilian neighbourhood, and the UN humanitarian office called it unacceptable for aid workers to be exposed to such danger. A Sudanese army source claimed the drones were launched from Bahir Dar airport in neighbouring Ethiopia, an allegation that could not be independently verified and that fits a pattern of accusations Addis Ababa has previously denied. On Monday Saudi Arabia and Egypt both condemned the strikes and called for civilians and international personnel to be protected. The UN has repeatedly criticised the widespread use of drones by both sides in Sudan’s war, which it says killed more than 1,100 people in the first half of 2026 alone.

Airstrikes and a Houthi Drone Attack Kill Five Across Yemen as Fighting With the Saudi-Led Coalition Intensifies

At least five people were killed and 18 injured across Yemen on Sunday in a fresh exchange between the Saudi-led coalition and the Houthis, according to Houthi-run media and a security official. An airstrike on an oil company facility in the Houthi-held capital, Sanaa, wounded six people and caused material damage, Houthi broadcaster al-Masirah reported, while strikes on homes in the Sabir al-Mawadim and Jabal Habashi districts of Taiz province killed two people and injured five. The coalition, for its part, said its air defences intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone launched toward the Riyadh region, the latest in a weekend of attempted attacks on Saudi Arabia. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea claimed 71 coalition strikes across eight provinces in the preceding 24 hours, and al-Masirah also reported a series of strikes on the Red Sea port of Hodeidah on Saturday evening, where residents described more than five powerful explosions. The exchanges mark a further unravelling of the calm that followed the 2022 truce, as government forces backed by the coalition and the Iran-aligned Houthis fight on multiple fronts.

Latest · Middle East · Tehran — Up to Two Hours of Cuts a Day22 November 2026

Iran Begins Rolling Power Cuts as War Damage to Gas Plants Deepens Its Winter Energy Crunch

Iran has begun scheduled electricity cuts as damage from the war with the United States and Israel compounds a fuel shortage that predates the fighting. Mohammad Allahdad, head of the national electricity company Tavanir, told state television that, given fuel supply conditions, distributors would be forced to impose restrictions from Sunday. Households and businesses should expect outages of around two hours every two days, rising to as much as two hours a day in some areas if temperatures fall further, with customers notified in advance. Allahdad said war damage knocked out about 230 million cubic metres a day of gas production capacity; roughly 100 million of that has since been restored, but output remains below last year’s level, leaving power plants short of fuel heading into winter. President Masoud Pezeshkian warned on Saturday that strikes on the country’s gas infrastructure — including processing facilities linked to the South Pars field — had aggravated the situation, and told Iranians to expect a difficult winter. Iran, despite holding some of the world’s largest gas and oil reserves, has imposed similar two-hour cuts in recent winters, a pattern officials and analysts link to years of underinvestment in the grid and the effects of long-running sanctions.

Latest · Americas · Guasave, Sinaloa — $5 Million FBI Reward21 November 2026

Mexico Captures “El Chapo Isidro,” an FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitive, in a Raid That Sparked Gun Battles in Sinaloa

Mexican special forces have captured Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, the alleged cartel leader known as “El Chapo Isidro,” in an operation in Sinaloa that triggered heavy fighting and sent spectators fleeing from a nearby baseball game. The defence ministry said elite army and National Guard units, acting with US intelligence support, used stealth and surprise to surround a house in Guasave on Friday night, came under fire and explosive attack, and arrested Meza-Flores along with two senior associates; seven other alleged members of the group were also detained, and the prisoners were flown to Mexico City. The ministry said four soldiers were killed in the operation and seven attackers died, while cartel members set up flaming roadblocks in nearby municipalities that security forces later cleared. Meza-Flores, 44, leads the Beltrán Leyva organisation, allied with the Los Mayos faction of the Sinaloa Cartel, and had been on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list since February 2025, with a $5 million US reward offered for information leading to his arrest. He faces organised-crime and drug-trafficking charges in Mexico and an extradition order to the United States, where prosecutors allege his network moved large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana across the border over two decades. Despite the shared nickname, he is not related to Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán.
