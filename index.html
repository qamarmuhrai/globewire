Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Rival Claims28 November 2026

US Military Publicly Rejects Iran’s Claims Over Tankers, Carrier Strike and Hormuz Tolls

US Central Command has issued a point-by-point rebuttal of claims made by Iran’s navy chief, Rear Admiral Shahram Irani, calling them false and fabricated in a statement posted on X. Washington denied that American forces seized two Iranian oil tankers which Tehran says its crews later retook while US personnel slept, insisting Iran no longer has a functioning navy and that the episode never happened. It also rejected the claim that Iranian forces struck the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln on the opening day of the war, saying the carrier group was never hit, completed its deployment and returned safely to the United States last month. A third claim — that ships are charged $2.5 million to pass through the Strait of Hormuz — was likewise dismissed: CENTCOM said there is no toll and that US and partner forces have kept the waterway open to free navigation. Tehran, for its part, says its navy continues to monitor waters from the eastern strait to the northern Indian Ocean, and its Revolutionary Guards reported two tankers hitting mines on unauthorised routes near Hormuz.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan — Local Elections28 November 2026

Taiwan Votes for Mayors and County Chiefs in Local Elections Watched Closely in Beijing

Voters across Taiwan went to the polls on Saturday to choose mayors, county chiefs and thousands of local officials in all 22 cities and counties, a contest seen as an early gauge of party strength ahead of the presidential election due in early 2028. Campaigning centred on everyday issues such as transport and the environment rather than relations with China, but Beijing has followed the vote closely. China has denounced Puma Shen, the governing Democratic Progressive Party’s candidate for mayor of Taipei, who is wanted by Chinese police over alleged separatism; Shen says he was never contacted and that whoever wins, the result is a victory for democracy under pressure. Taipei’s defence ministry said it detected 30 Chinese military aircraft around the island on Friday, 26 of which crossed the median line of the Taiwan Strait in a joint combat-readiness patrol with warships. The opposition Kuomintang, which favours closer ties with Beijing, is defending the 14 cities and counties it won in 2022 against five held by the DPP. Polls closed at 4 p.m. local time, with results expected in the evening.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — Hung Parliament27 November 2026

Final Results Leave New Zealand With a Hung Parliament and Luxon One Seat Short

New Zealand’s final election results, released on Friday, have produced a hung parliament in which neither the governing right bloc nor the opposition can command a majority on its own. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s National Party and its partners ACT and New Zealand First together hold 65 of 130 seats — one short of the 66 needed — after the counting of nearly 485,000 special votes cost National two seats compared with election night. Labour, which won the largest share of the party vote at about 30 percent, holds 36 seats but has no path to power even with the Greens, Te Pāti Māori and the centrist Opportunity Party combined. That leaves Opportunity, a newcomer led by Qiulae Wong with seven seats, holding the balance: Luxon said formal negotiations would begin immediately, and Wong indicated she would talk first with National as the largest party in parliament. Turnout was 77.2 percent. If no deal can be struck, the country faces the prospect of a fresh election, and the outgoing government remains in caretaker mode in the meantime.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Coalition Deadlock27 November 2026

Eisenkot Vows to Form Israeli Government in Eight Days as Netanyahu Is Said to Weigh Rotation Offer

Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief tasked with forming Israel’s next government, said on Friday that he intends to swear in a new administration within the eight days left on his mandate, accusing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud of dragging out unity talks without genuine intent to reach a deal. Israeli media reported that Netanyahu is preparing to signal he would serve second in a rotating premiership, letting Eisenkot take the top job first — an offer said to divide officials in the prime minister’s own office and to have been passed through unofficial channels. Eisenkot is reported to be deeply sceptical, and his ally Avigdor Liberman warned publicly that any such proposal would be a ploy to run down the clock and force another election. Eisenkot has pledged to partners including Liberman and Democrats leader Yair Golan that he will not enter a unity government with Likud without their consent. His bloc currently holds 48 seats, well short of the 61 needed, so he is also courting the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism and the Arab party Ra’am before his mandate expires on 6 December.

Latest · Europe · France — Recession27 November 2026

France Slips Into Technical Recession as Revised Figures Show a Third Straight Quarterly Fall

France has entered a technical recession after revised official data showed its economy shrinking for consecutive quarters, the national statistics office INSEE confirmed on Friday. Output fell 0.2 percent in the third quarter, double the initial estimate of 0.1 percent, while the second quarter — first reported as flat — was revised to a slight decline in unrounded terms, meaning GDP has now fallen every quarter since the end of last year. Marie Leclair, head of INSEE’s national accounts department, acknowledged plainly that the figures amount to a recession. The weakness was driven by falling investment, down 0.9 percent in the quarter, a sharp drop in exports linked to aeronautics and farm output, and only a modest 0.3 percent rise in household spending. The public deficit widened to 5.6 percent of GDP, above the government’s 5.4 percent target, as tax and social-security receipts slowed while welfare and debt-interest costs rose. With growth carried over at just 0.1 percent, hitting the official 0.5 percent target for 2026 would require an unlikely 1.4 percent jump in the final quarter. One consolation: inflation eased to 2.9 percent in November, from 3.4 percent in October.

Latest · Americas · Technology — OpenAI Countersues Apple27 November 2026

OpenAI Countersues Apple, Accusing It of Stealing Trade Secrets and Pressuring Suppliers

OpenAI has countersued Apple in federal court in California, accusing the iPhone maker of stealing its trade secrets and trying to block its push into consumer hardware. The filing, made on Friday, alleges that Apple regained confidential information by rehiring a former employee who had spent six months at OpenAI and, according to OpenAI, sent more than a hundred highly confidential files about an unreleased device to personal accounts before presenting the plans to senior Apple staff. OpenAI further claims Apple has pressed shared suppliers to reveal their work for the ChatGPT maker and has used employment terms designed to trap staff and stop them joining rivals, describing the approach as an attempt to compete unfairly by any means available. The countersuit answers a case Apple filed in July against OpenAI and two former Apple employees, alleging they misappropriated its secrets after OpenAI acquired io, the design studio led by former Apple design chief Jony Ive. Apple dismissed the counterclaims as a distraction, noting a court has already ordered OpenAI to stop using Apple’s confidential information; a trial in the original case is scheduled for July 2027.

Latest · Americas · United States — Thanksgiving Storm27 November 2026

Cleanup Continues After Thanksgiving Storm Left Nearly a Million US Customers Without Power

Utility crews worked through Friday to restore electricity after a powerful storm system battered the US East Coast and Midwest on Thanksgiving Day, cutting power to more than 900,000 homes and businesses at its peak. Winds gusting between 50 and 70 mph toppled trees and power lines, with the heaviest outages in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Maryland, Virginia and Missouri, and coastal flooding reported from Virginia and Delaware Bay to southern New Jersey and New England. Air travel was heavily disrupted during one of the busiest weeks of the year: more than 2,000 US flights were delayed and over 400 cancelled, with Washington Dulles and New York’s JFK among the worst-affected airports. In New York City, the storm forced the first postponement in the history of Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, over fears that gusts funnelling between Manhattan’s towers would make its giant balloons unsafe; the parade was rescheduled for Saturday morning. New York’s governor had declared a state of emergency for the city and surrounding counties ahead of the storm.

Latest · Global Affairs · Hunger — UN Warning27 November 2026

UN Agencies Warn Hunger Will Worsen in 16 Hotspots, With Famine Risk in Sudan and South Sudan

Acute hunger is expected to deepen across 16 hotspots worldwide between December 2026 and May 2027, the UN Food and Agriculture Organization and the World Food Programme warned in their latest joint Hunger Hotspots report. Sudan, South Sudan, Somalia, parts of Nigeria and Yemen are rated at the highest level of concern, with a risk of famine identified in 13 areas of Sudan’s North Darfur and South Kordofan through January and in parts of South Sudan. Afghanistan, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Haiti and Palestine are classed as very high concern. The list has grown from 13 hotspots in June: an intensifying El Niño has added Southern Africa, Central America’s Dry Corridor — where the first crop season was the driest in four decades, with losses above 75 percent in many areas — and northeastern Uganda, while Chad joins because of spillover from Sudan’s war. Conflict remains the main driver in 12 of the 16 hotspots, yet funding for food and nutrition aid in the worst-hit places has fallen by about 62 percent since 2022, from $5.1 billion to $2.0 billion. The agencies are seeking early action to protect some 9 million people in 22 countries.

Latest · Europe · Serbia — Presidential Candidate27 November 2026

Serbia’s Student-Led Movement Names Chemistry Professor as Its Presidential Candidate

Serbia’s student-backed movement, which swept last month’s parliamentary election on an anti-corruption platform, has named Radomir Saicic, a 65-year-old chemistry professor at the University of Belgrade, as its candidate for the presidential election on 27 December. Saicic, a member of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts, was among the first academics to join the student protest movement that began in 2024, and he was the figure who announced the Student List’s victory on election night, sparking celebrations in Belgrade and other cities. The list took 140 of the 250 seats in parliament. The December vote follows Aleksandar Vucic’s decision to cut short his second presidential term and run for prime minister after 14 years dominating Serbian politics — a gamble that ended in defeat for his Serbian Progressive Party. Vucic has repeatedly suggested his party might boycott the presidential ballot, with a final decision expected in mid-December. Saicic said it was time to restore dignity to the presidency.

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye Deepen Defence Cooperation at Islamabad Meeting

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye have pledged to strengthen defence cooperation and collective security under their Mecca Alliance, condemning continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia at a meeting of the pact’s Political and Defence Strategy Committee in Islamabad. Pakistan’s Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif received Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman and Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan on Saturday as the talks got under way, a day after the three countries denounced the attacks from Yemen and vowed to speed up support for the kingdom. The gathering comes amid the wider regional war: US forces are enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian ports, Tehran claims its navy still escorts shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, and Kuwait has formally protested to Iran over the drone strike that killed two Italian technicians this week — an attack Italy says was carried out with an Iranian-made drone, which Tehran denies. The alliance partners said closer coordination was needed as maritime and air threats across the Gulf continue to escalate.
