Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — Not a New Yalta, Says Moscow17 November 2026

“Games of Political Scientists”: The Kremlin Rejects the Yalta Label as APEC Week Opens in Shenzhen

APEC week opened in Shenzhen on Tuesday with the CEO Summit under way and the Economic Leaders’ Meeting to follow on Wednesday and Thursday — and with Moscow already pushing back against the framing of Thursday’s centrepiece. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed comparisons between the first-ever trilateral meeting of Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin and the 1945 Yalta conference as “games of political scientists” with nothing to do with reality, saying Russia and China both favour a multipolar order and do not intend to decide other nations’ fates. Beijing has said the three leaders will exchange views on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability; Washington says Trump wants nuclear arms reductions at the centre of the talks and intends to raise the Iran war, while Ukraine is not expected on the trilateral agenda — a point of alarm in Kyiv. Trump has also floated a possible encounter with North Korea’s Kim Jong Un while in China. On the sidelines, Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney and Xi agreed to further tariff reductions after talks on Tuesday, an early sign the week will produce bilateral deals as well as summit theatre.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — 317 Buildings Without Heating17 November 2026

Both Bridges Closed, a Metro Stretch Suspended, 317 Buildings Cold: Kyiv Counts the Cost of the Night Attack

Kyiv woke on Tuesday to the full accounting of the overnight Russian attack that hit the North (Pivnichnyi) and South (Pivdennyi) bridges over the Dnipro. City authorities said traffic across both crossings has been suspended, fires broke out in four districts — Holosiivskyi, Desnianskyi, Dniprovskyi and Obolonskyi — and damage to the heating network left 317 buildings in the Darnytskyi district without heat. Services on the green metro line between Osokorky and Chervonyi Khutir were also suspended, water supplies were disrupted in parts of the capital, and a 34-year-old man was hospitalised. Russia’s defence ministry said it had struck transport, military and energy facilities across several regions; Moscow also claimed to have downed well over a thousand Ukrainian drones overnight, more than 300 of them reportedly headed for the capital region. Ukraine answered again at the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to annexed Crimea, hit for a second consecutive day with traffic suspended, according to Crimea’s Moscow-installed governor. The exchange landed hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that intelligence pointed to major strikes on Monday evening and into Tuesday.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — A Named Tanker, an Empty Strait17 November 2026

The ON PHOENIX Burns off Oman, No Ship Crosses by Morning, and a Drone Raises the Alarm at an Iraqi Gas Field

The Hormuz crisis acquired a name and a silence on Tuesday. Britain’s UKMTO said a tanker struck by an unknown projectile about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, suffered an engine-room fire and was left disabled, with all crew accounted for; maritime monitor Vanguard identified the vessel as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers had struck naval mines on a southern route Tehran calls unauthorised, its regular navy said a third hit a mine east of the strait, and Guards statements have claimed six tankers caught fire since Sunday — claims that remain unverified and unnamed by Tehran. Tracking data underlined the chill: no commercial ship had crossed the strait at all by early Tuesday, against a pre-crisis norm of roughly 85 crossings a day, and the UN’s maritime agency counts 136 security incidents in the region since March. A drone alert at a major gas field in northern Iraq widened the risk picture to energy sites on land. Overhead, the USS Theodore Roosevelt’s arrival gives the US three carrier strike groups in the region, and Iran repeated that UN nuclear inspectors will not return until the war has permanently ended.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Tokyo — First Breach Since 202417 November 2026

Three Minutes, Twice, Before Dawn: Japan Scrambles Jets and Lodges a “Very Stern Protest” with Moscow

Japan scrambled fighter jets in the early hours of Tuesday after two suspected Russian aircraft entered its airspace northwest of Okinoshima, off Fukuoka in the southwest, each for about three minutes shortly after 4 a.m. The Air Self-Defense Force issued radio warnings as the aircraft crossed the Tsushima Strait, passed between Yonaguni island and Taiwan, and continued into the Pacific. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara called the intrusion a grave infringement of sovereignty and “completely unacceptable,” saying Tokyo had lodged a very stern protest through diplomatic channels and demanded steps to prevent a repeat; Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi gave a similar account. It is the first confirmed Russian violation of Japanese airspace since September 2024, off Rebun Island in Hokkaido. South Korea separately reported Russian aircraft entering and leaving its air defence identification zone over the East Sea the same morning — a zone that, unlike sovereign airspace, carries no legal duty to notify. Moscow had not publicly responded by Tuesday afternoon.

Latest · Europe · Vienna — The Rwanda Return Hub17 November 2026

Five EU Countries Unveil the Bloc’s First Offshore Return Hub — in Rwanda — Over Rights Groups’ Objections

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands unveiled a deal in Vienna on Monday to establish a migrant return hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers — the first such facility outside the European Union. The five stepped up the plan after the EU toughened its migration rules, clearing the way for offshore returns, and the centre is expected to begin operating from next year. The announcement is the culmination of a deportation push launched in 2024 as public opinion on migration soured across the bloc, even though arrivals have fallen steadily for three years as the EU struck deals to stem departures from Africa and the Middle East. Human Rights Watch condemned the arrangement over fears of repression and abuse of detainees, the UN refugee agency UNHCR has warned against it, and France’s President Emmanuel Macron has expressed doubts. Supporters argue a credible prospect of removal is needed to make asylum systems work; critics answer that the policy exports both people and legal responsibility.

Latest · Technology · Cybersecurity — 178 Sites, One Snippet17 November 2026

OPTIMJACK: How One Testing Tool Served Malware to the BBC, CNN, Zoom and 175 Other Organisations in a Single Day

None of the websites was breached — the malicious code arrived from a supplier they all trusted. On Monday, an attacker exploited a flaw in the account signup flow of Optimizely Web Experimentation, the A/B-testing platform loaded by thousands of sites, and wrote a loader into the project code of customer accounts. Security monitor Report URI checked 1,611 Optimizely snippets and found 188 of them, belonging to 178 organisations, carrying the payload — among them the BBC, CNN, Zoom, Salesforce, GitLab, HSBC, Equifax, IKEA, Air France-KLM, Ledger and Peacock, plus Optimizely’s own login page. The script, rebuilt 26 times during the day, swapped cryptocurrency wallet addresses across eight chains, skimmed payment cards typed into on-page forms, stole cookies and screenshots, and routed iPhone and iPad visitors to the Coruna and DarkSword exploit kits. Verified crypto theft was modest — about $7,040 within nine hours — but the reach was not. Optimizely says it has fixed the flaw; by late Monday only one infected snippet remained.

Two Rival Fossil-Fuel Clauses and a $120 Billion Gap: COP31’s Draft Meets Its Ministers

Ministers at the COP31 talks in Antalya began responding on Tuesday to the first draft of a final deal, and the criticism arrived from both directions. The 18-page text, drawn from more than 500 pages of submissions by over 160 countries, carries two rival wordings on fossil fuels — one repeating the 2023 pledge to transition away from them, and a stronger clause calling for accelerated phase-out with an immediate shift in energy systems — either of which could still be dropped before Friday’s deadline. It also proposes tripling adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year by 2035 and raising electricity’s share of final energy demand to 35 percent by 2035, while citing adaptation needs estimated near $305 billion a year. Negotiations president Chris Bowen defended the draft as a test of positions rather than a record of agreement, and declined to commit Australia, a major coal exporter, to phasing out fossil fuel exports. Campaigners said the text names the destination but drops any clear process for getting there. Around 30 mandates remain open with ministers now holding the pen.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Banda Sea Earthquake17 November 2026

Magnitude 6.6 Under the Banda Sea: A Strong Quake Near Tual, a Green Alert, and No Tsunami Threat

A magnitude 6.6 earthquake struck beneath the Banda Sea late on Monday, about 148 kilometres west-southwest of Tual in Indonesia’s Maluku Islands, the US Geological Survey reported. The quake struck at 21:11 UTC — early Tuesday morning local time — at a depth of about 68 kilometres. USGS issued a Green alert, indicating a low likelihood of casualties or economic damage, and estimated roughly 8,100 people were exposed to moderate shaking, with no population estimated at the strongest intensities. Indonesia’s BMKG agency measured the event differently, at magnitude 7.1 and 115 kilometres deep, and said it carried no tsunami potential; weak shaking was reported across Tual, Saumlaki, Dobo, Sorong, Manokwari, Fakfak and other towns in the region. Sri Lanka’s tsunami warning centre likewise confirmed no threat to its coasts. There were no immediate reports of casualties or damage. Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where offshore quakes of this size are frequent; the depth and distance from shore appear to have limited this one’s impact.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola at Six Months15 November 2026

400 to 500 New Cases a Week: Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Is the Widest Ever — and Still Spreading

Six months after it was declared, Congo’s Ebola outbreak is still not under control, the Africa CDC said in a joint stocktake with the WHO and UN partners. More than 11,200 infections and about 5,500 deaths have been recorded since 15 May, making this the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, driven by the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no licensed vaccine or treatment. The virus has spread from three health zones in one province to 67 zones across eight provinces, with cases also traced into neighbouring countries — the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak in Africa. Between 400 and 500 new cases are still reported each week, more than 80 percent of them outside known contact lists, and children account for over a quarter of cases and a third of deaths; seven in ten infected children under five have died. Responders cite deadly attacks on health workers — a recent assault in Cantine-Aloya killed 66 civilians, including three health workers — alongside unpaid staff and shortages. About 3,000 people have recovered. Africa CDC director-general Jean Kaseya warned that if the outbreak is not stopped now, it could become the worst the world has seen.
