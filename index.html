Latest · Africa · Benghazi, Libya — UN Calls for Independent Probe23 November 2026

Russian Corvette Hit at Benghazi Port as the UN Warns Libya Must Not Become a Battleground for the Ukraine War

Libya’s eastern authorities say a Russian naval vessel that had stopped at Benghazi to refuel and take on supplies was hit in a suspected drone-boat attack on Sunday, in what looks like a striking spillover from the war in Ukraine. Satellite imagery identified the ship as the Merkury, a Steregushchiy-class corvette, and pictures from the port showed much of the vessel capsized and partly sunk, with only the bow above water and smoke still rising from the pier. Medical sources said ten Russian and Libyan crew and personnel suffered minor injuries. Later the same day a Maltese-flagged commercial ship was also struck by a drone boat off the Benghazi coast, its crew rescued by Libyan naval forces, and local forces said they intercepted more than a dozen further explosive-laden sea drones that evening. No one has claimed responsibility and neither Kyiv nor Moscow has commented, though Libyan officials in the east have asked for scrutiny of whether the drones came from western Libya and whether foreign specialists were present in the country. The eastern government said a joint inquiry with Russia is under way and insisted Libya is not a party to the Russia-Ukraine conflict and will not let its territory become an arena for settling scores. On Monday the UN mission in Libya called for an immediate, independent and transparent investigation, stressing that the country’s sovereignty and the safety of its ports and citizens must be respected. The incident came a day after another Russian cargo vessel, the Sparta II, was hit by drones in the Baltic and towed out of Finnish waters.

Latest · Americas · Atlanta — More Than 1,100 Flights Delayed at the Hub22 November 2026

Delta Restores Its Weight-and-Balance System After a Day of Nationwide Delays at the Start of Thanksgiving Week

A failure in the software Delta Air Lines uses to calculate an aircraft’s weight and balance before departure disrupted travel across the United States on Sunday, just as the Thanksgiving rush was getting under way. Without the automated load instructions, ground crews had to count bags and complete the safety paperwork by hand, slowing departures to a crawl. The Federal Aviation Administration first ordered a ground stop for Delta flights heading to Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, the carrier’s main hub and the world’s busiest airport, and later replaced it with a ground-delay programme. By late afternoon FlightAware was counting 1,169 delayed flights in and out of Atlanta, including more than 1,000 Delta services, and by evening well over half of Delta’s flights nationwide were late and about 6 per cent had been cancelled. Delta said roughly 70 per cent of the disruption came from the automation fault and the rest from bad weather and air-traffic restrictions in New York and the Northeast. The airline said the system, known as ALIS, was working again by mid-afternoon, but warned that knock-on delays would linger. It apologised, rebooked passengers, and offered meals, hotel rooms and refunds where flights were cancelled. The airport expects more than four million travellers to pass through Atlanta between 19 November and 1 December.

Latest · Africa · Ituri, DR Congo — Staff Sheltering in a Maternity Ward22 November 2026

WHO Chief Pleads for Peace as Fighting Near a Congo Hospital Traps Ebola Responders and Puts Patients at “Grave Risk”

The head of the World Health Organization has appealed for calm in the epicentre of Congo’s Ebola outbreak after heavy fighting erupted beside a hospital in the northeastern province of Ituri. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said on Sunday that clashes had been raging since Saturday close to where the outbreak first emerged, disrupting both the hospital’s work and the wider response, and that WHO staff and partner teams had been sheltering inside the facility’s maternity ward for more than 24 hours. The agency is working with humanitarian partners to negotiate a safe exit and to ensure the hospital, its patients and its workers are protected. Tedros warned that attacks across eastern Congo have become more frequent since late October, seriously hampering surveillance, treatment and other emergency work. The outbreak, now six months old and still classified as a public health emergency of international concern, is the largest and deadliest in the country’s history: WHO figures to 17 November recorded 11,613 cases and 5,678 deaths. While a few areas have seen a slight dip, the agency said on Saturday that sustained, high-level transmission continues overall, with Ituri and North Kivu the hardest-hit provinces. “The best medicine is peace,” Tedros said.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Seoul — Resignation Accepted Within an Hour23 November 2026

South Korea’s Education Minister Quits Over Last-Minute AI-Cheating Rules That Threw the College Entrance Exam Into Confusion

South Korea’s education minister has stepped down over the scramble to stop artificial-intelligence cheating in the country’s high-stakes university entrance test. Choi Kyo-jin offered his resignation on Sunday, saying repeated rule changes in the final days before the College Scholastic Ability Test, known as the Suneung, had inconvenienced candidates and the public and that, as the minister in charge, he had to take responsibility. President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation within about an hour and ordered a comprehensive review of how national exams are managed and of the laws behind them. The turmoil began after the top scorer in a separate tax-accountant examination admitted using a miniature camera, a smartphone and generative AI to cheat. A ministry that had first focused on smart glasses suddenly widened its net: two days before the 19 November test it announced 8,400 extra staff, including device specialists and corridor supervisors, and a plan to seal clothing buttons with stickers to block hidden cameras; on the eve of the exam it changed the rules again so only black pencils could be brought in. Three nationwide phone alerts were also sent during the test to catch candidates who had not handed in their phones. About 550,000 students sat the exam, and the ministry later reported 111 suspected cheating cases involving electronic devices. Choi had already drawn criticism after telling lawmakers he doubted, at his age, that he could spot AI-assisted cheating himself.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pyongyang — Credentials From Ten New Ambassadors23 November 2026

Kim Jong Un Says North Korea Seeks Security, Not Supremacy, as He Personally Receives Ten New Ambassadors

North Korea’s leader has used a rare diplomatic ceremony to insist that his country’s growing arsenal is about self-defence, not expansion. State media reported on Monday that Kim Jong Un personally received the credentials of ten newly appointed ambassadors — from Russia, Sweden, Indonesia, Vietnam, Brazil, the Czech Republic, India, the Philippines, Thailand and Bangladesh — a task normally handled by a senior official rather than the leader himself. Kim described a world in the grip of an unstable security crisis and political and economic chaos, and said the situation around North Korea and in East Asia urgently required the country to secure the power and capability to guarantee its own safety and peace. At the same time he stressed that Pyongyang harbours no ambition to dominate others, saying the DPRK neither pursues supremacy nor expansion and that high-handedness and pressure have no place in its foreign policy. He said the North wants to widen its foreign relations on principles of independence, peace and friendship while defending its sovereignty, but will not accept attempts to restrict what he called the inherent rights of a sovereign state. The appearance came days after Kim rebuffed President Donald Trump’s push for a meeting during the APEC summit in China, and analysts in Seoul read the ambassador ceremony as a signal of a more active, sovereignty-centred diplomacy.

Latest · Middle East · West Bank — Four Villages Attacked Overnight22 November 2026

Eisenkot Brands Overnight Settler Arson in the West Bank “Jewish Terrorism” as Police Open an Investigation

Israeli authorities are investigating a wave of overnight arson attacks on Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank that the man trying to form Israel’s next government has condemned in unusually blunt terms. Residents said extremist settlers moved through villages near Ramallah in the early hours of Sunday, setting vehicles and homes ablaze and spraying racist graffiti, including the word “Revenge” and slogans invoking Judea. Reuters journalists saw burned-out cars in the area; in Kafr Malik an activist said about eight vehicles were torched at around 2 a.m. No injuries were reported. The army said soldiers and police were sent to Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha’al, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani after reports and footage of cars being set on fire, and that it strongly condemns the violence. Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief tasked with forming a government after last month’s inconclusive election, called the raids Jewish terrorism, saying extremist groups that burn vehicles and homes undermine regional stability, endanger lives and gravely damage Israel’s security and foreign relations. He blamed the policies of far-right ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich for bringing the area to the brink of anarchy. Ben-Gvir hit back, accusing Eisenkot of courting Arab parties whose support he may need for a coalition. Arab Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen said the attacks were the direct result of deepening occupation and government policy. Violence in the West Bank has surged since October 2023, and the UN counts 410 settler attacks linked to this year’s olive harvest alone since September.

Trump Says He Is Inclined to Resume Strikes on Iran After the New Year and Warns Any Ship That Tests the Blockade Will Be Sunk

President Donald Trump has raised the prospect of a return to major combat with Iran, telling Fox News on Sunday that he is inclined to resume military strikes after the New Year if indirect negotiations fail to produce an agreement. Trump accused Tehran’s negotiators of stalling — calling them professional tap dancers who talk endlessly without making concessions — and said any deal must permanently bar Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon and remain binding on future administrations. He also dared Iran to challenge the American naval blockade of its ports, warning that vessels attempting to break through would be fired on and sunk. The remarks landed amid a sharp rise in tension around the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint for a large share of the world’s oil. Two tankers were reported hit near Oman’s Khasab in recent days, one by an unknown projectile and another in its engine room, and maritime trackers say dozens of laden Iranian tankers are already positioned beyond the strait. Bahrain said its air defences intercepted Iranian aerial attacks and accused Tehran of striking a civilian industrial facility, causing a fire but no casualties. Iran, for its part, says the strait remains under its control, claims to have downed American drones, and its foreign minister has said it could reopen fully if Washington meets Tehran’s conditions. A framework signed in June to end hostilities has since stalled over how safe navigation through the strait would be guaranteed.

Nine People Shot at a Central California Concert, One Critically, in an Attack Deputies Believe May Be Gang-Related

Nine people were wounded when gunfire broke out at a concert in California’s Central Valley on Saturday evening. The Tulare County Sheriff’s Office said deputies were called at about 7:30 p.m. to a large gathering in Strathmore, a small rural community near the Sierra Nevada foothills, where roughly 150 people had gathered for a concert promoted as a Norteño event. A 26-year-old man shot in the chest underwent surgery and remained in critical condition on Sunday. Two other victims, aged 19 and 22, also required surgery, one for a stomach wound and one for a groin wound, while the remaining six were treated for injuries that did not need an operation. Investigators said early indications point to a possible gang connection, though they gave no further detail, and no arrests or suspects had been announced by Sunday. The sheriff’s office is asking anyone with information to come forward as the inquiry continues.

Attack on a Cattle Camp in South Sudan Kills 24 Civilians, Including Seven Children, Local Officials Say

An overnight raid on a cattle-herding community in northwestern South Sudan has left at least 24 civilians dead, according to local authorities. Machuei Bith, the acting commissioner of Tonj East County in Warrap State, told AFP the assault on the Bol Bol Boma area began late on Saturday and continued into Sunday, and that the attackers had come from neighbouring Unity State. He blamed fighters of the SPLA-IO, the armed opposition loyal to former vice president Riek Machar, who has been under house arrest since March 2025. The toll rose through Sunday from an initial count of 15; Bith said the dead included seven children, six women and a number of herders, and that four of the attackers, wearing SPLA-IO uniforms, were also killed. State officials condemned the raid and said cattle taken during the attack were later recovered by the community and security forces. South Sudan, independent since 2011, remains split by rival political factions and cycles of communal and cattle-related violence, and long-delayed national elections, once due in December, have been postponed again.

Latest · Global Affairs · Mercury — Only the Second Orbiter in History22 November 2026

BepiColombo Begins Orbiting Mercury After an Eight-Year Journey, Though ESA Reports a Shortened Thruster Burn

After eight years and a winding journey of roughly ten billion kilometres, the European-Japanese BepiColombo spacecraft has gone into orbit around Mercury, the smallest and least explored planet in the inner solar system. The European Space Agency confirmed the arrival on Saturday, making BepiColombo only the third spacecraft ever to visit Mercury and just the second to orbit it, after NASA’s Messenger mission of 2011 to 2015. On Sunday ESA said the capture had succeeded but that one thruster burn during the manoeuvre was shorter than planned because of a software issue; the spacecraft is safe and engineers at the European Space Operations Centre are working to understand what happened. Launched in 2018, BepiColombo is a stack of two orbiters with different jobs: ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter will map the cratered surface, geology and polar ice from a low orbit, while Japan’s Mio will study the planet’s magnetic field and how it interacts with the solar wind from a far more elongated path. The pair are due to separate on 9 and 10 December, work through a commissioning phase into March, and begin routine science in April 2027 for a nominal one-year mission, with an extension planned through 2029. A thruster problem in 2024 had already pushed the arrival back by almost a year.
