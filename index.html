Latest · Europe · Kyiv — The Bridge Barrage20 November 2026

Hours After the Peace Talk, Russia Hammers Kyiv’s Bridges

The contrast could hardly have been sharper. Hours after Vladimir Putin stood alongside Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen presenting the three powers as a force for peace, Russia launched one of its largest overnight assaults of the war — at least 110 missiles and some 400 drones, according to Ukrainian authorities, in an attack lasting more than ten hours. Eight people were killed, six of them in Kyiv, and at least 28 were wounded. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said ballistic missiles damaged three crossings over the Dnipro — the Podilskyi, Darnytskyi and Southern bridges — forcing traffic restrictions between the two halves of the capital, while power was knocked out in several districts and regions as temperatures fell below zero. Moscow said it targeted road and rail bridges used to move weapons, along with military, energy and port infrastructure around Odesa. Kyiv reads the timing as deliberate: retaliation for Ukraine’s strikes this week on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea, which Putin had vowed to answer painfully. France has called for a UN Security Council meeting, and Russia separately pushed back on Friday against any NATO-style security guarantees for Ukraine — a reminder that the diplomacy and the bombardment are now running on parallel tracks.

Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — The Tower Strike20 November 2026

Riyadh’s Tallest Tower Is Hit Again as the Houthi Campaign Reaches the Capital’s Symbols

Saudi Arabia’s financial showcase has been struck for the second time in under two weeks. A Houthi projectile hit the Public Investment Fund Tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District on Thursday, blowing out windows across seven to eight floors of the 80-storey, 385-metre building and bending parts of its metal facade. Civil defence reported material damage to the tower and neighbouring buildings but no injuries; entrances to the district were closed and occupants evacuated. The Houthis said they targeted the financial centre twice on Thursday and also claimed attacks on King Khalid International Airport, an air base at Khamis Mushait and a petrochemical site in Jubail. The Saudi-led coalition said it dealt with a missile threat toward the capital, intercepted another missile aimed at the kingdom’s south on Friday, and that Houthi-run media reported Saudi strikes on Sanaa in return. An Aramco tower in the same district was damaged on 8 November. Oil markets took note: Brent edged up to about $92.22 a barrel on Friday on supply-disruption worries, even as prices remained on course for a weekly fall ahead of OPEC+ meetings on 29 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Into Overtime20 November 2026

COP31 Heads Past Its Deadline With Fossil Fuels Written Out and Campaigners Urging Rejection

The climate summit that was meant to close on Friday is instead sliding into overtime. Negotiators from nearly 200 countries in Antalya remain divided over a revised draft that removed every reference to phasing out fossil fuels — language that had appeared in Monday’s first text — and dropped a proposed $120 billion adaptation-finance target, replacing it with a vaguer discussion of tripling funds by 2035. What survives is an electrification ambition, lifting electricity to 35% of global final energy use by 2035, but without a requirement that the power be renewable, plus bracketed targets for scaling carbon capture to hundreds of millions of tonnes a year. The Climate Action Network said it was unambiguously rejecting the text on the table and urged governments, especially in the Global South, to do the same. Host Türkiye, meanwhile, is counting a different kind of success: more than $26 billion in deals and plans announced during the conference, including a preliminary $15 billion renewable-energy arrangement with China and $5.1 billion in development-bank financing. Whether the final gavel produces a deal delegations can defend at home is now a weekend question.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — After the Handshakes20 November 2026

Spectacle Without Breakthrough: What the Three-Way Summit Did Not Settle

A day after the cameras left Shenzhen, the assessment of the first Trump–Xi–Putin summit is hardening into something cooler than the leaders’ own verdicts. Trump rated the meeting “a 12” out of 10 to NBC News, but analysts told Reuters the encounter exposed the limits of his personalised diplomacy: no arms-control talks were announced, and there was no visible movement on Ukraine, Iran, Taiwan, trade or artificial intelligence — only a broad declaration that nuclear war must never be fought and a pledge to expand refined-petroleum supplies. Trump arrived politically weakened after his party lost control of Congress in the midterms and with an unpopular stalemate with Iran dragging on his approval. Xi, by contrast, burnished his credentials as host of a great-power gathering on home soil, while Putin appeared as an equal of the world’s two strongest leaders without conceding anything. Comparisons in Russian commentary to Yalta in 1945 only sharpened the unease in smaller capitals that big-power theatre is substituting for settlements.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — The Debt Warning20 November 2026

“Urgent Attention”: The IMF Says World Public Debt Is Nearing 100% of GDP

The IMF used the APEC stage in Shenzhen to deliver one of its starkest fiscal warnings in years. Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva told leaders on Thursday that global public debt will soon reach 100% of world GDP — the highest level since the Second World War — and that the figure “requires urgent attention.” The Fund’s published path puts debt at about 94% of GDP in 2025 and approaching 100% by 2029, with higher interest rates translating directly into heavier government interest bills. Georgieva paired the warning with a more hopeful observation: 2026, she said, is the first year artificial intelligence has had a tangible impact on global growth, helping lift world growth to about 3.1% and APEC economies to 3.4%, even as energy-supply shocks pushed global inflation back up to 4.7%. She also flagged a fragility inside that AI boom — circular financing among AI companies exceeding $1 trillion, roughly two-thirds of it debt-supported — leaving the world economy, in her phrase, standing on one leg.

Latest · Americas · Washington — The Cyber Shake-Up20 November 2026

After a Week of Outages, the White House Replaces Its Cyber Director

The first major personnel casualty of America’s week of technology failures is the man in charge of coordinating its cyber defences. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the role, administration officials confirmed on Thursday, with a person close to the White House saying President Trump fully supported firing him over concerns about his credentials. He will be succeeded in an acting capacity by Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer; a permanent appointment needs Senate confirmation, in a chamber Democrats will control from January. The move follows a string of disruptions that officials insist were separate maintenance and technical failures, not a coordinated attack: a Microsoft outage affecting hundreds of millions of users, an FAA ground stop that disrupted more than 18,000 flights, and a temporary failure on the NSA’s classified internal network. Experts told Reuters the incidents likely share a less sinister cause — companies rushing to patch vulnerabilities newly exposed by powerful AI tools — but lawmakers from both parties are demanding fuller briefings, and incoming Democratic leaders say the administration was slow to explain itself and too deferential to tech firms.

Latest · Global Affairs · Deep Space — One Light-Day20 November 2026

A Message That Takes a Day to Arrive: Voyager 1 Crosses a Distance No Object Has Reached

Humanity’s farthest traveller has passed a milestone measured not in miles but in light. NASA confirmed on Thursday that Voyager 1 crossed one light-day from Earth at 5:16 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday — about 16 billion miles, or 26 billion kilometres, away — the first human-made object ever to travel so far. The agency waited a day to announce it for a fitting reason: the confirming signal itself needed roughly 24 hours to make the trip home. Launched in 1977 and still moving at nearly 38,000 miles per hour through interstellar space, the probe now requires a full day for any command to reach it and another day for a reply. It is heading toward the star Gliese 445, which it will pass within 1.7 light-years in about 40,000 years. Its twin, Voyager 2, is not expected to reach the same distance until 2035 — by which time Voyager 1’s power is forecast to be nearly spent, with communications expected to end around 2036 after almost six decades of flight.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Markets — The 36-Times Bond20 November 2026

Investors Queue 36 Deep for China’s $2 Billion Bond, Priced Level With US Treasuries

China has sold dollar debt on terms once reserved for Washington itself. Its $2 billion three-year sovereign bond drew more than $72 billion in orders — 36 times the amount on offer — and priced in line with US Treasuries rather than at the 25-basis-point premium initially suggested, according to a bookrunner message seen by Reuters on Friday. It is the second consecutive year China has raised $2 billion at a zero premium, and its first US dollar sovereign sale of 2026, confirmed by the Ministry of Finance. Bookrunners framed the result as investors treating China as a relatively safe credit at a moment when anxiety over the AI trade is pushing money toward havens. The timing also flatters Beijing’s broader week: the sale landed days after Xi Jinping hosted the APEC gathering and the trilateral summit in Shenzhen, projecting the image of a financial centre of gravity that global capital is content to fund at American prices.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Delhi — The Parcel Network20 November 2026

India Says It Broke a Network That Mailed Half a Million Opioid Tablets to US Buyers

India’s anti-narcotics agency says it has dismantled a drug pipeline run through messaging apps and the international post. The Narcotics Control Bureau announced on Friday that six people had been arrested in connection with a network that took orders for psychotropic substances and prescription opioids online and shipped them to buyers across the United States. Between June 2022 and November 2026, investigators say, the group sent nearly 3,000 parcels containing roughly 500,000 tablets in total. The case underlines how small-parcel e-commerce has become a preferred route for cross-border drug distribution: orders gathered in chat apps, payments and addresses handled digitally, and consignments small enough to blend into ordinary mail volumes. For Indian authorities, the bust is also a diplomatic signal to Washington, where pressure has grown for supplier countries to police pharmaceutical exports that feed the American opioid market.

Six Dead, a Toddler Pulled Alive, and a Day of Mourning After Tbilisi’s Gas Explosion

Georgia will hold a national day of mourning on 22 November for the six people killed when a suspected natural-gas explosion tore through a five-storey apartment block in Tbilisi’s Ponichala district on Thursday morning. Rescuers worked until around midnight and recovered six bodies, including two minors; a three-year-old girl was pulled alive from the rubble after nearly nine hours. Interior Minister Sulkhan Tamazashvili said early findings point to a gas heater on the fifth floor, where a resident described switching the heater off overnight and trying to relight it in the morning. The gas supplier, Tbilisi Energy, countered that the flat in question was registered as unoccupied and had recorded no gas consumption for a year — a discrepancy now at the centre of a criminal investigation into safety-rule breaches. A 31-year-old man remains in critical condition with burns to most of his body. Mayor Kakha Kaladze said 28 families, more than 100 people, would be moved to hotels after inspectors declared two sections of the building unsafe, and state flags will fly at half-mast across the country on Sunday.

Latest · Europe · Rome — The Gallery Fine20 November 2026

€805 Million for Looking at Your Photos: Italy’s Record Privacy Fine Lands on Meta

Italy’s privacy watchdog has imposed the largest fine in its history over a feature most users probably never knew was on. The Garante fined Meta €805 million (about $887 million) after finding that Facebook’s Android app accessed photos, videos and their metadata on Italian phones without specific consent and without properly informing users, through the “Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook” feature, available since 2023. The regulator estimated around 20 million Italian Android users were affected, noted that consent was bundled into a single app-wide permission with the feature on by default, and cited limited cooperation during an investigation opened in July 2025 after a consumer-group complaint in Bolzano. Meta has been given 60 days to switch the feature off by default — including for people who already granted gallery access — and to seek separate, clearly explained consent. The decision, based on the ePrivacy rules in Italy’s Privacy Code, did not cover AI training, which Meta denies. The company calls the findings unfounded and says it will appeal.

Latest · Africa · Nairobi — The Countdown20 November 2026

No Patients, No Contacts in Quarantine: Kenya’s 42 Days to an Ebola-Free Declaration

Kenya is counting down to the end of its first Ebola outbreak. Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale announced on 11 November that the country’s last patient had been discharged healthy and free of the virus, and that the final 11 contacts had completed monitoring and gone home — leaving, for the first time since the outbreak began, no one in treatment and no one in quarantine. The 42-day countdown, twice the virus’s longest incubation period, began on 12 November; if no new case appears, Kenya will be declared Ebola-free on 23 December. The outbreak of Bundibugyo virus disease was confirmed on 6 October, a day after the first imported case was identified in a traveller from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where a far larger regional outbreak declared in May has passed 11,000 cases. Kenya recorded nine confirmed cases in total: the first patient and his mother died, while seven patients recovered — five of them discharged on 3 November as the first Kenyans ever to recover from the disease. Duale said prevention measures will stay in place until the December date is reached, and the Africa CDC praised the response while urging continued vigilance.
