Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash30 October 2026

Spain's Train-Crash Toll Rises to 69 as Rescuers Work Through Mangled Carriages and Drivers Strike Over Rail Safety

Rescuers in central Spain picked through twisted carriages through the night into Thursday, lifting wreckage with cranes near Urda in Toledo province after a Madrid-to-Seville high-speed train ran into the back of a stopped train on Wednesday, in the country's second fatal high-speed crash this year. Regional leader Emiliano Garcia-Page said the death toll had risen to 69, with human remains found during overnight work and one overturned carriage still to be fully cleared, while officials cautioned they could not yet rule out further victims trapped inside. Spain's Foreign Ministry said 42 foreign citizens from seven countries were among those caught up in the crash, with at least 57 injured people still in hospital, and former labour minister Fatima Banez was among the dead. The government has declared three days of national mourning, Pope Leo sent condolences, and the leaders of both main parties suspended campaigning for the 29 November snap election. Train drivers went ahead with a previously planned strike pressing for greater investment in railway safety, as public anger grows over how a second disaster could happen on the same line involving the same operators.

Latest · Americas · US Economy29 October 2026

US Economy Grows at 3.4% in the Third Quarter, Beating Forecasts on AI Spending and Resilient Shoppers

The United States economy expanded at a 3.4% annual rate in the July-to-September quarter, the Commerce Department's advance estimate showed on Thursday, up from 2.2% in the spring and ahead of the 3.2% consensus forecast — the last major growth reading before voters decide control of Congress on 3 November. Consumer spending, which accounts for more than two-thirds of output, rose at a 3.8% pace, while business investment provided the other main engine: spending on information-processing equipment surged 37.4%, software rose 9.6% and research and development climbed 7.5%, reflecting the data-centre and artificial-intelligence build-out. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.9%. The strength came despite a drag from trade and from energy costs pushed up by the war with Iran, with petrol still above four dollars a gallon, and despite housing investment falling 3.3% for its sixth decline in seven quarters. A separate report showed September inflation still elevated, with headline PCE at 3.6% year on year and core at 3.1%, and economists quoted in coverage described the growth as real but fragile, concentrated among higher-income households while lower-income families report living pay cheque to pay cheque.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election Dispute29 October 2026

Police and Shin Bet Push Back as Netanyahu Steps Up "Stolen Election" Claims Against Arab Parties

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Thursday intensified, without presenting public evidence, his allegation that Arab parties' record gains in Tuesday's election were driven in part by voter fraud — a claim Arab leaders denounced as incitement and a toxic campaign to delegitimise the result. With nearly 99% of votes counted, Netanyahu's nationalist-religious bloc holds about 53 seats against 48 for the bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot, both short of the 61 needed to govern, while parties representing Israel's Arab minority are projected to win an unprecedented 19 seats, leaving neither side a path to power without Arab backing. Israel Police said election day produced fewer irregularities than in previous votes and that there is no investigative or intelligence evidence for widespread fraud, and the Central Elections Committee said there was no basis for claims of systematic harm to the election's integrity. The Shin Bet, asked by committee chairman Justice Noam Sohlberg to examine suspected foreign funding of turnout efforts, stressed its inquiry does not implicate an entire community that voted legally and democratically; after being briefed by Shin Bet chief David Zini, Eisenkot and Yair Lapid said nothing they heard justified delaying the new Knesset's swearing-in or casting doubt on the outcome. Qatar, named in some Israeli reporting as a possible funding source, has already denied any interference.

Latest · Americas · Pentagon29 October 2026

Hegseth Fires the Pentagon's Top Military Planner Over Alleged Classified Leaks, Referred to the Justice Department

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has fired Army Lieutenant General Brett Sylvia, director for strategy, plans and policy on the Joint Staff — the J5, the Pentagon's top planning post — over accusations he leaked classified information about the Iran war, the Russia-Ukraine war and other matters to journalists. A senior Pentagon official, speaking anonymously, said Sylvia failed three polygraph examinations about leaking, that he had met reporters outside the Pentagon to evade escort rules, and that the case has been referred to the Department of Justice; chief spokesman Sean Parnell said an extensive Air Force Office of Special Investigations inquiry had forced the personnel action and that the department has zero tolerance for unauthorised disclosure. President Donald Trump, misidentifying Sylvia as a woman, told reporters the general has "got problems" and faces "long-term problems," adding that the administration is actively hunting leakers. Sylvia, who served 32 years, could not immediately be reached for comment, and the Joint Staff thanked him for his service. He is the second J5 director removed under Hegseth, and the firing lands as Trump weighs renewed strikes on Iran after next week's midterm elections.

Latest · Africa · Ebola in DR Congo27 October 2026

Congo's Ebola Outbreak Passes 10,000 Cases With 4,887 Deaths — the Country's Largest and Deadliest on Record

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo have reached 10,060 with 4,887 deaths, government figures released on Tuesday show — a fatality rate of about 48.6% that makes this both the fastest-growing and the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history, and the second-largest globally after the 2014–2016 West African epidemic. The outbreak, declared on 15 May in Ituri province and caused by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment, has spread to 66 health zones across eight provinces, with Ituri accounting for more than 70% of cases. About 80% of new infections are arising outside the transmission chains health workers are monitoring, far from the 95% known-contact threshold the Africa CDC says is needed to call an outbreak controlled, and at least half of those who died were never admitted for treatment. Save the Children says more than 2,700 children have been infected, children under five face a fatality rate of 72%, and over 1,900 children have been orphaned or separated from caregivers. Cases have also been recorded in Kenya and Nigeria, Uganda has been declared Ebola-free, and the World Health Organization has begun the first clinical trial of a candidate vaccine, called Tumaini, in Ituri.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia — Kenya Displacement28 October 2026

Up to 50,000 People Flee Southern Ethiopia Into Kenya After Moyale Clashes Between Oromia and Somali Forces

Up to an estimated 50,000 people have crossed from southern Ethiopia into Kenya since last week to escape fighting around the border town of Moyale, the UN refugee agency said on Wednesday, citing local authorities — a sharp rise from the 36,000 recorded a day earlier, with fresh crossings still being reported. The violence erupted after a Somali regional flag was raised in Moyale, a city jointly administered between Ethiopia's Somali and Oromia regions, and has involved regional forces and armed civilians from the Oromo Borana and Somali Garre communities, whose disputes over boundaries and control date back decades. UNHCR's Kenya representative, Fatima Mohammed-Cole, said many of those fleeing are women and children, sheltering with relatives and host communities or in schools and churches, and that food is the most urgent need, followed by drinking water, shelter, sanitation and health care. UNHCR has sent an emergency team to Moyale and is preparing shelter and household supplies for nearly 12,000 people, while UN humanitarians say insecurity has forced some aid partners to suspend work and disrupted transport, markets and cross-border trade on both sides of the frontier.

Latest · Africa · Sudan War28 October 2026

UN Investigators Say Foreign Weapons, Fighters and Networks Are Fuelling Sudan's War, and Back a Nationwide Arms Embargo

The UN's Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan told the General Assembly in New York on Wednesday that it found reasonable grounds to believe external transfers of weapons, military technology, logistics and foreign personnel continue to sustain and intensify the war, now in its fourth year. Mission chair Mohamed Chande Othman said investigators traced a transnational network operating through individuals and entities in Chad, Puntland in Somalia, southeast Libya and the United Arab Emirates that supplied the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, while stressing the evidence does not yet link neighbouring governments as states — a distinction he said matters, noting Chad denies its territory is used and that inquiries continue. The mission documented up to 2,000 former Colombian military contractors serving alongside RSF units in Darfur and Kordofan, operating drones, artillery and advanced weapons, and said more than 1,000 people were killed in drone strikes in the first five months of 2026 alone. With around 13 million people displaced since April 2023, the investigators urged the Security Council to extend the arms embargo from Darfur to all of Sudan and to widen the International Criminal Court's mandate beyond Darfur, warning that as long as weapons and fighters keep flowing in, civilians will keep paying the price.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms & Markets30 October 2026

Prediction Markets Make a Democratic Sweep the Base Case for 3 November, as Investors Ask Whether Record Stocks Can Hold

With all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats contested on Tuesday, prediction markets are treating a Democratic sweep of Congress as the most likely outcome: pricing cited on 29 October put the chance of Democrats taking both chambers at about 67%, a Democratic House with a Republican Senate at 28%, and a Republican sweep at just 6%, with the probability of Democrats winning the House alone put at 93%. Republicans currently hold the House 218 to 214 and the Senate 53 to 47, so Democrats need a net gain of roughly four House seats to take the Speaker's gavel and control which bills reach a vote. FP Markets chief analyst Aaron Hill noted that US benchmarks sit at record highs and that, historically, the S&P 500 has risen in the year after every midterm since the 1950s in JP Morgan's studies — while warning that a Democratic sweep would raise questions about corporate taxes and regulation, whereas a divided Congress would change little and might ease pressure on longer-dated bond yields. The result will shape the remainder of President Trump's term, including his stated intention to decide on renewed strikes against Iran after the vote.
