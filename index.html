ASEAN Leaders Move Into Closed Retreat in Manila as Marcos Asks ‘What More Can We Do for Myanmar?’ and Protesters Clash Outside

The summit has moved from ceremony to closed discussion. After the morning opening and plenary at the Philippine International Convention Center, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. led the 11 leaders to the renovated Coconut Palace in Pasay City for the retreat session, putting energy security at the top of his remarks and asking directly what more the bloc can do on Myanmar, where the Five-Point Consensus remains stalled and representation stays below leader level. The plenary message was blunter on delivery than on the sea: Marcos promised that all priority economic deliverables will be finished before the Philippines hands the chair to Singapore, highlighted the Digital Economy Framework Agreement signed by economic ministers on Saturday, but gave no timeline for the South China Sea code of conduct — officials now put negotiations at about 70 percent, with the end-2026 target expected to slip into 2027 over scope, definitions and whether the pact should bind legally. Transboundary haze from Indonesian peat fires, the Middle East energy shock and AI disruption run through every session. Outside, the other Manila was visible: All Workers Unity protesters scuffled with riot police at Liwasang Bonifacio after being blocked from marching toward the venue, police made arrests among anti-Pax Silica demonstrators, and nearly 20,000 officers are deployed across the capital ahead of dialogue-partner meetings on Monday and Tuesday, the East Asia Summit on Tuesday, and a first Quad leaders’ meeting since 2024 expected Tuesday evening after President Donald Trump lands.

Latest · Global Affairs · Space — Starship Flight 1514 November 2026

SpaceX Catches Starship From Orbit for the First Time — 14 Laps, 26 Satellites, Then Straight Into the Tower Arms

A spaceship came home to the tower it left. On Saturday afternoon Texas time, Ship 42 dropped out of cloud over Starbase after about 22 and a half hours in space, flipped upright on its engines and settled between the Mechazilla tower’s chopstick arms above Launch Pad 2 — the first time an orbital upper stage has ever been caught this way. The flight, Starship’s 15th full-scale test and only its second trip to orbit, had lifted off Friday evening on Super Heavy Booster 22. The ship completed about 14 orbits at roughly 265 kilometres, released 26 Starlink V3 satellites and survived re-entry visibly scorched before the catch, to cheers from crowds along the Gulf coast and the 1977 song ‘Baby Come Back’ on the company livestream. The double catch SpaceX wanted did not happen: the booster ran low on fuel on the way back, its engines shut down early, and its guidance diverted it over the Gulf of Mexico, where the flight termination system destroyed it about six and a half minutes after launch — Elon Musk confirmed the fuel explanation on X, and engineer Kate Tice described the automatic diversion. SpaceX calls the ship catch proof that full reusability is within reach, while noting the vehicle has not yet shown it can fly again without major refurbishment. NASA is counting on a version of Starship as the Artemis lunar lander.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Recovery, Sunday Count15 November 2026

‘Worst Is Over,’ Says the FAA — But Sunday Starts With 875 Flights Already Cancelled After 6,400 Fell on Saturday

The final Saturday count is worse than the early-evening one. FlightAware tallies reported by Reuters put Saturday at more than 6,400 US flights cancelled and more than 8,000 delayed by 9:35 p.m. Eastern, after a Friday of roughly 9,200 cancellations, with 875 of Sunday’s flights already cancelled before Sunday began — up from 530 in the first counts. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford, who has apologised to passengers, says the system itself is stable and that operations should largely return to normal by late Sunday, with full schedule integrity hoped for by close of business; airlines including Delta, United, American and JetBlue — which cut nearly 45 percent of its Saturday schedule — are offering change-fee waivers, and travellers are being told to check their airline app before leaving home and to consider moving to Monday if they can. The failure behind it all is now described consistently by Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy: contractor L3Harris installed at least one new router at a remote facility while replacing ageing copper lines with fibre, the system overloaded, and the FAA imposed Friday’s nationwide ground stop of nearly five hours — the longest in 25 years, the first nationwide halt since January 2023 and only the second since 11 September 2001. No cyberattack is suspected, and further joint FAA–L3Harris testing is under way while aircraft and crews work their way back into position.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Donbas-for-NATO Question15 November 2026

Zelensky: Washington Asked for the First Time Whether Kyiv Would Leave Donbas if NATO Membership Were Offered

The exact wording matters, and Kyiv has now put it on the record. In remarks released Saturday, President Volodymyr Zelensky said US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff raised the question in these terms — what if Ukraine joins NATO, would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas — and stressed it was the first time the link between territory and membership had been raised in the talks. His answer has two parts: Ukraine is not ready to concede territory, but if a complete package were on the table — binding security guarantees, the right to keep a strong and well-funded army, and financed reconstruction — he would put the question to a national referendum and let Ukrainians decide. Zelensky read the question itself as a signal that NATO is back on the agenda, while warning that Moscow is betting everything on this winter’s campaign and pushing to redraw spheres of influence in Europe. He rejected Russia’s demand for a broad European security settlement before any deal on ending the war, rejected a local humanitarian ceasefire offered only for Kostiantynivka when other trapped settlements were named, and said Moscow refused an energy-infrastructure ceasefire in Abu Dhabi. Those two days of US-brokered talks ended without a breakthrough beyond an agreed prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people; Kushner says Washington is now arranging direct Moscow–Europe engagement and preparing the next round, with Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland named as Europe’s five representatives. Neither NATO nor Washington has commented on Zelensky’s account.

Trump Leans Toward Holding Off on Iran While Tehran Holds Hormuz Shut Pending Its Seven Conditions

Two positions are hardening at once. Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Floods, 226,000 Affected15 November 2026

Gaza Floods Have Damaged More Than 40,000 Shelters and Affected 226,000 People, the UN Says, as the EU–Israel Aid Row Sharpens

The humanitarian numbers behind the diplomatic argument are now specific. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, in its 13 November situation report, says rain that began on 6 November damaged more than 40,000 family shelters, may have affected some 46,000 households and potentially 226,000 people, and flooded about 70,000 tents within 12 hours at the peak, with early damage estimated by Hamas at around $3 million — all preliminary figures while assessments continue. Deliveries slipped in the same period: 262 UN and partner trucks were manifested for entry between 2 and 8 November but only 228 were offloaded at Kerem Shalom, a fall of about 42 percent on the previous week, which OCHA links to the suspension of the Egyptian corridor from 3 to 8 November and to funding shortfalls; shelter partners still reached about 10,690 households with tents, blankets and tarpaulins. The World Health Organization warns respiratory infections are rising in overcrowded, flooded camps. Politically, the European Union on Saturday demanded immediate, unimpeded access, sustained distribution at scale and the reopening of all crossings, and condemned Thursday’s strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, which it said killed six people including a surgeon. Israel on Sunday called that statement completely unbalanced, restating the IDF and COGAT position that more than 93,000 tents and over 8,600 truckloads of winter supplies have entered since September, that sewage trucks, water repairs, vaccine doses and mobile clinics have been facilitated, and that Hamas and uncollected supplies — not approvals — are the binding constraint. Switzerland is preparing 300 family tents, 2,000 tarpaulins and 2 million francs through the International Organization for Migration for about 3,500 people.

COP31 Takes Its Sunday Break After a 7,000-Strong March in Antalya, With Finance and Fossil Fuels Still Unsettled

The talks are quiet today; the argument they must settle is not. COP31 in Antalya is on its scheduled Sunday pause — premises closed for a cultural programme — after Saturday’s midpoint brought the summit’s largest street protest: police put the downtown march at more than 7,000 people, organisers at around 10,000, drawn from environmental groups, trade unions and rights organisations, marching in rain about 20 kilometres from the venue under close police supervision and without major incident. A parallel People’s Climate Summit of hundreds of Turkish and international groups opened alongside it, and coordinated Global Day of Action protests ran across Asia, including in Manila, Lahore and Karachi, demanding reparations, adaptation finance and a faster exit from fossil fuels ahead of a forecast Super El Niño. Inside the negotiations, nearly 200 countries remain far apart on the same three items as at the start: how much adaptation finance developing countries receive, how firmly the texts defend climate science, and whether any explicit shift away from fossil fuels is named at all. UN assessments in the room still point to breaching 1.5 degrees in the 2030s. Talks resume Monday with five days to the 20 November close, a first draft expected early in the week, and no landmark breakthrough anticipated against a conflict-driven energy crisis.

Pope Leo Drives Four Hours Into the Andes to Revisit a Town of Fewer Than 6,000 That Knew Him as Bishop

The most personal stop of a record-breaking tour was also the smallest. On Saturday Pope Leo travelled about 100 kilometres east from the Pacific coast to Santa Cruz de Succhabamba, a western Andean town of fewer than 6,000 people in Cajamarca — one of Peru’s poorest departments, where up to 40 percent live in poverty — retracing a journey he made repeatedly as bishop of Chiclayo from 2015 to 2023. Leo, born in Chicago, lived some 20 years in Peru as a missionary and holds both US and Peruvian citizenship; he is the first Peruvian citizen to become pope, and Peru is the last and most emotional leg of a 12-day, three-nation tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru that the Holy See says has drawn about 12 million people, including an estimated 3 million on the streets of Lima for his arrival and 2.5 million at an outdoor Mass in Buenos Aires. The tour, covering some 31,000 kilometres and 36 speeches, comes barely a month after lung surgery, which the Vatican says he has fully recovered from. On Sunday, his penultimate day in Peru, he travels to Cusco, the former Inca capital, and to Pucallpa on the edge of the Amazon, before returning to Lima and flying to Rome on Monday, arriving 17 November.

South Sudan Registration Runs to 28 November for a First Election Now Set for 11 February — the Third Postponement Since Independence

Registration is open, and the calendar around it is now fully published. The National Elections Commission’s revised timetable keeps voter registration from 14 to 28 November at 3,161 centres across the 10 states and three administrative areas — caretaker governors were photographed registering first in Central Equatoria and the Bahr el Ghazal states — followed by a provisional register on 29 November, an objections window from 30 November to 6 December, and a final register on 9 January. Polling for the presidency and parliament, South Sudan’s first national elections since independence in 2011, moves from 22 December to Thursday 11 February 2027, a 51-day shift and the third major postponement, announced Saturday without a stated reason; commission spokesperson Josephine Achiro Fortelo said the extra time follows stakeholder consultations and is needed to finish registration, distribute materials and build polling infrastructure. Candidate stages run through December and January, with the final candidate list due 18 to 21 January, a 45-day campaign from 27 December to 9 February, a silence day on 10 February, and results scheduled for 15 February. The constraints have not changed with the date: 22,127 officials recruited but materials still moving from state capitals to individual centres, only $21 million received of $276 million requested, former vice-president Riek Machar under house arrest since March 2025, and a caretaker administration in place since September.

Latest · Global Affairs · Space and Markets — The Week Ahead18 November 2026

Two Wednesday Milestones: Voyager 1 Reaches One Light-Day at 10:16 UTC, and Anthropic Prices an IPO Bankers May Push Above Its Range

Wednesday carries one milestone measured in light and one measured in orders. At 10:16:07 UTC on 18 November — 05:16 Eastern — Voyager 1 becomes the first human-made object one light-day from Earth: 25.902 billion kilometres, 16.094 billion miles, 173.14 astronomical units, the distance light needs a full 24 hours to cross, which stretches every command-and-reply exchange with the Jet Propulsion Laboratory team to 48 hours. The probe, launched 5 September 1977 for a five-year Jupiter–Saturn tour with about 69 kilobytes of memory, crossed into interstellar space in 2012, still returns data on a roughly 23-watt signal, and lost contact briefly on 18–19 October before its signal returned on its own. NASA’s Shine Bright invitation asks people worldwide to flash V-G-R in Morse toward the sky after sunset on Tuesday 17 November as the sunset line moves; Voyager 2 is not expected at the same distance until 2035. The same Wednesday, Anthropic is reported to price its initial public offering: 825 million shares marketed at $70 to $78, about six times oversubscribed on Bloomberg’s 12 November reporting, raising up to $64.4 billion publicly and valuing the Claude maker near $1.4 trillion fully diluted, plus a $26 billion private placement to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC for a combined $90.4 billion. Filings show $33.2 billion of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 against large losses and very large compute commitments, and the Financial Times is reported to say bankers are weighing pricing above $78 toward a $1.5 trillion valuation — no filing above the stated range has appeared, allocations will favour existing backers, and first trading under the ticker ANT on Nasdaq is reported for Thursday 19 November.
