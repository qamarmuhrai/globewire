Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — ICC Narrows Afghanistan Case13 November 2026

ICC Judges Close the Part of the Afghanistan Inquiry That Could Examine US Troops, Leaving Only the Taliban Probe

The International Criminal Court narrowed its Afghanistan work sharply on Friday. Judges formally closed the strand of the prosecutor’s investigation that had previously covered alleged crimes by US forces, together with a related line on the Islamic State Khorasan group, and directed that the case focus only on alleged Taliban actions since 2021. The chamber said prosecutors had not shown they could effectively pursue the other allegations alongside the Taliban file. The prosecutor’s office told Reuters it has appealed, without giving further detail. Arrest warrants against four Taliban leaders remain in force. The ruling is the latest turn in a long dispute between judges and prosecutors over the scope of the Afghanistan case, and it lands after Washington imposed institutional sanctions on the court, citing both the Afghanistan inquiry and warrants concerning Israeli officials.

Latest · Africa · Guinea — Mining Ban Clashes13 November 2026

Ten Killed in Guinea as Security Forces Enforce a Ban on Artisanal Gold Mining in the North-East

A crackdown on informal gold mining turned deadly in Guinea this week. The territorial administration ministry said ten people, including five foreign nationals, were killed and 13 injured in clashes on Wednesday in the Siguiri gold region, when defence and security forces were checking compliance with a ban on artisanal mining imposed on 2 November. Reporting on the operation put arrests across Siguiri and Kérouané at more than 1,400, with about 200 people facing proceedings before the economic and financial crimes court, and described the violence as erupting around Sètiguiyah in the Balato district during a joint patrol. Authorities say unregulated digging has contaminated farmland and rivers and destroyed vegetation. The expulsions are spilling across borders: Mali says around 100,000 people have reached Bamako and nearly 60,000 more the border town of Kouremale after being forced to leave Guinea, while Burkina Faso says it has repatriated 45,406 of its citizens.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Recovery Widens13 November 2026

Microsoft Services Come Back Online After Nearly 18 Hours Down, With Email Queues Approaching Normal

Microsoft’s long outage finally eased on Friday night. The company said service availability was improving, email queues had fallen significantly and were approaching normal levels, and broader recovery was visible across North America and much of Europe, with recovery actions completed across parts of the affected Asia-Pacific infrastructure. Some users may still hit intermittent access problems and delayed delivery while the remaining infrastructure is restored. The failure, which began on Thursday and hit Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services across at least 74 countries, disrupted businesses, schools, government agencies and airline operations; Downdetector reports for Outlook peaked above 130,000 in the United States. Microsoft says a large block of Exchange Online servers became unavailable after failing automated health checks, says there is no evidence of malicious activity, and has kept the email timeout window extended to 48 hours so delayed messages are not lost while backlogs clear.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Power Grid Before Winter13 November 2026

Ukraine Races to Rebuild Power Plants Wrecked by Russian Missiles, With Blackouts Already Reaching 18 Hours a Day

Ukraine’s energy crews are working against the calendar. Reuters visited a DTEK thermal plant torn apart by a missile strike this month — its location withheld for security — where technicians described the latest hit as the most devastating yet, one that could leave an entire region without heat and power this winter. Russia resumed large-scale attacks on energy sites in mid-autumn; three of DTEK’s plants have been hit by dozens of missiles and drones already in November and forced to stop generating, after the company’s five plants were attacked 11 times last winter. DTEK says its workers have restarted plants from scratch more than 70 times during the war. Kyiv’s defences are stretched by a shortage of Patriot interceptors — the only system able to stop Russian ballistic missiles — and by new jet-powered attack drones, while strikes on transmission lines are leaving central and eastern regions, including Kyiv, without electricity for up to 18 hours a day even as western plants export surplus power they cannot move east.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Kaziranga Highway Crash13 November 2026

At Least 23 Killed as a Minibus and a Container Truck Collide Head-On Near Kaziranga National Park

One of India’s deadliest road crashes of the year unfolded in Assam on Friday. Police said at least 23 people were killed and around 20 injured when a passenger minibus travelling from the Golaghat side toward Nagaon collided head-on with a container truck heading toward Upper Assam on National Highway 715 at Rangajan, near the Hathikuli Tea Estate in the Kohora range by Kaziranga National Park. Golaghat police superintendent Swapnaneel Deka said about 30 people were aboard the bus, that seven injured people had been referred to hospital in early counts, and that the toll was likely to rise; other officials said many of the dead were college students returning from classes, including two women. Police suspect the minibus was travelling at very high speed in breach of restrictions near the park and lost control. Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma said he was deeply distressed, sent a senior minister to oversee relief, and an enquiry has been ordered into the cause.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Hormuz and Bahrain Claims13 November 2026

Iran Says a Gas Tanker Hit a Mine in Hormuz and Claims a Strike on a US Radar in Bahrain, as Trump Dismisses the Need for a Deal

The Gulf crisis produced competing claims through Friday. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said a tanker carrying liquefied gas struck a naval mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz after using what it called an unauthorised and dangerous route; no vessel name, flag or casualty details were released and there was no independent confirmation. Iranian media had reported four oil tankers hit by explosions in the waterway on Thursday, and analyst counts cited by Reuters put at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly total since the conflict began in late February. Separately, the Guards said they had destroyed a US military early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for a US strike on a telecommunications tower at Sirik in southern Iran. In Washington, President Donald Trump said the United States does not need an agreement with Tehran, hardening the diplomatic line even as energy shipping through Hormuz remains under near-daily threat.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Talks and Prisoner Exchange13 November 2026

Europe’s Seat at the Ukraine Table Is Agreed in Dublin, but Moscow Cautions the Details Are Still Being Worked Out

The shape of the next Ukraine negotiations firmed up overnight into Friday, with the fine print still contested. After meeting Presidents Putin in Moscow and Zelenskyy near Dublin, US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner said the next round would include European representatives as full members of the negotiating team, and that the sides had finalised work toward what they called the largest prisoner-of-war exchange of the war, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families. Zelenskyy said the trilateral format had been unblocked after this week’s Abu Dhabi session — the first direct Russian–Ukrainian meeting in nine months — and that further multilateral meetings were being prepared. In Moscow, presidential aide Yuri Ushakov confirmed the trilateral track would continue but appeared to question European participation and said the exchange parameters were still being agreed, with no date or location announced. Territory remains the core dispute: Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine and Kyiv refuses to cede the rest of the Donbas.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria — US Religious Freedom Concern13 November 2026

US Officials Say Violence Against Christians in Nigeria Remains a Top Concern a Year After the Religious-Freedom Designation

Washington kept Nigeria’s security crisis in focus on Friday. Assistant Secretary of State Frank Garcia told a congressional panel that the State Department treats violence against Christian communities as a top priority and is working closely with Nigerian authorities to hold those responsible to account, a year after the Trump administration designated Nigeria a country of particular concern over religious freedoms — a step Abuja rejected as based on misinformation. Fellow Assistant Secretary Riley Barnes said Nigeria had hired more police, deployed forest guards and increased prosecutions and arrests since the designation, but that the measures had not yet made Christians safer in the Middle Belt. Insecurity is set to dominate campaigning ahead of Nigeria’s presidential and parliamentary elections on 16 January 2027, as Africa’s most populous country battles an Islamist insurgency, banditry and farmer–herder clashes in the north and kidnapping for ransom in the south. Nigeria’s information minister did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Technology — Nvidia Fallout in Southeast Asia13 November 2026

Nvidia Fallout Puts Southeast Asia’s AI Boom Under Scrutiny as Lawmakers Push to Close the Cloud Loophole

The scandal around Nvidia’s former chief executive is widening beyond Washington. Executives and analysts in Southeast Asia expect tougher scrutiny of the region’s chip and data-centre industries after the resignation of Jensen Huang, with Singapore and Malaysia in particular focus. US lawmakers from both parties want tighter restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI chips, including closing a loophole that lets Chinese firms rent computing power from overseas data centres running the restricted hardware. Possible measures include new export rules and licensing requirements covering Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Singapore. At the centre of the case sits Singapore-based cloud firm Megaspeed, whose executive Alice Huang has been named in reporting on the scandal and which Singapore authorities have investigated since February last year over potential false declarations on server purchases. The stakes for the region are large: Malaysia has approved about $94.7 billion of data-centre investment since 2021, and Malaysia said on Thursday it was weighing a law to monitor data-centre operations and high-performance chip use.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — Pressure on the Houthis13 November 2026

France Backs More Pressure on Yemen’s Houthis as Saudi Arabia Presses the EU for a Terrorist Designation

European pressure on Yemen’s Houthi movement is building. France’s foreign ministry spokesperson Pascal Confavreux said on Friday it is necessary to increase pressure on the group so it ends its attacks in the region, notably on Saudi Arabia, describing them as unacceptable attacks and provocations. He was responding to a question on whether Paris backs designating the Houthis a terrorist organisation in the European Union. Saudi Arabia is pressing the EU for exactly that move, but has yet to overcome European concerns that a listing could undermine a future peace process, six European officials told Reuters on Thursday. The diplomatic push follows the Houthis’ claimed attack this week on Saudi Arabia’s East–West oil pipeline and a facility at the Red Sea port of Yanbu, and Thursday’s unanimous UN Security Council renewal of financial and travel sanctions on individuals deemed to threaten peace in Yemen, alongside a call for the movement to halt military escalation and attacks on civilians.

South Korea’s Foreign Minister Asks China to Help Bring North Korea Back to Dialogue, Ahead of the Shenzhen APEC Summit

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi Air Emergency13 November 2026

Delhi Becomes the World’s Most Polluted Major City as Hazardous Smog Holds Through Friday

Delhi’s air emergency deepened across Friday. The capital overtook Lahore to become the world’s most polluted major city, with Swiss monitor IQAir putting its air quality index in the hazardous band at 334 while India’s Central Pollution Control Board rated conditions still worse, at 403 in the severe category, as smog cut visibility across the city of 20 million. Local monitoring through the day kept readings in the severe range, touching 418 around AIIMS and 402 at Rajokri Border, with the Dwarka Expressway at 369 in the very poor band. An index up to 50 is considered good with little or no health risk, underlining how far current levels sit above safe thresholds. The seasonal mix behind the crisis — crop-residue burning, traffic emissions and cooling, still air over the Indo-Gangetic plain — showed no sign of breaking, leaving residents facing another night of haze and fine-particle levels far beyond recommended limits.
