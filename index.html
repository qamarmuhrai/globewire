Latest · Europe · France19 October 2026

Macron Refuses His Interior Minister’s Resignation After Police “Lost Control” in Weekend Protests

France’s interior minister, Laurent Nunez, admitted on Monday that police “lost control” during nationwide cost-of-living protests that turned violent over the weekend, but President Emmanuel Macron refused his offer to resign and instead ordered a security review. Around half a million people demonstrated across France on Saturday, and the unrest that followed — town halls and shops looted and set ablaze — was among the worst the country has seen in years. More than 2,500 people were arrested and almost 1,000 members of the security forces were injured; one protester lost a hand to a police crowd-control grenade. Nunez conceded a “lack of anticipation” in deploying roughly 60,000 officers, and Macron directed him to produce new operational methods for tackling rioters and full transparency over the planning and intelligence failures.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pakistan19 October 2026

Pakistan and Afghanistan Trade Heavy Fire as Border Clashes Shut Torkham and Chaman Crossings

Pakistan and Afghan Taliban forces exchanged gun and artillery fire along the border in the heaviest direct confrontation of the current escalation, after Afghan forces opened fire on Pakistani posts late on Saturday in the Upper Kurram area. Kabul said the action retaliated for alleged Pakistani airstrikes inside Afghanistan earlier in the week, including one said to target a Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan leader in Kabul; Islamabad has not officially acknowledged the strikes, and casualty claims from both sides remain unverified. Pakistan said several Afghan border posts were destroyed, including the Shipola base camp. The fighting shut the Torkham and Chaman crossings on Sunday, along with Kharlachi, Angoor Adda and Ghulam Khan, stranding hundreds of passengers, cargo trucks and medical patients, while Islamabad insists talks with Kabul cannot be unconditional but says it welcomes sincere mediation efforts.

Latest · Middle East · Egypt19 October 2026

Egypt Says It Is Ready to Help Defend Saudi Arabia as Houthi Attacks Intensify

Egypt has declared itself ready to give Saudi Arabia whatever support it needs to defend its security and stability, as Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis step up attacks on the kingdom, including recent strikes on Riyadh’s international airport and the state oil giant Saudi Aramco. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi asked parliament to hold an urgent session to authorise the Egyptian military — one of the region’s largest — to assist if Riyadh requests it; a vote expected on Sunday slipped into further discussions on Monday. The offer, made after a National Defence Council meeting chaired by Sisi, raises the stakes in a Yemen fight that has become another front in the wider Iran war since a truce collapsed, and it threatens the Bab el-Mandeb shipping route on which Egypt’s Suez Canal revenues also depend. Cairo said it would pair the military pledge with intensified diplomacy aimed at de-escalation.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China19 October 2026

China Puts Export Controls on Tunnel-Boring Machines in a Blow to Indian Infrastructure Projects

China announced on Monday that exports of tunnel-boring machines and related equipment will come under formal export controls from November 18, a step its commerce ministry said is meant to safeguard national security and meet non-proliferation obligations, noting the machinery’s clear dual civilian and military uses. The controls also cover walking excavators and high-altitude wet shotcrete machines, and adjust existing rules for some dredging equipment. The move lands hardest on India, which relies on Chinese boring machines for metro networks, railway corridors and tunnels in border areas and has repeatedly raised customs delays on such imports with Beijing. It follows a Chinese draft plan, released days earlier, to restrict exports of dozens of cutting-edge technologies, including some advanced solar manufacturing and artificial-intelligence platforms.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza19 October 2026

UN Food Agency Cuts Monthly Aid for 500,000 People in Gaza, Citing a Funding Shortfall

The World Food Programme says funding shortages are forcing it to shrink its Gaza operation, cutting planned monthly food assistance from 1.6 million people to 1.1 million and leaving roughly 500,000 people without monthly aid. The agency says it needs about $450 million over the next six months and stresses the cuts are driven by money, not by any fall in need: about 1.4 million people in Gaza already face crisis-level or worse food insecurity, and two thirds of the population remains acutely food insecure. Aid groups warn the progress made against famine since the ceasefire is fragile, especially after commercial goods stopped entering the territory last week amid a tax dispute between local merchants and Hamas.

Latest · Global Affairs · Ukraine19 October 2026

Ukraine’s Record Drone Barrage Draws a Public Rebuke From Trump as Putin Floats a Three-Way Summit

Russia’s defence ministry said it destroyed 1,015 Ukrainian drones overnight into Sunday — the largest single-night figure Moscow has ever published — as Ukrainian strikes set fires at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and other sites and cut power to about 20,000 people in the Leningrad region. President Donald Trump responded by accusing President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of not wanting to end the war and complaining that attacks on Russian diesel refineries are hurting world fuel markets; Zelenskyy, speaking on US television, warned that siding with Russia would be a “historic mistake.” Separately, US reporting said President Vladimir Putin has proposed a trilateral summit with Trump and China’s Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the APEC meeting in Shenzhen on November 18–19, while Zelenskyy confirmed talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner on the next diplomatic steps.

Latest · Americas · United States18 October 2026

Trump’s Approval Falls to a Record Low of 33% Two Weeks Before the Midterm Elections

President Donald Trump’s approval rating has dropped to 33% in a Washington Post–ABC News–Ipsos poll released on Sunday — the lowest figure that survey has recorded for him in either term — with 66% of US adults disapproving and 52% strongly disapproving. The cost of living is the dominant grievance: about three quarters disapprove of his handling of inflation, 71% now call gasoline unaffordable, and 48% say their finances have worsened since he returned to office, the highest such reading since 1986. For the first time since 2014, voters trust Democrats more than Republicans on the economy, and Democrats lead the generic House ballot by eight points ahead of the November 3 midterms. Separate Fox News and NBC News polls put Trump’s approval at similarly low levels of 36% and 34%.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom19 October 2026

UK Police Probe Vandal Attacks on Defence Executives’ Homes Claimed by a Pro-Palestinian Group

British police and counter-terrorism officers are investigating a string of vandalism attacks on the homes of defence industry executives and employees, after a pro-Palestinian group calling itself “The List” claimed responsibility online. The group says it carried out three attacks in recent weeks, including daubing red paint over the Dunfermline home and cars of an engineer at defence contractor Leonardo and slashing the tyres of a car belonging to an executive at Ultra Intelligence & Communications in Hampshire; it says it targets firms it accuses of working with the Israeli arms company Elbit. Leonardo confirmed it is assisting Counter Terrorism Police, and Police Scotland said inquiries into incidents reported on October 11 in Dunfermline and South Queensferry are ongoing.

Latest · Global Affairs · Armenia19 October 2026

World Biodiversity Summit Opens in Yerevan for First Global Review of Nature Pledges

The 17th meeting of the Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity, known as COP17, opened in Yerevan, Armenia, on Sunday, bringing governments together for the first global stocktake of progress under the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. Delegates will assess how far countries have come in implementing the landmark agreement to halt and reverse nature loss, which set targets including protecting 30% of land and seas by 2030. The review is expected to expose wide gaps between the pledges governments made and the action and financing delivered so far, setting the agenda for the rest of the decade’s conservation effort.

Latest · Middle East · Israel19 October 2026

Israel Trims Ministry Budgets by 2.6 Billion Shekels to Fund Urgent Defence Purchases

Israel’s government approved a cut of 2.6 billion shekels — about $840 million — from most ministries’ 2026 budgets on Sunday to pay for what it called “urgent defence procurement,” according to state broadcaster Kan TV News. The across-the-board trim comes as Israel’s wars continue to strain public finances eight days before the October 27 national election, and one day after US President Donald Trump gave Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu his “complete and total endorsement” for re-election. The budget move underlines how heavily defence spending is crowding out civilian services as the campaign enters its final week with polls pointing to a tight race and no clear coalition path for any candidate.
