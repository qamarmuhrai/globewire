Latest · Global Affairs · Nvidia — Huang Resigns After 33 Years12 November 2026

Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia Chief With Immediate Effect, CFO Colette Kress Takes Over as Shares Plunge

Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and led it for 33 years into the world’s most valuable chipmaker, resigned on Thursday as president, chief executive and board member with immediate effect, the company announced. Finance chief Colette Kress, at Nvidia since 2013, will serve as interim chief executive while the board searches for a permanent successor among internal and external candidates, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones becomes the company’s first board chairman. The exit follows days of turmoil: on 8 November Nvidia said independent directors, helped by outside lawyers, were investigating whether Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation, after reports about his relationship with a Singapore-based executive US officials suspect of spying for China. Nvidia’s shares fell more than 13 percent on the news. The company gave no reason for the resignation in its statement, thanked Huang for his contributions, and confirmed its third-quarter results will still be released on 17 November.

Latest · Europe / Global Affairs · Moscow Talks — Envoys Claim Progress12 November 2026

Witkoff and Kushner Leave the Kremlin Saying Progress Was Made and a New Round Is Set, as Putin Holds His Line on Territory

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner have ended roughly two hours of talks with President Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin and flown on to Dublin to brief President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, closing a day that began with their arrival in Moscow after the Abu Dhabi negotiations. Speaking at Vnukovo airport, Witkoff said progress had been made, that the trilateral format with Ukraine had been restarted and that a new meeting had been set, adding that European representatives are expected to join the next round — something Putin said Russia had never objected to in principle. But the Russian leader kept his hard public line: he rejected proposed “grey zones” in favour of a dividing line at the state border, and repeated that any European troops deployed in Ukraine after a deal would be unacceptable and treated as legitimate targets. The Abu Dhabi talks, the first direct Russian-Ukrainian discussions in nine months, produced agreement on a large prisoner exchange but no ceasefire, and Kyiv says territory remains the central unresolved question.

Latest · Americas · Brooklyn — Collapse Toll Rises to Eight12 November 2026

Brooklyn Collapse Toll Rises to Eight Dead and 19 Injured as Crews Search a Second Day and Rain Threatens the Work

The death toll from the collapse of a three-storey apartment building in Brooklyn’s Flatbush neighbourhood has risen to eight, Mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed on Thursday, with at least 19 people injured, seven of them children. The building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue fell into the adjoining excavation pit shortly after 4 p.m. on Wednesday, and firefighters worked through the night pulling bodies and at least one survivor from the wreckage. Six of the dead were recovered from the rubble and two died in hospital. Officials still cannot say how many people were inside the building or working at the construction site when it came down, and have asked anyone unable to reach a loved one to call 311. The Department of Buildings has imposed a full stop-work order on the neighbouring excavation at 2625 Bedford Avenue, where records show a previous citation for working despite a stop-work order; the cause of the collapse remains under investigation, and forecast heavy rain may hamper the search.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Logan Circle Shooting12 November 2026

Five Shot, One Killed, in Washington’s Logan Circle a Mile From the White House; Gunman Dies After Barricading Himself

Five people were shot early on Thursday in the Logan Circle neighbourhood of Washington, about a mile north of the White House, in an attack that began inside a VIDA Fitness gym at 15th and P Streets shortly before 8 a.m. and continued outside. One victim died and another was left in critical condition, while the remaining injuries were not life-threatening. Interim police chief Jeffery Carroll said the suspected gunman, who was in a dispute with a homeowners’ association, retreated to a seventh-floor apartment and shot himself as officers negotiated with him for nearly an hour; he was pronounced dead at the scene and a shelter-in-place order was lifted. A witness in the gym, which held about 50 people, described hearing two shots inside and five or six more outside. Federal agents from the ATF and the FBI assisted District police, and Mayor Muriel Bowser said the White House was kept informed throughout.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal — Makwanpur Toll Rises to 1412 November 2026

Nepal Crash Toll Rises to 14, Six of Them From One Family, as Officials Open an Inquiry Into the 500-Metre Plunge

The death toll from Thursday morning’s electric microbus crash in Nepal’s Makwanpur district has risen to 14, police confirmed, after searchers recovered more bodies from the slope below the Bhimphedi–Kulekhani–Kathmandu road. The vehicle, carrying 20 people from Kathmandu to Sarlahi for the Chhath festival, left the road at Chisapani Gadhi at about 5:45 a.m. and fell some 500 metres. Six of the dead were members of one family from Chakraghatta in Sarlahi — Punam Devi Yadav, 35, her daughters aged ten, six and five, and two male relatives — and all 14 victims have now been identified, among them the driver, Ravi Shankar Kumar Mandal. Six injured passengers are being treated at Hetauda Hospital. The district administration has formed a five-member committee, given seven days to report, to establish why the bus left the road on a route typical of Nepal’s hazardous mountain highways.

Vice President JD Vance is considering direct talks with Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, in a renewed attempt to end the war that began with US and Israeli strikes on 28 February, according to US and Middle Eastern officials cited by the Wall Street Journal. G7 Foreign Ministers Warn China’s Moves East of Taiwan Threaten Stability; Beijing Calls the Statement Interference

G7 foreign ministers have warned that China’s increasingly assertive activity in the waters east of Taiwan threatens regional stability, freedom of navigation and the safety of international shipping, in a joint declaration issued late on Wednesday after their meeting in Paris. China, which claims Taiwan as its own territory, has stepped up coast guard patrols off the island’s east coast since June, a shift the ministers described in their strongest unified language yet on the issue, reaffirming opposition to any unilateral change to the status quo by force or coercion. Beijing’s foreign ministry rejected the statement on Thursday, saying it disregarded facts, interfered in China’s internal affairs and smeared China. President Xi Jinping separately urged complete “reunification” of Taiwan with the mainland, which he pledged to pursue peacefully, while Taipei welcomed the G7 declaration.

Latest · Europe · School Bomb Hoaxes — Italy, UK and Ireland11 November 2026

One Hoax Email Reaches 6,300 Italian Schools and Hundreds More in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland; No Device Found Anywhere

Investigations are continuing across four countries after a single emailed bomb threat reached about 6,300 schools in Italy on Wednesday, forcing at least 300 evacuations, while closely similar messages went to roughly 1,000 schools in Scotland, more than 300 in Northern Ireland and schools in the Republic of Ireland. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni convened ministers and intelligence chiefs and said checks had found no concrete danger; the Italian message, seen by Reuters, also threatened Meloni and was signed “Mikhail Andropov”. Police Scotland and Northern Ireland’s authorities judged their messages hoaxes originating abroad, advised schools to stay open and reported no disruption to classes, unlike Italy, where parents rushed to collect children. No explosive device was found at any site, and officials in Rome describe the episode as part of a wider pattern of disruption and suspected hybrid-warfare harassment hitting European supermarkets, blood banks, air traffic and broadcasters.

Latest · Africa · Ebola — Kenya Countdown, Congo Out of Control12 November 2026

Kenya Begins Its 42-Day Countdown to Ebola-Free Status While Congo’s Outbreak, the Second Largest on Record, Passes 11,000 Cases

Kenya on Thursday began day one of the 42-day countdown after which it can be declared free of Ebola, having discharged its last patient and released its final 11 quarantined contacts on Wednesday. Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said the country’s outbreak — nine cases and two deaths, all traced to a traveller who returned from the Democratic Republic of Congo through Uganda in early October — had seen no new infection since 19 October, and that arrivals from Congo will now face 21 days of quarantine; if the countdown holds, the Ebola-free declaration falls on 23 December. The picture at the outbreak’s source is far graver: Congo’s government reported 11,042 infections and 5,387 deaths from the Bundibugyo-strain epidemic declared in May, now the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record and spread across four countries, and Médecins Sans Frontières says that after six months it remains out of control.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Oman Island Dispute12 November 2026

Iran Says It Struck Navigation Systems on an Omani Island Guiding Hormuz Traffic; Oman Disputes That Any Were Hit

Iran says its Revolutionary Guards struck the navigation and communications systems on Didamar Island, the small Omani island whose aids help guide shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, and three sources confirmed to Reuters that the island’s systems were attacked. Oman disputes the claim: a senior Omani official told The Maritime Executive the navigation aids had not been struck. The contested incident comes on top of Wednesday night’s explosions that disabled two supertankers carrying more than four million barrels of oil in the strait, which Tehran attributed to mines on an unauthorised southern route. With roughly a fifth of the world’s oil normally passing through the waterway, even disputed reports of attacks on its guidance infrastructure add to the pressure on energy markets already strained by the war, and underline how hard basic facts in the strait have become to verify independently.

Latest · Americas · SS United States — World’s Largest Reef11 November 2026

The SS United States, Once the Fastest Liner Afloat, Settles Upright on the Gulf Floor as the World’s Largest Artificial Reef

The ocean liner SS United States has completed her final voyage, settling upright on the seabed of the Gulf of Mexico at 9:59 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, about 22 nautical miles southwest of Destin, Florida, to become the world’s largest artificial reef. The 990-foot ship — longer than the Titanic and still the fastest passenger liner ever to cross the Atlantic — took seven hours and 19 minutes to flood and sink after crews opened her hull plugs at about 2:40 p.m., with no explosives used, and now rests in roughly 180 to 200 feet of water. Okaloosa County bought the liner, laid up for decades and facing the scrapyard, for a 13.5 million dollar reef project; her sinking had been moved forward a day because of forecast winds, and a court challenge by preservationists was denied on 6 November. Divers are expected to treat the wreck, whose upper decks sit within recreational reach, as a landmark site alongside the previous record holder, the carrier USS Oriskany off Pensacola.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Korea — Seongnam Bus Pile-Up11 November 2026

A City Bus Ploughs Into 32 Vehicles Waiting at a Seongnam Traffic Light, Injuring 57 People in the Morning Rush

Investigators in South Korea are examining how a city bus careered into a queue of traffic in Seongnam, just south of Seoul, striking 32 vehicles — four buses, a cargo truck and 27 cars — over 100 to 150 metres before hitting a barrier at 7:34 a.m. on Wednesday. Fifty-seven people were injured, nine of them seriously, though the Gyeonggi Province fire department said none of the injuries was life-threatening. The driver, a man in his 60s who was among the seriously hurt, tested negative for alcohol and told police the brakes had failed; officers plan drug testing and are reviewing dashcam and CCTV footage to establish the cause. The crash, at the Beolmal intersection in the city’s Bundang district during peak commuting, blocked traffic in both directions and caused severe congestion across the area.
