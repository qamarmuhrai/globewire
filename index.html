Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Second Strikes, Crews Missing17 November 2026

Hit Twice off Khasab, Abandoned in the Strait: Hormuz Turns from Blockade to Rescue Operation

The Hormuz emergency deepened sharply on Tuesday afternoon. Britain’s UKMTO said a tanker first struck on Monday about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman — identified by monitors as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX, disabled by an engine-room fire — was hit a second time, caught fire again, and its crew is now unaccounted for, reversing the morning’s assurance that all aboard were safe. Separately, India’s shipping minister Sarbananda Sonowal said a second attack on the Panama-flagged LPG tanker TARA GAS forced its entire 25-member crew to abandon ship, with 24 accounted for and one Indian seafarer still missing; the vessel had first been attacked on Monday. Iran’s navy and Revolutionary Guards continued to say tankers had struck mines on routes Tehran calls unauthorised — a fourth such incident in 24 hours by Tehran’s count — while Bahrain said it intercepted Iranian drones aimed at a US radar site at Sheikh Isa Air Base, a claim US Central Command said caused no damage. Diplomacy ran in parallel and without a breakthrough: Iran’s foreign minister Abbas Araghchi met Oman’s Badr Albusaidi in Muscat to discuss safeguarding maritime corridors, as Tehran pressed a short truce proposal Washington has not formally answered.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — Two Dead, Bridges Inspected17 November 2026

Seven Missiles Aimed at Two Bridges: Kyiv Confirms Two Dead as Engineers Inspect the Dnipro Crossings

Kyiv’s picture darkened through Tuesday. The State Emergency Service said two people were killed in the Obolonskyi district, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said four ballistic missiles targeted the Southern Bridge and three the Northern Bridge, and Mayor Vitali Klitschko posted video from the Northern Bridge showing debris across a damaged deck while specialists from the National Infrastructure Development Institute began technical inspections to judge when traffic can return. Latest · Asia-Pacific · Manila — Singapore Takes the Gavel, Quad Meets17 November 2026

The Gavel Passes, the Quad Returns: ASEAN Closes in Manila with One Big File Left at 80 Percent

The 49th ASEAN Summit formally closed in Manila on Tuesday evening as President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. handed the ceremonial gavel to Singapore’s Prime Minister Lawrence Wong at the Philippine International Convention Center. Singapore will chair the 11-member bloc in 2027 — its 60th-anniversary year — under the theme “Deeper Integration, Wider Partnerships,” with Wong pledging steps toward a single ASEAN market and faster clean-energy and digital links; Singapore last chaired in 2018. The summit’s largest unfinished item carries over with him: ASEAN and China did not complete the long-negotiated South China Sea code of conduct, with about 80 percent of the draft provisionally agreed. President Donald Trump arrived late in the afternoon and went first into the first Quad leaders’ meeting in two years, joining India’s Narendra Modi, Japan’s Sanae Takaichi and Australia’s Anthony Albanese — his first Quad summit since returning to the White House — ahead of a US–Thailand trade signing, the ASEAN–US Special Summit and a leaders’ dinner, before he flies on to Shenzhen.

Latest · Europe · Paris — 5.8 Million Called Out17 November 2026

17 Stations Shut, a March Across Paris, a Pay Index Frozen for a Fourth Year: France’s Strike Day

France’s public-sector strike — the third united day of action since September — played out on Tuesday with marches in nearly 180 towns and cities. In Paris, police expected 40,000 to 80,000 people on the main march from Place de la Nation to Place d’Italie, with 220,000 to 300,000 expected nationwide, while RATP closed 17 Metro and RER stations from 11 a.m. at the police prefecture’s request and regional rail and the RER D and several Transilien lines ran reduced services; mainline TGV, Ouigo and Intercités trains ran normally and no airport disruption was announced. The coalition behind the call — CGT, CFDT, FO, UNSA, FSU, Solidaires, CFE-CGC and FAFP, representing 5.8 million public employees — wants the point d’indice that sets basic civil-service pay unfrozen and linked to inflation, arguing purchasing power has fallen about 16 percent since 2017. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s government, whose draft budget rules out a general rise for a fourth straight year, has offered targeted increases of up to €650 gross for more than 1.3 million lower-paid staff and further talks on 11 December — an offer CGT leader Sophie Binet dismissed as worth about €5 a month on the minimum wage.

Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — The Speaker Vote17 November 2026

Kahana Against Katz at 4 p.m.: The Knesset Votes on Who Controls Its Agenda While a Government Is Still Unbuilt

Israel’s deadlocked parliament moved to settle its first contest on Tuesday evening, with debate opening at 4 p.m. on replacing Speaker Amir Ohana of Likud. The bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot — tasked by President Isaac Herzog with forming a government after the 27 October election left neither camp with a majority, on the strength of 67 recommendations against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu — nominated Matan Kahana of Eisenkot’s Yashar party, backed by Yashar, Together, the Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu and the two Arab parties. Likud countered late by nominating Ofir Katz, and in a notable shift the Haredi parties Shas and United Torah Judaism — which had declined to recommend Netanyahu to Herzog — said they would back Katz, though Kahana was still expected to prevail on a simple majority. The speakership matters beyond ceremony: the holder chairs plenary debates and controls what reaches the floor and when, and Eisenkot’s bloc hopes a win lets it begin advancing legislation, including repeal of the judicial overhaul, as early as Wednesday — even though the parties behind him have not yet agreed to govern together.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — 150,000 Tents at the Border17 November 2026

“Stop the Delays Now”: Four Governments Press Israel as Winter Floods Sweep Gaza’s Tent Camps

Four foreign ministers — Australia’s, Canada’s, Italy’s and Britain’s — have jointly urged Israel to allow immediate, rapid and unimpeded humanitarian access across Gaza, saying recent floods have brought further suffering to a population largely living in makeshift shelter. Their statement says 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at the border and prevented from entering, even as heavy rain this month swept away tens of thousands of tents in the territory’s first winter storm. The Global Shelter Cluster estimates residents need at least 1.7 million mattresses and 121,000 tents for the cold, wet season; UN reporting cites around 46,000 households alerted and more than 40,000 shelters damaged in early November, and the appeal follows similar warnings from the UN humanitarian team and the European Union. Israel’s aid-coordination agency COGAT says more than 91,000 tents and over 8,200 trucks of winter supplies have entered under a winter plan coordinated with international partners — a figure the ministers’ statement implicitly disputes by demanding the held supplies be released before winter fully sets in.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Tokyo — 04:07 and 04:0917 November 2026

Two Entries, Three Minutes Each, Timed to the Minute: Japan Gives the Precise Account of the Airspace Breach

Japan’s Defence Ministry filled in the timeline on Tuesday: one suspected Russian aircraft was inside Japanese airspace from about 04:07 to 04:10 and a second from about 04:09 to 04:12, both over territorial waters northwest of Okinoshima Island in Fukuoka prefecture, before crossing the Tsushima Strait, passing between Yonaguni and Taiwan, and reaching the Pacific. Air Self-Defense Force fighters from the Western Air Defense Force scrambled and issued radio warnings. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara called the violation a serious infringement of sovereignty and completely unacceptable, confirming a very stern protest through diplomatic channels; Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi gave the same account and demanded preventive measures. Neither official identified the aircraft types, and Tokyo still describes the planes as suspected Russian. It remains the first confirmed Russian violation since 23 September 2024 off Rebun Island, Hokkaido, and it arrived the same morning South Korea reported Russian aircraft transiting its air defence identification zone — a notification buffer that, unlike sovereign airspace, carries no legal bar on entry. Moscow has not publicly responded.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — Carbon Markets Join the Fight17 November 2026

Antalya’s Second Front: As Ministers Argue Over Fossil Words, 17 Countries Sign Up for Carbon Markets

COP31’s ministerial week developed a second track on Tuesday. While ministers responded to the 18-page, 144-item draft — with its two rival fossil-fuel clauses, a proposed tripling of adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year by 2035 against needs near $305 billion, and a push to lift electricity to 35 percent of final energy demand by 2035 — a closed-door roundtable hosted by the Coalition to Grow Carbon Markets produced the Antalya Statement on carbon credit markets. Colombia, Ghana and Peru signed, taking its supporters to 17 countries, while Colombia and the Philippines joined the coalition itself, lifting membership to 18. UN climate chief Simon Stiell told the roundtable high-integrity carbon markets could play a critical role as the world overshoots the 1.5°C limit, and Singapore’s environment minister Grace Fu called the statement a key outcome of the summit. Negotiations president Chris Bowen continued to defend the draft as a test of positions rather than a record of agreement ahead of Friday’s consensus deadline; campaigners at 350.org said it names the destination on fossil fuels but drops any clear process for getting there.

Latest · Europe · Travel — A Europe-Wide Holiday Alert17 November 2026

Same Words from London to Sofia: US Embassies Warn Americans Across Europe Ahead of the Holidays

US embassies across Europe posted a coordinated security alert on Monday, in near-identical wording, warning American citizens that terrorist groups and lone actors may attack with little or no warning during the holiday season. Missions in London, Berlin, Madrid, Dublin, Lisbon, Sofia and elsewhere listed markets, transportation hubs, shopping malls, places of worship and other public areas as potential targets, and pointed travellers to the State Department’s Smart Traveler Enrollment Program. The notices name no specific plot, city or date and do not change any country’s formal travel advisory level; they sit alongside the standing Worldwide Caution. They also flag two practical disruptions: strikes and demonstrations have become more frequent in recent months, and hoax bomb threats against civil aviation in November — which disrupted dozens of flights in several countries, with nearly 30 passenger flights targeted on a single day earlier this month — could recur. For the millions heading to Christmas markets in the coming weeks, the message is vigilance rather than avoidance.

Latest · Europe · Vienna — The Rwanda Return Hub17 November 2026

Five EU Countries Unveil the Bloc’s First Offshore Return Hub — in Rwanda — Over Rights Groups’ Objections

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands unveiled a deal in Vienna on Monday to establish a migrant return hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers — the first such facility outside the European Union. The five stepped up the plan after the EU toughened its migration rules, clearing the way for offshore returns, and the centre is expected to begin operating from next year. The announcement is the culmination of a deportation push launched in 2024 as public opinion on migration soured across the bloc, even though arrivals have fallen steadily for three years as the EU struck deals to stem departures from Africa and the Middle East. Human Rights Watch condemned the arrangement over fears of repression and abuse of detainees, the UN refugee agency UNHCR has warned against it, and France’s President Emmanuel Macron has expressed doubts. Supporters argue a credible prospect of removal is needed to make asylum systems work; critics answer that the policy exports both people and legal responsibility.
