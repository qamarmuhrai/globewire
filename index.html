Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — The Declaration19 November 2026

Four Pledges, No Deal on the Wars: Shenzhen Ends With a “Declaration Toward Peace”

The first trilateral summit of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders has ended in words, not settlements. After roughly 90 minutes of talks at Kylin Villa on the sidelines of the APEC summit, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin adopted a joint text — the Russia-China-US Heads of State Declaration Toward Peace — built on four pledges: reforming the United Nations so it can respond better to global challenges, keeping energy flows stable and increasing the production and supply of refined petroleum products, respecting state sovereignty and the immunity of government officials, and opening a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation. The three reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought — a live question since the New START treaty expired in February, leaving the two largest arsenals without a cap. What the declaration does not contain is equally clear: it names no nuclear limits, sets no timelines, and mentions neither Ukraine nor Iran. The Kremlin said the Ukraine war was “not a central topic” of the three-way session, though it dominated Trump’s earlier 40-minute bilateral with Putin, after which the Russian leader was heard telling Xi the conversation had gone better than expected. Trump has since left Shenzhen for Washington, and whether the three meet again — possibly as early as next month — is now the open question.

Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — The Verdict19 November 2026

“The Only Reasonable Inference”: World Court Finds Myanmar Committed Genocide

Seven years after The Gambia filed its case, the International Court of Justice has done something it had never done in its 80-year history: held a state responsible for genocide. By nine votes to six, the court found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya of northern Rakhine State during the 2017 military campaign, with President Yuji Iwasawa reading the judgment over nearly three hours in the Great Hall of Justice. The bench found the military engaged in indiscriminate killings — some 10,000 Rohingya were violently killed in the clearance operations — alongside widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and concluded that the scale and nature of the attacks “went well beyond what would be required to punish the population” and pointed to an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group. Myanmar was ordered to make reparations, to allow and facilitate the safe, voluntary and dignified return of those who fled, to restore lost property, and to investigate and punish those responsible. The ruling is final and binding but the court has no means of enforcement; that falls to the UN Security Council. Its reasoning — including that genocidal intent can coexist with military aims — will be studied closely in the other genocide case now before the same court, and in Cox’s Bazar, where about 1.2 million Rohingya watched the reading on big screens in the camps.

Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — The Tallest Tower19 November 2026

Windows Blown Across Seven Floors: A Houthi Strike Reaches Riyadh’s Financial District

The war in Yemen has again landed in the showcase of Saudi economic ambition. In the early hours of Thursday, after a powerful blast was heard across Riyadh and civil defence alerts warned of an aerial threat, debris from an intercepted Houthi missile damaged the Public Investment Fund Tower — at 385 metres and 80 storeys, the tallest building in the capital and the headquarters of the sovereign wealth fund chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. Witnesses described the facade damaged across several floors with windows blown out, and glass shattered on surrounding buildings in the King Abdullah Financial District, some of them residential; there were no immediate reports of casualties, and most staff in the tower have worked remotely since early September. The Saudi-led coalition said it had “addressed a missile threat” toward Riyadh and separately intercepted five ballistic missiles launched toward the kingdom’s southern region, while the Houthis’ military spokesman said the group had targeted the capital’s international airport and financial district with cruise missiles. It is the second strike on the district in under two weeks: a Houthi attack damaged the Aramco headquarters tower on 8 November, part of an intensifying campaign against Saudi airports, ports, energy sites and now its financial centre.

Crew Safe After Engine-Room Hit: The Tanker Strike, Clarified

One detail from the overnight Hormuz attack has firmed up: nobody was hurt. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre confirmed that the tanker struck by an unidentified projectile six nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, as it sailed outbound through the strait, took the hit in its engine room but that all crew members are safe and accounted for, with the origin of the projectile still unknown. The strike sits inside a wider choke on the waterway: Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says three tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines east of the strait after ignoring warnings and entering restricted waters with navigation systems off, and Iran says it shot down a US MQ-9 drone east of Hormuz following US strikes on Qeshm Island. The energy stakes are now being priced in annual terms — industry reporting on Thursday put the forecast rise in global oil and gas import costs at about 25%, to roughly $1.6 trillion in 2026, after conflicts disrupted supply and destroyed an estimated 2.8 billion barrels of production capacity, with record diesel prices among the consequences. The International Energy Agency, publishing its annual outlook, has described the Middle East crisis as the greatest threat ever observed to energy security.

Yunus’s Ancestral Home Torched as Bangladesh Shuts Down

Bangladesh’s political crisis turned violent on the day of a nationwide shutdown. Police said two members of the ruling party were killed and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus in Chattogram, about 300 km southeast of Dhaka, was set ablaze late on Wednesday — flammable material apparently thrown into an unused ground-floor room while family members on the upper floors escaped unharmed. The banned Awami League of exiled former prime minister Sheikh Hasina called Thursday’s lockdown as the culmination of a week of protests against the government’s refusal to issue her a new passport and identity card for a planned return in December, and reporting through the day put violence across as many as 28 districts. To contain the unrest, 97 platoons of Border Guard Bangladesh deployed across seven districts, including Gopalganj and Faridpur. The shutdown falls days before the first anniversary of the verdict that sentenced Hasina, 79, to death in absentia for ordering the deadly 2024 crackdown on the student uprising that drove her from power. In a separate development on Thursday, Bangladesh connected its first nuclear power plant, built with Russian technology at Rooppur, to the national grid.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bay of Bengal — 105,304 Moved19 November 2026

105,304 in Shelters, 9,299 Ready: Odisha’s Race Against Akvan

The evacuation count climbed all day on India’s east coast, and by Thursday afternoon it had passed six figures. Odisha’s chief secretary Anu Garg said 105,304 people had been moved into 1,224 operational cyclone and flood shelters, with the process still running: Ganjam district, which contains the expected landfall point, accounts for 45,364 of them and Gajapati for 18,000, and the state has identified 9,299 shelters in all, deployed 305 emergency response teams and closed schools across the exposed districts. The Special Relief Commissioner’s office said those moved include close to 10,000 pregnant women, and the state’s stated aim is two lakh — 200,000 — people out of harm’s way under an explicit zero-casualty target. The India Meteorological Department’s track has settled after a day of uncertainty: severe cyclonic storm Akvan, moving north at about 12 km/h, is expected to cross the coast close to east of Gopalpur, between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, around noon on Friday 20 November, with sustained winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110. Red warnings for extremely heavy rain cover the coastal districts, three express trains have been rescheduled and services on the Visakhapatnam–Kirandul route cancelled as the last hours before landfall run down.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — The Words Removed19 November 2026

Fossil Fuels Vanish From the Text: COP31’s Deal Shrinks a Day Before the Deadline

The centrepiece fight of the Antalya climate talks has been settled, for now, by deletion. A slimmed-down revised draft of the COP31 deal, published on Thursday, contains no reference at all to ending the use of fossil fuels — the phase-out language that headlined Monday’s 18-page, 144-paragraph first draft, with its proposed immediate transition in energy systems and a tripling of adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year by 2035, has been stripped out. The revised text still acknowledges that warming is projected to overshoot the Paris 1.5°C threshold and the need to bring temperatures back down, but the fossil-fuel clauses that Russia, Saudi Arabia and the Arab group had resisted — some objecting to a cover decision existing at all — are gone. Australia’s Chris Bowen, presiding over the negotiations under a split presidency with Türkiye, had said from the start that Monday’s draft was a broad document meant to be narrowed; campaigners counter that a transition pledge without the words is no pledge at all. The text is expected to be revised again before it can be agreed by nearly 200 parties, and with the conference scheduled to close on Friday 20 November, recent summits suggest the real deadline will run at least a day past it.

Screams Heard From the Beach: Dozens Missing After a Migrant Boat Goes Down off Senegal

For the second time in under two weeks, the Atlantic route to Europe has claimed a boat off Senegal. A wooden pirogue capsized at about 2 a.m. on Wednesday off Yenne, south of Dakar; a village official said people on the beach could hear screaming from the vessel before the badly damaged wreck washed ashore. A survivor questioned by police put the number aboard at about 100, including five women — a local rights group, citing another survivor, said around 50 — and by Thursday at least one death was confirmed, with some local media reporting four bodies recovered and dozens of people still missing as search operations resumed. The context is a route surging back to life: the UN refugee agency says nearly 8,200 migrants reached the Canary Islands in the first 12 days of November alone, and nearly 600 people have been reported dead or missing on the West Africa route in 2026 — the previous wreck, off Kébémer earlier this month, killed at least 39.

Latest · Europe · Kemerovo — The An-12 Inquiry19 November 2026

19 Dead on a Scheduled Flight: Investigators Comb the Siberia Crash Site

Russia’s crash investigators are working through the wreckage of one of the country’s worst military aviation losses of the year. An Antonov An-12 transport aircraft came down at about 9:30 p.m. local time on Tuesday near the village of Yagunovo, some 13 km from Kemerovo in southwestern Siberia and roughly 3,000 km east of Moscow, during what the Defence Ministry called a scheduled flight; all 19 people on board, crew included, were killed, and emergency crews extinguished a fire at the site. The ministry’s preliminary assessment points to a technical malfunction, without specifying what failed. An Aerospace Forces commission is examining the circumstances at the site, and the Investigative Committee has opened a criminal case under Article 351 of the criminal code, covering violations of flight rules or of preparation for them — the standard legal track after a Russian military crash. Early reporting had put the toll at 17 before the ministry confirmed 19. The An-12 is a Soviet-designed four-engine turboprop that has been in service since the late 1950s, a longevity that keeps questions about the age of Russia’s transport fleet in view each time one is lost.

Latest · Global Affairs · Deep Space — One Light-Day19 November 2026

A Signal That Takes a Day to Arrive: Voyager 1 Crosses One Light-Day From Earth

Humanity’s farthest object has passed a distance no machine has reached before. At 5:16 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday, NASA’s Voyager 1 crossed one light-day from Earth — about 16.1 billion miles, or 25.9 billion kilometres, the distance light itself needs 24 hours to travel. A command radioed from the Deep Space Network now takes a full day to reach the probe, and its answer another day to come home. Voyager 1 has held the farthest-object title since 1998, crossed into interstellar space in 2012, and is still working nearly five decades after its September 1977 launch, travelling at close to 38,000 mph toward the star Gliese 445, which it will pass within 1.7 light-years in about 40,000 years. The milestone changes nothing physically aboard the spacecraft: it is down to two working science instruments, its plutonium power fades a little each year, and its twin, Voyager 2 — more than 13.4 billion miles out, with signals taking about 20 hours — is not expected to reach one light-day until November 2035, if it is still answering by then. “It’s never happened before, and it may never happen again,” the mission’s manager at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory said of the crossing.
