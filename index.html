Trump Gives Netanyahu His “Complete and Total Endorsement” Nine Days Before Israel’s Election

President Donald Trump on Sunday formally endorsed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for re-election in Israel’s October 27 parliamentary vote, calling him a “Great Warrior Prime Minister” who has been “Strong, Brave, and Brilliant in the face of tremendous opposition.” In a Truth Social post, Trump tied his backing directly to the war with Iran, warning that if Israel chose “the wrong Leader, which would be anyone other than Bibi, America will have to do this work ourselves, and I am not inclined to do so,” and adding that the United States may “soon finish the job” if Tehran does not change course. The vote is Israel’s first national election since the Hamas attack of October 7, 2023, and polls show a tight race. Netanyahu quickly thanked Trump for his “tremendous support,” while Trump told Axios he had always intended to endorse him: “I just didn’t want to do it too soon.”

Latest · Europe · France18 October 2026

Macron Denounces “Unbridled” Violence in Address to the Nation as Arrests Near 2,400

President Emmanuel Macron condemned the weekend’s cost-of-living unrest in a televised address on Sunday evening, saying “nothing justifies” violence he described as “unbridled” and unprecedented. “Tonight, we all feel anger and shame,” he said, distinguishing protesters with “legitimate demands” from “those who destroyed, smashed, and looted,” and vowing that “those who have destroyed must pay.” The interior ministry said nearly 2,400 people were arrested on Saturday, with almost 1,000 police and gendarmes injured, seven of them seriously, while fresh clashes flared in Paris on Sunday as police fired tear gas and stun grenades at an unauthorised gathering. Macron stopped short of a state of emergency or curfew, but ordered a review of intelligence gathering and police preparedness, empowered local officials to ban certain gatherings, and promised talks this week with unions, students, teachers and business leaders to produce “concrete proposals.” An interministerial crisis meeting is due on Monday morning.

Ukraine Defies Trump With One of Its Biggest Drone Barrages of the Year on Russia

Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones at Russia overnight, setting the St Petersburg oil terminal and the city’s Ruchi oil depot ablaze and also hitting a logistics centre of the retailer Ozon and a data centre of the tech company VK. Russia’s Defence Ministry said its air defences destroyed 1,015 Ukrainian drones, and St Petersburg governor Alexander Beglov said one person was killed and five wounded. The strikes deepen Kyiv’s public rift with President Donald Trump, who last week called Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries a “disgrace” and said on Sunday that Zelenskyy “doesn’t want to end this war.” A week ago Trump announced an energy ceasefire that neither side acknowledged; Ukraine briefly paused such strikes, then resumed them, while Russia never stopped. Ukrainian authorities said Russian attacks killed 11 people and injured nearly 50 across Ukraine in the 24 hours to Sunday morning, including a major strike on energy facilities in the Odesa region. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had attacked Ukraine’s energy sector 76 times since the ceasefire announcement, and vowed the strikes on Russia would continue.

Rubio Says Trump Will Take Up to 60 Days to Weigh the Russia Sanctions He Just Waived

Secretary of State Marco Rubio says President Donald Trump will use up to 60 days to assess the economic impact of the Russia-related sanctions and tariffs he waived on Friday, just days before they were due to take effect. The measures, adopted under the Graham Act, include duties on Russian goods and penalties on countries that buy Russian oil and gas — a provision Rubio noted would reach partners such as India, which remains heavily dependent on Russian energy. “The president wants to take the 60-day period of time to sort of analyze what imposing both sanctions and tariffs will mean to the global economy and even to the US economy,” Rubio said, adding that conflicts around the world are “starting to crash into each other” and driving up oil and diesel prices. Trump himself was noncommittal: “A lot of you don’t know what my decision is yet, but we’ll figure it out, and you’ll figure it out.” Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has warned the waiver will embolden Vladimir Putin and prolong the war.

India’s Three-Part Truce Plan Described by Kyiv as the “Broadest” Proposal to End the War

India has put forward a three-part truce proposal for the Russia-Ukraine war that would halt attacks on energy facilities and other infrastructure, keep grain and energy flowing through Black Sea ports, and end attacks on commercial shipping, according to a Geopolitical Monitor report cited by IANS. The plan, conveyed to Ukrainian officials during External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar’s visit to Kyiv in September, goes beyond separate initiatives from Türkiye, Egypt and the United States. Latest · Americas · United States18 October 2026

Trump’s Approval Falls to a Record-Low 33% as Democrats Pull Ahead Before the Midterms

President Donald Trump’s approval rating has fallen to 33%, with 66% of Americans disapproving, in a Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll released on Sunday — his lowest mark in the outlets’ polling across both of his terms, down from 37% in April and July. Some 52% of respondents said they strongly disapprove, and the results nearly mirror a Fox News poll released on October 14 that also set a record low. The survey of 2,545 adults, conducted October 9–13, comes just over two weeks before the November 3 midterm elections. Democrats now lead the generic House ballot by eight points among registered voters, up from three points in July, and for the first time in over a decade are seen as more trustworthy than Republicans on the economy, by 40% to 33%. Around three in four Americans disapprove of Trump’s handling of the cost of living and inflation, and voters blame the Iran war and its effect on fuel prices, with Republicans defending a competitive Senate map in states including Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas.

Latest · Americas · Canada19 October 2026

Alberta Votes Today on Whether to Start Down the Path Toward a Separation Referendum

Albertans go to the polls on Monday in a ten-question referendum whose most closely watched item, Question 10, asks whether the province should remain part of Canada or instruct its government to begin the legal process toward a binding vote on separation. Polls are open from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Mountain Time, and the result is not binding. Every major poll since September has put “remain in Canada” far ahead — 70% to 22% in a Léger survey in early October, and 66% to 34% among decided voters in the latest Angus Reid polling. Premier Danielle Smith opposes separation and says she will vote to remain, while urging yes votes on the other nine questions, covering immigration and constitutional changes, to pressure Ottawa; she has also said she will not call another separation referendum if the remain side wins. Prime Minister Mark Carney campaigned in Alberta in the final days, calling the separation path a “dangerous gamble” and comparing it to Brexit. Separatist organisers say they will keep pushing whatever the outcome.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China18 October 2026

China Proposes Export Controls on 12 Solar-Manufacturing Technologies

China has proposed adding 12 solar photovoltaic manufacturing technologies to its export-control catalogue on national security grounds, as part of a broader draft revision covering 40 technologies across artificial intelligence, biomedicine and solar power. The Commerce Ministry said greater oversight is needed because solar technologies are increasingly used in sensitive fields such as aerospace and national defence. The draft, announced on Friday as Announcement No. 48 of 2026, would ban or restrict technologies used to make thin silicon wafers of 70 micrometres or less, perovskite and perovskite-silicon tandem cells, and large-scale fluidised-bed polysilicon, with public consultation running until November 15. Exporters must meanwhile register certain overseas technology transfers. Analysts say Beijing is seeking to preserve its dominance of solar manufacturing as India builds up domestic production, and Reuters has reported the curbs could complicate expansion plans by US firms such as Tesla.

Champi Weakens to a Typhoon but May Regain Super Typhoon Strength Before Entering Philippine Waters

Typhoon Champi weakened from a super typhoon on Sunday evening but is expected to re-intensify — possibly back to super typhoon level — before entering the Philippine Area of Responsibility on Tuesday, the state weather bureau PAGASA said in its late-night bulletin, updated in the early hours of Monday. The cyclone was located 1,830 kilometres east of southeastern Luzon, with maximum sustained winds of 175 km/h and gusts of up to 215 km/h, moving west-northwest at 15 km/h with typhoon-force winds extending up to 680 kilometres from its centre. On entering Philippine waters it will be named Rosal. PAGASA said wind signals may be raised over the northern and eastern parts of northern Luzon and parts of the Bicol region as early as Monday evening, with Signal No. 3 or 4 possible in the far north, and heavy rain is expected over extreme northern Luzon from Wednesday, alongside hazardous seas along the northern and eastern seaboards.

Seven Arrested Over Kolkata Pandal Stampede as Police Point to “Gross Mismanagement”

Seven people, including organisers and private security staff, have been arrested in connection with the stampede at the Chorbagan Durga Puja pandal in north Kolkata that killed 19 people — among them 13 women and two minors — and injured 25 on Saturday evening. Police say preliminary evidence points to “gross mismanagement” by the organisers: there was no comprehensive plan to regulate visitors, instructions on entry, exit and crowd movement were inadequate, and neither the gates nor the approach road could handle a sudden surge. Investigators believe heavy rain drove people outside to rush in for shelter just as an announcement warning that the structure might collapse spread panic; eyewitnesses also complained that security was concentrated near the VIP entrance. The West Bengal government has formed a Special Investigation Team, is examining CCTV footage, and will pay ₹25 lakh (about $30,000) and a government job to each bereaved family, with ₹5 lakh for each injured person; Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari is due to hand over compensation cheques to families on October 20 and 21.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo18 October 2026

Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Is a Strain With No Approved Vaccine, as Kenya’s Cases Include Health Workers

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is being driven by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment, leaving health authorities to rely on isolation, contact tracing and infection prevention while vaccine research continues. The outbreak, declared in May, has become the largest and deadliest in Congo’s history and the second-largest globally after West Africa’s 2014–2016 epidemic; it has now reached 66 health zones across eight provinces, with cumulative totals of 9,415 confirmed cases and 4,536 deaths. Across the border, Kenya had confirmed seven cases as of October 17, including three health workers who treated the country’s first patient — a Kenyan man who travelled from Congo through Uganda and died on October 5. Uganda, which recorded 20 cases earlier in the year, declared its own outbreak over in August after 42 days without a new infection.
