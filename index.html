Latest · Asia-Pacific · Diplomacy — A First Trilateral?7 November 2026

Three Presidents, One Photo? Beijing Confirms Talks on a Xi–Trump–Putin Meeting in Shenzhen

An encounter that has never happened before is now officially under discussion. Beijing has confirmed for the first time that it is in communication with Washington and Moscow about a meeting of Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of this month’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, on 18–19 November. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told reporters China is talking with the relevant parties about a gathering of the three heads of state, while Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said work is under way to arrange it and he hopes it will be finalised in coming days. The White House has not confirmed the plan. The three leaders have met in pairs many times but never sat down together in a formal trilateral, and the timing gives the idea weight: Trump is already expected in Shenzhen for his third meeting with Xi this year, with a fragile trade truce due to expire in January, and Putin proposed the three-way format in an October phone call. Analysts quoted in coverage are sceptical that anything substantive would emerge — Trump has floated nuclear arms talks, Putin’s priority is Ukraine, and German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier has said Xi told him Ukraine would not be on the table because Beijing sees it as a European conflict — but even a carefully choreographed photograph of the three together would be a diplomatic event in itself.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Strikes Confirmed on Qeshm7 November 2026

“Minor Strikes” and a $20 Million Bounty: Trump Confirms Hitting Qeshm as Iran Warns It Is Stronger Now

The Qeshm exchange is no longer a matter of rival claims. President Trump confirmed on Friday that US forces carried out limited strikes against an Iranian air-defence system on Qeshm Island, at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, telling reporters the Americans knocked out what Tehran had built “in about 15 minutes, maybe five”, while insisting Washington has made “a lot of progress” in talks. Iranian state media reported two projectiles striking the Suza area of the island early on Saturday, and the army said its defences had destroyed a US MQ-9 drone overhead; US Central Command called the situation “relatively calm” and, in the same news cycle, announced a reward of up to $20 million plus possible relocation for information on Iran’s nuclear programme, its facilities and its scientists. Tehran’s answer on Saturday was defiance: army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said Iran’s forces are stronger and more confident than when the war began on 28 February, that the Strait of Hormuz remains under Iranian control and closed until Washington meets its seven conditions, and that any fresh attack would draw a response with “no geographical limits”. Qatar’s prime minister said Doha’s mediation continues precisely to stop the spiral — and for now, mediation, strikes and threats are all running at once.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Overnight Attack on Kyiv7 November 2026

Kyiv Hit Overnight, Chernihiv Dark: Zelenskyy Says Every Power Plant Is Damaged — and the Refinery War Goes On

Four days before talks resume in Abu Dhabi, the energy war escalated on both sides overnight. Russian ballistic missiles and drones struck Kyiv and its region into Saturday, damaging homes, a kindergarten, the Northern Bridge and energy infrastructure, according to Ukrainian reporting, while the northern city of Chernihiv lost electricity completely after a drone attack and suspended electric transport. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has now put the cumulative damage in stark terms: every power plant in Ukraine has been hit “in one way or another”, including strikes on equipment that nuclear stations depend on, and roughly 600,000 people are without reliable power. In his Friday night address he warned that intelligence shows Russia preparing a further wave aimed at the power grid, the bridges across the Dnipro and civilian infrastructure, and appealed for faster delivery of Patriot interceptors. Kyiv’s reply is already in motion: Zelenskyy confirmed after a military briefing that Ukraine’s campaign against Russia’s oil-refining industry continues in direct response, with fires reported overnight at the Saratov refinery and at an industrial plant in Oryol. With each capital openly targeting the other’s energy system, the Abu Dhabi agenda item on halting infrastructure attacks has rarely looked harder — or more necessary.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 98% Counted7 November 2026

98% In, ANC Below 31%: South Africa’s Count Nears Its End with 110 Councils Hung

South Africa’s local election count is all but complete, and the shape of the result has settled. By Saturday morning the Electoral Commission’s dashboard showed 98 per cent of votes counted: the ANC on 30.95 per cent with just under 8.9 million votes, the Democratic Alliance on 24.56 per cent, and Jacob Zuma’s MK Party third on 9.62 per cent — the ANC’s worst national performance since 1994, down from 45.6 per cent in 2021. The commission says results for 4,190 wards and 190 municipalities have been finalised and 7,372 seats allocated, with counting, capturing and verification done in 23,210 of 23,696 voting districts. The defining number, though, is 110: that many councils have no outright winner. In KwaZulu-Natal the MK Party finished first with 34.6 per cent, pushing the ANC down to about 21 per cent in its former stronghold, while most Gauteng metros are led by the DA without a majority. The formal declaration is still expected on Sunday, 8 November; after it, councils have fourteen days to elect mayors and speakers, and the coalition bargaining that will decide who actually governs South Africa’s cities moves from speculation to deadline.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Trade — Rare Earths Deadline Nears7 November 2026

“Fully and Immediately”: Washington Presses Beijing on Rare Earths Ahead of the Trump–Xi Meetings

The trade truce has a compliance problem, and Washington said so on the record on Friday. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Trade Representative Jamieson Greer used a video call with Vice Premier He Lifeng, China’s lead trade negotiator, to insist Beijing implement its commitments under the deal struck at Busan in October 2025 — above all on rare earths and other critical minerals, and on agricultural purchases. US officials have complained for months that American firms cannot obtain sufficient quantities; reporting in September put China’s fulfilment of its rare-earth pledge at roughly two-thirds. The Busan arrangement capped US tariffs on Chinese goods from the second Trump term at about 20 per cent, in return for which China pledged to buy 25 million metric tonnes of US soybeans over three years and to boost other farm purchases by $17 billion, while pausing new export controls. One deadline inside the deal is days away: China’s published suspension of its October 2025 rare-earth controls runs only to 10 November, even though the wider truce has been extended to 10 January. Xinhua reported that He agreed to keep using consultation for stable ties. The argument now runs straight into the calendar: Trump and Xi are expected to meet at APEC in Shenzhen on 18–19 November and again at the G20 in Miami on 14–15 December.

Latest · Americas · Health — Measles Milestone7 November 2026

5,182 Cases and Counting: US Measles Hits a 35-Year High Days Before Its Elimination Review

A disease the United States declared eliminated in 2000 has crossed a line not seen in a generation. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported 5,182 confirmed measles cases for 2026 as of 5 November — the highest annual total since 1991, more than double the 2,289 cases recorded in all of 2025, and up 330 in a single week. The cases span 45 states plus Washington, DC, with 43 outbreaks this year; 4,993 of them were outbreak-associated, 562 patients — 11 per cent — were hospitalised, and 96 per cent of infections were in people unvaccinated or of unknown vaccination status. Pennsylvania is the hardest-hit state, reporting 1,487 cases. Even the death toll is disputed: the CDC counts four measles-associated deaths using federal mortality data, while state departments have reported at least eight, including six in Pennsylvania — a gap the agency says it is working to standardise. The timing could hardly be sharper: on 10 November the Pan American Health Organization reviews whether the United States keeps the measles-elimination status it has held for 26 years, a status lost if one strain is found to have circulated continuously for twelve months. On the current numbers, that review will be a verdict on a resurgence already visible in the data.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Environment — Delhi Tops Pollution Table7 November 2026

581 on the Index: Delhi Is the World’s Most Polluted Major City on the Eve of Diwali

Delhi woke on Saturday to the worst air ranking on the planet. Swiss group IQAir’s live table put the Indian capital first among major cities at 10:35 a.m., with a US air quality index reading of 581 — double the 285 recorded in Lahore, across the border in Pakistan. India’s own Central Pollution Control Board, which uses a different scale, put Delhi at 363 at 10 a.m., in the “very poor” category, and local media reported that Friday’s air was the worst recorded before Diwali in eight years. The comparison between the two indices is not direct, but the direction is not in doubt, and the worst may be immediately ahead: Sunday is Diwali, the festival of lights, widely marked with firecrackers that reliably push pollution higher still. Authorities have ordered schools in and around the capital to offer online classes and encouraged people to work from home ahead of the festival. For a city that enters this season near the top of the global pollution table most years, Saturday’s double-sized lead over the next city is a measure of how severe this year’s pre-festival build-up has become.

Ten Governments Condemn, Hong Kong’s Lawmakers Condemn Back: the Joshua Wong Row Escalates

The diplomatic argument over Joshua Wong’s sentence widened on Saturday. Ten governments — including the United States, Britain, Germany, France, Australia, Canada, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and New Zealand — issued a joint statement on Friday condemning the further 10-year jail term handed to the Hong Kong democracy activist on Thursday for colluding with foreign forces under the national security law, calling the prosecutions further examples of the law being used against political expression and urging an end to unjust cases. Rights groups including Amnesty International called the new sentence cruel and timed just before his eligibility for release. Advocacy groups are now urging President Trump to raise the case with President Xi at this month’s APEC meeting in Shenzhen — which would put Hong Kong’s most famous prisoner on the agenda of the month’s biggest summit.

Latest · Global Affairs · Health — Gene Therapy Under Investigation7 November 2026

From “Theoretical” to Confirmed: FDA Investigates Tumour Risk in Novartis’s Zolgensma Gene Therapy

A risk that regulators long described as theoretical now has confirmed cases behind it. The US Food and Drug Administration said on Friday it is investigating a serious risk of malignancy linked to Zolgensma, Novartis’s one-time gene therapy for spinal muscular atrophy in children under two, first approved in 2019. Novartis has received nine reports of tumours in treated patients; in two children, molecular analysis of tumour tissue found the therapy’s viral vector DNA integrated into the genome at the GLI1 oncogene, a gene that can drive cancer when activated — a mechanism called insertional oncogenesis — and a third GLI1-linked case is under investigation. Reported tumours include those of the central and peripheral nervous systems, the spinal region and the kidney. The FDA said the same risk applies in principle to Itvisma, Novartis’s related product for patients aged two and older, which uses the same vector, though no cases have been reported after Itvisma. Current prescribing information still describes tumour risk from vector integration as theoretical; the agency is now reviewing whether that wording and the long-term follow-up studies are adequate, while stating that for now the benefits of both products continue to outweigh their risks for their approved uses. For the thousands of families who chose a pioneering therapy for a devastating disease, the investigation’s outcome — and the label’s next wording — will matter enormously.

Latest · Americas · Haiti — Salvadoran Deployment7 November 2026

2,000 Troops and a Gang Leader Asking for Talks: El Salvador’s Haiti Intervention Tests the Gangs’ Grip

Haiti’s gang war has acquired a foreign army. More than 2,000 Salvadoran troops have arrived in little over a week after four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force disappeared in an ambush on 18 October near Croix-des-Bouquets, outside Port-au-Prince; this week Haitian police, working with the force and Salvadoran units, recovered the burned vehicle the men had used, in the Santo neighbourhood, but not the men themselves. President Nayib Bukele has posted videos of his heavily armed soldiers patrolling and of helicopters firing on gangs, writing “the gangs no longer have the upper hand” and challenging gang members to gather and fight. Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé insists the foreign forces operate under Haitian authority and support — not replace — the state; a UN spokesman noted only about 200 of the Salvadorans fall under the Gang Suppression Force’s jurisdiction, which is authorised for 5,550 personnel but remains far below strength. The most telling reaction has come from the other side: Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò Sanjou, leader of the 400 Mawozo gang, has written to the UN Secretary-General asking for dialogue and mediation. Gangs still control most of the capital, and Haiti has now set its first general elections in over a decade for July and October 2027 — a timetable that depends on whether the new force can actually loosen the gangs’ hold.

“No Doubt” He Would Come: Leo Celebrates Mass in Paysandú on the Fullest Day of His Uruguay Visit

Pope Leo XIV’s first visit to Latin America reached its busiest day on Saturday. The 71-year-old pontiff flew in the morning to Paysandú, the cultural and economic hub on Uruguay’s border with Argentina, to celebrate Mass at Parque París Londres, with pilgrims crossing from Colón in Argentina among the crowds expected. Friday’s address to Uruguay’s authorities praised Mercosur, peaceful coexistence and care for the environment, while defending life in the womb and questioning euthanasia in Latin America’s most secular country. On Sunday he celebrates Mass in Florida before the shrine of Our Lady of the Thirty-Three, Uruguay’s patroness, then flies on to Buenos Aires for the Argentine leg of the 6–17 November tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Peña Before the Economic Crimes Unit7 November 2026

From Palace to House Arrest in a Day: Peña Faces Court as Alliana Takes Paraguay’s Helm

Paraguay’s abrupt change of power moved from parliament to the courts on Saturday. Santiago Peña, removed from the presidency after the Senate voted to convict him — 44 senators backed removal in the decisive vote, on the reports — spent his first night out of office under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust, and was due before the economic crimes unit during the day. Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in to serve out the term to August 2028. Prosecutors have three criminal cases open around the collapse of Ueno Bank, which froze accounts belonging to nearly half of the country’s more than six million people; an arrest order also names Carlos Carvallo, who ran the finance firm that became Ueno before Peña appointed him central bank chief in 2023. Lawmakers accused the ousted president of steering hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds into the bank while regulators overlooked risky insider lending — and chose impeachment over accepting his resignation precisely because conviction strips the lifetime Senate seat, and with it the parliamentary immunity, that a former president would otherwise carry. Peña is only the second Paraguayan president removed by impeachment since democracy returned in 1989; what happens in the courtroom now decides whether the episode ends as a political removal or becomes a criminal one as well.
