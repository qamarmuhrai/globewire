Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Negotiators Leave Doha, Draft Circulating2 November 2026

Iran's Team Leaves Doha With a Draft Deal Circulating — and Tehran Rules Out Handing Over Its 60% Enriched Uranium to Any Third Country

The talks have moved from the room to the page. Iranian negotiators departed Doha on Sunday after sessions with Qatari mediators aimed at ending the war with the United States, an official briefed on the discussions told Reuters on Monday — stressing the departure was not caused by difficulties — and a draft proposal is now being circulated, with a follow-up meeting possible. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi had been in Doha exchanging messages with Washington through the Qataris, and Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei confirmed the framework remains Iran's seven-step September plan, now compressed so the Strait of Hormuz could reopen and security be restored within two days if Tehran's conditions are met: an end to the war on all fronts, frozen assets released, oil sanctions lifted and the US naval blockade ended. But one door closed on Monday. Asked whether Iran would transfer its stockpile of 60%-enriched uranium to a third country to reach a deal, Baghaei answered with a single word: "No." China added its weight the same day, Foreign Minister Wang Yi telling senior Iranian security official Ali Bagheri Kani in Beijing that China supports Tehran in safeguarding its sovereignty and security. President Trump and Prime Minister Netanyahu discussed the state of the negotiations by phone on Saturday, and the fear in every capital is the same: that whatever restraint diplomacy has bought expires with Tuesday's US midterm vote.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Campaign Closes2 November 2026

Trump Closes at the Reflecting Pool as Every Final Measure Points One Way: Democrats +8 on the Generic Ballot, 92–94% for the House on Prediction Markets

The campaign is over; only the counting remains. President Trump delivered his closing message at Washington's Reflecting Pool on Monday afternoon, capping a final blitz through competitive states, while Democrats held their own get-out-the-vote rallies ahead of Tuesday's vote for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships. The final numbers describe a country that has largely made up its mind: the RealClearPolling average gives Democrats 51.4% to Republicans' 43.2% on the generic ballot, the last PBS News/NPR/Marist poll of likely voters has it 52% to 44%, and prediction markets price a Democratic House at 92% on Kalshi and 94% on Polymarket, with the Senate at 69–72%. More than 54 million ballots were already cast before election day — eight million more than at the same point in 2022, according to the University of Florida Election Lab — and the early voters break heavily Democratic. Trump's standing is the undertow: 36% approval in the New York Times tracker, the lowest for any president heading into a midterm in 80 years, with an AP-NORC poll finding just 25% of adults approve of his handling of the economy. The machinery is already being contested — a federal judge on Sunday dismissed a Justice Department lawsuit that sought to block certification of Alaska's Senate race if the Republican incumbent loses, and California's governor has warned the administration may try to seize ballots. First polls close at 6pm Eastern on Tuesday; the last, in Alaska, at 1am.

100 Kilometres Out and Slowing: Murasu Creeps Toward Ongole as IMD Warns Seawater Could Push 330 Metres Inland and a 2-Metre Surge Builds

The storm has slowed, and that is not necessarily mercy. Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu was centred about 100 km east of Nellore at 2:30pm on Monday, having decelerated from 27 kmph to about 10 kmph — a slower approach that prolongs the rain over the same coastline. The India Meteorological Department still expects landfall south of Ongole, between Nellore and Narsapur, between 11:30pm Monday and 4am Tuesday, with sustained winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph. The Cyclone Warning Centre at Visakhapatnam warns of a storm surge of 1.5 to 2 metres above the astronomical tide; the IMD projects seawater reaching up to 330 metres inland in Ongole mandal and 190 metres in Bapatla. Nineteen flights were cancelled across Vijayawada, Tirupati and Visakhapatnam airports, with 11 more at Chennai, schools in Chennai and Tiruvallur stayed shut, and Odisha pressed on with evacuations from low-lying districts. Red alerts are in force for two Telangana districts on Tuesday, with orange alerts in three more, as the system is expected to weaken into a depression and track northwest after crossing. Andhra Pradesh takes this hit less than a week after Cyclone Arnab killed seven people and caused an estimated Rs 4,600 crore in damage on 28 October — a state still counting one storm's cost as the next one arrives.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — First Day After the Truce2 November 2026

A Drone Hits a Kherson Medical Facility on the Truce's First Morning After — and Moscow Answers Kyiv's Rearming Charge by Assuming Kyiv Did the Same

The war's longest local pause is now a subject of mutual accusation. Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces used the six-day Oleshky humanitarian ceasefire — brokered with US First Lady Melania Trump's involvement and Pope Leo's participation, during which nearly 800 people were evacuated — to rotate troops, fortify positions and stockpile ammunition. Moscow's response, reported Monday, was not a denial so much as a shrug: it said it assumed Kyiv had done the same. Russia refused Ukraine's offer to extend the truce, which expired at 2100 GMT on Sunday, and by Monday morning a Russian drone had struck a medical facility in the centre of Kherson, opposite Oleshky across the Dnipro; a second drone hit the city's outskirts, wounding a 62-year-old woman and a 73-year-old man. Governor Oleksandr Prokudin counted shelling, drone and air attacks on 16 settlements in the 24 hours to early Monday. The wider war never paused at all: Russian strikes on energy sites in four regions left hundreds of thousands of homes without power over the weekend, more than 60,000 customers in Dnipropetrovsk were still waiting for reconnection by Sunday evening, and a strike in the Vinnytsia region killed two people, one of them a 13-year-old girl. Ukraine, for its part, said it hit a major missile-fuel producer in the Rostov region, damaging four production facilities. US-mediated talks, frozen since February, are expected to resume this month.

Latest · Global Affairs · Binance — The Morning After the Heist2 November 2026

$3.64 Billion Out the Door — 2.1% of Everything It Holds: Binance's Customers Deliver Their Verdict on the $6.3 Billion Heist, and Withdrawals Begin to Stabilise

The world's largest crypto exchange has survived its first full day back — at a measurable price. Binance said customers withdrew a net $3.64 billion, 2.1% of its assets, as withdrawals resumed in phases on Sunday after the theft of $6.3 billion from its hot wallets late on 29 October, a raid the FBI attributes to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit and analysts describe as possibly the biggest financial robbery ever. The exchange processed $4.65 billion of withdrawals against $1.01 billion of deposits between 06:00 and 15:30 GMT on Sunday, with all 15 networks in the first phase — bitcoin, ether, XRP, dogecoin and major stablecoins among them — live by 12:36 UTC, and said the initial spike was fading while deposit volumes stayed substantial. It has covered 95% of the stolen assets with company funds, buying replacement tokens, and insists no user lost money; about $3 billion of the stolen crypto was frozen by partner exchanges. Analytics firm DefiLlama put outflows higher, at $4.36 billion, a figure Binance disputes. The exchange, with 335 million registered users and $171 billion in assets as of 28 October, faces its next test in the proof-of-reserves update scheduled for Monday. The bulk of the theft itself took about 20 minutes. President Trump, asked about attribution, was noncommittal: the US is "not certain" North Korea was involved, he said, but "if it is, we'll talk to North Korea about it."

Latest · Europe · Cyberattack — The Freezers2 November 2026

Freezers Set to 50°C in the Night: A Second Coordinated Hack Overheats Supermarket Refrigeration in Seven European Countries, Three Weeks After France

Europe's grocers spent Monday throwing food away. In a suspected coordinated cyberattack overnight Sunday to Monday, hackers seized control of internet-connected refrigeration systems at more than 100 stores across Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland, switching units into defrost mode and driving temperatures as high as 50°C — 55°C at one ICA store on the Swedish island of Öland, and reportedly 70°C in some Belgian shops. Delhaize confirmed about 40 stores hit in Belgium and Luxembourg; Carrefour about 15 in Belgium; Ireland's SuperValu 13 of its 233 stores; at least 17 businesses in the Netherlands; single stores and small chains in Estonia and Finland. One Delhaize branch in Hoeilaart estimated its spoiled stock at €100,000 alone. The method matches the 12 October attack on France, where some 700 supermarkets were hit and fires broke out at several; the equipment again involves older remote-control systems, including units from Danish maker Danfoss. Sabcobel, which operates Delhaize's refrigeration, said its teams worked through the night disconnecting units from the network. No group has claimed either attack. EU agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen called it a deliberate disruption attempt against the EU food system; Britain's National Cyber Security Centre said it was working with partners, with no UK incidents reported.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Earned Settlement, Final Terms2 November 2026

Ten Years Standard, Three for Top Earners, Thirty for Small-Boat Arrivals — and Five Kept for Care Workers: Mahmood Lays the Final Settlement Rules Before Parliament

Britain's settlement system is now a ladder, and Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood set out every rung on Monday. Under the "earned settlement" rules laid before Parliament, most migrants will wait ten years before they can settle permanently, replacing the current five. The climb is priced by contribution: those earning above £125,000 can settle in three years; those earning above £50,271, along with doctors, nurses and teachers, in five. The backlash mattered — care workers, who faced ten years in the draft and up to fifteen in earlier proposals, keep a five-year route if they meet conditions on English, a clean record and national insurance payments, a concession Mahmood made after unions and Labour MPs revolted. The bottom rungs are punitive: people who breached immigration rules wait at least 20 years, and those who arrived by small boat or other clandestine routes face up to 30. "Settled status is one of the most valuable rights this country can offer," Mahmood said. "From today, it must be earned." The reforms answer the post-Brexit surge that took net migration to a record 944,000 before it fell back sharply — to 171,000 last year, from 331,000 the year before — and they land in a country where the politics of arrival now runs ahead of the numbers themselves.

Fifteen Power Stations in a Week: Drones Black Out Kassala State as Khartoum Takes Its Case Against Ethiopia to the Security Council and the African Union

Sudan's war has found the switch. Latest · Africa · Ebola — A Negative Test, a Growing Outbreak2 November 2026

Nigeria's Two Suspected Ebola Cases Test Negative — Twice — as the Bundibugyo Outbreak Passes 10,000 Cases in DR Congo and Two-Thirds of Pledged Funds Sit Undisbursed

For Nigeria, Monday brought the answer it needed. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed that samples from two suspected Ebola cases in the country's northeast tested negative after two rounds of laboratory testing; both patients had been isolated while contacts were traced. They were never likely to be the outbreak's edge — Nigeria's only confirmed case remains the 42-year-old Swiss woman who had travelled to Uganda and tested positive in Abuja on 23 October before returning to Switzerland with her husband on 26 October. But vigilance is the point, because the source outbreak is still growing: the Bundibugyo-strain epidemic centred on DR Congo has passed 10,000 cases, killed more than 5,000 people since it was declared in May, and reached Kenya, which has recorded two deaths among nine cases, while the Central African Republic has tightened border checks. The strain has no approved vaccine or treatment, though trials are under way to test whether the Ervebo vaccine protects against it. The money is lagging the virus: Africa CDC director-general Jean Kaseya warned on Monday that two-thirds of the international funding pledged for the response remains undisbursed, even as the outbreak spreads across four countries.
