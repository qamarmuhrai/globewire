Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash Protests29 October 2026

"Minister of Death": Madrid Protesters Clash With Police and Demand Puente Quit Over Crash That Killed 68

Anger over Wednesday's high-speed train collision near Urda spilled onto Madrid's streets on Wednesday night, when hundreds of demonstrators — broadcaster TVE put the crowd at about 1,500 — gathered outside the Transport Ministry on Paseo de la Castellana chanting for Transport Minister Óscar Puente and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez to resign, some branding Puente the "minister of death" and holding placards reading "Leave!", "Criminals" and "Corruption kills." Police used tear gas and batons after bottles and flares were thrown; no injuries or arrests were reported, and the protest, backed by far-right groups including Falange and Revuelta, broke up at about 21:30. The CSIF civil-service union also demanded Puente's resignation, accusing him of shifting blame onto an Adif traffic chief and saying it had warned in September about excessive hours at the state rail-infrastructure operator. Freshly reported audio from the control centre has sharpened the questions: recordings suggest a train was cleared to move even though another service was stalled ahead on the same line, after a signalling fault had halted traffic on a 63-kilometre stretch and manual control was taken over. Investigators have not established the cause. Railway drivers' union Semaf is pressing ahead with a strike on Thursday in defence of railway safety, rescue crews are still working through the wreckage, and Spain's three days of national mourning run to 31 October.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election Inquiry29 October 2026

Israel's Election Chief Orders Shin Bet Probe Into Suspected Foreign Funding as Deadlock Fight Turns Bitter

Central Elections Committee chairman Justice Noam Sohlberg on Thursday instructed Shin Bet chief David Zini to investigate what he called serious suspicions of improper foreign influence on Tuesday's election, asking for findings within a week. Committee sources said the allegations centre on suspected funding — linked in preliminary reporting to Qatar and Turkey, in part through channels previously associated with militant groups — intended to boost turnout in Arab communities, alongside reports of intercepted envelopes containing cash and voting slips for the Ra'am party. A committee source said the inquiry will not delay the final results or President Isaac Herzog's choice of a candidate to form a government, and Qatar's foreign ministry categorically denied interfering. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas welcomed the investigation and turned the accusation back on Benjamin Netanyahu, whose Likud claimed the election had been stolen and whose far-right partners Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben-Gvir demanded Arab party heads be interrogated or arrested. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of running an influence campaign to undermine the result, while a senior police official told Haaretz there were no indications of fraud and committee officials said there is no evidence of a systematic effort to compromise the vote. The row lands on top of a deadlocked count that left neither main bloc near the 61 seats needed to govern, with Arab parties at a record 19.

Latest · Middle East · Iran29 October 2026

Iran's Supreme Leader Calls for an End to "Neither War Nor Peace" as Washington Tests a Back Channel

Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has called for the country to move out of its current state of "neither war nor peace," a directive President Masoud Pezeshkian told his cabinet on Wednesday the government would pursue earnestly and with all its capacity, according to state news agency IRNA. Pezeshkian ordered ministers and senior officials to maintain full readiness for any scenario, use available resources to raise productivity and national strength, and build resilience against threats, sanctions and external pressure, insisting adversaries should not imagine they can weaken Iran's will or force surrender. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi briefed the cabinet on regional consultations, including visits by a Qatari delegation and Pakistan's army chief — the same mediation track on which Tehran this week dismissed Doha's latest seven-day draft as unacceptable ideas rather than an offer. The leadership's message coincides with a separate American outreach reported by The Wall Street Journal, in which Vice President JD Vance has written to Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei, with President Donald Trump's approval, to test whether a framework for renewed negotiations exists. No agreement to restart formal talks has been announced, and Washington continues to weigh possible fresh strikes after the 3 November midterm elections.

Latest · Americas · Pentagon29 October 2026

Hegseth Fires the Pentagon's Top Military Planner as Trump Weighs Renewed Strikes on Iran

Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has removed Army Lieutenant General Brett Sylvia as director of strategy, plans and policy on the Joint Staff — the post known as the J5, whose holder drafts and coordinates military plans for the chairman of the Joint Chiefs and the president — a defence official said on Wednesday. No reason was given and no immediate replacement named; the Joint Staff thanked Sylvia and his family for 32 years of service. He is the second officer Hegseth has forced out of the same job, after Lieutenant General Joseph McGee was made to retire last October, and the removal extends a long series of senior dismissals under Hegseth that has drawn criticism from lawmakers in both parties. The timing is sensitive: US Central Command has prepared options for strikes on Iran that could be executed if the White House authorises them after next week's midterm elections, and President Donald Trump has vowed to "finish the job" in the eight-month war while declining to rule out further action. A former Joint Chiefs official quoted in US reporting warned that this is not the moment to lose the J5.

Strike on a Gaza Economy-Ministry Worksite in Al-Mawasi Kills Four Staff and Wounds More Than 30

An Israeli strike on Wednesday evening hit a Ministry of Economy worksite in al-Mawasi, in the southern Gaza Strip, killing four civilian employees and wounding more than 30 people, at least six of them seriously, Gaza's Government Media Office said. The office said the staff were carrying out routine duties — inspecting incoming goods, checking their suitability and blocking drugs and other prohibited items — at a site in a densely populated area where tens of thousands of displaced Palestinians shelter in tents in a zone Israel long ago designated as safe. It condemned the bombing as an attack on the civilian institutions that keep daily life functioning and urged the ceasefire's mediators and guarantors to intervene. Funerals for the four men were held on Thursday. Other violence was reported across the Strip the same day, including artillery fire east of Gaza City and Jabaliya and a strike on an empty apartment block in Bureij camp; the Israeli military had not commented on the al-Mawasi reports in the accounts reviewed. Palestinian health authorities say more than 1,500 people have been killed in Gaza since the ceasefire took hold in October 2025.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine Truce & Diplomacy29 October 2026

Oleshky Truce Enters Its Third Day With No Violations Reported — and Trump Says a Putin–Zelenskyy Meeting Is Coming "Very Soon"

The localised ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky, in Ukraine's southern Kherson region, entered its third day on Thursday with Ukrainian and Russian human-rights officials both reporting no violations in its first 30 hours. The pause, brokered with the involvement of US First Lady Melania Trump and running from 27 October to 1 November, is meant to let aid convoys deliver food and medicine to the roughly 2,000 residents still in the devastated city and to open safe evacuation routes; demining of access roads has begun, and four American journalists from Newsmax have arrived as neutral observers to document where evacuees choose to go. Members of the bipartisan Congressional Ukraine Caucus welcomed the truce while warning that forcibly transferring civilians from occupied territory is a war crime, amid Ukrainian fears Russia could steer residents toward Russia or Crimea rather than Ukrainian-held ground. Fighting continued elsewhere, including a Russian drone attack on Kharkiv that wounded five people. Above the local truce, President Donald Trump told reporters after returning from Texas campaign rallies that a meeting between Presidents Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskyy would be set up "very soon" — which would be their first face-to-face encounter since the 2022 invasion — though he gave no date, venue or format, and neither capital has confirmed arrangements.

Latest · Europe · ECB & Markets29 October 2026

Lagarde Confirms She Will Leave the ECB Early in February as Markets Rally Ahead of an Expected Rate Hold

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde confirmed on Thursday, after months of speculation, that she will step down early and leave her post in February — reported as 7 February 2027 — a disclosure that landed hours before the ECB's latest policy decision. Markets widely expect the bank to pause its tightening cycle and hold the deposit rate at 2.50%, with attention on what Lagarde says about France's bond turmoil: the gap between French government yields and German Bunds stood near 153 basis points on Thursday after touching an almost 15-year high last week, a widening analysts say gives the ECB cover to avoid more aggressive hikes despite the energy-price surge from the Iran war. European stocks and bonds rallied in the meantime — the STOXX 600 rose about 0.5% in later updates after an early 0.11% gain to 644.80 — even as Nasdaq futures fell about 1% on Alphabet's heavy AI spending, which looked set to erase nearly $400 billion from its value. Shell lifted the energy sector with third-quarter net profit of $10.81 billion, its strongest since 2022, and bigger buybacks, while Airbus gained after holding its full-year forecasts. RBC strategists expect a hold now and a final rise to 2.75% in December, though money markets price as many as three further increases.

Latest · Europe · Hungary–US Energy29 October 2026

Post-Orbán Hungary Moves to Make the US Nuclear Deal Legally Binding as Its Foreign Minister Lands in Washington

Hungary's government has asked parliament to make legally binding the civilian nuclear cooperation agreement with the United States that Viktor Orbán's government signed in February, signalling that Prime Minister Péter Magyar — who ended Orbán's 16 years in power in April's election, despite Washington's open endorsement of his predecessor — intends to keep the deal. The bill, submitted as Foreign Minister Anita Orbán (no relation to the former premier) arrived in Washington to meet Secretary of State Marco Rubio and sign memorandums on energy and law-enforcement cooperation, would anchor an accord covering civilian nuclear energy including small modular reactors and encouraging Hungary to choose US technology and companies as its main suppliers for new projects. Approval is expected, since Magyar's government commands a two-thirds parliamentary majority. Budapest is also reviewing the long-delayed, Russian-led expansion of its Paks plant and sounding out alternatives to Rosatom, with a decision expected late this year or early in 2027; it previously agreed to buy American nuclear fuel and US spent-fuel storage technology for the Russian-built plant, partnered with Poland's Synthos Green Energy on SMR rights, and is lobbying to extend a US sanctions exemption for Russian oil and gas imports that expires on 21 November.

Latest · Europe · Poland Church Trial29 October 2026

Polish Bishop Convicted of Failing to Report Two Priests' Abuse of Minors in a Landmark Church Case

A court in Tarnów on Thursday convicted Bishop Andrzej Jeż of failing to notify authorities about the sexual abuse of minors by two priests in his diocese — the highest-profile case of its kind against a serving Catholic leader in Poland, and the first prosecution of its type since a 2017 law made reporting such crimes obligatory. Jeż, who pleaded not guilty and attributed the delay to ambiguous regulations and the circumstances of each case, was sentenced to two years of restriction of liberty, to be served through deductions from his salary; the verdict is not final. In one case he cited jurisdictional doubts because much of the alleged abuse occurred in Ukraine, and in the other said he long did not know any victims were under 15; reporting on the underlying cases describes several dozen victims, and one priest was ultimately stripped of the priesthood. The conviction deepens a run of abuse scandals — a bishop and an archbishop resigned over negligent handling in 2024 — that has eroded trust in the Church in one of Europe's most Catholic countries: pollster IBRiS found in 2025 that only 35% of Poles said they trust the Church, down from 58% in 2016.

US and Japan Agree a Missile Assembly Plant in Japan for the F-35's AMRAAM — Beijing Warns Against Harming Regional Peace

The United States and Japan have agreed to establish a facility in Japan for the final assembly and inspection of AIM-120 AMRAAM advanced medium-range air-to-air missiles under a joint effort dubbed "Operation Supercharge," Defence Minister Shinjirō Koizumi announced late on Wednesday in Cebu, Philippines, after meeting US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth on the sidelines of the ASEAN defence ministers' gathering. In Beijing on Thursday, Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun responded that US–Japan security cooperation should not damage regional peace and stability, be directed against other countries or harm their interests, and accused Japan's current government of repeatedly breaching international and domestic rules under the pretext of responding to threats.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia–Kenya Displacement29 October 2026

Up to 50,000 People Have Fled Clashes in Southern Ethiopia Into Kenya, UN Agencies Say, With Food the Most Urgent Need

Local authorities estimate that up to 50,000 people have crossed from southern Ethiopia into Kenya's Moyale area since movements picked up on 22 October — a sharp rise from the 36,000 recorded on Tuesday — and the figure remains fluid as registration continues and fresh crossings are reported, UNHCR's representative in Kenya, Fatima Mohammed-Cole, said. The flight follows clashes between forces of Ethiopia's Somali and Oromia regions around the jointly administered border city of Moyale, sparked on 20 October when a Somali regional flag was raised there; several people have died, and transport, markets and cross-border trade have been disrupted. Many of those arriving are women and children: some are staying with relatives or host communities, while others shelter in schools and churches. UN humanitarian officials list food as the most pressing need, followed by safe drinking water, emergency shelter, sanitation and health care; insecurity has forced some aid partners to suspend work, and a joint assessment mission is planned once conditions allow. UNHCR has deployed an emergency team to Moyale and is mobilising relief supplies for nearly 12,000 people initially, working with Kenya's Department of Refugee Services, county authorities, the Kenya Red Cross — which runs six health points and malnutrition screening — and partners including IOM, UNICEF and the World Food Programme, while looking for safer accommodation further from the border.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India Economy29 October 2026

India's Finance Ministry Sees 7.3% Growth for FY27 but Flags El Niño, Oil and Inflation Risks

India's finance ministry expects the economy to grow 7.3% in the fiscal year ending March 2027, its October economic review said — slightly above the central bank's 7.1% forecast — with its nowcasting model putting July–September growth at about 7.35%, down from 7.8% in the previous quarter but supported by industrial output, energy consumption, port traffic, GST collections and automobile sales. The ministry cautioned that near-term inflation hinges on food-supply management, global energy volatility and liquidity conditions: headline retail inflation accelerated to 5.54% in September from 4.83% in August, driven by food prices, and the review warns that an intensifying El Niño threatens soil moisture ahead of winter sowing. Whether the Reserve Bank of India's recent rate increase contains secondary price effects will depend on its active liquidity management, the ministry added. Pressure on the rupee persists from oil prices, portfolio outflows and global uncertainty, though foreign-exchange reserves of $721 billion as of 16 October provide 10.3 months of import cover, and recent RBI steps to supply dollars to state oil companies and curb speculation may damp volatility. The IMF has separately lifted its FY27 forecast for India to 7.0%.
