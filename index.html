Latest · Europe · Brussels — Summit Runs Into Friday26 November 2026

EU Leaders Wrestle Over €1.6 Trillion Budget and a €45 Billion Loan for Ukraine

European Union leaders met in Brussels on Thursday divided over the bloc’s proposed budget for 2028–2034, worth nearly €1.6 trillion, and over how to keep financing Ukraine. The European Commission wants to borrow €45 billion and lend it to Kyiv for 2027 — €25 billion for the armed forces and €20 billion for the wider economy — with repayment due only when Russia pays war reparations. Together with an expected €25 billion from non-EU partners including Britain, Norway, Japan, Switzerland and Canada, the package would close Ukraine’s roughly €70 billion funding gap for next year. Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Bulgaria object to all 27 members contributing, while Bulgaria’s prime minister said his country would back only the civilian portion. Germany and others have made shared participation a condition of any broader budget deal. Council President António Costa hopes the summit clears the way for a revised proposal and a final agreement at the next meeting on 17–18 December; the long-term budget also includes up to €100 billion for Ukraine over seven years, which diplomats say falls well short of Kyiv’s needs.

Latest · Europe · Odesa — 100,000 Tons of Grain Aboard26 November 2026

Ukraine Says Russia Struck Two Grain Ships Near Odesa Ports Overnight

Russia attacked two foreign cargo vessels carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain near Ukraine’s Black Sea port of Odesa overnight, Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on Thursday, without specifying where the grain was loaded or originated. Kyiv says shipments from the Odesa hub have all but halted since late July, when Moscow sharply stepped up strikes on grain terminals and merchant shipping; Russia maintains it does not target civilian sites. Before the escalation, the three ports around Odesa handled about 90 percent of Ukraine’s agricultural and metallurgical exports. Shipowners have since largely stayed away as insurance costs soared, and Kyiv has redirected farm exports to Danube River ports and rail routes across its land borders. Ukraine has exported 7.6 million tons of grain so far in the 2026/27 season, down from 11.5 million tons a year earlier, although rail data show grain wagons dispatched toward Odesa rose fourfold in mid-November, suggesting some cargo is still moving toward the coast.

Latest · Europe · Moscow — Warning After Libya and Baltic Strikes26 November 2026

Kremlin Accuses Ukraine of Trying to Widen the War to the Mediterranean and Baltic Seas

The Kremlin on Thursday accused Kyiv of attempting to spread the war beyond Ukraine, calling recent Ukrainian strikes far from the front line very dangerous. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov pointed to Sunday’s sinking of a Russian naval corvette in a Libyan port by Ukrainian maritime drones and a drone attack a day earlier on a Russian military-linked cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland. His remarks answered a joint statement by President Donald Trump and NATO chief Mark Rutte, issued after their Florida meeting on Wednesday, urging Russia to avoid steps that expand the war. Peskov said nobody wanted the conflict to widen but insisted it was Ukraine, not Russia, doing the widening, and warned the actions would concern coastal states and the NATO alliance as a whole. Libya condemned the attack in its waters, Italy told Kyiv it was wrong to strike Russian ships there, and Norway — a strong backer of Ukraine — urged caution around Baltic operations.

Latest · Africa · Cairo — 31 Dead, 21 Injured25 November 2026

Fuel Tanker Crash Sparks Deadly Inferno on Cairo’s Ring Road

At least 31 people were killed and 21 injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on Cairo’s Ring Road on Wednesday evening, sending flames across the busy highway in Egypt’s Giza governorate and engulfing surrounding vehicles. Footage from the scene showed a vast blaze and thick black smoke rising over the interchange as crowds gathered and emergency crews moved in; dozens of ambulances responded. Reports said the privately operated tanker lost control and struck a barrier before erupting, and that DNA testing was under way to identify victims. Firefighters later brought the fire under control, and authorities began investigating the tanker’s cargo and safety compliance. Egypt’s president offered condolences and ordered the highway cleared quickly. The crash is among the deadliest road disasters in Egypt in recent years.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Islamabad — Talks on 27–28 November26 November 2026

Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey to Hold Mecca Alliance Defence Talks in Islamabad

Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey will meet in Islamabad on Friday and Saturday to discuss their mutual defence agreement, two Pakistani sources said on Thursday, with the Saudi and Turkish foreign ministers travelling to Pakistan for the talks. The three countries signed the joint defence pact in Mecca in August, against the backdrop of the US–Israeli war against Iran and rising regional security concerns. The grouping brings together three of the Muslim world’s most consequential military powers: Turkey fields NATO’s second-largest armed forces, Saudi Arabia is one of the world’s biggest oil exporters and home to Islam’s holiest sites, and Pakistan is the only Muslim-majority state with nuclear weapons. The Islamabad session is expected to work through how the three would consult and respond under the agreement.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bogor — 325 Rescuers Deployed26 November 2026

Rescuers Battle Flooded Indonesian Gold Mine as Toll Rises and Miners Remain Trapped

Rescue teams in Indonesia’s West Java province pressed on through darkness, high water and slippery, narrow shafts on Thursday, searching for miners still trapped in a flooded illegal gold mine near Bogor, south of Jakarta. Heavy rain on Monday sent the Ciseureuh River overflowing into the excavation, which runs about 100 metres deep in the mountainous Pongkor area; eleven miners escaped from one section before the water filled the shaft. By Thursday, 16 bodies had been recovered and about 10 miners were still believed trapped, with some 325 rescuers working two adjacent shafts using ropes and pumps. Officials said the alarm was raised only about 16 hours after the flooding, because the digging was unauthorised and local miners first tried to pump the water out themselves. State miner Antam, which operates a licensed gold mine nearby, said the disaster occurred at an unauthorised site unconnected to its operations and that all its own employees were safe.

Latest · Global Affairs · Trade — China Faces Extra 20 Percent26 November 2026

Trump’s New Tariffs Put China at the Centre of a Bid to Reorder Global Trade

Washington’s latest tariff round is aimed squarely at Beijing, analysts say, using pressure on third countries to prise supply chains away from China. The administration plans to raise tariffs on Chinese goods by a further 20 percent, on top of 25 percent levies dating from President Donald Trump’s first term — the ceiling agreed under the current truce, which expires on 10 January ahead of a fourth leaders’ summit set for Miami next month. The measures follow an eight-month investigation into excess industrial capacity, and US reports on partners such as Vietnam and the European Union mention China hundreds of times, criticising them for doing too little to curb Chinese imports and investment. Beijing rejected the accusations of unfair industrial practice but stopped short of threatening retaliation, and this week extended its suspension of rare-earth export controls while a Chinese airline agreed to buy 50 Boeing jets — signs the truce is holding. Economists warn, however, that the new levies hand Washington fresh ammunition if the trade war reignites, and that tariff and non-tariff barriers worldwide are likely to multiply.

Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — Case 4000 in Doubt25 November 2026

Israeli Judges Urge Prosecutors to Drop the Main Bribery Case Against Netanyahu

Judges hearing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s corruption trial recommended on Wednesday that prosecutors drop Case 4000, the most serious of the three cases against him, and reconsider another. In Case 4000, prosecutors allege Netanyahu granted regulatory favours worth around 1.8 billion shekels (about $500 million) to Bezeq, Israel’s largest telecom company, in exchange for favourable coverage on the Walla news site, both then controlled by Shaul Elovitch. Presiding Judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman told Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara, who attended the hearing in a rare move, that a conviction looked unlikely and the case should be dropped; the panel also questioned whether the conduct alleged in Case 2000 — a proposed bargain over newspaper coverage — amounts to a criminal offence, and suggested a deal be explored in the gifts case, Case 1000. The court stressed its remarks were preliminary impressions, not a ruling, and applied to Netanyahu alone. Baharav-Miara said she would review the recommendations with prosecutors. It was the third time the judges had urged the bribery charge be reconsidered since 2023; Netanyahu, whose trial began in 2020, denies wrongdoing.

Latest · Americas · Technology — Full Network Due by 204025 November 2026

FCC Clears SpaceX to Deploy One Million Satellites as Data Centres in Orbit

The US Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday authorised Elon Musk’s SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites that would work as data centres in space — a first-of-its-kind approval in the United States. The constellation, called Starmind, would place vast computing capacity in orbit to process data for use on Earth, which the FCC said would help meet rapidly growing demand for artificial-intelligence computing; its chairman hailed the decision as a step into an era of “super intelligence.” Under the licence, SpaceX must put at least one satellite in place by November 2033, 100,000 by 2035, half the fleet by 2038 and all one million by 2040, and the company has said it could begin demonstrating the technology in 2027. The approval came over objections from Amazon, satellite operator Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, and some scientists voiced scepticism and alarm about the scale of the plan and its effect on the night sky. The FCC said the system complies with its orbital-debris rules.

Latest · Global Affairs · Sport — Under 0.1 Percent of Data Affected26 November 2026

FIFA Confirms Cyberattack Exposed a Limited Set of Personal and Business Data

FIFA said on Thursday that it had fallen victim to a criminal cyberattack that gave unauthorised access to personal and business information. World football’s governing body condemned the unlawful acquisition and publication of the material as a serious violation of privacy, but did not say how many people or organisations were affected, when the intrusion happened, or where any data may have appeared. A person with direct knowledge of the matter said less than 0.1 percent of the data FIFA holds was affected, and that no files from the abandoned FIFA Forward Enterprise project were accessed. That project was at the centre of a plan, scrapped by President Gianni Infantino in July after a widespread backlash, to raise up to $4.2 billion by selling a 20 percent stake in a new unit that would run FIFA events, including the World Cup; FIFA said files connected to that selloff were not touched in the breach.
