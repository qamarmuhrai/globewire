Latest · Americas · US Politics — Miller Takes Over7 November 2026

Defeat, Then Double Down: Trump Names Stephen Miller Chief of Staff After Midterm Rout

Three days after Republicans lost control of both the House and the Senate, President Trump has chosen confrontation over retreat. In a Truth Social post on Friday he announced that Stephen Miller, 41, his deputy chief of staff and the architect of the administration’s hardline immigration and deportation campaign, will become White House chief of staff on Monday, 9 November. Susie Wiles, the first woman to hold the job and the organiser of Trump’s three presidential campaigns, resigned on Thursday evening and will move to the Trump Political Operation; Trump thanked her for what he called an outstanding job, barely three weeks after insisting she would stay “for a long time”. Reporting on Saturday described a president furious at the strategy behind the losses, urged on by allies including Steve Bannon who had targeted Wiles, and determined to signal that the agenda voters rejected will not soften. Senate Democrat Chuck Schumer said the appointment showed Trump had learned nothing from the defeat. With Democrats about to run Congress, Miller’s elevation — he is privately nicknamed “the prime minister” by allies and critics alike for his reach across domestic policy — points to two years of sharper fights over immigration, executive power and legislation, starting with the near-certain collapse of any early hope of a comprehensive immigration deal.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — Toll Rises to 22 Near Sanaa7 November 2026

52 Dead in Two Days: Yemen’s Dormant War Returns with Strikes on a Village and a Market

Yemen’s revived war produced its bloodiest two days since fighting resumed in July. Houthi officials said on Saturday that air strikes on the village of al-Shariya, in the Bani Hashish district of Sanaa province near the capital, killed 22 people and wounded 30, most of them women and children, after five houses were destroyed. The Houthi-run Saba news agency said rescue teams were still pulling victims from rubble and accused the aircraft of striking paramedics twice as they worked; a health ministry spokesman, Anees Alasbahi, blamed Saudi Arabia, which had not commented by Saturday evening. The attack followed Friday’s strike on al-Hadara market in the al-Waziyah district of Taiz province in the south-west, which Houthi health officials said killed 30 people and injured eight — a toll the two sides blamed on each other, with neither account independently verified. Saudi Arabia, meanwhile, said its air defences intercepted a Houthi cruise missile over Riyadh. Taken together, the reported deaths reach at least 52 in 48 hours, a scale of civilian loss that underlines how completely the years of relative calm between the Iran-backed Houthis and the Saudi-backed government have collapsed — and how Yemen has again become an active front in the wider regional war.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — 11 Ships Attacked in a Week7 November 2026

Three Ships Sunk, Traffic Down 65%: Hormuz’s Numbers Explain Why Oil Still Fell Below $100

The shipping data behind the Qeshm strikes is now clearer, and it cuts two ways. Al Jazeera, citing the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency, reported that at least 11 vessels were attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz in the week ending 6 November, three of them sinking, with 88 projectile strikes recorded since hostilities resumed in early July — and a warning that most attacks go unreported. Transits through the strait, at 170 outbound and 155 inbound in that week, remain roughly 65 per cent below pre-conflict levels. A seafarer was killed on 5 November in an explosion aboard the Marshall Islands-flagged products tanker MT Velos Sapphire in the Gulf, believed to have been a missile strike. Yet markets spent the week betting on diplomacy, not escalation: Brent crude settled on Friday at $98.32 a barrel, up just 3 cents on the day but down more than 16 per cent on the week — its steepest weekly fall since the pandemic era on some measures — while US West Texas Intermediate closed at $90.83. Qatar has floated a two-day “bridge deal” to lower tensions and open wider nuclear talks, and regular futures trading is closed for the weekend. The contradiction is the story: the waterway is measurably more dangerous, and the price says traders think a deal is closer than the strike reports suggest.

Latest · Europe · Security — Third Day of Hoaxes7 November 2026

Fighters Escort Airliners, Newsrooms Evacuate: Europe’s Bomb-Hoax Wave Reaches Day Three

A wave of hoax bomb threats that began on Tuesday widened on Friday from aircraft to the media itself. Broadcasters in three countries — Ireland’s RTÉ, which evacuated its Dublin headquarters and suspended programming, Britain’s Sky and Bulgaria’s Nova TV — received emailed threats from a purported sender calling himself Mikhail Andropov and warning of further attacks on European transport; Reuters could not establish who sent them or whether they came from the same source as the flight threats. In the air, disruption eased compared with Thursday’s two dozen affected flights but remained serious: a Wizz Air service from Prague to Rome diverted to Venice, Czech police responded to threats involving nine flights to or from Prague, and Hungarian fighter jets escorted three airliners through Hungarian airspace on NATO orders — the alliance’s first reported involvement. Earlier in the week, Italian Eurofighters had escorted a Ryanair flight into Rome, Warsaw’s airports were disrupted, and Dutch military police detained a man at Amsterdam’s Schiphol over a threat to a Prague-bound flight. No device has been found on any aircraft and nobody has been injured. Spain’s foreign minister, José Manuel Albares, called the pattern an apparent attempt at hybrid aggression and psychological warfare; Italy’s prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, warned that civilian infrastructure faces growing threats. No government has attributed the campaign, and security experts will meet in Brussels next week to coordinate a response.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 36 Ballistic Missiles Overnight7 November 2026

Nine Wounded in Kyiv, a Bridge Closed, 190 Battles on the Front: the Count Before Abu Dhabi

Saturday brought fuller numbers for the overnight assault on Ukraine, three days before US-led talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. President Zelenskyy said Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and its region alongside 172 drones across the capital and 12 other regions, wounding nine people including a child; Ukraine’s air force said defences destroyed or jammed four missiles and 149 drones, with hits or falling debris recorded at 34 locations. Homes, commercial property and a kindergarten were damaged, fires broke out in the Solomianskyi district, and officials closed the Northern Bridge over the Dnipro after Russia’s defence ministry confirmed it had targeted the crossing as a military transport route. Separately, rescuers in Komyshuvakha recovered nine more bodies, taking the death toll from an earlier strike on an aid centre to 55, while a Ukrainian drone strike killed one person in Russia’s Belgorod region and Moscow claimed 411 Ukrainian drones downed overnight. On the ground, Ukraine’s general staff counted 190 combat engagements on 6 November, the fiercest in the Kostiantynivka sector, alongside 75 Russian airstrikes and 261 guided bombs. Zelenskyy also warned European partners, in a CNN interview, that discussing a new continental security framework before ending the war would be dangerous — even as he confirmed Ukraine backs a ceasefire it says Moscow has shown no sign of accepting.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — First Winter Storm7 November 2026

70,000 Tents Under Water in 12 Hours: Gaza’s First Winter Storm Overwhelms the Camps

The first winter storm of the season turned Gaza’s displacement camps into flood zones overnight. The territory’s Government Media Office said rainwater inundated some 70,000 tents within 12 hours, with initial losses estimated at $3 million, while the civil defence agency logged about 1,500 distress calls in 10 hours as water rose 30 to 40 centimetres in several shelter sites. Footage from Gaza City, Khan Younis, Deir el-Balah, Nuseirat and Jabalia showed families wading through ankle-deep water mixed with mud and sewage, dragging soaked mattresses and bedding out of collapsed shelters; in northern Gaza, two people were injured when a wall of a war-damaged building gave way, and in Gaza City’s Zeitoun a roof collapse left a person trapped. The exposure was predictable: field surveys cited by officials indicate more than 162,000 of roughly 270,000 displacement tents are already severely damaged or unusable, UN figures put nearly 950,000 displaced people in areas of moderate or high flood risk, and in al-Mawasi alone more than 900,000 people are sheltering near a coast where officials warned 125,000 families risked having tents swept away. Gaza’s emergency committee says the territory urgently needs about 200,000 weather-resistant tents plus heating supplies — a request that existed before the rain, and that the storm has now converted from a warning into a count of what is already lost.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 99% Counted7 November 2026

99% In and 128 Councils Hung: South Africa’s Count Ends, the Coalition Era Begins

South Africa’s municipal count moved from near-complete to effectively decided during Saturday. Live tallies drawing on Electoral Commission data showed 99.0 per cent of voting districts counted, 7,784 of 8,825 council seats declared and more than 29.2 million valid votes recorded: the ANC on 30.9 per cent with 3,147 seats, the Democratic Alliance on 24.6 per cent with 1,474, the MK Party on 9.6 per cent with 696 and the EFF on 6.3 per cent with 495 — confirming the ANC’s fall from 45.6 per cent in 2021 as the defining result. The number of councils with no outright winner kept climbing as the last districts certified, reaching 128 finished hung councils on one live count, with 109 of 175 certified councils hung in another Saturday snapshot. In the metros, the DA leads Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni without majorities, and in Nelson Mandela Bay it took 53 of 120 seats, eight short of governing alone. The political reckoning started before the declaration: EFF leader Julius Malema, whose party lost more than 600,000 votes and finished with no leading councils, blamed an international smear campaign and the lobby group AfriForum, while conceding the scale of the setback. The commission is scheduled to declare the official results on Sunday evening, 8 November; councils then have fourteen days to elect mayors and speakers, which is when these percentages become governments.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Diplomacy — Shenzhen, 11 Days Out7 November 2026

Still Undecided in Beijing: What Is Known — and Not Known — About the Xi–Trump–Putin Plan

With eleven days left before the APEC leaders’ summit opens in Shenzhen on 18–19 November, the proposed first-ever meeting of Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin remains a plan without a confirmation. What is established: China’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Wednesday that Beijing “is in communication with relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state” — the first public acknowledgement; Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said a day earlier that work is under way and he hopes it will be finalised within days; and Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov has said Putin and US envoy Steve Witkoff discussed the idea in St Petersburg on 21 October, after Putin raised a three-way format in a 9 October phone call with Trump. What is not established: whether Beijing has decided to proceed at all, whether Putin will attend APEC, and what would be discussed — Trump has spoken of nuclear arms talks, while German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier says Xi told him Ukraine would not be on the table because Beijing treats it as a European conflict. Trump is separately expected in Shenzhen for his third meeting with Xi this year, with the US–China trade truce expiring in January. Analysts quoted across the coverage expect choreography more than substance if the three do sit down — but the absence of a decision, this close to the summit, is itself now part of the diplomacy.

Latest · Americas · Health — The Two Death Counts7 November 2026

Eight Deaths by State Counts, Four by the CDC’s: the Measles Gap Behind the Wisconsin Infant

The human detail behind the US measles milestone is a counting dispute that now has names and dates attached. Wisconsin’s Department of Health Services announced on Thursday that an infant in the south-west of the state had died, with a coroner listing measles as the immediate cause — the state’s first measles death since 1990, in a state that has recorded 431 cases across 13 counties and nine hospitalisations. It was the third state-reported measles death in a single week: Pennsylvania confirmed its sixth death of the year on 4 November, and New York announced its first in 30 years on 2 November, taking the state tally for 2026 to eight. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, however, lists four deaths, and the gap is methodological: under a policy shift this year the CDC now builds its toll from National Center for Health Statistics mortality data rather than automatically adopting state confirmations, and says figures will be updated as reviews are completed. The case count is not in dispute — 5,182 confirmed infections as of 5 November, the highest since 1991, more than double 2025’s total. On Tuesday, 10 November, the Pan American Health Organization reviews whether the United States retains the measles-elimination status it has held since 2000 — a decision that will be taken while the country cannot yet agree with itself on how many people the disease has killed.

Latest · Europe · Technology — Cranes, Flares, 200 Detained7 November 2026

Up the Crane with Flares: Amsterdam’s Data-Centre Occupation Opens a 16-Country Weekend

The backlash against the AI building boom turned physical in Amsterdam on Friday. More than 100 protesters — organisers put the wider turnout far higher — broke into the construction site of a data centre in the Sloterdijk district that is being built by Pure Data Centres, a project of more than $1 billion for a 78-megawatt facility reported to be leased entirely to Microsoft. Demonstrators climbed a crane, set off flares, hung banners and danced inside the perimeter while police stood by, before officers moved in; Dutch authorities detained between 150 and 200 people on suspicion of trespassing. The occupation, led by the collective Geef Tegengas — “Push Back” — with Fossielvrij and Extinction Rebellion, opened two days of action by the Stop Dirty Data Centres coalition across some 15 to 16 countries, including a march in Dublin, a protest outside parliament in Warsaw and a demonstration at another Microsoft site in Denmark. The groups are demanding a moratorium on large new facilities until governments set clearer rules, citing electricity and water consumption; the Amsterdam group argues the finished campus would draw power on the scale of the city’s households. Microsoft said it respects peaceful expression but does not condone unlawful disruption, and site owner Pure Data Centres said it remains committed to engaging local stakeholders. The Netherlands alone has about 200 data centres planned, under construction or operating.

“No Doubt” He Would Come: Leo Celebrates Mass in Paysandú on the Fullest Day of His Uruguay Visit

Pope Leo XIV’s first visit to Latin America reached its busiest day on Saturday. The 71-year-old pontiff flew in the morning to Paysandú, the cultural and economic hub on Uruguay’s border with Argentina, to celebrate Mass at Parque París Londres, with pilgrims crossing from Colón in Argentina among the crowds expected. He returns to Montevideo for an afternoon with the social community of Casavalle, one of the capital’s poorest neighbourhoods, at the parish of Our Lady of Guadalupe, a meeting at the Catholic University of Uruguay, and a 7 p.m. prayer vigil at the Estadio Centenario, the stadium that hosted the first World Cup final. The journey itself is part of the story: Leo arrived on Friday after a 13-hour flight from Rome, barely three weeks after surgery to remove a lung nodule confirmed as cancer, telling journalists aboard that he was “convinced that this trip had to go forward” and had never doubted he would make it. Friday’s address to Uruguay’s authorities praised Mercosur, peaceful coexistence and care for the environment, while defending life in the womb and questioning euthanasia in Latin America’s most secular country. On Sunday he celebrates Mass in Florida before the shrine of Our Lady of the Thirty-Three, Uruguay’s patroness, then flies on to Buenos Aires for the Argentine leg of the 6–17 November tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Peña Before the Economic Crimes Unit7 November 2026

From Palace to House Arrest in a Day: Peña Faces Court as Alliana Takes Paraguay’s Helm

Paraguay’s abrupt change of power moved from parliament to the courts on Saturday. Santiago Peña, removed from the presidency after the Senate voted to convict him — 44 senators backed removal in the decisive vote, on the reports — spent his first night out of office under house arrest at his residence in Asunción on suspicion of breach of trust, and was due before the economic crimes unit during the day, prosecutor Silvia Margarita González Vester told reporters. Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in to serve out the term to August 2028. Prosecutors have three criminal cases open around the collapse of Ueno Bank, which froze accounts belonging to nearly half of the country’s more than six million people; an arrest order also names Carlos Carvallo, who ran the finance firm that became Ueno before Peña appointed him central bank chief in 2023. Lawmakers accused the ousted president of steering hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds into the bank while regulators overlooked risky insider lending — and chose impeachment over accepting his resignation precisely because conviction strips the lifetime Senate seat, and with it the parliamentary immunity, that a former president would otherwise carry. Peña is only the second Paraguayan president removed by impeachment since democracy returned in 1989; what happens in the courtroom now decides whether the episode ends as a political removal or becomes a criminal one as well.
