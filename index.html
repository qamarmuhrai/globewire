Latest · Middle East · Iran — Radar Claim and Hormuz Tanker13 November 2026

Iran’s Guards Say They Hit a US Early-Warning Radar in Bahrain, as an LNG Tanker Strikes a Mine in the Strait of Hormuz

The confrontation around the Gulf sharpened on Friday. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted an American early-warning radar installation in Bahrain — the first claim of an attack on US military assets in a Gulf state since early September — describing it as retaliation for an air attack on a telecommunications tower in Sirik county, in Iran’s southern Hormozgan province. Neither Washington nor Manama had commented on the claim. Hours later, the Guards’ navy said a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas had struck a naval mine and exploded while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on what it called an unauthorised and dangerous route; no details of the vessel were given. Shipping data cited by analysts counts at least 10 vessels hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October — the highest monthly tally since the conflict began in late February. In Washington, President Donald Trump told an interviewer he would have “no hesitation” about resuming strikes on Iran if the situation turned, saying fresh action was possible “right now” — days after he had called renewed strikes unlikely.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Nasser Hospital Strike13 November 2026

Six Killed, Including a Surgeon and a Boy of 13, as an Israeli Strike Hits the Surgical Wing of Gaza’s Nasser Hospital

An Israeli airstrike on the main hospital in southern Gaza killed at least six Palestinians overnight, among them surgeon Rezik Abushira and a 13-year-old boy, and wounded more than two dozen others, medics and the World Health Organization said. Two missiles tore through the roof of the five-storey surgery building at Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, knocking out a department that serves roughly half of Gaza’s two million people. Israel’s military said the strike targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, whom it identified as the commander of Hamas’s Khan Younis brigade, and accused the group of operating from inside the complex — a claim Hamas denies; the commander was not among those listed as killed. The UN’s acting Middle East coordinator said he was deeply alarmed, and hospital officials noted the complex has now been struck at least seven times since the war began. The attack came despite the ceasefire that took effect in October 2025, with near-daily strikes reported since.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Talks11 November 2026

Russia and Ukraine Agree Their Biggest Prisoner Exchange Yet in Abu Dhabi, as US Envoys Carry the Talks to Moscow and Dublin

Two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi — the first direct meeting between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in nine months — ended with agreement on what American envoy Jared Kushner called the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners of the war, with the final numbers still being settled and the exchange expected within two to three weeks. Russia’s chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky and Ukraine’s Rustem Umerov both confirmed the deal, which also covered the return of detained civilians and children, and negotiators reported progress on how a future ceasefire could be monitored. Territory, however, remains the hardest unresolved question, and Kushner acknowledged there is still no trust between the sides. He and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff were due in Moscow to brief President Vladimir Putin, then in Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskyy alongside European leaders; Zelenskyy has welcomed bringing Europe into the process, arguing it fits Ukraine’s ambition to join the EU.

Latest · Europe · Baltic Sea — Warship Strike12 November 2026

Finland Detects a Strike on a Russian Warship in the Gulf of Finland as Ukraine Claims Its First Baltic Naval Hit

Ukraine’s SBU security service says its drones struck the Naro-Fominsk, a Buyan-M-class missile corvette of Russia’s Baltic Fleet armed with Kalibr and Oniks cruise missiles, in the Gulf of Finland in the early hours of Wednesday — in what is thought to be the first Ukrainian attack on a Russian warship in open Baltic waters. Finland’s armed forces confirmed they had detected a strike on a Russian warship, with a senior official telling broadcaster Yle that sensors recorded flashes and smoke near Russian-held Gogland Island, about 40 kilometres off the Finnish coast. Estonia’s defence minister Martin Herem reported an explosion aboard a Russian vessel at about 5:30 a.m. but said Tallinn could not yet say whether it was an attack or an accident, nor confirm it was the same ship. The strike formed part of a wider overnight drone operation against the Ust-Luga oil export terminal, where Ukraine’s general staff said five storage tanks were damaged; Moscow has not commented, and the extent of damage to the corvette is unknown.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — Pacific Drills13 November 2026

China Massed More Than 100 Vessels in Unannounced Pacific Drills Around Taiwan, in What Taipei Calls “Silent Intimidation”

China carried out a large, unannounced military exercise across the western Pacific and around Taiwan in early November, a senior Taiwan security official has told Reuters, describing the deployment as a campaign of silent intimidation. At its height, Chinese naval and coast guard forces massed more than 100 vessels — a deployment larger than Beijing’s last formally named anti-Taiwan drills nearly a year ago and among the largest it has ever mounted — rehearsing simulated attacks and, officials say, broader operations drawing on all three of China’s theatre commands, suggesting practice for a blockade and beyond. A Chinese submarine also test-launched an intercontinental ballistic missile from the Yellow Sea into the mainland during the exercise. The fleet has since thinned to around 70 vessels inside the first island chain. The drills come days before the APEC summit in Shenzhen, where Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping are expected to meet for the third time this year; China’s defence ministry did not respond to requests for comment.

Latest · Europe · Dublin — First EU AI Summit13 November 2026

EU Leaders Hold Their First Summit Devoted Entirely to AI in Dublin, With Ukraine Forcing Its Way Onto the Agenda

The European Union’s 27 leaders gathered in Dublin on Friday for their first-ever meeting dedicated solely to artificial intelligence, debating how the bloc can close the gap with the United States and China on funding, infrastructure and talent while setting guardrails for a technology that has alarmed governments after a series of incidents involving rogue AI agents. European Council President António Costa convened the informal summit, hosted by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, with briefings from Mistral AI chief executive Arthur Mensch on competitiveness and AI sovereignty, and from Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton on the technology’s impact on society, security and trust. Ukraine was added as a second major topic after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner flew in to brief leaders and meet President Zelenskyy following their Moscow talks, putting Europe’s role in any peace settlement — and the security guarantees it could offer — at the centre of the afternoon’s discussion.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Outage Reaches Canberra13 November 2026

Australia’s Spy Agency and Federal Police Were Caught in the Microsoft Outage, Triggering a Priority-Restoration Deal

The global Microsoft failure that knocked out Outlook, Teams and Microsoft 365 across 74 countries also hit the core of Australia’s security establishment. ASIO Director-General Mike Burgess and Federal Police Commissioner Krissy Barrett confirmed at a joint news conference on Friday that their organisations were affected, and Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said Canberra had activated an agreement guaranteeing Australia priority in the restoration of services. Burke said Microsoft had assured him the outage was not caused by a cyberattack — an account the company has repeated, blaming servers that failed automated health checks and left remaining capacity overloaded. The Australian impact underlines how deeply government business now runs on a single vendor’s cloud: while consumer email complaints dominated outage trackers overnight, officials in several countries spent Friday establishing which public services had quietly gone down with it.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Nvidia Investigation13 November 2026

A Day After Resigning, Former Nvidia Chief Jensen Huang Agrees to Testify Before Congress Over an Alleged China Intelligence Link

The fallout from Jensen Huang’s abrupt resignation as Nvidia’s chief executive has moved to Capitol Hill. Huang has tentatively agreed to give closed-door testimony to House committees investigating his reported relationship with Alice Huang, a businesswoman US officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence and of using the connection to move export-restricted Nvidia chips to China — an allegation Beijing denies and on which the Justice Department says it has reached no conclusion. Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast said the agreement was reached on Wednesday night, hours before the resignation was announced, and warned a subpoena would follow if Huang withdrew. Three House panels had jointly requested his appearance on 4 November; in the Senate, Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott has also summoned Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose department cleared Nvidia chip sales to China, to testify in December. Lawmakers from both parties say the inquiry now reaches beyond one executive to the security controls around America’s most important AI hardware.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Air Pollution13 November 2026

Delhi Overtakes Lahore as the World’s Most Polluted City, With Air Quality in the “Severe” Band for the First Time This Season

India’s capital woke under thick smog on Friday and, by the measures that rank the world’s big cities, briefly held the worst air on the planet. Swiss monitoring group IQAir put Delhi’s air quality index at 334 — hazardous, and enough to push it past Lahore in Pakistan to the top of its global table — while India’s Central Pollution Control Board recorded a higher reading of 403 and classified the air as severe, the first time this season the citywide average has entered that band. The two numbers differ because the systems use different scales, but both describe the same reality for Delhi’s 20 million residents: visibility down, traffic crawling through haze, and fine-particle levels many times above the threshold of 50 that is considered good. The spike follows the seasonal pattern of crop-residue burning, vehicle emissions and cooling weather trapping pollutants over the Indo-Gangetic plain, with readings at individual stations running higher still.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal — Makwanpur Crash12 November 2026

Nepal Confirms 14 Dead, Six of Them From One Family, After an Electric Microbus Plunged 500 Metres Off a Mountain Road

All 14 victims of Thursday’s crash in central Nepal have now been identified, police said, after an electric microbus carrying families home for the Chhath festival left the road at Chisapani Gadhi in Makwanpur district and fell about 500 metres down a steep slope. The vehicle, travelling from Kathmandu to Sarlahi with 20 people aboard, went off the Bhimphedi–Kulekhani road at around 5:45 a.m.; six passengers were injured and taken for treatment. Among the dead were Punam Devi Yadav, 35, and her daughters Anu, 10, Diksha, 6, and Deepika, 5 — six members of one extended family in total — and most of the victims came from a single village in Sarlahi district. A 14-month-old boy was pulled from the wreckage as his family’s only survivor. District officials have formed a five-member committee to establish within a week why the vehicle left the road, with overloading and driver fatigue among the questions investigators are examining.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria — World Bank Financing6 November 2026

World Bank Adds $500 Million to Restore 1.4 Million Hectares of Degraded Land Across Northern Nigeria

The World Bank has approved a further $500 million for Nigeria’s Agro-Climatic Resilience in Semi-Arid Landscapes programme, taking total financing to $1.2 billion and extending the project to 2031. The programme works across the 19 northern states and the Federal Capital Territory, rehabilitating watersheds, tackling erosion and flooding, and supporting farming livelihoods in regions where land degradation and water scarcity drive both poverty and local conflict. The bank says the expanded phase aims to bring 1.4 million hectares under restoration and reach 6.6 million people; since 2022 the project has already placed about 550,000 hectares under sustainable management and benefited more than 4.4 million people. The approval lands amid continued scrutiny of Nigeria’s borrowing: figures from the Debt Management Office show the country’s debt to the World Bank rose by more than $6 billion between March 2023 and June 2026, to $20.73 billion — roughly 38 percent of its external public debt.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia — Bushland Rescue12 November 2026

Found Alive After Six Days in the Victorian Bush: A 17-Year-Old Survived on Creek Water and a Bag of Chips

A six-day search in dense bushland east of Melbourne ended in the outcome rescuers had begun to fear they would not get. William, 17, who has level 2 autism and had never camped before, wandered from a campsite near Walhalla on 6 November and survived alone in rugged country on sips of creek water and a family-sized bag of chips that he rationed across two days, wrapping himself in towels against the cold. On Thursday morning a Victoria Police helicopter crew, zooming in on a fishing tackle box spotted in the scrub about two kilometres from where he was last seen, found him lying on a flat rock beside a creek; footage shows him pumping his fists as the aircraft hovered overhead. A rescuer was winched down to lift him out, and he was reunited with his mother, Elizabeth Barkley, before being taken to hospital dehydrated, sunburnt and with low blood pressure — but, police said, in good spirits. His mother’s verdict, delivered through tears: he is now grounded for life.
