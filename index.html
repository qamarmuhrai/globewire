Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Tankers Ablaze2 November 2026

Two Tankers Catch Fire After Striking Mines in the Strait of Hormuz, as Trump Says the Iran War Will End Very Soon, One Way or the Other

The waterway at the centre of the war produced its most dangerous incident in weeks overnight. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two crude oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on what it called an unauthorised route, hours after Gulf states condemned Friday's attack on a Qatari LNG carrier and a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile. In Doha, Iranian negotiators spent a third consecutive day with Qatari mediators on a plan that Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says could fully reopen the strait within two days of an agreement — the United States would act on the first day, and Iran would restore normal navigation on the second. No conclusion has been reached. President Donald Trump, who has said there will be no new US attacks before Tuesday's midterm elections, repeated that the war will end very soon, warning Tehran to make a deal or face the alternative, and said he had a good talk with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — who, US and Israeli officials told Axios, is pressing for strikes immediately after the vote, while Trump prefers to let talks run. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said no decision on new strikes has been taken. Tehran's warnings hardened in parallel: Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered an updated target list and said any attack would draw an immediate response without geographic limits, and an army spokesman warned a new war could last longer and spread further than the last. The UAE's presidential adviser Anwar Gargash, speaking in Bahrain, said military options offer no quick exit and voiced hope for a diplomatic breakthrough through the Qatar channel. Oil stayed above $100 a barrel in early Monday trading, with Brent near $111.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Michigan Shock2 November 2026

A Democrat Endorses a Republican in Michigan, and the Senate Race That Could Decide Congress Is Thrown Open Two Days Before America Votes

The strangest moment of the midterm campaign arrived in a Detroit church. Representative Haley Stevens, who narrowly lost Michigan's Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed in August, appeared on Sunday at Perfecting Church alongside Republican nominee Mike Rogers and endorsed him, saying in a video that El-Sayed had told her she was no longer welcome in his campaign — and that he was right, because she did not belong in it. Stevens had been expected at a joint event with El-Sayed and Representative James Clyburn the same day; instead her campaign asked her to stay away after she demanded El-Sayed condemn an outside group's advertisement, and she went to church with Rogers and his wife instead. The fallout was immediate: members of her political staff resigned, including her communications director, and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer — who had backed Stevens in the primary as the Democrat most likely to beat Rogers — called the move a despicable betrayal. President Trump, campaigning in Iowa, praised the endorsement as brave and urged Stevens to join the Republican Party. El-Sayed responded that he does not think about Haley Stevens and that the race is about the cost of living and about Trump, whose endorsed candidate Rogers is. The stakes explain the reaction: Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, Democrats need a net gain of four seats for an outright majority, and Michigan — where recent polling averages had El-Sayed ahead by roughly three to seven points — is one of a handful of races that will settle control. Nationally, more than 51 million ballots have already been cast, and the RealClearPolling generic average gives Democrats about 51.5% to Republicans' 43.1% ahead of Tuesday's vote for all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria — Official Count2 November 2026

It Is Official Now: Bulgaria's Election Commission Puts Iotova at 57.64% With 98.43% of Protocols Counted, and Gyurov Has Conceded

Exit polls have become a count. Bulgaria's Central Election Commission published official figures from Sunday's presidential runoff early on Monday showing acting President Iliana Iotova and her running mate Kiril Valchev on 57.64% — more than 1.077 million votes — against 39.40%, about 736,000 votes, for former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov and Georgi Kandev, with 98.43% of polling-station protocols processed as of 3am local time. About 3% of voters chose the option supporting neither ticket. The final declaration is due by 4 November, and the winner takes office when the current term ends on 22 January 2027. Gyurov conceded after polls closed. Iotova, 62, who became acting president in January when Rumen Radev resigned to run for prime minister — a post he went on to win — will be the country's first elected female head of state. The contest split Bulgaria over Russia and the war in Ukraine: Iotova, backed by Radev's Progressive Bulgaria, favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Kyiv, while Gyurov ran with the pro-Western Continue the Change and Democratic Bulgaria and wanted a stronger NATO presence in the Black Sea. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen congratulated Iotova. Turnout stayed historically low — about 23% in the first round on 25 October, a record, and an estimated 27% in the runoff — in a campaign shadowed by drone attacks on commercial shipping off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast that left 21 people missing.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Truce Is Over2 November 2026

The Longest Truce of the Ukraine War Has Ended: Russia Declines to Extend the Kherson Ceasefire, and Zelenskiy Offers a Trade — Refineries for Patriots

The pause is over, and both sides are already describing what comes next. Ukraine's human rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets confirmed that the local ceasefire covering the Kherson region from 27 October to 1 November — the longest of the war, brokered with the involvement of US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo XIV — ended after Russia refused an extension. The truce held without reported violations and allowed a humanitarian mission into Russian-held Oleshky, whose remaining residents, fewer than 2,000 of a pre-war 24,000, were offered evacuation to either side or resupply where they were; on its final day Ukraine evacuated 167 more civilians, including 22 children, from threatened areas nearby. Kherson's governor Oleksandr Prokudin urged residents to respond immediately to air-raid warnings. They had reason: overnight Russian strikes on energy infrastructure in four regions left hundreds of thousands of households without power, a drone strike on a dormitory sheltering displaced people in Vinnytsia region killed two civilians including a 13-year-old girl, and Ukraine's air force said the national alert app had likely been hacked after false warnings of a radiation threat appeared. Ukrainian drones, in turn, set fire to the Lukoil refinery at Kstovo, one of Russia's largest. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy added a diplomatic condition on Sunday: Ukraine would stop striking Russian refineries, as President Trump has demanded, if Washington supplies Patriot interceptor missiles. US-mediated talks, frozen since February's strikes on Iran, are expected to resume this month.

Latest · Americas · Florida Jet Crash1 November 2026

All Nine Aboard Killed as a Bahamas-Bound Learjet Nose-Dives Into a South Florida Neighbourhood and Erupts in a Fireball Beside a Busy Road

A Sunday afternoon flight ended in a residential street. A Learjet 60 business jet carrying nine people crashed at about 12:50pm Eastern Time near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in Greenacres, Palm Beach County, scattering debris across a mobile-home neighbourhood, starting several fires and causing extensive damage, the Federal Aviation Administration said. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed that everyone on board was killed and called it a horrific tragedy; Governor Ron DeSantis said there were no immediate reports of deaths or injuries on the ground, a remarkable outcome given how close the fireball came to homes along the roadway. The aircraft had departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was bound for North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas. Recordings published from air-traffic channels captured the crew reporting moderate turbulence, ice accumulation and precipitation at around 35,000 feet before the descent; those reports describe what the pilots said, not why the jet was lost, and officials have not identified the victims or announced any cause. The National Transportation Safety Board will lead the investigation, with an FAA team supporting it, examining flight data, weather and aircraft systems. Greenacres, a city of about 45,000 people, lies roughly seven miles southwest of West Palm Beach.

Latest · Americas · Canada–US Trade2 November 2026

Trump Floats the Concept of a Canada Deal — With the Keystone XL Pipeline Inside It — as Carney Says Negotiators Will Work in the Coming Weeks

Two leaders, one phone call, and the outline of a bargain neither side has yet written down. Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Sunday that he had a good conversation with President Donald Trump that builds on recent constructive discussions about a mutually beneficial trade deal creating jobs, improving affordability and fully respecting Canada's languages, culture and sovereignty, adding that both countries' teams will work in the coming weeks to deliver it. Trump, speaking to reporters in Dubuque, Iowa, before a midterm campaign event, went further on one point: the concept, he said, includes reviving the Keystone XL pipeline, cancelled under President Biden, with Canada building and paying for it while the United States provides the route — a project he said in August might be awoken from the grave. The thaw follows a rupture: high-level talks broke down in August after Ottawa rejected demands Carney said threatened Canadian sovereignty, French-language protections and the auto industry, and Washington imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, drawing retaliation. The two sides have since discussed relief on steel and automobile tariffs, though no terms have been published, and as recently as Wednesday Carney said a deal before Tuesday's midterms was not within reach. The dispute is itself a midterm issue in border states such as Michigan and Maine. Ottawa is hedging meanwhile: Canada and the European Union launched a new economic and security alliance on Friday, and Carney has set a goal of doubling exports to markets outside the United States within a decade.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Settlement Rules2 November 2026

Britain Prepares to Double the Wait for Settlement to Ten Years — but Care Workers Already in the Country Look Set to Keep the Five-Year Route

For up to 1.6 million people, Monday's statement in the House of Commons decides how long the road to permanence is. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood is expected to set out the government's final rules on indefinite leave to remain, the status that lets a migrant settle in Britain permanently. The central proposal doubles the standard qualifying period from five years to ten, with the wait shortened for those who earn more or can show community contribution under an earned-settlement model, and lengthened — in earlier drafts to as much as fifteen years — for some lower-paid workers. After a consultation that drew more than 200,000 responses and a public dispute inside Labour, ministers are now expected to exempt social care workers already living in Britain from the longest waits, keeping them on the existing five-year path; the BBC reported the exemption as likely, and the Home Office has declined to confirm anything before the statement. The politics are already loud: shadow home secretary Chris Philp accused Mahmood of a U-turn forced by the Labour left, Reform UK said it would abolish indefinite leave to remain altogether, and Prime Minister Andy Burnham has endorsed the principle that settlement must be earned without committing to every detail. Mahmood's own argument, made in March, is that arrivals between 2021 and 2024 matched what Britain previously saw across four decades, and that some 350,000 lower-skilled care-route workers and dependants would otherwise qualify over five years. Transitional arrangements for people already part-way through the five-year route have not been finalised; until the new rules take effect, the current route remains in place.

Latest · Europe · Russia — A US Tip-Off1 November 2026

Russia Detains a Teenager Over an Alleged Plot Against a Moscow Park — and Thanks the United States for the Warning That Led to Him

Adversaries cooperated, and a popular park stayed open. Russia's Federal Security Service said on Sunday that it had detained a minor suspected of planning a terrorist attack at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve, the palace and park complex in southern Moscow, after receiving information from the United States through established partner channels — and added, in its statement, that it was grateful to the American side. The US Embassy in Moscow had issued a security alert on Saturday warning of a credible threat of a mass casualty event at Tsaritsyno on 31 October, urging people to avoid the area; the complex was temporarily closed and visitors moved out, including during a concert. According to the FSB, cited by Interfax, the suspect — a Russian citizen who has not been named because of his age — had posted TikTok photographs of himself in a balaclava and tactical clothing holding an object resembling an automatic weapon, and photographs taken at Tsaritsyno captioned to the effect of having found a place. A search of his home recovered clothing matching the posts, and his phone held a subscription to a neo-Nazi Telegram channel and many searches about terrorist attacks and mass killings. Russian officials have not said whether he had obtained weapons or moved beyond planning. The Investigative Committee is deciding what legal steps to take. The episode echoes March 2024, when a US warning preceded the Crocus City Hall concert attack by an Islamic State affiliate that killed more than 140 people — a precedent both capitals are aware of, and one reason this tip was acted on publicly and fast.

Latest · Global Affairs · Binance — Reserves Published2 November 2026

Binance Publishes Its Reserves Snapshot: Ten Major Assets Backed at 1:1 or Better on New Wallets, With the Full Cryptographic Proof Due Tuesday

The document customers were promised for Monday has begun to appear. Binance has refreshed its proof-of-reserves listing with a new set of wallet addresses — built after the 29 October breach, so that withdrawals now flow through fresh infrastructure — and says an initial snapshot shows ten major assets, including bitcoin, ether, USDT, BNB, Solana, USDC and XRP, backed at one-to-one or better. The full zero-knowledge proof, which will let individual customers verify that their own balances are covered, takes an estimated 40 to 50 hours to generate and is expected by Tuesday, 3 November. The exchange says it has now covered about 95% of the roughly $6.3 billion taken, drawing on its corporate treasury and its Secure Asset Fund for Users, with the remaining 5% in less liquid tokens outside the top ten that take longer to source; the FBI has formally attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, the North Korean state unit previously blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft, and about $3 billion of the stolen assets have been frozen by industry partners. Withdrawals reopened in phases from Sunday morning across 15 networks. The first day's flows showed how much reassurance is still needed: Binance processed about $4.65 billion of withdrawals against $1.01 billion of deposits on Sunday, a net outflow of $3.64 billion — about 2.1% of assets under management. Analysts reading the snapshot note the surpluses over customer balances in bitcoin and USDT are far thinner than a month ago: the pledge is met, but at the line rather than with room to spare.

India's Voter-Roll Battle Moves to the Streets Again as Amit Shah Leads a Counter-March in Hyderabad and Attacks the Opposition Rally's Timing

The argument over India's electoral rolls now has rival processions. Union Home Minister Amit Shah was scheduled to lead a foot march in Hyderabad on Monday, from the Babu Jagjivan Ram statue at Nizam College to Mozamjahi Chowrasta, as part of the BJP's campaign against what it calls a false opposition narrative on the Special Intensive Revision of voter lists. The march follows Sunday's INDIA bloc rally at Delhi's Ramlila Maidan, where thousands demanded the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over a revision that has removed more than 130 million names; Congress leader Rahul Gandhi told the crowd Kumar would be removed from his post, alleging that 15% of voters were struck off after the 2024 general election. He accused Gandhi of deliberately holding the event on the anniversary of the 1984 anti-Sikh riots, saying the BJP had publicly asked the opposition to avoid 1–3 November out of respect for victims' families, and invoked Rajiv Gandhi's remark that when a big tree falls, the ground shakes. The Election Commission maintains the revision removes duplicate, deceased and ineligible entries and that anyone wrongly deleted can re-register; the BJP's Telangana leadership notes that a Congress minister in Karnataka has endorsed the exercise there. Human Rights Watch has said the pattern of deletions raises serious questions about the fairness of future elections; India's next general election is due in 2029.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand Votes Saturday2 November 2026

Five Days From Polling Day, New Zealand's Election Turns on a Two-Speed Economy: Booming Farms and Tourist Towns, Squeezed Cities

New Zealand votes on Saturday, and the campaign has narrowed to the price of ordinary life. In Lower Hutt, outside Wellington, 70-year-old part-time bus driver Liz Hartley told Reuters she still works because little is left at the end of the week, and plans a tactical vote in Hutt South aimed at unseating Chris Bishop, one of the National government's most senior figures. Three years after urban voters helped National win on promises to cut inflation and debt, the recovery has arrived unevenly: tourism and agricultural exports have lifted the rural South Island, where second-quarter retail spending rose 8.6% year on year and unemployment fell 0.6 points, while the North Island's cities lag — spending up 5.8%, unemployment up 0.7 points, and house prices down almost 30% from their peak in Wellington and 24.7% in Auckland. Kiwibank's chief economist Jarrod Kerr described shock after shock postponing the promised recovery. Data from more than 400,000 users of 1News Vote Compass tells the same story across generations: the cost of living is the top issue for voters aged 18–25 (30.9%) and for those over 70 (25.8%), with healthcare close behind among older voters at 23.8%, while the environment has slipped to fourth among the young. Polls show no clear path to government for any coalition, with voters drifting back towards Labour or towards smaller parties such as the centrist Opportunity Party, which could enter government for the first time for a new party since 1996. A left-leaning coalition is promising a bigger state role on household costs, public services and housing; National argues it has laid the foundations for growth.
