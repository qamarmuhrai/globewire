Latest · Middle East · Hormuz Diplomacy1 November 2026

Iran Sets Out Its Price for Reopening Hormuz in Two Days: Sanctions Lifted, Frozen Funds Released and US Forces Drawn Down

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, has spelled out the conditions attached to Tehran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days. Speaking after talks with Qatari mediators, he said the timetable had been cut from an earlier seven-day proposal — first to five days at Qatar's suggestion, and now to two — but only if Washington acts first. Tehran's demands include lifting the sanctions imposed after the collapse of the Islamabad memorandum in July, restoring the oil waivers granted in June, releasing frozen Iranian funds including money held in Qatar, reducing the American military presence in the region, and recognising a new shipping route through the strait agreed with Oman while closing other routes. Top security official Mohsen Rezaei has insisted that all seven of Iran's conditions must be met before negotiations resume and the strait reopens, and that they also cover ending the war on all fronts, including Israeli attacks on Lebanon. The exchange follows a letter from US Vice President JD Vance delivered through Qatari intermediaries, which Iranian officials have described as a marked change in tone. Hormuz normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil, and the standoff continues to shape global energy markets: Oman said on Friday it had rescued 25 crew from a Panamanian-flagged merchant ship set ablaze in an attack, while Iran reported halting three tankers that had strayed onto what it calls unauthorised routes.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War1 November 2026

Russia Sends 433 Drones at Ukraine in One Night as 264 Clashes Are Recorded Along the Front, Pokrovsk the Hottest Sector

Russia launched one of its largest drone attacks of the war overnight, sending 433 drones — Shahed-type attack craft, including 64 jet-powered ones, alongside Gerbera and decoy types — from launch areas in Russia and occupied Donetsk, Ukraine's air force said. By 07:30 on Sunday, air defences had shot down or jammed 371 of them, but hits were recorded at 16 locations and debris fell at 14 more, with more than 40 drones still in the air as the count was made. Explosions were heard in Kyiv, where a drone crash in the Desnianskyi district started a fire and set parked cars alight, and in Cherkasy; a late strike on 31 October destroyed the executive committee building of Kramatorsk City Council. On the ground, the General Staff recorded 264 combat engagements over the past day, the heaviest in the Pokrovsk sector, where defenders repelled 55 attacks. Russian forces carried out 74 airstrikes dropping 230 guided bombs, used more than 6,500 kamikaze drones and shelled positions and settlements over 2,500 times. Kyiv put Russian losses for the day at 2,100 personnel, along with 944 drones and 259 vehicles — figures that are being refined and cannot be independently verified. The barrage came a day after Washington approved a possible $2.1 billion sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptors to Ukraine, to be funded by allies, though the missiles must still be manufactured.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea1 November 2026

North Korea Tests a Cluster Warhead It Says Can Scour 13 Hectares, and Vows to Field Four Types by Year's End

North Korea has tested a cluster-bomb warhead designed for its tactical ballistic missiles and says its units will be able to choose from four categories of the weapon by the end of this year, state media reported on Sunday. The Missile Administration carried out the test on Saturday to judge the warhead's combat adaptability and power, according to the Korean Central News Agency, and its research institutes concluded that the Standard Type 2 warhead can devastate a target area of 12 to 13 hectares — roughly 18 football pitches — at its highest density. South Korea's military said it tracked a launch towards the Yellow Sea from around Chongju county in the northwest, and a Seoul defence analyst identified the missile as apparently belonging to the Hwasong-11 Ra family. Photos released by KCNA showed a projectile leaving a mobile launcher and cluster warheads striking an island target. Leader Kim Jong Un did not attend this test, which was overseen by General Kim Jong-sik; Kim had personally watched an earlier cluster-warhead launch in April alongside his daughter Ju-ae. The test is the latest in a run of recent trials that have included attack drones, a precision-guided torpedo system and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile. Cluster munitions, which scatter submunitions over a wide area, are banned by more than 100 countries under a 2008 convention that neither Korea has signed.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Three Days Out1 November 2026

Forecasters Give Democrats a Commanding Edge for the House and a Real Shot at the Senate as Early Voting Tops 43 Million

Three days before Americans vote on Tuesday, the major forecasts all point the same way: Democrats are strongly favoured to take the House of Representatives, while the Senate is closer but leaning their way too. RealClearPolling's national average puts Democrats ahead by 8.2 points, 51.4% to 43.2%, on the generic congressional ballot. Silver Bulletin's model, updated on 29 October, gives Democrats a 95.1% chance of winning the House with a projected 240 seats, and a 77.7% chance in the Senate; Decision Desk HQ is more cautious at 80% for the House and 60% for the Senate, while prediction markets price a Democratic House at above 90%. Republicans currently hold the Senate 53–47, and Democrats need a net gain of four seats for outright control, since Vice President JD Vance would break a 50–50 tie for Republicans. More than 43 million people had already voted early in person or by mail five days before election day, an Associated Press analysis found — well above 2022 levels, especially in competitive states, though early totals say nothing about who those voters chose. Individual Senate races remain tight: CNN polling has Democrat James Talarico ahead in Texas and Abdul El-Sayed ahead in Michigan, and forecasters warn that a handful of close contests could still swing either way on polling error or a late shift. President Trump, whose approval sits at 37% in Wisconsin polling by Marquette and at similar lows nationally, has reinstated closing rallies in Kansas and Iowa for Sunday.

Latest · Europe · Youth Protests Spread1 November 2026

Europe's Youth Protests Reach a Third Weekend: 320,000 March in France, Looting in Frankfurt, 34 Detained in The Hague and Barricades in Zurich

The youth-led protest wave that began over living costs, housing and education has now rolled through a third consecutive weekend and across most of Western Europe. France's interior ministry counted about 320,000 demonstrators nationwide on Saturday, including some 70,000 in Paris, under a deployment of roughly 70,000 police and gendarmes; a preliminary tally put arrests at 422. In Germany, the movement calling itself “Gen Z regelt!” rallied in more than 30 cities, and in Frankfurt masked groups looted shops and set fires, with police using water cannon and pepper spray in several cities and several officers injured. In the Netherlands, police dispersed crowds near The Hague's central station and detained 34 people, most of them minors, suspected of violence, illegal fireworks and wearing face coverings; one officer was hospitalised with suspected hearing damage after lit fireworks were thrown, and shops had boarded up their windows in advance. In Zurich, an unauthorised student demonstration saw burning barricades in the Langstrasse district, while in Lisbon thousands marched peacefully over the housing crisis, and Spanish police stepped up security in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia. Organisers stress that most marchers are peaceful and that the grievances — unaffordable housing, food and education costs, cuts to public services — are shared across borders, which is why the protests keep jumping from country to country.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Quad and G2031 October 2026

Quad Ministers Meet for the Third Time This Year in Atlanta, Warning of Economic Coercion and Pledging $85 Million for Trusted AI

The foreign ministers of the United States, India, Japan and Australia met in Atlanta on Saturday for their third Quad ministerial of the year, after New Delhi in May and Manila in July, on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting. In a joint statement they expressed grave concern over economic coercion — arbitrary export restrictions and practices that disrupt supply chains — and committed to building reliable, open supply chains and resilient infrastructure. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who convened the meeting, said the four are securing critical minerals, supporting next-generation technologies and increasing maritime cooperation. India's S. Jaishankar stressed supply-chain resilience, technology, critical minerals and energy security, countering non-market practices, and taking forward work on counter-terrorism and maritime security. The practical package includes more than $85 million in assistance to widen access to trusted artificial intelligence across the Indo-Pacific, a US commitment of $10 million for critical-minerals recovery facilities in India and ASEAN countries, a first Quad Business Leaders' Forum, and a planned meeting of the four countries' export-credit agencies in Tokyo. A voyage by representatives of all four aboard an Indian Coast Guard vessel from Chennai to Perth in September and October was cited as maritime cooperation in action. Moves are also under way for a Quad leaders' summit hosted by President Trump alongside the ASEAN and East Asia summits in mid-November. At the G20 itself, Jaishankar ruled out military solutions to conflicts and called for energy-market stability and diversified manufacturing, and he also met Russia's Sergey Lavrov on the sidelines.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal Rescue1 November 2026

Six Indian Trekkers Missing in Nepal's Dolpa After Heavy Snowfall Cuts Off the High Passes to Shey-Phoksundo Lake

Six Indian tourists — four men and two women — have gone missing in Nepal's remote Dolpa district after heavy snowfall disrupted trekking routes near Shey-Phoksundo Lake, the country's deepest lake, officials confirmed on Sunday. The group was returning from Dho towards Phoksundo Lake when contact was lost around the Numla and Bagla passes, which link the Dho-Tarap valley with the lake area and have no mobile phone coverage. Dolpa's chief district officer, Junu Hamal, said the tourists are believed to have become stranded in the snow; local residents had reportedly warned them that crossing the passes in the deteriorating weather would be dangerous. A Nepal Army team has been deployed to search for the group and deputy superintendent of police Anil Raj Giri said helicopter assistance has been requested, though there was no confirmed timetable for an air rescue. Heavy snow has hit the high-altitude municipalities of Shey-Phoksundo, Dolpo Buddha and Chharka Tangsong while lower areas saw rain, and the district administration had warned against trekking in vulnerable areas from 28 October. Local reporting links the weather to Cyclone Arnab over the Bay of Bengal, though that connection has not been independently established. The district says 244 tourists in total have reached Upper Dolpa this season.

Latest · Americas · Canada–EU30 October 2026

Canada and the EU Launch an 'Alliance for the Future' — but the 'Associate Member' Label Is Dropped From the Final Text

Canada and the European Union have launched what they call an Alliance for the Future after a two-day summit in Montreal, with more than twenty measures covering trade, defence, critical minerals, technology and research. Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, who had first floated making Canada the EU's first “associate member” in September. That phrase did not survive into the final communiqué, which instead commits both sides to work towards an “even closer association” and to define its substance, scale and scope over the coming months, ahead of the next summit in 2027. Reporting on the talks says Germany led objections, arguing the label has no basis in the EU treaties and could create a back-door precedent for Ukraine, with France and Greece also raising concerns. Carney brushed off the change, saying the alliance is not a marketing exercise and the name does not matter. The concrete results include a concluded digital trade agreement — Canada's first of its kind — a competition agreement, Canada joining Europe's EuroHPC supercomputing network with access to the JUPITER machine, a framework for the European Investment Bank to invest in Canadian critical minerals, cooperation on AI safety, and a study on increasing Canadian liquefied natural gas exports to Europe. The EU remains Canada's second-largest trading partner, and both sides framed the deepening ties as a sovereignty measure amid Canada's trade dispute with the United States.

Latest · Europe · Serbia1 November 2026

Serbia Marks Two Years Since the Novi Sad Station Collapse With a Memorial Walk and 16 Minutes of Silence at 11:52

Serbia is marking the second anniversary of the Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse on Sunday with a memorial walk organised by the student movement that the disaster gave birth to. Sixteen people died when the concrete canopy fell at 11:52 on 1 November 2024, and one young woman was seriously injured. Columns were set to gather at 09:30 at three points — outside the Faculty of Medicine, in Liman Park and at the Varadin Bridge — and depart at 10:00, merging near Futog Market before walking together to the station, where the commemoration begins at exactly 11:52 with 16 minutes of silence, one for each victim. An indictment has been filed against 13 people over the collapse, which investigators and protesters link to corruption and failures in a renovation carried out as part of the Belgrade–Budapest high-speed railway project; the station had recently been rebuilt by Chinese contractors and, reporting on the case notes, was used for months without an operating permit. The movement that grew from the tragedy spent nearly two years demanding accountability and institutional reform, and last Sunday won Serbia's parliamentary election, ending Aleksandar Vučić's long rule — a backdrop that gives this year's commemoration a weight beyond mourning. Thousands were expected to take part in Novi Sad, many of them students who had walked for days from across the country for the first anniversary a year ago.

Latest · Business · Binance Heist1 November 2026

FBI Formally Blames North Korea for the $6.3 Billion Binance Theft as the Exchange Says It Has Covered 90% of Losses

The FBI has officially attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about 29 October to North Korea, naming the TraderTraitor group — the same unit blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025 and linked to the Lazarus Group. In a joint statement with the Secret Service and the IRS criminal division, the bureau said the attackers have been converting the stolen funds mainly into Ethereum and are expected to launder them further through decentralised finance and instant-swap services. It published hundreds of suspect addresses — more than 600 on Ethereum alone — and urged exchanges, node operators and analytics firms to block transactions derived from them. Binance, for its part, says it has now replenished about 90% of what was taken, buying replacement tokens with money from its $1 billion Secure Asset Fund for Users, a futures insurance fund and its corporate treasury, using half of the SAFU fund for the purpose according to co-founder Yi He. The exchange has set aside more than $6.34 billion in company funds in total, says users will bear no losses, and aims to restore withdrawals around Sunday. Its co-chief executive has described the breach as a complex attack involving a compromised laptop and evidence-deleting malware. President Trump, asked about the hack on Saturday, said his administration would talk to North Korea about it.

Latest · Americas · Trump v. Wall Street Journal1 November 2026

Judge Throws Out Trump's $10 Billion Lawsuit Against The Wall Street Journal for Good, Calling It 'Grasping at Straws'

A federal judge in Miami has dismissed President Trump's multibillion-dollar defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch for a second time — and this time permanently. US District Judge Darrin Gayles ruled on Saturday that Trump had again failed to plausibly allege that the newspaper acted with actual malice, the standard a public figure must meet under the Supreme Court's 1964 New York Times v. Sullivan decision, and dismissed the case with prejudice, meaning it cannot be refiled. Gayles described the president's arguments as conclusory, vague and at times inconsistent with the article itself, and rejected the claim that the Journal should have withheld its story after Trump denied it, saying such a rule would let any public figure block unfavourable reporting simply by issuing a denial. The case stemmed from a July 2025 story about a sexually suggestive letter bearing Trump's name in a 2003 birthday album compiled for Jeffrey Epstein. Trump denied writing the letter and sued Dow Jones, News Corp, its executives and two reporters, seeking at least $10 billion. He first had the suit dismissed in April but was allowed to amend it; the amended version, filed in May, has now met the same fate, leaving an appeal as his remaining option.

Latest · Europe · Bosnia Crash30 October 2026

Four Italians Killed as EU Peacekeeping Helicopter Crashes on a Training Flight in Bosnia

Four Italian nationals serving with the European Union's force in Bosnia have been killed and 11 others injured after their CH-47 Chinook helicopter crashed during a routine training mission in the Brčko region, about 180 kilometres north of Sarajevo. EUFOR said 15 people were on board when the aircraft came down near the village of Gorice on Thursday, shortly after takeoff according to Italian reporting. Three died at the scene and a fourth later in hospital, a Brčko hospital spokesman told AFP. Italy's defence ministry said the dead were two army soldiers — identified in Italian reporting as Sergeants Simone Ruggia and Claudio Marchesi — and two members of the Red Cross Volunteer Military Corps, Lieutenant Franco Adriano Zecchillo and Sergeant Laura Procacci. None of the 11 injured is in a life-threatening condition, the ministry said, and no civilians were hurt and no civilian property damaged. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni paid tribute on Friday, writing that four lives were cut short while serving Italy. EUFOR, which has overseen implementation of the Dayton Peace Agreement since taking over from NATO in 2004 with between 1,000 and 1,500 personnel, says a full investigation into the cause is under way. Fatal accidents are rare for the force; the last comparable loss was in June 2008, when two German officers and two Spanish pilots died in a helicopter crash in Bosnia.
