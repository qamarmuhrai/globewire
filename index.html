Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — First Trilateral Set for Thursday16 November 2026

Three Nuclear Powers, One Table: Trump, Xi and Putin Will Meet Together for the First Time in Shenzhen — With No Agreement Expected

The week’s biggest diplomatic event is now a date, not a rumour. The White House and the Kremlin have confirmed that Presidents Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin will sit down together on Thursday, 19 November, on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen — the first trilateral meeting of the three countries’ leaders, bringing together the holders of the world’s largest nuclear arsenals. Trump says he wants to open a conversation about reducing nuclear weapons and to raise the war with Iran; a senior US official, briefing anonymously, was blunt that Washington does not expect any agreement to come out of Shenzhen, and that Ukraine is not on the trilateral agenda. The timing explains both the ambition and the caution: the New START treaty, the last pact capping US and Russian strategic arsenals, expired in February, leaving no binding limits for the first time in more than half a century, and Beijing has repeatedly said it is neither fair nor realistic to ask China — whose stockpile is far smaller — to join a replacement framework. Trump will also hold a bilateral working lunch with Xi, his third one-on-one with the Chinese leader this year, but has no separate meeting scheduled with Putin. Analysts quoted across the coverage read the gathering as heavy on symbolism: for Moscow, a seat at a three-power table; for Beijing, a showcase of Shenzhen itself; for Trump, a shift of attention abroad days after midterm losses at home.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Another Night of Mass Drone Attacks15 November 2026

132 Drones in One Night: Chornomorsk Loses Power for 41,000 Homes, Kharkiv Is Hit in the Street, and Kyiv Strikes Deep Inside Russia in Reply

The air war ran at full tempo through the weekend while the diplomacy stalled. Ukraine says Russia launched 132 strike drones overnight into Sunday — many of them jet-powered — and that air defences brought down or suppressed 106, with hits recorded at around 20 locations across at least nine regions. In the Black Sea port of Chornomorsk, drones struck an electricity substation and energy facilities: six people were injured, a gas pipeline and homes were damaged, and about 41,000 customers of the regional grid operator were left without power. In Kharkiv, fibre-optic FPV drones attacked in daylight — one hit near a civilian car in the Shevchenkivskyi district, injuring two men and a woman, another struck a bus roof, and rescuers reported being targeted a second time as they worked; Zaporizhzhia reported six more injured, including a two-year-old. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy put the week’s toll at more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 guided bombs and 15 missiles, and warned again that speed restrictions on the US Starshield satellite system are breaking the link between Ukrainian interceptor drones and their operators above about 160 km/h. Kyiv answered in kind: its General Staff said Ukrainian drones hit a major petrochemical plant and refinery at Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan, more than 800 kilometres from the front, and an aircraft plant in the Bryansk region, while Moscow claimed — unverifiably — to have downed 533 Ukrainian drones in a single night.

Manila, Day Two: Modi Arrives, Trump Lands Tuesday, and Washington Fights a Report That Its Quad Is on “Life Support”

The 49th ASEAN Summit moves into its heaviest stretch on Monday, with some 20 leaders in Manila and the programme widening to the ASEAN Plus Three, the ASEAN–UN meeting and Tuesday’s East Asia Summit. India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi is in the Philippine capital from Monday to Wednesday for the ASEAN meetings and the East Asia Summit, while President Donald Trump remains the summit’s late arrival — due in at about 5:30 p.m. on Tuesday, barely before the closing ceremony, for an ASEAN–US special summit and a bilateral with host President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. The sharpest pre-summit row is about the Quad: after Politico reported on Saturday that the four-country grouping of the US, India, Japan and Australia was faltering under the Trump administration, a senior State Department spokesman called the claim “utterly ridiculous,” pointing to five Quad foreign-ministers’ meetings in two years and work to formalise a leaders’ summit in Manila — which would be the Quad’s first since 2024. New Zealand, in caretaker mode after its 7 November election, is represented by Foreign Minister Winston Peters, who cannot make major new commitments. Hosts list four intended deliverables for the Philippines’ chairmanship year, including a South China Sea code of conduct with China — though Philippine reporting suggests that code will not be finished this week.

Latest · Climate · COP31 — Final Week Opens in Antalya16 November 2026

COP31’s Decisive Week: Ministers Take Over in Antalya With Finance, Adaptation and Fossil Fuels Still Wide Open — and an Electrification Push From the Hosts

The climate summit stops talking about process on Monday and starts deciding. COP31’s second and final week opens in Antalya with ministerial-level negotiations on a comprehensive draft text, after a first week that made technical progress but carried the hardest questions forward: how much adaptation finance developing countries get and in what form, how strongly the texts defend climate science and the Global Stocktake, and whether any explicit move away from fossil fuels is named. The presidency is split in an unusual arrangement — Türkiye presides, Australia’s Chris Bowen chairs the negotiations — and Ankara is pushing its own centrepiece: an electrification drive to lift electricity’s share of global energy demand from about 20 percent today to 35 percent by 2035, ideally written into the summit’s cover decision. Campaigners are demanding a time-bound plan to triple adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year, in grants rather than loans; the EU, UK, Japan and Canada want tighter links to stocktake outcomes, while India, China and the Arab and African groups insist on nationally determined action backed by finance. UN climate chief Simon Stiell has urged governments to drop tactical delays. The talks close on Friday, 20 November, against a backdrop of a war-driven energy crisis that has tempered expectations of a breakthrough.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan — Death Sentences for the Karak Church Attack15 November 2026

Jordan Sentences Three Men to Death by Hanging for the Funeral Shooting That Killed Nine at a Greek Orthodox Church

Jordan’s State Security Court delivered its verdict on Sunday in one of the kingdom’s rarest and deadliest attacks on its Christian minority. Three men — two brothers and a relative by marriage — were sentenced to death by hanging for the 23 October assault on St George’s Greek Orthodox Church in Hammoud village, in the southern Karak governorate, where gunmen opened fire on mourners gathered for a funeral, killing nine people and wounding 25. The court convicted them of carrying out terrorist acts resulting in death, weapons and conspiracy offences, and promoting the ideology of a terrorist organisation; two further defendants received six-year prison terms. The attackers were arrested on 26 October after a shootout at an abandoned house in Karak. Church bells rang in Hammoud as the sentences were announced, and churches have set aside Christmas festivities in mourning for the dead. The death sentences are not final: they go automatically to review by the Court of Cassation, and the defendants may also appeal.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Unity-Government Clash15 November 2026

“Another Spin”: Eisenkot Rejects Netanyahu’s Public Unity Appeal — While Their Negotiators Sit Down Anyway

Israel’s coalition drama played out in public and in private at the same time this weekend. Caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu used a prerecorded Saturday address to urge Gadi Eisenkot — the former military chief tasked by President Isaac Herzog on 8 November with forming the next government after Netanyahu’s bloc lost its majority in the 27 October election — to open unity-government talks without preconditions, arguing that Iran is reviving its nuclear programme and that only a broad coalition can remove what he called an existential threat in time. Eisenkot dismissed the appeal as “another spin,” noting that Likud and Yashar negotiating teams were already scheduled to meet on Sunday — which they did, despite the exchange. Eisenkot’s conditions remain the obstacle: any emergency government under his leadership, he says, must accept his governing principles and break with extremist allies, and his camp has signalled an offer of a unity government led by Eisenkot alone, with no rotation that would return Netanyahu to the premiership. He has 28 days from his mandate, extendable by 14, and the next public test comes Tuesday, when the Knesset elects its speaker.

Latest · Europe · Technology — Italy’s Record Privacy Fine14 November 2026

€805 Million: Italy Fines Meta Its Largest Penalty Ever, Saying Facebook Read 20 Million Users’ Phone Photos Without Proper Consent

Italy’s privacy watchdog has handed Big Tech one of Europe’s biggest bills. The Garante announced on Saturday a fine of €805 million — about $887 million — against Meta Platforms Ireland, by far the largest penalty it has ever imposed, after finding that the Facebook app on Android phones accessed users’ photographs, videos and associated metadata without specific consent and without properly informing them. The case centres on the “Get suggestions from your gallery when you browse Facebook” feature, switched on by default since 2023, which the authority says reached the galleries of roughly 20 million Android users in Italy under a single permission that bundled together functions needing different levels of access. The ruling rests on Italy’s Privacy Code implementing the EU’s ePrivacy rules on data stored on a person’s device. Meta has 60 days to turn the feature off by default — including for people who already granted gallery access — and to ask for separate, clearly explained consent before switching it back on. The investigation, opened in July 2025 after a complaint from a consumer centre in Bolzano, did not examine whether gallery material was used to train AI models, a use Meta denies.

Latest · Europe · Spain — Record Day on the Atlantic Route15 November 2026

1,488 People in 14 Boats in a Single Day: The Canary Islands See Their Busiest Migrant Arrivals Since 2020

Spain’s Atlantic migration route has set a grim record. The maritime rescue service says 1,488 people in 14 boats were rescued and brought to the Canary Islands on Saturday — the highest single-day total since 7 November 2020, when 1,461 people arrived — continuing a surge from West Africa that began in October. Twelve of the boats reached El Hierro, the archipelago’s smallest island, with one each to Gran Canaria and Tenerife; a single boat carried 240 people, among them 12 women and 36 children, and most of those rescued came from Senegal, Gambia and Guinea-Conakry. Spain’s territorial policy minister was due on El Hierro on Sunday to discuss the surge with local authorities, whose reception capacity is built for far smaller numbers. The crossing from West Africa to the Canaries is among the world’s deadliest migration routes. The record day sits oddly against the year’s trend: arrivals from January to the end of October were down 5.5 percent on the same period last year, which makes the autumn spike — rather than the annual total — the pressure Madrid now has to answer.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — A Unified Command Takes Shape15 November 2026

After a Weekend of Drones and Missiles, the Gulf States Approve a Joint Command Headquarters in Kuwait

The weekend’s attacks are producing institutions, not just statements. Gulf Cooperation Council defence ministers have approved an advanced unified command headquarters in Kuwait, with forces allocated to it, designed to coordinate member states against Iran across the water and against Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia — a step given urgency by Sunday’s interceptions over both Bahrain and Riyadh. Around the same architecture, the pieces are moving: Egypt has deployed a Rafale fighter squadron, helicopters and transport aircraft to Saudi Arabia and shifted warships toward the Bab al-Mandab Strait; the US Central Command has stood up a new unit, Task Force Iron Gate, to police Iranian trade across land and Caspian routes, working through neighbours’ border and port controls since naval forces cannot close those routes; and Iraq — OPEC’s second-largest exporter, and almost wholly dependent on Hormuz — has agreed with Washington to export oil through a corridor on Oman’s coast instead. Any overland Gulf force bound for the Yemen front would still have to cross Saudi territory, which is why the Kuwait headquarters matters less as a building than as a standing decision about who commands.

Latest · Africa · Cape Verde — Voters Choose a President15 November 2026

Cape Verde Votes for a President With Jobs and Emigration at the Top of the Ballot

Voters in Cape Verde went to the polls in a presidential election shaped less by ideology than by the two pressures that define life in the Atlantic archipelago: work at home, and the pull of work abroad. Immigration and employment dominated the campaign in a country whose diaspora is famously larger than its resident population and whose economy leans on tourism, remittances and its position on Atlantic routes between West Africa, Europe and the Americas. The vote comes in a busy African week: the World Bank is preparing additional development financing for Niger, Egypt is exploring new ways to bring down its public debt, and Türkiye is strengthening its defence partnership with Qatar as Gulf and African diplomacy increasingly overlap across the Red Sea. For Praia, the immediate question the new president inherits is the same one driving the Canary Islands arrivals further north — how coastal West African states create enough opportunity at home to slow the departures.
