Latest · Middle East · Iran — Trump Says War “Effectively Over”10 November 2026

“Effectively Over”: Trump Rules Out New Iran Strikes for Now, and Oil Slides to Its Lowest Since August

President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday, after meeting Qatar’s prime minister at the White House, that the war with Iran is militarily “effectively over” and that further American strikes do not seem necessary at this point, though he said the United States remains “locked and loaded” and will keep its naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz in place for now, maintaining what he called the status quo at the request of Iran’s neighbours. Markets took the remarks as a de-escalation signal: Brent crude fell 5.85% on Monday to settle at $92.57 a barrel — its lowest close since 31 August — and slipped below $92 in early Tuesday trading, while US West Texas Intermediate hovered near $86. Diplomacy, however, remains stuck on sequencing. Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, met Qatari mediators in New York and said he hoped for Washington’s final response by Tuesday; an American official described the indirect exchanges as positive and constructive but insisted there would be no agreement unless Tehran’s nuclear programme is addressed.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Talks Begin10 November 2026

First Talks Since February: Ukraine and Russia Sit Down in Abu Dhabi as Washington Pushes for a Regular Rhythm

Russian, Ukrainian and American delegations open two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, their first direct round since February, with US officials saying they see a genuine opening for progress and want to turn the meetings into a regular negotiating rhythm after months of daily contact with both capitals. The agenda is split in two: one session on how a ceasefire would be monitored and stopped from becoming a pause to rearm, and a second on the hardest questions — territory and post-war security, including guarantees for Kyiv and a possible path towards EU membership. Moscow’s team is again led by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky, now joined for the first time by Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov; Rustem Umerov leads Kyiv’s side. Russia has made clear it does not want Europeans inside the negotiations, so Ukrainian officials will brief their allies — Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the EU — in separate meetings. Ahead of the talks, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said battlefield gains and mounting Russian losses were strengthening Kyiv’s hand, and spoke by phone with India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who reaffirmed New Delhi’s support for a peaceful settlement; Zelenskyy said India is ready to help mediate secure food corridors for grain exports through the Black Sea.

Latest · Americas · Markets — Wall Street’s Best Day Since April9 November 2026

Dow Leaps 1,104 Points: Cheaper Oil Cools Inflation Fears and Wall Street Rebounds From Its Midterm Selloff

US stocks surged on Monday as investors regained confidence after last week’s sharp midterm-driven selloff. The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,104 points, or 2.15%, to 52,389.75 — its best day since April — while the S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94, its biggest single-day gain in seven months, and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 2.74%. The rally was powered by the steep fall in oil prices, which eased worries that energy costs would reignite inflation, and by bargain-hunting in technology shares beaten down the previous week; the Dow has now swung by 1,000 points or more on three of the past four trading days. The notable exception was Nvidia, which fell more than 4% against the tide as its board investigates reports surrounding chief executive Jensen Huang. Attention now turns to inflation data: the October consumer price index is due on Tuesday and the producer price index on Friday, both of which will shape expectations for the Federal Reserve’s December decision.

Latest · Europe · France — The Supermarket Fridge Attack9 November 2026

France Blames Iran’s Revolutionary Guards for Cyberattack That Spoiled Food in 700 Supermarkets

France on Monday formally blamed Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps for last month’s cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration systems, summoned the Iranian ambassador, and said it would pursue sanctions at European level. The attacks on 11 and 12 October remotely triggered defrost cycles in fridges and freezers at about 700 stores — chains including E.Leclerc, Intermarché, Carrefour, Monoprix and Coopérative U were hit — sending temperatures soaring, spoiling large quantities of food and, in some cases, starting fires. The national cybersecurity agency ANSSI attributed the operation to Shahid Kaveh, an offensive cyber unit linked to the Guards and known in the industry as CyberAveng3rs. Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau opened a judicial investigation into organised cybercrime committed in the service of a foreign power, and prosecutors have sought international arrest warrants for the unit’s leader and two deputies. Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, speaking on the sidelines of the G7 meeting in Paris, called it an attack of unprecedented scale and aggressiveness. Tehran gave no immediate response and routinely denies carrying out malicious cyber operations.

Latest · Africa · Sudan — Drone Wave and Power-Grid Campaign10 November 2026

More Than 160 Killed in Sudan Drone Attacks as Strikes Spread to the Power Grid

A wave of drone attacks across Sudan has killed more than 160 people in recent days, according to medics and rights monitors, in an escalation of the civil war between the army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces that has raged since April 2023. The Emergency Lawyers group said drone strikes on Khartoum killed three children on Monday, while the heaviest losses were in the west: an attack near a market in al-Dabba, in RSF-held West Kordofan, on Sunday evening, and a strike on a gathering of displaced people in Blue Nile province on Friday that killed 41. In parallel, a campaign against the electricity network intensified — three substations were hit on Monday, at New Halfa and Aroma in eastern Kassala state and al-Ghazali in Northern State, bringing the reported strikes on power facilities to 18 in a week and causing widespread blackouts. Sudan’s military government accuses Ethiopia of helping the RSF carry out the attacks and said on Sunday it had filed complaints against Addis Ababa with the UN Security Council and the African Union’s Peace and Security Council.

In Paris, G7 Ministers Press Rubio on a Fair Ukraine Settlement and No New Iran Escalation

Foreign ministers of the Group of Seven opened two days of talks in Paris on Monday, under the French presidency, with European governments set to press US Secretary of State Marco Rubio on two fronts: ensuring any settlement of the Ukraine war is fair to Kyiv, and avoiding a fresh military escalation against Iran. It is Rubio’s first overseas trip since last week’s midterm elections, in which Republicans lost control of Congress — a result widely linked in part to the unpopularity of the Iran war — and allies are watching for any shift in Washington’s approach on Ukraine, the Middle East, energy security and artificial intelligence. France has signalled the meeting will not produce a joint communiqué; Brazil, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Ukraine were invited to join parts of the discussions, with Ukraine’s foreign minister Andriy Sybiga due at a dedicated Ukraine session on Tuesday. On the sidelines, Canada announced fresh sanctions on Russia on Monday, and the French finance ministry hosted a donor conference to raise funds to repair the protective dome over the Chernobyl reactor.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Trade — Beijing Extends Tariff Truce9 November 2026

Two More Months: China Extends Its Suspension of Extra Tariffs and Measures Against US Companies

China will extend the suspension of its additional 24% tariff on US goods for a further two months from Tuesday, while keeping a 10% additional tariff in place, the Customs Tariff Commission of the State Council has announced — a move analysts say is aimed at stabilising bilateral trade ties and giving businesses greater predictability. In parallel, the Ministry of Commerce said the suspension of measures against 11 US entities placed on China’s unreliable entity list in April 2025 will also run for two more months, and that export restrictions on dual-use items involving 16 other US entities will remain suspended for the same period, though exporters supplying those firms must still obtain licences. A ministry spokesperson said the extensions implement the consensus reached at the eighth round of China-US economic and trade consultations, held in New York and Washington in September. Chinese companies that want to do business with the 11 listed entities may apply for approval under the extended arrangement.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Finance — Singapore’s Gold Ambition10 November 2026

Singapore Opens Its Vaults: Central Banks Offered Gold Storage in a 500-Tonne National Facility

Singapore will launch a vaulting service to store bullion on behalf of foreign central banks, international financial institutions and sovereign entities, the Monetary Authority of Singapore announced on Tuesday, as the city-state competes for a larger share of the global precious-metals trade. The gold will be held in Singapore’s national currency vault, which has capacity for 500 metric tons. Alongside the vault launch, MAS and the Ministry of Finance plan a new tax framework from April next year extending relief to wholesale gold services between institutional participants — vaulting, refining, recasting, assaying and broking. The push builds towards an over-the-counter gold clearing system by the end of this year, with live trading and market operations expected to begin by March 2027 and liquidity building through the year; SGX’s physically deliverable gold kilobar futures are targeted for the second quarter of 2027. The move mirrors efforts in Hong Kong, whose government is also expanding bullion storage to develop the city as an international gold trading centre.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Earthquake Aftermath9 November 2026

First Quake This Strong in Aichi Since 1997: Japan Counts 22 Injured and Braces for Aftershocks

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake that struck central Japan at 1:04 a.m. on Sunday injured 22 people — 19 in Aichi Prefecture and three in neighbouring Shizuoka — the Fire and Disaster Management Agency has confirmed, with no deaths reported. The quake’s hypocentre was about 41 kilometres beneath western Shizuoka, and shaking reached upper 5 on Japan’s seven-level seismic intensity scale in the city of Chita, Aichi: the first time a quake of upper 5 or stronger has been recorded in Aichi since March 1997. Storefront windows shattered, goods were knocked from shelves and train services, including Shinkansen lines, were disrupted across a wide area felt from Hamamatsu to Kyoto and Nara; several of the injured were hurt in falls as they fled buildings. No tsunami warning was issued for the inland quake, and Chubu Electric Power reported no abnormalities at the Hamaoka nuclear power plant in Shizuoka. The Japan Meteorological Agency has warned residents to brace for further strong quakes over the coming week, especially the next two to three days, and to watch for mudslides and falling rocks.

“Little Time Left”: Tuvalu’s Warning Opens COP31’s Second Day as El Niño’s $11 Trillion Price Tag Lands

Tuvalu’s prime minister, Feleti Teo, has warned world leaders at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya that his country has “little time left” to respond to climate change, as the Pacific nation presses for deeper emissions cuts, a faster shift to renewable energy and stronger adaptation support for vulnerable states facing rising seas and extreme weather. IPCC chair Jim Skea urged governments to put scientific evidence at the centre of their decisions and to accelerate implementation. The warnings coincide with a stark economic assessment highlighted at the summit: the UN agency for rural poverty, IFAD, says the El Niño weather pattern could cost the global economy up to $11 trillion and seriously damage food security, with exceptional extreme temperatures expected to continue into next year. Island territories, including French Polynesia and New Caledonia, are using the conference to push practical proposals for better access to international climate finance, while the Turkish presidency continues to frame COP31 as an implementation summit centred on electrification rather than new pledges.

Latest · Global Affairs · Culture — Booker Prize 20269 November 2026

At 81, the Oldest Winner Ever: M. John Harrison Takes the Booker for “The End of Everything”

British author M. John Harrison has won the 2026 Booker Prize for “The End of Everything”, a darkly satirical, AI-tinged portrait of post-apocalyptic Britain — and at 81 he becomes the oldest winner in the prize’s history. The judges, chaired by historian Mary Beard, debated the six shortlisted novels for a full day before reaching a unanimous decision, and Beard insisted the award was no lifetime-achievement gesture. At 198 pages, Harrison’s 13th novel was the shortest book on the shortlist: it follows beachcomber Phillip Tennent and his free-spirited, dementia-stricken aunt Marnie in the near-future coastal town of Dalston-on-Sea, in the shadow of the iGhetti — mysterious entities whose origins and even physical existence remain uncertain. Harrison beat former winners Marlon James and Douglas Stuart, along with Elizabeth Strout, Luke Kennard and Rebecca Perry, in a field drawn from 163 submissions. The prize, announced at Old Billingsgate in London on Monday, is worth £50,000 and is the first Booker win for his publisher, Serpent’s Tail.

Pope Leo Meets Abuse Survivors in Argentina Before Heading to Peru, Where Survivors Are Pressing for Accountability

A day after celebrating an outdoor Mass before an estimated 2.5 million people in Buenos Aires — the largest crowd of his papacy — Pope Leo XIV met survivors of clergy abuse on Monday during the Argentina leg of his 12-day South American journey, a meeting the Vatican had built into the trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru. Leo travels on to Peru on Wednesday for the final, six-day stretch of the tour, and survivors there say they will press the Church on accountability. The Peruvian case carries personal history for the pope: as Robert Prevost, a missionary and later bishop of Chiclayo, he quietly worked with survivors and advocates who exposed the Sodalitium Christianae Vitae community — attending meetings and gathering information — before the Vatican ultimately dissolved the group in the final days of Pope Francis’s papacy. Survivors such as Peruvian journalist Pedro Salinas say they hope that hands-on approach can translate into wider change, while Argentine campaigners argue their Church should have been at the forefront of confronting abuse under Francis. The journey, Leo’s first to the region as pope, ends on 17 November.
