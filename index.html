Latest · Europe / Global Affairs · Ukraine Talks — Moscow to Dublin12 November 2026

Putin and US Envoys Agree to Keep Ukraine Talks Going, With a Dublin Meeting Next and a Joint Aid Mission Floated

The Kremlin has described Thursday’s meeting between President Vladimir Putin and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner as useful, saying the two sides agreed to resume direct discussions on ending the Ukraine war after a nine-month pause and that Washington backed a Putin proposal for a joint humanitarian mission to a city in eastern Ukraine. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov added that Putin expects to continue the conversation with President Donald Trump at an APEC summit in China later this month, while restating Moscow’s position that the current line along Ukraine’s border is the line of control. The envoys, who were met on arrival by Putin’s representative Kirill Dmitriev, now travel to Dublin on Friday to brief President Volodymyr Zelenskyy after the Abu Dhabi round that produced a planned prisoner exchange but no ceasefire. Even as the diplomacy moved, Ukrainian officials said Russian ballistic missiles and dozens of drones struck overnight, killing one person.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Nasser Hospital Strike12 November 2026

Israeli Strike on Gaza’s Nasser Hospital Kills Six, Including Surgeon Dr Rizk Abu Shaira and a 13-Year-Old Patient

An Israeli airstrike in the early hours of Thursday tore through the surgical wing of Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, one of the last major hospitals still functioning in Gaza, killing six people and wounding at least 28, according to Gaza’s Health Ministry. Among the dead were general surgeon Dr Rizk Abu Shaira and at least two patients, one of them a boy aged 13. Hospital director Raed al-Dahshan said a missile passed vertically through several floors, badly damaging the surgery department and intensive care unit, and warned the toll could rise. Israel’s military said the operation, carried out with the Shin Bet, was aimed at a Hamas brigade commander and other fighters it said were inside the complex, although the commander it named was not reported among the dead. Gaza officials called the attack a war crime; reporters at the hospital described collapsed ceilings, dangling cables and staff salvaging equipment from wrecked rooms, in what local officials said was the seventh strike on the complex in two years.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Vice President Resigns12 November 2026

Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Resigns Two Years Into His Term as Courts Reject His School Credentials

Gibran Rakabuming Raka, the 39-year-old son of former president Joko Widodo, handed his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto and to parliament’s upper house on Thursday, stepping down two years into a five-year term. In a five-paragraph letter read out by assembly speaker Ahmad Muzani, Gibran said the decision was his own and taken in the interests of the government and the nation, citing growing public sentiment; senior minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra described the reasons as personal. The resignation follows rulings last month by the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court that the documents for Gibran’s overseas schooling did not meet election requirements, and a push by lawmakers toward impeachment. A vice-presidential vacancy does not trigger a fresh election in Indonesia: Prabowo has 14 days to put forward two names and the assembly must choose a replacement within 60 days. The government insisted the economy had not been disrupted, although the rupiah slipped to a fresh record low during the day’s political turmoil.

Latest · Middle East · Iran War — Seven Conditions for Hormuz12 November 2026

Iran Says Hormuz Stays Shut Until Washington Meets Seven Conditions, Warning the Waterway Will Never Return to Its Pre-War Rules

Iran’s parliament speaker and chief negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, says Tehran has sent Washington a clear answer through Qatari mediators and is still waiting for an official reply, insisting there can be no return to talks or normal shipping until seven conditions drawn from June’s Islamabad memorandum are implemented. As reported, the demands include lifting the US naval blockade on Iranian ports, ending sanctions and freeing frozen Iranian funds, halting fighting on all regional fronts, and formally declaring the war over rather than settling for a ceasefire. Ghalibaf said Iran wants to move beyond what he called a state of “no war and no peace”, warned President Donald Trump that resuming the war would end in another defeat for the United States, and stressed that the Strait of Hormuz — through which only a handful of commodity vessels passed last weekend — would never go back to its pre-war arrangements. Analysts cited by Al Jazeera describe the present stalemate, with full-scale fighting on hold but no peace deal, as inherently unstable.

Latest · Europe · Spain — General Strike Aftermath12 November 2026

Spain Counts 73 Arrests and More Than 1,000 Incidents After Its First General Strike in 14 Years Over Housing Costs

Spain is assessing the fallout from Wednesday’s 24-hour general strike, the first in 14 years, which unions say drew around four million workers and largely shut down transport, factories, ports and shops before clashes and fires broke out in Barcelona in the evening. Police recorded at least 73 arrests linked to 1,044 incidents across the country. The walkout, called by the UGT and CCOO unions together with tenants’ groups, was triggered by a housing emergency: the Bank of Spain puts the shortfall at about 750,000 homes, rising above one million by 2028, while the Youth Council says average rent of 1,176 euros a month now consumes 98.7 percent of a young worker’s typical pay. Anger hardened after the September eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from her Madrid home of 70 years, who died days later, and after parliament rejected tenant-protection decrees — prompting Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez to call a snap election for 29 November, less than three weeks away.

Latest · Global Affairs · COP31 — Pacific Finance Plea12 November 2026

At COP31 in Antalya, Pacific Leaders Say Seas Rising Two to Three Times the Global Rate Have Already Swallowed Inhabited Islands

Pacific island leaders used the COP31 climate summit in Antalya on Thursday to press wealthy countries for grant-based finance that actually reaches them, warning that the negotiations’ promise means little while adaptation money trickles in. Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Cooper Wale said his country lives in near-constant recovery from climate disasters, losing an estimated 79 million dollars a year — about 5 percent of GDP — with seas around the islands rising 7 to 10 millimetres annually, two to three times the global average, and several inhabited islands already lost to erosion and inundation. Tonga’s prime minister, Fatafehi Fakafanua, urged a faster exit from fossil fuels and an end to subsidies for them, restating Tonga’s targets of 70 percent renewable electricity by 2030 and 100 percent by 2035. Summit officials have separately noted that Pacific small island states receive only about 0.2 percent of global adaptation finance, a figure the talks’ president of negotiations, Australia’s Chris Bowen, says must change if the 1.5-degree limit is to remain a survival line for the region.

Latest · Europe / Americas · Russia — Ambassador to US Dies11 November 2026

Russia’s Ambassador to Washington, Alexander Darchiev, Dies in Moscow at 66 After a Chronic Illness

Russia’s foreign ministry has announced that Alexander Darchiev, its ambassador to the United States since March 2025, died suddenly in Moscow on Wednesday at the age of 66. Spokeswoman Maria Zakharova attributed the death to an unspecified chronic illness and warned against conspiracy theories, while a source familiar with the matter said Darchiev had left the Washington embassy and returned home for treatment of a long-term condition. The ministry called him an exceptional professional; the US State Department offered condolences to his family and referred further questions to Moscow, and no funeral date has been announced. A specialist in US–Russia relations who previously directed the ministry’s North America department and served as ambassador to Canada from 2014 to 2021, Darchiev took up the Washington post shortly after President Donald Trump returned to the White House, at a moment when the two governments were attempting to rebuild contacts strained by the Ukraine war.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Historic Mississippi Result11 November 2026

Scott Colom’s Narrow Win Makes Him Mississippi’s First Black Senator Since 1874 and Its First Democratic Senator Since 1982

Democrat Scott Colom has defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi’s Senate race, US media projected on Wednesday, in one of the last contests to be settled from the 3 November midterms. The 43-year-old district attorney finished about 4,500 votes ahead — roughly half a percentage point — after a prolonged count of absentee and provisional ballots, becoming the first Democrat to win a Mississippi Senate seat since John Stennis in 1982. The result is historic in a deeper sense: the last Black senator elected from the state was Blanche Bruce, a formerly enslaved man chosen in 1874 during Reconstruction, and Mississippi, despite one of the largest Black populations of any US state, has had no statewide elected Black official in more than 140 years. Hyde-Smith conceded in a video statement after calling Colom to congratulate him. The win extends the Democrats’ strong midterm showing, giving them a 55–44 edge in the 100-member Senate with only Alaska still outstanding.

Saudi Defences Down Houthi Missiles and Drones Aimed at Riyadh, Mecca and the West for a Second Straight Day

Saudi air defences intercepted a fresh wave of Houthi missiles and drones on Wednesday, the Saudi-led coalition said, destroying three cruise missiles bound for the Riyadh region, two drones directed toward Mecca and southern areas, and two missiles aimed at the west of the kingdom. It was the second consecutive day of interceptions over the capital, after a ballistic missile and a cruise missile were brought down on Tuesday, alongside strikes the Houthis said targeted Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport and an oil facility at Abqaiq. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree denied that the group had targeted Mecca, saying its operations are directed at oil installations and military bases. Saudi civil defence issued early-warning alerts in Riyadh and the southern cities of Abha and Khamis Mushait before later announcing that the threat had passed, as the exchange underscored how the Yemen conflict is again spilling across the kingdom’s skies.

A Russian Drone Tears Into a Nine-Storey Kyiv Block, Killing a 44-Year-Old Woman, in an Overnight Barrage of 123 Drones and Missiles

A Russian drone struck a nine-storey residential building in Kyiv’s Solomianskyi district at about 8 a.m. on Thursday, setting fire to apartments from the third to the sixth floors and partly destroying the fifth floor, the city prosecutor’s office said. A 44-year-old resident was killed and seven other people were injured, most of them suffering acute stress reactions, while three residents were rescued from the building. Prosecutors have opened a war-crimes investigation, and falling debris from a separate daytime drone attack later set cars and a bus alight in the Shevchenkivskyi district, injuring one more person.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pakistan — Imran Khan’s Sisters Released12 November 2026

Punjab Releases Two Sisters of Jailed Former Prime Minister Imran Khan After Withdrawing Their Detention Orders

Punjab’s provincial government has released two sisters of former prime minister Imran Khan from custody after withdrawing the detention orders under which they were being held, leaders of Khan’s Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf party confirmed in reports carried on Thursday. Khan, who was removed from office in 2022 and has been imprisoned since 2023 across a series of cases he and his party describe as politically motivated, remains in jail, and his sisters have been among the most visible family members campaigning for access to him and for his release. The releases were reported without a detailed official explanation of why the orders were dropped at this point, and they come amid continuing legal and political battles over Khan’s detention, his party’s organisational freedom and the treatment of his supporters. Party figures presented the move as a confirmation that the detentions should not have been imposed, while there was no immediate indication of a wider change in the government’s stance toward the former premier himself.
