Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — The Genocide Verdict20 November 2026

A First in 80 Years: The World Court Finds Myanmar Committed Genocide

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya — the first time in the court’s 80-year history that a state has been held responsible for the crime. In a 9–6 decision read out over nearly three hours on Thursday, President Yuji Iwasawa said Myanmar’s forces killed at least 10,000 Rohingya in northern Rakhine State during the 2017 crackdown, alongside widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, in a campaign that drove more than 730,000 people to flee to Bangladesh. The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019. The court ordered Myanmar to halt all acts of violence, investigate and prosecute those responsible, and provide reparations — including facilitating the safe, voluntary return of the displaced, restoring lost property, helping families find and bury the disappeared, and funding rehabilitation. The judgment is final and cannot be appealed, though the court has no power to enforce it; Myanmar must file a plan within two years, with compensation to be set later. Survivors’ advocates called it a turning point, while warning that justice has been decided but not yet delivered.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Odisha Coast — After Landfall20 November 2026

Akvan Is Ashore and Weakening — and Odisha Reports No Major Damage

Cyclone Akvan has crossed the coast. The storm came ashore close to Gopalpur on Friday morning between 7:30 and 8:30 a.m., with sustained winds of 70–80 km/h gusting to 90, racing in faster than forecast the day before. By late morning the system had already slipped to 60–70 km/h gusting to 80 as it moved north-northwest at 26 km/h, and forecasters expect it to fade to a deep depression within hours and then a depression. It is India’s third cyclone in less than a month, and the third to threaten Odisha in 24 days. This time the preparation appears to have held: Odisha’s Special Relief Commissioner told Reuters no damage or untoward incident had been reported so far, and Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari confirmed no major damage or loss of life. The rain, though, was intense — Patrapur in Ganjam district recorded 209 mm, Hinjili 207 mm and Gopalpur itself 190 mm — with heavy rain now forecast to spread inland across Jharkhand, West Bengal, Bihar and the northeast into Saturday. Nearly 300,000 people had been evacuated and more than 9,300 shelters opened before landfall.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — The Trilateral20 November 2026

“A Nuclear War Must Never Be Fought”: Trump, Xi and Putin Close a Historic Summit

The first-ever three-way summit of the American, Chinese and Russian presidents has ended in Shenzhen with a joint statement long on principle and short on specifics. Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin agreed to open a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation, declaring that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and said their countries would work to increase supplies of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations. The 90-minute meeting, held on Thursday at the secluded Kylin Villa guesthouse on the sidelines of the APEC gathering, was proposed by Putin as a venue to discuss ending the Ukraine war — but it produced no visible mechanism to stop that fighting, and the statement outlined only broad areas of agreement. Trump, who arrived with his party reeling from midterm defeats and spoke of making history, was greeted on the tarmac by Xi in a reciprocal airport welcome. For Beijing, hosting the 21 APEC economies under the theme of building an Asia-Pacific community was itself the showcase: a shifting order in which US dominance is openly contested by Moscow and Beijing alike.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Final Day, No Deal Yet20 November 2026

Crunch Time in Antalya: COP31 Enters Its Last Day With Fossil Fuels Still Off the Text

Negotiators in Antalya have reached the scheduled final day of COP31 still searching for a deal. Friday marks the official end of the two-week summit, yet the talks have so far failed to advance any big commitment to move away from fossil fuels — the coal, oil and gas driving more intense heat waves, droughts and floods. The revised draft cover decision published this week removed every mention of fossil fuels, a retreat from Monday’s first draft, which had restated the COP28 transition pledge and called for an accelerated phase-out. What remains is the “35 by 35” electrification goal — raising electricity’s share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035 — though even that wording would still allow coal and other fossil fuels to supply part of the power. More than 90,000 people were expected in Antalya for the conference, co-shaped by Türkiye’s presidency and Australia’s Chris Bowen leading the negotiations. As with most climate summits, the deadline is expected to slip: delegations are braced for talks to run past Friday night.

Latest · Americas · New York — The Record Debut20 November 2026

$81 Billion in a Day: Anthropic’s Debut Becomes the Biggest IPO in History

Anthropic has completed the largest stock-market debut ever recorded. The San Francisco maker of the Claude AI chatbot sold 980 million shares at $83 each, raising $81.3 billion and eclipsing the $75 billion mark set by SpaceX in June; a parallel $26 billion private placement takes the total haul to about $107 billion and values the company at roughly $1.5 trillion. Shares opened on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT at $111.50 — 34% above the offer price — pushing the valuation briefly past $2 trillion before closing up 17% at $97.17. Anthropic is the first major frontier AI lab to go public, arriving barely six years after its founding and with $6.2 billion of debt already on its books. Its prospectus devoted dozens of pages to risk, warning that its models could develop capabilities faster than the company can evaluate them or put safeguards in place, and that it spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity in the first half of 2026 alone. Rival OpenAI has postponed its own listing until next year.

Iraqi Forces to Deploy in a Militia Stronghold Under a Deal to Ease Tensions

Iraq’s security forces will deploy on Friday in Jurf al-Sakhar, the stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah, under a compromise designed to calm tensions with Iran’s most powerful militia ally in the country, security officials told a news conference. The town, southwest of Baghdad, has long been controlled by the armed group and largely closed to state forces and to its original displaced residents, making any agreed entry by government troops a significant test of Baghdad’s authority. Officials framed the move as a negotiated arrangement rather than a confrontation — an attempt to assert a state presence without triggering a clash with a faction that retains deep political and military influence inside Iraq’s security landscape. The deployment is the latest chapter in the government’s long effort to balance pressure from Washington to rein in Iran-backed groups against the domestic cost of moving against them directly.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taipei — The Influence Accounts20 November 2026

Taiwan Flags Nearly 2,700 Accounts to Meta Ahead of Local Elections

Taiwan has handed Meta a list of nearly 2,700 social media accounts it says form part of a Chinese information-warfare campaign aimed at next week’s elections, according to three government officials and internal reports reviewed by Reuters. The accounts were identified in recent weeks on Threads, Meta’s text-based platform, which is popular on the island. Of 2,400 accounts detailed in one government report, 74% listed China as their location, about 25% disclosed no location and 1% listed Russia. Officials assess the batch was created in bulk by operators in China or Russia to influence the 28 November vote for mayors and county chiefs — a key barometer of support for President Lai Ching-te’s Democratic Progressive Party. The disclosure puts Meta in the position of deciding, days before polling, how to act on a government-supplied list in one of Asia’s most contested information environments.

Prabowo and Xi Discuss the Next Phase of South China Sea Cooperation

Indonesia’s President Prabowo Subianto met China’s President Xi Jinping on Friday to discuss economic and investment partnerships and the future of cooperation in the South China Sea, the Indonesian government said. The meeting keeps Jakarta walking its familiar line: deepening trade and infrastructure ties with Beijing while guarding its position in waters where China’s sweeping claims overlap with Indonesia’s exclusive economic zone near the Natuna Islands. Any language on joint development in the disputed area is watched closely in Jakarta, where officials insist cooperation cannot concede sovereignty, and in other Southeast Asian capitals wary of bilateral deals that cut across a collective negotiating position. No detailed readout of maritime terms was released, but the fact of the discussion itself — at head-of-state level, on the same week as the APEC gathering in Shenzhen — signals both sides want the relationship’s economic momentum to outrun its maritime friction.

Dollar Near a 19-Month Peak as AI Jitters Send Investors Running for Safety

The dollar is holding near its strongest level in 19 months. The dollar index sat close to 103.9 on Friday — its highest since early April 2025 — on course for a fourth straight week of gains after eight consecutive rising sessions, as anxiety over the AI trade pushed investors into safe-haven assets and US equities fell sharply overnight. The rally accelerated on 10 November, when hotter-than-expected US inflation reinforced bets that the Federal Reserve will tighten policy; the 10-year Treasury yield eased to a one-week low of 5.016%. Latest · Europe · London — Two Beats in a Row20 November 2026

Britain’s Shoppers and Its Books Both Beat Forecasts in October

Two pieces of better-than-expected news have landed for Britain’s finance minister John Healey. Retail sales volumes rose 0.7% in October from September — beating every forecast in a Reuters poll — helped by a government call for households to stock up on long-life items and by stronger clothing sales; annual growth climbed to 2.3%, a two-month high. Separately, the Office for National Statistics put public sector net borrowing in October at £17 billion, below the £18 billion economists expected, and revised down borrowing for April to September by £3.6 billion on stronger tax receipts, taking cumulative borrowing for 2026/27 so far to £111.3 billion. Consumer sentiment is inching back too: the GfK index rose two points to −22 in November as shoppers eyed Black Friday purchases, recovering a little of October’s 11-point plunge. The figures arrive almost a month after Healey’s budget, and soften — without erasing — the deficit picture he inherited.

After the Tanks Withdrew, Gazans Came Back to See What Was Left

Palestinian families returned to central Gaza on Thursday to check their belongings and assess the damage, hours after Israel’s military declared its rare tank raid complete. Separately, Gaza’s civil defence said four Palestinians were killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza City on Thursday. Hamas called the raid a blatant violation of the truce.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Jakarta — The 19,000 Channels20 November 2026

Indonesia Asks Google to Explain the Removal of 19,000 YouTube Channels

Indonesia’s digital ministry has formally asked Google to justify its removal of more than 19,000 YouTube channels supportive of the Indonesian government, warning that the takedowns risk being misinterpreted. The channels were disclosed in Google’s third-quarter bulletin, published on 30 October, which reported the removals without the fuller explanation Jakarta now wants on the record. The request puts a familiar tension in public form: platforms say mass removals target coordinated inauthentic behaviour, while governments whose supporters are swept up read the same action as political interference. How Google answers — and whether it publishes criteria detailed enough to distinguish spam networks from genuine supporters — will be watched well beyond Indonesia, where several governments are pressing platforms for more transparent moderation ahead of election cycles.
