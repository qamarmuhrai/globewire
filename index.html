Latest · Europe · Kerch Bridge — Third Strike19 November 2026

“Far More Painful”: Putin Rules Out Any Truce After the Third Bridge Strike

Diplomacy in Shenzhen collided with the war it barely mentioned. Ukraine struck the 19-kilometre Kerch Bridge linking Russia to Crimea for the third time in four days overnight, using domestically made Neptune missiles against the railway section and halting train traffic while damage was assessed; services resumed in the afternoon and Crimea’s Moscow-appointed authorities said free ferries would run from Friday to replace lost capacity. The earlier strikes this week had already shut the road section, with satellite images showing two impact areas and reporting that Tuesday’s attack destroyed the central road span. Speaking to reporters after the trilateral summit, a visibly angry Vladimir Putin said he had asked his Security Council to consider a halt to attacks at sea and on energy sites, but that after the bridge strikes “there will be no agreements” — Moscow would no longer consider a Black Sea or energy moratorium, and its response would be “far more painful” than the damage to the bridge. He insisted the crossing is used exclusively for civilian purposes and said he had ordered it closed to military hardware and ammunition, while vowing to rebuild it. Kyiv describes the bridge, opened by Putin in 2018, as an illegal structure and a supply artery for Russian forces in the south.

Latest · Europe · Berlin — Coalition of the Willing19 November 2026

40 Partners in Berlin: Europe Tries to Stay in the Room on Ukraine

While the three-power talks ran in China, Ukraine’s backers staged their own show of numbers in Germany. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy arrived in Berlin on Thursday for a Coalition of the Willing meeting bringing together more than 40 partners, hosted by Chancellor Friedrich Merz and co-chaired by France’s Emmanuel Macron and Britain’s Andy Burnham, with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte attending and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner taking part. The coalition — 36 countries by France’s count, reaching beyond Europe to Canada, Japan, Australia and New Zealand — was built to coordinate military help, keep pressure on Moscow and prepare post-ceasefire security guarantees: support for Ukraine’s armed forces as the first line of defence, a multinational force to deploy after hostilities end, and binding legal and political guarantees. The timing carried the anxiety. Diplomats quoted in reporting from Berlin worried the forum has lost momentum, and that a direct Trump–Putin understanding could render it irrelevant; Kushner said the envoys’ priorities were a common position on a lasting peace and the agenda for the next round of multilateral talks, and Trump himself called into the Berlin discussions after his sessions with Putin and Xi, according to his son-in-law. Germany’s line going in was that guarantees must be robust and legally binding, and take effect the moment a ceasefire does.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — The Yalta Argument19 November 2026

“Nothing Wrong With Yalta”: Putin Defends the Comparison Others Fear

The argument over what Shenzhen meant outlasted the meeting itself. Vladimir Putin, asked about comparisons between Thursday’s three-way summit and the 1945 Yalta Conference, rejected the suggestion there was anything wrong with either event and denied that Yalta divided the world into spheres of influence — a reading much of Eastern Europe, which spent the Cold War under governments installed in Moscow’s shadow, has never accepted. Ukraine’s foreign minister mocked the parallel, noting that Kyiv’s forces had just knocked out the bridge to Crimea, the peninsula that hosted Yalta itself. The contrasts are hard to miss: Yalta ran eight days at the edge of victory in a world war and shaped the order for decades; Shenzhen lasted under two hours and produced a five-paragraph declaration, while two of its three principals remain stuck in unfinished wars, Putin in Ukraine and Donald Trump in Iran. European reaction in the summit’s live coverage ran the same way — Germany’s Friedrich Merz warned a Yalta-style format would not bring peace, Austria’s chancellor condemned a Russian drone strike that also damaged Austria’s embassy in Kyiv, and Russia used the occasion to float closer co-operation with China on the Northern Sea Route, through which it shipped 21 million barrels of oil to Asia between July and November, about 50% more than last year.

Latest · Middle East · Washington — The January Hint19 November 2026

“We’ll See What Happens After January 1”: Trump Leaves the Iran Threat Hanging

Donald Trump has added a date to the Iran standoff without explaining it. In a Fox News interview broadcast on Thursday during his China trip, asked about a CNN report this week that Iran was planning to detonate a radiological “dirty bomb” in Washington, Trump said people in the capital should not be alarmed — “I don’t think so” — but warned that if such an attack happened, “we wipe them out,” and said there were indications Tehran was “looking at gases, looking at bio,” because US action had made the nuclear path harder. He then added, without elaboration: “We’re going to have a new year coming up, and we’ll see what happens after January 1. But they’ve been nasty.” Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman had already dismissed the CNN report as baseless on Tuesday, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday he saw no imminent threat to Washington. Separately, reporting on Thursday said Trump is weighing a fresh round of strikes on Iran under Israeli pressure over Tehran’s rebuilt missile and drone capacity, with no decision yet taken — leaving January 1 as either a deadline, a decision point, or a line designed to be wondered about.

$111.50: Anthropic Opens a Third Above Its IPO Price and Touches $2 Trillion

The waiting ended in a single print. Anthropic’s shares opened on Nasdaq on Thursday at $111.50 under the ticker ANT — 34% above the $83 offer price set the night before — and were last reported trading at $108.72, a level that put the Claude maker’s market value at about $2 trillion on a fully diluted basis and carried it past SpaceX’s June debut as the defining listing of the year. The sale itself had already broken records before a share changed hands: 980 million shares sold at $83, above the marketed $70–78 range, raising roughly $81.3 billion against demand described by early investors as many times oversubscribed. Co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei rang the opening bell in Times Square. The numbers underneath remain extreme in both directions — revenue of $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026 in the filings, alongside very large reported losses driven substantially by accounting charges — and the debut, landing in the same week as Nvidia’s earnings, was read across the market as the AI trade passing its biggest public test so far. The offering closes on Friday, and index providers plan to add the stock within about 15 days.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bay of Bengal — The Last Night19 November 2026

Close to 300,000 Moved, 39 Trains Cancelled: Akvan’s Final Approach

The evacuation outran its own target as the storm closed in. By Thursday evening Odisha had shifted close to three lakh — 300,000 — people to safer ground ahead of severe cyclonic storm Akvan, well beyond the 200,000 goal officials had set in the afternoon, with 9,299 shelters identified across the state. The India Meteorological Department’s 5:30 p.m. bulletin put Akvan over the west-central Bay of Bengal moving north at about 13 km/h, on course to turn north-northwest and cross the north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts close to Gopalpur by noon on Friday 20 November, at sustained winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110. The surge warning narrowed through the evening: water 0.5 to 1 metre above the tide is now expected to flood low-lying parts of Kakinada, Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram and Srikakulam in Andhra Pradesh and Ganjam and Puri in Odisha, with south Odisha coast winds raised to 80–90 km/h gusting to 100 from midnight. East Coast Railway cancelled 39 trains across 19–21 November, telecom providers were ordered to switch on intra-circle roaming in six districts for Friday, and red warnings for extremely heavy rain remain over Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur, Puri, Ganjam, Gajapati and Kandhamal through the night.

Latest · Africa · Mogadishu — 72 Hours19 November 2026

“Open Invasion”: Somalia Gives the UAE Ambassador 72 Hours to Leave

Somalia has taken its long quarrel with the United Arab Emirates to the edge of a full diplomatic break. After a weekly cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre, Information Minister Abdifatah Kasim Mohamud announced on Thursday that the UAE ambassador — named in Somali reporting as Ahmed Juma Al-Rumaithi — must leave within 72 hours, that Somalia’s own envoy is being recalled from Abu Dhabi for consultations, and that measures will be taken against anyone who co-operates with what he called the UAE’s open invasion of Somali sovereignty, security, land and unity. The government did not detail the specific acts behind the order when announcing it. It follows a televised address on Tuesday in which President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud accused Abu Dhabi of signing port agreements for Berbera, Bosaso and Kismayo directly with the Somaliland, Puntland and Jubaland administrations, maintaining military arrangements outside federal command, and backing regional governments against Mogadishu. The rupture has been building since January, when Somalia annulled all its agreements with the UAE, including port and defence deals, and closed its airspace to Emirati military and cargo flights. The UAE, which trained Somali troops for years and still funds salaries and logistics for thousands of Somali security personnel around the capital, has long denied meddling and had not formally responded to the expulsion order by Thursday night.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Yongbyon — Watched From Space19 November 2026

Externally Complete by Late October: North Korea Widens Its Enrichment Plant

The world’s nuclear watchdog is again reading North Korea from orbit. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi told the agency’s 35-nation Board of Governors that a further extension to the original uranium-enrichment facility at the Yongbyon complex has been under construction since mid-September and was externally complete by late October, calling the continuing expansion of the programme a cause for serious concern. Yongbyon, which Pyongyang calls the heart of its nuclear effort, can produce both highly enriched uranium and weapons-grade plutonium: Grossi said the site’s five-megawatt reactor probably continues to operate and is in its seventh irradiation cycle, after which spent fuel would be removed for reprocessing, and a light-water reactor there has been running since April. None of it is verified on the ground — North Korea expelled IAEA inspectors in 2009, and the agency monitors through satellite imagery and open sources. The assessment landed in the same week Donald Trump, in Asia for the Shenzhen summit, expressed eagerness to meet Kim Jong Un, and South Korea’s President Lee Jae Myung renewed his call for dialogue with Pyongyang; no meeting has been confirmed.

A General, a Colonel and a Compound Owner: US Sanctions Hit Southeast Asia’s Scam Centres

The United States has aimed its sanctions machine at the armed protection around Southeast Asia’s fraud compounds. The Treasury Department on Thursday sanctioned three individuals and two companies it links to scam centres that target Americans and Europeans with fraudulent virtual-currency investment schemes: Zhao Hui, a Cambodian national who owned a compound running such scams; General Saw Shwe Wah, commander-in-chief of Myanmar’s Democratic Karen Benevolent Army, an armed group already sanctioned for supporting scam centres; and Colonel Saw A One, the DKBA commander overseeing security at the Three Pagodas Pass near the Thai border in Karen State. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the department, under President Trump, has aggressively targeted overseas scam compounds to protect Americans from fraud. The designations freeze any US-linked assets of those named and bar Americans from dealing with them, and they land on the same day the World Court’s genocide judgment put Myanmar’s military conduct back at the centre of international attention.

Latest · Europe · Germany — Friday Walkout19 November 2026

3.7 Million Workers, One Meagre Offer: IG Metall Calls Nationwide 24-Hour Strikes

Germany’s car and steel industries are set to stop for a day. IG Metall, the country’s largest union, on Thursday called 24-hour strikes for Friday at hundreds of auto and steel makers, escalating a pay dispute covering 3.7 million workers at companies including Audi, Mercedes-Benz and Tata Steel. The union, led by Christiane Benner, is demanding a 5% wage increase, measures to safeguard jobs and production sites, and profit-sharing in sectors performing strongly; it called the employers’ offer meagre and said it showed a lack of commitment to jobs and plants. That offer is a one-off payment of 1% in July 2027 followed by a 1.3% wage increase from July 2028 — a timetable the union reads as asking workers to wait nearly two years for a real rise. No date has been agreed for a fourth round of negotiations, which leaves Friday’s walkout as both a pressure tactic and a warning of what an open-ended dispute in Europe’s largest manufacturing economy would look like.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cepu & the Baltic — Two Files Closed, One Opened19 November 2026

A Refinery Death in Java, and a Baltic Pipeline Case Dropped in Hong Kong

Two energy stories closed the day at opposite ends of Asia. In Indonesia, the Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry said one worker has died after an explosion in a tank at a ministry-controlled oil refinery in the Cepu district of Central Java on Wednesday, during a maintenance shutdown; the fire was brought under control and the ministry says it will investigate the cause of a blast first reported by local media late on Wednesday. In Hong Kong, prosecutors have dropped the criminal-damage case against Wan Wenguo, the Chinese captain of the Hong Kong-registered container ship Newnew Polar Bear, accused of severing the 77-kilometre Balticconnector gas pipeline between Estonia and Finland in October 2023 by dragging the vessel’s anchor. Prosecution lawyers told the magistrate’s court that several witnesses from the Chinese mainland could not be contacted; Wan, in his 40s and in custody since December 2024, had pleaded not guilty to the damage charge but guilty to two lesser safety-compliance charges, and is expected to be deported to mainland China. Finnish investigators maintain the anchor severed the pipeline — a finding now left without a courtroom to test it in.
