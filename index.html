Latest · Americas · Ukraine War21 October 2026

Musk Ordered Starlink Slowdown That Blindsided Ukraine, Journal Reports

Elon Musk personally ordered a slowdown of the military-grade Starlink service that Ukraine's forces rely on, The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter. The speed caps, imposed last week, left some Ukrainian offensive and interceptor drones virtually useless and triggered alarm in Kyiv and confusion inside the Trump administration. Both Musk and President Trump have voiced anger at Ukraine's recent strikes on Russian diesel refineries, which Trump blames for high energy prices. Musk posted on X that limits on the service are decided by the US military and ultimately the president, while Trump told reporters on Monday he did not know who was responsible for the slowdown but wanted to find out. The two men discussed the decision in a call soon after it became public, the Journal said.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate21 October 2026

UN Says 1.5°C Climate Goal Is Lost as World Heads for 2.6°C of Warming

The world can no longer avoid overshooting the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C limit and is on course for 2.6°C of warming this century even if countries honour their latest pledges in full, the UN Environment Programme warned in its annual Emissions Gap Report on Wednesday. Sticking only to current policies would push warming to 2.8°C. Global emissions in 2025 stayed broadly at the record levels of 2024, with emissions from burning coal, oil and gas edging slightly higher. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said national climate plans "fall dangerously short" and pointed to deadly floods, brutal heatwaves and raging wildfires as consequences already unfolding, with those least responsible suffering first and worst. The report lands weeks before leaders gather for the COP31 climate summit in November.

Trump Says India–US Trade Deal Is 'Pretty Close' as Rubio Holds Talks in Mumbai

President Donald Trump said on Tuesday that the United States and India are "pretty close" to a trade agreement, telling reporters at the Oval Office it would be "a much different kind of deal" from earlier pacts. His remarks came as Secretary of State Marco Rubio visited Mumbai for talks with External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar covering trade, energy, technology and defence. Rubio said only a small number of items remained to be wrapped up and that the two sides were "about as close as you can be without having executed the agreement." The countries announced a framework for the first phase of a bilateral trade agreement in February, but shifting US tariff policy — and disagreements including India's purchases of Russian oil — sent negotiators back to the table. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal met US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer earlier this month to work through the finer points.

Latest · Europe · France21 October 2026

France Extends Curfews and Puts Soldiers on Guard Duty Ahead of New Protests

France extended targeted nighttime curfews in several cities on Tuesday and will deploy soldiers from Operation Sentinelle — the domestic military mission created after the 2015 Paris attacks — to guard sensitive sites during fresh demonstrations called for Saturday, officials said. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told the National Assembly that police deployments for the weekend would be "massive," conceding that the security response to last weekend's protests over soaring prices — which descended into some of the worst street violence in years, with rioting and looting — had been too static. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said troops would take over static guard duties at places such as stations, airports and tourist sites, freeing police and gendarmes for mobile crowd control. Curfews were set for parts of Paris from 11 p.m. and in Marseille from 10 p.m., with similar measures in Lyon.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria21 October 2026

Nigeria Confirms First Ebola Case in Abuja as Regional Outbreak Spreads

Nigeria has confirmed an imported case of Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease in its capital, Abuja — the fifth country to detect the virus since an outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May. The patient, a woman with recent travel history to Uganda, developed symptoms on 7 October, sought care at a private hospital in Abuja on 11 October and tested positive on 20 October; she is now in isolation. Health Minister Muhammad Pate said 44 contacts had been identified and were being monitored, and the national disease control agency has activated its incident management system. Uganda says the woman only transited through the country after a business trip to eastern Congo and insists it has no active cases. The Africa CDC's director general, Jean Kaseya, has called the outbreak — with a fatality rate near 50% — the most geographically extensive in Ebola's history; Kenya has recorded nine infections.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia21 October 2026

Student Protests Spread Across Indonesia on Prabowo's Second Anniversary

Students took to the streets across Indonesia on Tuesday as President Prabowo Subianto completed two years in office, protesting rising living costs, choking forest fires and government policies. Demonstrations were held in Jakarta, Yogyakarta, Bandung, Pekanbaru and Makassar, with more than 9,000 security personnel deployed in the capital, where hundreds of students gathered at the Tugu Tani monument. Protesters demanded the fires be declared a national disaster, prosecutions for companies behind environmental damage, cheaper education and healthcare, action on corruption and stronger protection for free expression. Clashes broke out near Yogyakarta's regional legislature and several protesters were detained. The fires — burning across Sumatra, Kalimantan and Papua in a severe El Niño dry season — have blanketed parts of Southeast Asia in toxic haze, disrupting flights and closing schools.

Ukraine Says Special Forces Killed 108 Russian Soldiers in Seven-Week Sumy Operation

Ukrainian special forces killed 108 Russian soldiers, captured 29 and struck more than 1,100 targets during a seven-week operation in the northern Sumy region, Ukraine's military intelligence agency HUR announced on Tuesday. The Group 10 unit said it cleared Russian troops from more than 600 hectares of forest and handed the positions to regular defence forces, foiling Russian plans to cut Ukrainian supply routes. Ukraine's wider defensive operations slowed the pace of Russia's territorial advances by more than half in the first half of 2026, according to its then commander-in-chief. Separately, Russian assaults on power plants caused outages across twelve Ukrainian regions on Tuesday morning. The Kyiv Independent noted it could not independently verify HUR's figures.

Latest · Americas · Colombia20 October 2026

Collapse at Illegal Gold Mine Kills 14 in Remote Corner of Southwest Colombia

Fourteen people were killed and seven injured when an unlicensed open-pit gold mine collapsed on Monday near Santa Bárbara de Iscuandé in Colombia's Nariño province, regional authorities said. Search and rescue operations ended the same night. The site, in the rural Chambero sector, sits in dense rainforest reachable only by boat or small aircraft, which hampered the response. Governor Luis Alfonso Escobar urged the national government and mining agencies to speed up formalising informal and small-scale mining to improve safety. It was Nariño's second deadly mine collapse in under three months — 13 people died in a similar accident in Policarpa in August — and the province's government said the two disasters together had killed 27 people. Hundreds of Colombian mines operate without permits; some are controlled by armed groups involved in the clandestine gold trade.

Man Faces Court Over 'Targeted' Car Attack That Cost Australian MP a Leg

The man accused of deliberately ramming Australian lawmaker Helen Haines with a car appeared in court in Wangaratta on Wednesday, as police warned that online extremism is fuelling political polarisation. Haines, an independent MP, is in critical but stable condition in a Melbourne hospital after being struck twice on Monday near her office in the rural Victorian town — witnesses said the driver reversed and hit her again before a bystander intervened. She has lost one leg and doctors are fighting to save the other. Brendan Nugent, 43, is charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and the federal offence of harming a public official, which carries up to 10 years in prison. He did not apply for bail, and his next hearing is set for 4 February. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has said the attack was plainly deliberate and linked it to an ugly, polarised climate of political debate.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza Aid20 October 2026

UN Food Agency Cuts Aid to 700,000 Palestinians in Gaza and West Bank as Funds Run Short

The World Food Programme has cut food assistance to 500,000 people in Gaza and 200,000 in the West Bank because of a serious funding shortfall, its acting head Carl Skau said on Monday, appealing for $450 million to restore the help over the next six months. In Gaza the agency now reaches 1.1 million people a month, down from 1.6 million, with rations halved and daily hot meals cut from 250,000 to 150,000; West Bank assistance was halved from 400,000 to 200,000 people in September. Skau, who had just visited both territories, described life in Gaza as "absolutely brutal," with UN teams recording about 50 attacks a day in the past week. Some 1.4 million people — 67% of Gaza's population — face crisis-level food insecurity or worse, and around 200,000 need immediate aid to avoid dying of hunger, though famine has been pushed back since last October's ceasefire.
