Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash29 October 2026

Spain Begins Three Days of Mourning After 68 Die in Second High-Speed Train Crash This Year

At least 68 people have died after a Spanish high-speed express slammed into a stationary train near Urda, in Toledo province south of Madrid, on Wednesday morning — Spain's second fatal high-speed rail crash this year and Europe's deadliest since 2013. Regional authorities in Castilla-La Mancha put the provisional toll at 68, with 44 of the dead aboard an Iryo service and 24 on Renfe's Madrid–Seville AVE train, and more than 100 people injured as at least three carriages plunged down an embankment. Transport Minister Óscar Puente said a computer communications failure had triggered a red signal that automatically halted traffic, after which an erroneous decision was taken to restart the Renfe train — a sequence he described as inexplicable, while stressing that investigators have not yet established the cause. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, speaking at the crash site, declared three days of national mourning from 29 to 31 October, promised to get to the bottom of what happened and vowed a strong response if negligence or safety failures are found, calling it unacceptable for Spain to suffer two such accidents in one year after January's crash near Adamuz, Córdoba, which killed 46. Opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo demanded immediate, fully resourced and independent investigations and a review of the whole rail network.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea Missiles29 October 2026

North Korea Fires Around a Dozen Short-Range Ballistic Missiles Into the Sea as Border Row With Seoul Deepens

North Korea fired around a dozen short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea — also called the Sea of Japan — on Thursday, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, in Pyongyang's first ballistic launch since 12 October. South Korean and Japanese officials said more than ten missiles flew about 250 kilometres and are believed to have fallen outside Japan's exclusive economic zone, with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi ordering her government to gather and analyse information and no damage reported. Seoul said it had tracked the launches from the preparation stage alongside the United States and was maintaining a posture to respond overwhelmingly to any provocation. The volley came less than a week after South Korea proposed military talks over a worsening border dispute: a joint Seoul–UN investigation concluded earlier this month that North Korea planted the landmines that exploded near the Demilitarised Zone in September, wounding three South Korean soldiers. Pyongyang has denied planting them, and Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of leader Kim Jong Un, dismissed Seoul's dialogue overture as the climax of a farce and rejected an offer of medical equipment. North Korea has also recently tested a precision-guided torpedo system and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile it says use AI technology.

Latest · Europe · Russia–Ukraine War29 October 2026

Moscow Says 613 Drones Headed for the Capital Overnight, Shutting Three Airports and Turning Back Flights From Türkiye

Moscow came under one of the largest reported drone barrages of the war overnight on 28–29 October, with Mayor Sergey Sobyanin saying 613 drones flew toward the capital region and 231 were destroyed as they approached the city — figures Russian authorities have not independently substantiated and Kyiv has not confirmed in those terms. Air defences began engaging targets before 22:30 local time and the attack continued for hours in successive waves, with residents near Naro-Fominsk, about 50 kilometres south-west of Moscow, and in neighbouring Kaluga region reporting explosions, and emergency crews attending debris sites. Russia's aviation authority Rosaviatsiya restricted arrivals and departures at Vnukovo, Domodedovo and Zhukovsky airports; the Zhukovsky limits were later lifted while the other two operated only by special arrangement, and two flights from Istanbul turned back because of the Vnukovo closure. No casualties or specific damage in the capital were reported by officials. Separately, eight drones were reported downed over Sterlitamak in Bashkortostan, where debris fell on an industrial site — monitoring channels identified the target area as a chemical plant described as Russia's sole producer of a compound used in ballistic-protection fibres — causing minor damage and no injuries, according to the city administration. Drone attacks have disrupted Moscow aviation repeatedly through October.

Latest · Middle East · Iran Mediation29 October 2026

Tehran Calls Qatar's Latest Draft to End the War "Unacceptable Ideas, Not an Offer" as a Seven-Day Plan Hangs in the Balance

Qatari mediators presented Iran on Sunday with a new draft agreement to end the eight-month war with the United States and Israel, an official briefed on the negotiations said, but a senior Iranian official involved in the talks dismissed what Doha brought as unacceptable ideas that did not amount to an offer, adding that Tehran's refusal to show flexibility on the nuclear issue is unchanged. The draft sets a seven-day timeline addressing both sides' concerns through daily confidence-building steps, could begin early next week with several countries acting as guarantors, and Tehran is expected to respond by the end of the week. Qatar, alongside Pakistan, has led mediation in a conflict launched in February that has disrupted global shipping and oil supplies and recently spread to Saudi Arabia and Yemen. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Wednesday that Iran remains in close contact with intermediaries to review ideas that could ensure implementation of its own seven-day proposal, that Tehran continues to give diplomacy a chance, and that its plan could be carried out in less than a week. The exchange unfolded amid fears Israel's prime minister could resume attacks this week and after reporting that Washington has warned Gulf states to prepare for a possible short, intense campaign after the 3 November US midterms.

Vance Writes to Iran's Security Chief in a Bid to Restart Talks — Oil Slips as Traders Weigh Diplomacy Against Strike Risk

US Vice President JD Vance has sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and formerly the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' longest-serving commander, in an effort to revive peace negotiations, The Wall Street Journal reported, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter. President Donald Trump told reporters on Wednesday evening that Vance had asked his view and that he approved the contact, remarking that Iran needs help with medical supplies and other things; one person familiar with the approach said the letter was sent at Trump's direction. The discussions so far have centred on a possible framework for negotiations rather than a detailed peace proposal, and Vance is testing whether enough common ground exists to persuade Trump not to order a short, intense attack in the coming weeks — an option the Journal separately reported Washington has told Gulf Arab states to prepare for after next week's midterm elections, with no final decision taken. Rezaei said last week Iran would not negotiate until specific core demands are met. Oil markets softened on Thursday as traders weighed the diplomacy against supply risk: December Brent fell 1.1% to $114.02 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate dropped 1.4% to $101.37. Early Thursday the Saudi-led coalition said its air defences had destroyed another Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election Count28 October 2026

With 85% Counted, Israel Stays Deadlocked: Netanyahu Bloc on 53 Seats, Eisenkot Camp on 48, Arab Parties Surge to 19

Counting in Israel's first election since the October 2023 attacks has confirmed a deadlock, with more than 85% of ballots tallied leaving Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc projected at 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc at 48, and parties representing Israel's Arab citizens surging to a record 19 — leaving both main camps well short of the 61 needed to govern. Turnout reached about 77.7%, the highest in more than two decades. The Arab parties' historic gains make them potential kingmakers, yet Eisenkot has all but ruled out forming a government with them; Joint List figure Yousef Jabareen said any coalition seeking to replace Netanyahu must negotiate with Arab lawmakers, while signalling possible outside support for a minority government rather than formal membership. Netanyahu's Likud accused the Arab lists of stealing the election, alleging bribery, threats and double voting at Arab polling stations without presenting evidence — claims Joint List leader Ahmad Tibi dismissed as the rhetoric of the defeated. The stalemate could keep Netanyahu in office as caretaker through weeks of bargaining or force another election, an outcome President Isaac Herzog has urged Israel to avoid; for Washington, the uncertainty lands as President Trump weighs next steps on Iran and Gaza with the ally he endorsed now lacking a majority.

Latest · Africa · Sudan, Darfur28 October 2026

Drone Strike on a Crowded Nyala Market: Hospital Receives 36 Bodies and Treats More Than 150 Wounded, Toll Disputed

A drone strike by the Sudanese Armed Forces hit a busy market in western Nyala, capital of South Darfur, at about 20:00 on Monday, followed by a second strike on a civilian neighbourhood, Médecins Sans Frontières said. The MSF-supported Nyala Teaching Hospital received 36 bodies — 21 of them in civilian clothing — and its emergency room saw more than 150 wounded, many with shrapnel injuries and severe burns, brought in on tuk-tuks and donkey carts as staff worked through the night. Other counts are far higher and unverified: the health ministry of the RSF-aligned Tasees government reported 188 dead and 356 injured, a rights group tracking the war put deaths above 100, and an army-aligned spokesman claimed the strike precisely hit a camp where more than 150 militia recruits were killed — claims that could not be independently confirmed amid active operations around the RSF-held city. MSF's project coordinator in Nyala asked why markets, where civilians gather for food, are being targeted, and the group insisted markets, homes and civilian infrastructure are not targets and that all parties must protect civilians. A similar strike on Nyala's Al-Mawashi market on 16 October killed at least four people, and aid workers warn such attacks are deepening hunger as malnutrition rises.

Latest · Africa · Ebola Outbreak, DR Congo28 October 2026

Ebola Passes 10,000 Cases With Nearly 4,900 Deaths in Congo — the Country's Deadliest Outbreak, With No Approved Vaccine for This Strain

The Ebola outbreak driven by the Bundibugyo strain has infected 10,060 people and killed 4,887 since it was detected in May, Congolese health authorities said on Tuesday, making it the largest and deadliest Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo's history and the second-largest recorded anywhere. Some 2,684 people have recovered, putting the reported fatality rate close to 50%. First identified in north-eastern Ituri province after spreading undetected for weeks — the country's 17th outbreak — the virus now spans eight provinces and more than 66 health zones, concentrated in Ituri and North Kivu, where armed-group violence, poor healthcare access and mistrust of state institutions hamper the response; parts of North Kivu are held by the Rwanda-backed M23. There is no approved vaccine or specific treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, so responders rely on surveillance, isolation, contact tracing and infection control, while trials test whether the Ervebo vaccine — proven against the Zaire strain — also protects against Bundibugyo, alongside two antiviral approaches. The outbreak has crossed borders: Kenya has reported nine cases and two deaths since 6 October, Nigeria recorded one case in a Swiss traveller evacuated to Geneva, and Uganda, which had earlier cases, was declared Ebola-free by the WHO in August. More than 2,700 children are among the infected, and last week a nurse was killed and another health worker seriously injured while answering an Ebola alert in Tshopo province.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China–US Trade29 October 2026

Beijing Condemns Washington for Adding Three Chinese Medical-Equipment Firms to the Uyghur Forced-Labour List

China's commerce ministry on Thursday condemned the United States for adding three Chinese medical-equipment makers — MGI Tech, BGI Genomics and Shenzhen BGI Technology, also known as BGI Group — to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List, saying the move contradicts the consensus reached by the two countries' leaders and vowing steps to safeguard Chinese interests. Inclusion on the list instructs US Customs and Border Protection to block the firms' goods from entering the United States unless exporters can rebut the presumption they were made with forced labour; Washington says listed entities use or facilitate forced labour of Uyghurs and other minorities or source material from Xinjiang. MGI Tech rejected the allegations, saying it has zero tolerance for forced labour, and BGI Genomics and its parent said they are not engaged in any form of forced labour. A group of UN experts said in January there was a persistent pattern of alleged forced labour affecting Uyghur, Kazakh and Kyrgyz minorities and Tibetans in Xinjiang and other provinces — allegations Beijing rejects as groundless. The dispute extends a tit-for-tat in the medical sector: in early 2025 China placed US rival Illumina on its own unreliable-entity list.

Rand Softens as Gold and Platinum Slide; Investors Await Producer Inflation Expected to Quicken to 5.7%

South Africa's rand weakened in early trade on Thursday, changing hands at 16.65 to the dollar, about 0.4% softer than its previous close, as falls in the country's key export metals — gold down nearly 1% and platinum down 1.5% — weighed on the commodity-linked currency while investors assessed the US Federal Reserve's decision to leave interest rates unchanged under Chair Kevin Warsh. Hello Kitty Maker Sanrio Scraps Spy-Show Tie-In After Backlash in China, Days After Mbappé Sponsors Walked Away

Sanrio, the Japanese company behind Hello Kitty, has cancelled a collaboration with the hit TBS spy drama VIVANT after days of anger on Chinese social media, its Chinese subsidiary announced on Wednesday — the second such episode in days to show the tightrope foreign brands and celebrities walk in the Chinese market. The current season of VIVANT, also streaming on Netflix, centres on Japanese spies and depicts a fictional Chinese intelligence agency capturing and torturing some of them; Sanrio's tie-in dressed its soft-toy characters, including Hello Kitty, as characters from the show. Chinese state media amplified the condemnation this week, and Sanrio's subsidiary apologised for the concern caused, saying the collaboration contained expressions inconsistent with the Sanrio Group's corporate philosophy and that it was terminating the project and all related promotion. TBS had no immediate comment; its president told a press conference the drama is entirely fictional and not intended to disparage any country. The cancellation follows French footballer Kylian Mbappé's apology for remarks about the late Chinese leader Mao Zedong, after which several Chinese sponsors cut ties with him. Japan–China relations have deteriorated since Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said late last year that a hypothetical Chinese attack on Taiwan could trigger a Japanese military response.
