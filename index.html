US and Iran Give Preliminary Backing to a Pakistan–Qatar Compromise Proposal, Reports Say

Pakistan and Qatar have put forward a fresh compromise aimed at pulling the United States and Iran back from escalation and back to talks, and both sides have given it a preliminary positive response, Arab media reported on Tuesday. Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, carried the plan to Iranian officials in Tehran. As described by sources cited by Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, the middle-ground proposal pairs a confidential, unannounced visit by the International Atomic Energy Agency to inspect Iranian nuclear sites with a limited financial concession — allowing Tehran to receive interest earned on $6 billion in Iranian funds frozen in Qatar, rather than releasing the full amount at once. The core disputes remain unresolved: Washington still wants Iran's nuclear programme dismantled, while Tehran wants all its frozen assets abroad freed immediately. Neither government has publicly confirmed a final agreement, but mediators were reported to expect a possible breakthrough announcement this week.

Latest · Europe · Italy20 October 2026

Italy to Send 500 Troops With Police Powers Into Milan After Weekend Looting and Riots

Italy's government will approve sending 500 troops with police powers into Milan after weekend cost-of-living protests turned violent, Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said on Tuesday. The deployment is part of a package Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's cabinet was expected to approve later in the day, following an emergency security meeting she chaired on Monday. The unrest began on Saturday on the sidelines of a demonstration over rising living costs and continued on Sunday, as groups of youths smashed shop windows and looted stores and supermarkets in the city centre. Fourteen police officers were injured by stones, bottles and flares, and more than 270 people were detained — around 80 percent of them minors. Tajani said the package would also make it harder for minors to commit crimes, and stressed that peaceful protest is legitimate in a democracy while those who devastated downtown Milan were criminals.

Latest · Americas · Canada20 October 2026

Alberta Votes Overwhelmingly to Stay in Canada in Its First-Ever Separation Referendum

Voters in Canada's western province of Alberta have chosen by a large majority to remain part of the country, CBC News projected after Monday's nonbinding referendum — the province's first vote on separation. With 1,961 of 5,587 polling stations reporting early on Tuesday, about 76.6 percent backed remaining in Canada, while 23.4 percent wanted to start the legal process toward a binding vote on leaving. Around 1.36 million residents cast ballots through advance and special voting. Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed the result, saying Albertans had made a clear choice for unity and to build together. Premier Danielle Smith, whose government organised the vote under pressure from her party's separatist wing while she herself backed staying, said she was pleased with the outcome. The ballot carried ten questions, with the first nine covering immigration and constitutional matters.

Latest · Americas · United States20 October 2026

Trump's Approval Falls to a Record Low of 31% as Democrats Open a 12-Point Lead Before the Midterms

President Donald Trump's approval rating has slipped to 31 percent, the lowest of either of his terms, in a Reuters/Ipsos poll that closed on Monday, as worry over high gasoline prices weighs on voters two weeks before the 3 November congressional elections. The four-day survey of 1,679 US adults found approval down a point from two weeks earlier. On the generic congressional ballot, 47 percent of registered voters picked Democrats and 35 percent chose Republicans — a 12-point Democratic advantage, the widest of this election cycle and up from seven points a fortnight ago. Much of the shift came from women, half of whom said they would vote Democratic against 32 percent for Republicans. Losing either chamber of Congress would complicate Trump's agenda for the final two years of his term, though national polls do not capture the district-by-district contests that decide control.

Latest · Americas · Colombia19 October 2026

Collapse at an Illegal Gold Mine Kills 14 in Colombia's Remote Nariño Province

Fourteen people were killed and seven injured when an unlicensed open-pit gold mine collapsed in south-western Colombia, local authorities said on Monday, and search and rescue operations have now concluded. The accident happened in the rural Chambero sector of Santa Bárbara de Iscuandé, a municipality in Nariño province surrounded by dense rainforest that can only be reached by boat or small plane, which hampered the response. Nariño Governor Luis Alfonso Escobar called on the national government and mining agencies to speed up formalising informal and small-scale mining to improve safety. The disaster is the province's second deadly mine collapse in under three months — 13 people died in a similar accident in Policarpa in August — underlining how common unregulated mining without basic safety infrastructure remains in rural Colombia.

Jakarta Deploys More Than 9,000 Police as Students Protest Two Years of Prabowo's Presidency

More than 9,000 police officers were deployed across Jakarta on Tuesday for protests marking two years since President Prabowo Subianto took office, as polling shows his popularity at its lowest yet. City police said students from at least two universities planned demonstrations at several locations, requiring around 9,500 officers. The University of Indonesia's student body said it would protest over recent forest fires and a stock-market rout, which — together with a weakening rupiah — it called a sign of the national economy's fragility. Greenpeace activists also draped a banner on a major Jakarta monument urging action on the climate crisis and the cost of living. Prabowo, sworn in on 20 October 2024 after winning by a huge majority, has seen his approval in SMRC surveys fall steadily to 44.5 percent this month, from 81.2 percent last November.

World Food Programme Cuts Gaza Food Aid by a Third, Leaving 500,000 People Without Support

The UN World Food Programme said on Monday that funding shortages have forced it to cut food assistance in Gaza by around a third as winter approaches, ending monthly support for about half a million people. The agency now reaches 1.1 million people, down from 1.6 million last month; rations have been halved to one food parcel per family instead of two, plus a smaller bag of wheat flour, and daily hot meals have fallen from 250,000 to 150,000. Cash assistance has been reduced for 75,000 families, and assistance in the occupied West Bank has also been halved, from 400,000 to 200,000 people. WFP estimates 1.4 million people — 67 percent of Gaza's population — face crisis-level food insecurity or worse, with some 200,000 in emergency conditions, and says it urgently needs $450 million to sustain operations in Gaza and the West Bank through March 2027.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine20 October 2026

Ukraine Airlifts a Wounded Soldier Out by Heavy Drone in What It Calls a World First

Ukraine's military says it has evacuated a wounded soldier from a front-line position using an aerial drone, in an operation its drone unit described as the first of its kind. Video released by the army shows a large hexacopter settling to the ground, the soldier in combat gear fastening himself to an onboard winch, and the drone lifting him away to a safer position, where comrades arranged his onward transfer to hospital. The manufacturer, Gurzuf Defence, said the man had spent over a year in a dangerous position that could not be reached safely by conventional means, and that any attempt to reach him would have put a rescue team at deadly risk. The army said the operation succeeded, the soldier is now in treatment and rehabilitation, and his life is no longer in danger. It did not disclose where the evacuation took place.

GCash Parent Mynt Debuts in the Philippines' Largest-Ever IPO, Raising P60.9 Billion

Mynt, the company behind the GCash e-wallet, made its Philippine Stock Exchange debut on Tuesday in the largest initial public offering in the country's history, raising P60.9 billion including its overallotment option. Trading under the ticker GCASH, the shares opened at P6.64, reached a high of P6.76 and closed exactly at the P6.60 offer price, giving the fintech a market value of about P441.5 billion — roughly $7 billion — and making it the exchange's seventh-largest domestic company. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. led the bell-ringing in Taguig City. Exchange president Ramon Monzon highlighted how widely the offer reached small investors: 140,725 GCash subscribers put about P1.1 billion into the IPO through the app, many buying shares for the first time. The listing drew 19 foreign and six domestic cornerstone investors.

Latest · Americas · Cuba20 October 2026

China Adds a Rare Extra Flight From Havana as Cuba's Fuel Crisis Strands Travellers

China has organised a rare supplementary flight out of Havana for passengers unable to leave Cuba, as US sanctions deepen the island's oil crisis and international airlines suspend services over refuelling problems. The Chinese embassy in Cuba said a replacement flight, CA590, had been added for Air China's Havana–Beijing service after its scheduled flight, CA866, changed repeatedly and was sometimes cancelled for what it called technical reasons; it did not mention the fuel crisis directly. The extra flight was scheduled to depart late on Monday, and flight-tracking records showed its inbound leg had arrived in Havana. Reuters reported that Washington has piled pressure on Cuba's government, including a nine-month de facto fuel blockade and reinforced sanctions that have produced an unprecedented economic crisis on the island. China's foreign ministry referred questions to other departments, and Air China did not immediately comment.
