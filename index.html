Kuwait Base Claimed, a Tanker Crew Hurt: The Gulf War Finds New Targets

The nine-month US–Iran war widened again on Wednesday, even as mediators from Oman and Qatar kept talking. Iran’s army said its drones had struck US drone and missile equipment, including Patriot systems, at Kuwait’s Ali Al Salem base — Tehran’s first claimed attack on Kuwait since September — after Kuwait said its air defences were engaging hostile drones. Bahrain said it had intercepted Iranian aerial attacks for the third time this week, following Tuesday’s strike attempt on a US radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base that prompted American jets to hit a Revolutionary Guards missile site on Qeshm Island. In the Strait of Hormuz itself, ADNOC Logistics & Services said one of its vessels was attacked on Tuesday evening, leaving one crew member missing and 11 injured, while the Saudi-led coalition said it had destroyed a Houthi cruise missile aimed at Riyadh and struck 60 Houthi targets across four Yemeni cities. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said he saw no imminent threat to the US capital, but the strait that once carried a fifth of world energy remains partly blocked.

Two Speeches Against the West, Then a Bilateral: Shenzhen Before the Trilateral

Shenzhen’s diplomatic order of play is now clear: Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin first, Donald Trump on Thursday. The Chinese and Russian presidents met around midday on Wednesday — their fifth encounter this year — to discuss trade, energy, finance and regional security, with a smaller session expected on Ukraine and North Korea. Earlier, both used the APEC stage to attack Western policy: Xi pledged to resist trade protectionism and keep opening China’s markets, while Putin denounced sanctions and described Western powers as former hegemons, speaking hours after Ukraine destroyed the Kerch road bridge. Trump, arriving from Manila where he met regional allies, joins them on Thursday for the first-ever meeting of the three nuclear powers’ leaders, with Iran, Ukraine, trade, artificial intelligence and nuclear arms on the table — the last US–Russia arms cap expired in February. Analysts keep comparing the moment to Yalta in 1945, and keep warning that symbolism may outweigh substance.

Latest · Americas · Washington — 5,400 Cases18 November 2026

26 Years Undone in One Announcement: America Is No Longer Measles-Free

A milestone earned in 2000 has been formally withdrawn. The Pan American Health Organization announced on Tuesday that the United States has lost its measles elimination status, after finding the virus has circulated without interruption for more than a year. The country has recorded more than 5,400 cases in 2026 — the highest annual total since 1991, more than double last year’s 2,289 — across 45 states plus New York City and Washington, DC, with 43 outbreaks and about 10 deaths this year; 96% of cases were in people unvaccinated or of unknown status. Kindergarten coverage for the MMR vaccine stands at 92.4%, below the 95% needed for herd immunity, with some states as low as 75%. CDC Director Erica Schwartz responded that the designation itself does not protect Americans — vaccination does — while Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. vowed to stop outbreaks and regain the status, which now requires at least 12 months without sustained transmission. Mexico and Bolivia lost their status in the same review; the UK, Spain and several other countries lost theirs earlier this year.

A 19-Month Dollar High and a December Hike at 96%: Gold Breaks Its Floor

Gold’s worst year in more than a decade deepened on Wednesday. Spot prices fell nearly 2% to about $3,778 an ounce, touching the lowest level since September 2025, as the dollar held near a 19-month high and Treasury yields stayed close to 5.1%. New York Fed President John Williams said on Tuesday that another rate rise will likely be needed to lean against inflation, though he resisted calls for a more aggressive path — and markets now price roughly a 96% chance of a 25-basis-point hike in December, taking the target range to 4.00%–4.25%. Silver fell more than 2.5% in the same selloff. The next test arrives at 19:00 GMT, when the minutes of the Fed’s 27–28 October meeting — held on a 10–2 vote at 3.75%–4.00% — will show how broad the support for further tightening really is.

Latest · Europe · Kemerovo — An-12 Down18 November 2026

Nineteen Aboard, None Survived: A Soviet-Era Transport Falls in Siberia

Russia is investigating the loss of one of its oldest workhorses. An Antonov An-12 military transport — a four-engine turboprop in service since the late 1950s, normally crewed by six — crashed on Tuesday near the village of Yagunovo in the Kemerovo region of southwestern Siberia, about 3,000 kilometres east of Moscow, killing all 19 people on board. The Defence Ministry said the aircraft came down in an unpopulated area during a scheduled flight and that preliminary findings point to a technical malfunction; Governor Ilya Seredyuk said emergency crews extinguished a fire at the site. An Aerospace Forces commission is examining the wreckage, and the Investigative Committee has opened a criminal case under Article 351 into possible violations of flight safety rules, the standard procedure after such crashes. Early reports differed on the toll — one cited 17 — but the ministry’s figure of 19 was carried consistently by subsequent reporting. The victims have not been identified.

Latest · Africa · Eastern Congo — 11,000 Cases18 November 2026

Seven in Ten Under-Fives Die: Ebola’s Toll on Congo’s Children

Six months after it was declared, Congo’s Ebola epidemic has become the largest in the country’s history — and children are bearing a disproportionate share. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus told the third meeting of the Emergency Committee on Tuesday that cases have more than doubled in three months to over 11,000, with more than 5,400 deaths across 67 health zones in eight provinces, and that children are paying a terrible price. UNICEF figures to 4 November count 2,959 confirmed infections under 18 and 1,737 child deaths: children are 28% of cases but 35% of deaths, and nearly 72% of infected under-fives have died. Some 400 to 500 new cases are still reported each week, two-thirds of deaths occur outside treatment centres — a sign infections are found too late — and more than 2,280 children have been orphaned or separated. The outbreak is caused by the rare Bundibugyo virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment.

The Speaker Changes Hands First: Netanyahu Loses the Knesset’s Timetable

Israel’s transfer of power, if it comes, has started with the chair. Lawmakers on Tuesday elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party as the Knesset’s 26th speaker by 66 votes to 52 for Likud’s Ofir Katz, replacing Amir Ohana, a Netanyahu ally who had held the post since 2022. The speakership controls the parliamentary timetable, so the Eisenkot bloc — which has already installed interim chairs on the finance and defence committees — can now advance its pledge to roll back Netanyahu-era policies as early as Wednesday. The vote follows the deadlocked 27 October election, in which Netanyahu’s coalition won 53 of 120 seats and the opposition 67, including a record 19 for two Arab parties whose votes helped elect Kahana. President Isaac Herzog has given Eisenkot up to about four and a half more weeks to form a government; Netanyahu, who called the result a disgrace, remains caretaker prime minister, and Eisenkot’s own Jewish-party bloc holds only 48 seats.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Okinoshima — Three Minutes Each18 November 2026

Two Aircraft, Five Minutes, One Stern Protest: Japan Scrambles Over Okinoshima

Japan has lodged what it calls a very stern protest with Moscow after two suspected Russian aircraft entered its airspace before dawn on Tuesday. The Defence Ministry timed the incursions precisely: one aircraft over territorial waters northwest of Okinoshima Island, in Fukuoka Prefecture, from about 04:07 to 04:10, and a second from 04:09 to 04:12 — roughly three minutes each, overlapping for a minute. Air Self-Defense Force fighters scrambled and issued radio warnings; the aircraft then crossed the Tsushima Strait, passed between Yonaguni Island and Taiwan, and continued into the Pacific. Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi called the flights a serious infringement of sovereignty but declined to name the aircraft types or speculate on intent. South Korea said Russian aircraft also entered its air defence identification zone without violating its airspace. It is Japan’s first confirmed Russian airspace violation since September 2024, off Rebun Island in Hokkaido. Moscow has not commented.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Draft Under Fire18 November 2026

144 Paragraphs, Every Fault Line Included: COP31’s Draft Meets Its Ministers

COP31’s endgame has begun with a text designed to provoke. Ministers took over the Antalya talks on Tuesday to confront an 18-page, 144-paragraph draft cover decision, distilled by the Turkish and Australian presidencies from some 500 pages of submissions, that carries rival wordings side by side — including two options on fossil fuels, one repeating the COP28 pledge to transition away from them. The draft proposes tripling adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year by 2035, against estimated needs of about $305 billion, and an electrification goal of 35% of final energy use by 2035, up from about 23% today. Campaigners say the finance falls far short; large developing countries are pushing back on sectoral targets. In parallel, governments backed the Antalya Communiqué on extreme heat and sustainable cooling, with over $20 million in new support for the Global Cooling Pledge and Japan set to host the 2028 follow-up in Tokyo. Any final deal, due Friday, needs all 194 delegations.

Washington is signalling openness to Pyongyang — carefully, and without a date. Speaking to reporters in Manila early on Wednesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Donald Trump remains willing to meet North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, arguing that talking to adversaries should never be treated as a concession and that dialogue is how serious disagreements get resolved. Latest · Europe · Prague — 46 Injured18 November 2026

Low Speed, High Toll: Two Rush-Hour Trains Meet Head-On in Prague

Prague’s morning commute produced the Czech capital’s worst rail accident in years. At about 07:20 on Tuesday, two regional passenger trains — one from Mladá Boleslav, one bound for Milovice — collided head-on at low speed on the railway bridge over Seifertova Street in Prague 3, near the main station. Forty-six people were injured: four seriously, including both drivers, 12 moderately and 30 lightly; 16 of the injured were foreign nationals, among them 15 Ukrainians. Ten ambulance crews, four physician-led teams and a specialist evacuation vehicle responded, and services through the main station were disrupted for nearly six hours before full operations resumed around 13:00. Transport Minister Ivan Bednárik said early findings suggest one train passed a signal at danger; the Rail Inspectorate and police are investigating jointly, with material damage estimated above CZK 16 million. No deaths were reported.

Shallow, at 10 Kilometres: A Magnitude 6 Quake Strikes Papua New Guinea

A shallow earthquake struck Papua New Guinea on Wednesday, in one of the world’s most seismically active regions. The German Research Centre for Geosciences put the magnitude at 6.0, at a depth of just 10 kilometres — shallow enough for strong shaking near the epicentre — with an initial location of 5.82 degrees south, 146.65 degrees east, at 03:42 GMT. No reports of casualties or damage, and no tsunami warning, were available in the first hours, and no US Geological Survey reading had been published; agencies routinely revise magnitude, depth and location as more data arrives. Papua New Guinea sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and was last hit by a magnitude 6.4 quake in its Eastern New Guinea region on 20 September. Assessments from the epicentral area, where communications are often slow, are still awaited.
