Latest · Europe · France24 October 2026

France Deploys 70,000 Police and Shuts Paris Museums as Protests Return After a Week of Looting

France put around 70,000 police officers on the streets on Saturday for a new round of nationwide protests, 10,000 more than a week earlier, as the government tried to prevent a repeat of the looting and burning that shocked the country on October 17. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned officers to be hyper-reactive, with helicopters, armoured vehicles and water cannon deployed and soldiers patrolling the Champs-Élysées. In Paris the Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay, the Picasso Museum and the Grand Palais all closed for the day, Art Basel cancelled its weekend programme, and shopkeepers around Place de la République boarded up storefronts after some 3,000 shops were damaged or looted last weekend. The movement began with high-school students protesting learning conditions and has widened into anger over living costs and public services, drawing more than half a million people last week. It comes six months before elections to replace President Emmanuel Macron, with a new poll putting far-right leader Marine Le Pen — holding her party congress in Orléans this weekend — in front.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Philippines24 October 2026

Four Philippine Air Force Pilots Killed as Two Trainer Jets Collide Over Batangas Base

Four Philippine Air Force pilots were killed on Friday when two SF-260 trainer aircraft crashed during a training flight at Fernando Air Base in Batangas province, south of Manila, the military confirmed on Saturday. Local media reported the Italian-made planes collided in mid-air at about 3:51 p.m. and came down inside the base perimeter, with no other casualties. The air force has grounded its entire SF-260 fleet while investigators work out what happened, and said the pilots' names are being withheld at their families' request. The type has a troubled recent record in the Philippines: two pilots died in an SF-260 crash in the mountainous north in May, and another two were killed in a training crash in 2023. The aircraft were also used in combat during the months-long battle for Marawi City in 2017, after militants linked to Islamic State seized parts of the city.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Vanuatu24 October 2026

Tsunami Warning Lifted After 6.9-Magnitude Quake and a Swarm of Aftershocks Off Vanuatu

A tsunami warning issued after a powerful earthquake off Vanuatu on Saturday was lifted within the hour, the Pacific Tsunami Warning Centre said, after the shallow tremor — first measured at 7.2 and later revised to 6.9 — struck about 200 kilometres north-west of Port-Olry on the island of Santo at a depth of roughly 10 kilometres. A second quake of 6.3 followed nearby, and the US Geological Survey logged at least ten tremors of magnitude 5.0 or stronger between 02:31 and 05:23 UTC. Vanuatu's Geological Hazards Department had urged coastal residents to move to higher ground; there were no immediate reports of deaths or damage. Former prime minister Alatoi Ishmael Kalsakau said aftershocks were continuing and urged people to stay alert. Vanuatu, a low-lying archipelago of about 80 islands, sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is regularly shaken by strong quakes.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War24 October 2026

Ukraine Hits a Major Russian Petrochemical Plant as Both Sides Trade Massive Drone Barrages

Ukraine said on Saturday it had struck the Togliattikauchuk petrochemical plant — one of Russia's largest makers of synthetic rubber — in the Volga city of Togliatti, where the mayor confirmed several industrial sites were damaged but reported no casualties. Kyiv's general staff also claimed a hit on the Proton electronics plant in the Oryol region, which it says makes components for Iskander-M ballistic missiles and Pantsir-S1 air defences; Oryol's governor said three people were killed on Friday in a Ukrainian missile strike on a civilian facility there. Russia's defence ministry said its air defences destroyed 1,297 drones in 24 hours, along with a French-made SCALP cruise missile and four Neptune missiles. Ukraine, for its part, said Russia launched at least 126 drones overnight, of which 101 were shot down, wounding two people in Kyiv. Ukrainian officials also reported that Starlink connectivity, disrupted for a week, had returned to normal speeds, while a separate strike cut water and power in Russian-occupied Crimea and killed three civilians in occupied Kharkiv region, according to Moscow-installed officials.

Latest · Middle East · West Bank24 October 2026

Israel Launches Days-Long West Bank Operation Around Hebron After Two Soldiers Are Killed Days Before an Election

Israel's military launched an operation in the occupied West Bank on Friday that it says will run for several days, with searches, questioning and building demolitions centred on Hebron, after two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three others at the Farsh al-Hawa checkpoint. Israeli forces shot both attackers dead at the scene, where an AFP photographer witnessed the exchange of fire. In a joint statement, the army and the Shin Bet said troops were working at dozens of locations to dismantle militant infrastructure and were demolishing structures deemed a security threat. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, locked in a tight race ahead of Tuesday's election, visited Hebron, ordered a lockdown and said he and Defence Minister Israel Katz were considering an operation on a scale not seen since 1967. Hamas praised the attack as heroic without claiming responsibility and called for more. Security has become the central issue of the campaign in its final days.

Latest · Americas · United States24 October 2026

Trump Lifts 50% Tariffs on Some Canadian Goods Days Before the Midterms — and Adds New Ones Elsewhere

President Donald Trump signed an order on Friday removing the 50% tariffs he imposed in August on a set of Canadian agricultural and forestry products, in a move the White House linked to lobbying by Maine Senator Susan Collins, who faces a tough re-election fight in the November 3 midterms. The exemptions, taking effect on October 30, cover plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and refrigeration equipment, packing crates and boxes, and fresh and dried seaweed — products Collins said are crucial to Maine's blueberry, potato, apple and seaweed producers. At the same time Trump added other Canadian goods to the tariff list, including carpets, paints, shampoos, cocoa powder, wooden coffins, ice-cream sticks, air-conditioning machines, chest freezers, archery equipment and canoes. Canada sends roughly 70% of its exports to the United States, and ties have been strained since trade talks broke down in August. Ottawa's US-Canada trade minister Dominic LeBlanc said Canada takes note of the measures.

Latest · Americas · Brazil24 October 2026

Brazil Goes Into Sunday's Presidential Runoff in a Statistical Dead Heat

Brazilians vote on Sunday in a presidential runoff that polls say is too close to call, between Senator Flávio Bolsonaro and incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. A Datafolha survey of 2,154 voters taken October 20–22 put Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, on 47% and Lula on 46%, inside the poll's two-point margin of error, with 5% planning a blank vote and 2% undecided — almost unchanged from a week earlier. Bolsonaro surprised forecasters by leading the October 4 first round with 47.03% to Lula's 45.16%, after most surveys had Lula ahead. The campaign has been dominated by the cost of living, corruption and public insecurity, and both frontrunners skipped the major television debates. A Bolsonaro win would extend a run of right-wing victories in Latin America by candidates aligned with President Trump; a Lula win would keep Brazil at arm's length from Washington and closer to China. The winner takes office on January 5, 2027, leading Latin America's largest economy and its 214 million people.

American Express Cuts 3,500 Jobs Despite Rising Profits, as Volvo Sheds 2,500 More and Shares Plunge

American Express said on Friday it will cut about 3,500 jobs — 5% of its global workforce — even as it reported a 9% rise in third-quarter sales to $3.71 billion in profit terms and raised its outlook for the year, saying customers keep spending. Chief executive Stephen Squeri told analysts the company needed to become more agile and efficient to accelerate innovation, and the firm booked a $336 million restructuring charge as it prepares for wider AI automation. Shares fell as much as 3%. The announcement capped a week of large layoffs: Barclays said Thursday it will cut 5,000 jobs and eBay 1,000, and earlier on Friday Volvo Cars said it will cut another 2,500 jobs, about 6% of its staff, on top of 3,000 cuts earlier this year, aiming to save a further $500 million. The Swedish carmaker, majority-owned by China's Geely, is struggling in a weak Chinese market against intense competition; its shares fell 25% in Friday trading after a third-quarter loss.

Latest · Global Affairs · Iran24 October 2026

UN Nuclear Chief Says Inspectors Are 'Ready' to Return to Iran — but Only a Deal Will Last

The head of the International Atomic Energy Agency, Rafael Grossi, told the UN General Assembly on Friday that the agency has finished most of the technical groundwork needed to verify Iran's nuclear activities and is ready to resume inspections, but warned there will be no lasting, stable solution without a diplomatic agreement. Iran has sharply restricted access since the June 2025 strikes on its nuclear sites during a 12-day war with Israel, and in September the IAEA board referred Iran's non-compliance to the Security Council — a move Tehran contests. The agency estimated in March that just over 200 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60% remained at Isfahan, with more at Natanz, and Grossi has said his inspectors cannot currently account for every gram. He urged all sides to redouble efforts and return to the negotiating table, noting the IAEA has been working with Pakistan, Qatar, Oman and Egypt to rebuild a verification framework, and pointing to its work in Ukraine, where it has brokered eight local ceasefires to repair power lines at the Zaporizhzhia plant.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza24 October 2026

Gaza Faces a Renewed Famine Threat as Aid Is Cut by a Third and Commercial Food Shipments Stall

Gaza's residents are warning of a slide back towards famine as both aid and commercial supplies shrink at the same time, a year into a ceasefire that has not stopped Israeli strikes. The World Food Programme said on October 19 that funding shortages had forced it to cut food assistance by about a third, leaving some 500,000 people without critical support, while an estimated 1.4 million people — 67% of the population — already face crisis levels of acute food insecurity or worse as winter approaches. Commercial shipments were halted for days in a dispute over a 10,000-shekel-per-truck fee Israel said Hamas was demanding; Hamas called it a voluntary contribution to a relief fund, and the head of Gaza's truckers' union blamed Israeli restrictions, saying shortages of vegetables and bread should ease by the start of next week after assurances to traders. Israel said on Monday it would resume shipments gradually, prioritising essentials, with tighter oversight. Gaza health officials say about 1,500 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, and more than 74,400 since October 2023.
