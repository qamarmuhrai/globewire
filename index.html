Latest · Middle East · Kuwait — Air-Defence Station Hit26 November 2026

Two Italian Technicians Killed in Drone Strike in Kuwait as Iran Denies Role

Two Italian technicians working on an air-defence system in Kuwait were killed on Thursday when drones struck a military installation, in what Rome and Kuwait City described as an Iranian attack and Tehran denied carrying out. Italy’s foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, told the Senate the men were Rheinmetall Italia staff maintaining a Skyguard surveillance station when several Iranian-made drones targeted Kuwaiti sites around 2 p.m.; a powerful explosion killed both men. A Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were also injured. Kuwait’s Defence Ministry said its forces had detected and engaged multiple drones and confirmed material damage at a vital facility. Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, told his Kuwaiti and Italian counterparts that Tehran had no involvement. The strike was the second reported Iranian attack on the US-allied Gulf state this month, as fighting over the Strait of Hormuz continues to spill across the region.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War — Zaporizhzhia Plant27 November 2026

UN Watchdog Seeks Local Ceasefire to Restore Power at Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Plant

The International Atomic Energy Agency said on Thursday it is working with Russia and Ukraine to arrange a fresh local ceasefire around the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant so crews can repair its external power link. Both offsite lines are down for the 28th time since the war began, the agency said, leaving Europe’s largest nuclear station — seized by Russian forces in early 2022 and currently producing no electricity — dependent on diesel generators to keep fuel cool. The outage was reportedly linked to military activity, while the plant’s Russian operators said protective systems had triggered the disconnection and the generators were working normally. IAEA chief Rafael Grossi called the front-line location and repeated outages an extremely precarious situation and urged both sides to halt fighting nearby and allow repairs to proceed.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · The Hague — ICC Trial Opens Monday27 November 2026

Families Seek Justice as Duterte Faces Crimes-Against-Humanity Trial at The Hague

Former Philippine president Rodrigo Duterte, 81, is due to go on trial at the International Criminal Court on Monday over his six-year anti-drug campaign, in one of the most closely watched cases in the court’s history. Prosecutors link him to 85 murders and two attempted murders between 2011 and 2018, spanning his years as Davao City mayor and the start of his presidency, and say those cases sample a wider toll that may reach 30,000 people killed by death squads; Philippine authorities have acknowledged 6,248 deaths. More than 500 alleged victims will be represented, and a conviction on any of three counts could bring a life sentence. Duterte’s lawyers deny any state policy to murder and say he is unfit to stand trial, though judges have ordered proceedings to go ahead with shortened sessions. Relatives are travelling to The Hague for the opening, while his daughter, Vice President Sara Duterte, argues no foreign court should override Philippine sovereignty. The trial also tests an ICC under pressure from US sanctions and calls for its closure.

Latest · Americas · United States — Thanksgiving Storm26 November 2026

Thanksgiving Storm Floods US East Coast, Cuts Power to Over a Million and Delays the Parade

A powerful coastal storm battered the US East Coast on Thanksgiving Day, bringing gusts of up to 70 mph, heavy rain and coastal flooding that knocked out power to more than a million homes and businesses by Thursday evening. Virginia and Maryland were hit hardest, with roughly 200,000 and 177,000 customers dark respectively, alongside tens of thousands more in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and North Carolina; snow added another 116,000 outages in Missouri. FlightAware counted more than 400 cancellations and over 2,000 delays, with Washington Dulles and New York’s JFK among the worst affected, while Maryland closed the Bay Bridge in both directions. In New York, a 56 mph gust at LaGuardia helped force the first weather postponement in the 100-year history of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, now rescheduled to Saturday. Forecasters said the system would move away overnight into Friday.

Latest · Americas · Diplomacy — G20 in Florida27 November 2026

Trump Says Putin Is Welcome at Florida G20 if He Wants to Attend

President Donald Trump said on Thursday that Russian President Vladimir Putin could attend next month’s G20 summit in Florida if he wishes, even as he acknowledged others would object. Speaking to reporters at his Florida golf resort during the Thanksgiving holiday, Trump said he did not know whether Putin would come to the 14–15 December meeting but added that, if it were up to him, he would like to have him there — while doubting Putin himself would want to make the trip. The Kremlin said no decision had yet been taken. Putin said after meeting Trump last week he would consider attending once he had weighed the situation in Russia. Earlier on Thursday, Trump also posted that he wanted Russia to pull back on military aggression across Europe, saying he hoped that could help produce a quick settlement of the war in Ukraine.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Defence Budget Debate27 November 2026

Japan Weighs $353 Billion Five-Year Defence Plan, Short of What the Military Sought

Japan’s government is considering a defence budget of 56.3 trillion yen, about $353 billion, for the five years from fiscal 2027, the Asahi newspaper reported — a rise from the current plan’s 43 trillion yen but well below the 80 trillion yen some defence officials and ruling-party lawmakers had expected. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said on Friday that spending should follow actual defence needs rather than a preset figure. On Reuters calculations, the reported total would still leave Japan short of its goal of lifting defence outlays to 2 percent of GDP, while Washington has pressed allies including Tokyo to reach 3.5 percent. Asahi said the government plans to brief the United States on its thinking and will revise its national security strategy and defence documents by year-end; the final figure could still be adjusted upward.

Latest · Africa · Guinea-Bissau — Vote Pushed to 202826 November 2026

Guinea-Bissau Pushes Elections to 2028, Extending Military Transition After Coup

Guinea-Bissau’s military rulers have postponed presidential and legislative elections by roughly two years, abandoning the one-year transition promised after last November’s coup. A decree from transitional president Major-General Horta Inta-a moves the vote, originally due next month, to November 2028, saying the country lacks the technical, material and financial conditions for a credible ballot. The army seized power last year as a presidential vote was under way, ousting incumbent Umaro Sissoco Embalo as he sought another term. The regional bloc ECOWAS had pressed for a short, structured and transparent return to civilian rule, and voters in August approved constitutional changes expanding presidential powers. Prime Minister Ilidio Vieira Te had already warned last month that December elections were not feasible, though he offered no new date at the time.

Latest · Americas · Venezuela — Debt Talks26 November 2026

US and Qatar Bridge Loans Could Help Venezuela Clear Development-Bank Arrears

Venezuela is negotiating separate bridge loans with the US Treasury and Qatar to settle arrears owed to two regional development banks, a step that could reopen multilateral lending after years of sanctions, four people familiar with the talks said. Under one plan, Treasury money from its Exchange Stabilization Fund would clear Caracas’s $2.735 billion in principal and interest owed to the Inter-American Development Bank, which cannot lend while a borrower is in arrears; new IDB lending could then help repay Washington, leaving Venezuela ultimately liable. Qatar is separately considering a bridge loan covering the $1.423 billion owed to CAF, the Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean. Acting President Delcy Rodriguez has put potential multilateral financing at about $10 billion, down from $17 billion cited earlier this month, while Caracas this week put total public-sector liabilities at $237.5 billion at the end of 2025, or 242 percent of GDP.

Latest · Europe · Romania — Opposition Leader Charged26 November 2026

Romania Charges Hard-Right Leader George Simion With Inciting Violence

Romanian prosecutors have formally charged George Simion, leader of the opposition Alliance for Uniting Romanians, with inciting violence against election officials ahead of last year’s contested presidential contest. The case stems from March, when election authorities barred far-right frontrunner Calin Georgescu from a re-run after the original December 2024 vote was cancelled over suspected Russian interference — a move that sparked violent protests in Bucharest. Prosecutors say Simion told supporters online that officials who staged a “coup” should be “skinned alive in the public square”; he later said he did not mean it literally, and his lawyer calls the remark protected political hyperbole. Simion, who lost the May 2025 re-run to centrist Nicusor Dan, dismissed the charge on Thursday as a politically motivated “judicial farce.” His EU-critical party, which opposes military aid to Ukraine, remains Romania’s most popular in polls at about 35 percent ahead of the 2028 general election.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Oil Near Month High27 November 2026

Shares Steady After a Bruising Month as Oil Stays High on Hormuz and Refinery Attacks

Asian markets steadied on Friday after a punishing November, helped by a US holiday lull and firmer futures, while oil held near its highest levels of the month on fears for Middle East supply. Japan’s Nikkei rose 0.7 percent, South Korea gained 2 percent and the broader Asia-Pacific index added 0.5 percent, though it remains down 8.2 percent for the month; S&P 500 futures were up 0.8 percent. Tokyo core inflation hit a 16-month high, strengthening expectations of a Bank of Japan rate rise. Brent crude slipped 0.4 percent to about $96.14 a barrel but was still up 3.9 percent for the week — its first weekly gain in four — as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites and Ukrainian drone hits on Russian refineries, including a fire at the Slavyansk ECO plant, tightened fuel supplies. The dollar headed for a third straight monthly gain, while Nvidia remained a drag after S&P placed its credit rating on downgrade watch.
