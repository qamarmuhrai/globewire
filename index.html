France Braces for a New Weekend of Protests as Yellow Vests File Plans for a March Through Paris

France's Yellow Vest movement has filed plans for a major march through central Paris on Saturday, setting up the government's next big test a week after rioting and looting swept French cities. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu was due to meet union leaders on Wednesday to try to defuse the crisis, while ministers promised a vastly reinforced security operation after admitting the response on 17 October had been too static, with intelligence failures and police leave left uncancelled. Troops from the counterterrorism force will back up police and gendarmes in largely static roles so that officers can move faster against violence. Targeted overnight curfews in Paris and other cities have restored calm since the weekend, when the government estimates some 550,000 people protested against rising living costs and deteriorating public services — organisers put the figure at 800,000 — and around 3,000 shops were looted. Investor anxiety has pushed France's benchmark 10-year borrowing costs to 5 percent, their highest since July 2002, and President Emmanuel Macron is to meet intelligence chiefs on Thursday ahead of the new demonstrations, which student groups also plan to join with demands for more education funding.

Drone Strike on a Market in Disputed Abyei Kills at Least 153, Officials Say

At least 153 people were killed when a drone strike hit Amiet Market in Abyei, the oil-rich border region disputed by Sudan and South Sudan, and ignited a nearby fuel depot, local officials said. Yohana Akol Ngor, the Abyei administration's caretaker information minister, said the toll from Monday's attack had climbed from 73 to 153 as the fire kept burning and recovery work continued, and he blamed Sudan's army for the strike. A senior Sudanese military officer said the army had targeted fuel tankers arriving from South Sudan that were bound for the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, insisting those killed were RSF members and fuel smugglers — an account that could not be independently verified. Doctors Without Borders said more than 20 wounded people, several in critical condition and others with burns, were treated at Ameth Bek Hospital. South Sudan's foreign ministry summoned Sudan's ambassador to protest and urged Khartoum to keep Abyei's status a matter for diplomacy. Sudan's war between the army and the RSF, raging since April 2023, has increasingly spilled across the country's borders.

Government Websites Go Dark Across Europe as Critical Flaws in PHP Spark a Patching Scramble

Government websites in the Netherlands, Finland, Germany, France and Denmark were taken offline on Wednesday after two critical vulnerabilities were disclosed in PHP, the programming language behind a large share of the internet. The flaws had lain hidden in the software for years until they were uncovered with the help of artificial intelligence, and PHP's developers released emergency fixes on Wednesday afternoon, urging all users to update immediately. The Dutch government's main portal, the Port of Rotterdam site and hospital websites were among those pulled down as a precaution — the Netherlands' own cybersecurity agency took its site offline too — while Germany's federal IT provider ITZBund deactivated several sites and Denmark suspended services including a national school platform. The EU's cybersecurity response team, CERT-EU, had advised organisations that could not patch in time to consider going dark, and Cloudflare said it was already seeing early attacks. PHP powers roughly three-quarters of websites whose server-side language is known, including WordPress, Drupal and Magento sites, and experts warned that because AI tools can reverse-engineer the bug quickly, a race to exploit unpatched servers is under way.

Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia Sign 'Badr' Defence Industry Pact in Doha

Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia signed a defence cooperation agreement in Doha on Wednesday aimed at joint production, technology transfer and closer integration of their defence industries. The 'Badr Memorandum of Understanding' was signed by Turkish Defence Minister Yaşar Güler, Qatar's deputy prime minister and defence minister Sheikh Saud bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, Indonesian Deputy Defence Minister Yusuf Jauhari and Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Muhammad Asif. Türkiye's defence ministry said the accord would foster cooperation in military production, technical training and research and development, drawing on each country's complementary manufacturing strengths. Asif said the pact reflected a shared commitment to regional peace and opened the way to greater self-reliance and industrial collaboration. The deal builds on the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement signed in August by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye, under which an attack on one party is treated as an attack on all, and extends Ankara's push — backed by NATO's second-largest army and a fast-growing arms industry — to build multilateral defence partnerships across the Gulf and Asia.

Gulf States Hold Extraordinary Military Meeting in Riyadh as Houthi Attacks on Saudi Arabia Escalate

The six Gulf Cooperation Council states held an extraordinary meeting in Riyadh on Wednesday to tighten coordination among their armed forces, as Yemen's Houthi movement steps up attacks on Saudi Arabia. The talks, convened by the GCC's Armed Forces Operations and Training Committee at the bloc's secretariat headquarters, covered joint military cooperation in operations and training and ways to raise readiness and efficiency, according to a UAE defence ministry statement. The GCC — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman — issued no decisions from the meeting, but its secretary-general, Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi, had called on Sunday for effective international action against the Houthis. The gathering follows Houthi strikes in recent days on Riyadh's international airport and facilities of state oil giant Saudi Aramco, and comes as fighting in Yemen intensifies between the Houthis and Saudi-backed government forces after the collapse of a truce that had largely held since 2022.

Egypt's Sisi Says Any Saudi Defence Role Would Be Defensive Only, With No Attack on Yemen

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi has said any Egyptian military effort in support of Saudi Arabia would be limited to defending the kingdom's territory and borders and would not include an attack on Yemen, according to a presidency statement on Wednesday. The clarification came as Egypt's parliament delayed until 26 October a vote on authorising the armed forces to support Saudi Arabia if requested, extending debate over a deeper Egyptian role after Houthi attacks on Riyadh's airport and Saudi Aramco facilities. Sisi said Egypt would also defend any other Arab country's territory if asked, framing support for Gulf states as part of shared Arab national security, and that Cairo would act in line with the directives of Saudi King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who visited Cairo on 15 September to discuss security and Red Sea navigation. Egypt and Saudi Arabia are close strategic and economic partners, and Cairo joined the Saudi-led coalition that intervened in Yemen in 2015, though its role has remained limited.

Seoul Vows 'Resolute Punishment' After North Korea Threatens New Border Weapons and Broadcasts

South Korea has vowed a stern response if North Korea acts on threats to deploy more tactical strike weapons along their border and resume propaganda loudspeaker broadcasts. In a statement on Tuesday, Seoul's defence ministry expressed grave concern at Pyongyang's threats and said South Korea's own broadcasts and warning shots near the Military Demarcation Line were legitimate defensive measures to stop incursions, warning that if the North used them as a pretext for further provocations, 'we will punish it resolutely.' The exchange of threats raises the risk of clashes along the heavily fortified border and undercuts the diplomatic opening pursued by President Lee Jae Myung since taking office.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate21 October 2026

UN Says Overshooting the 1.5°C Warming Limit Is Now Unavoidable, but Deep Cuts Can Still Bring It Back

The world can no longer avoid breaching the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C warming limit, the UN Environment Programme concluded in its Emissions Gap Report 2026, published on Wednesday under the title 'The Point of Return.' Decades of insufficient action have made some overshoot inevitable, the report found, but rapid cuts could cap peak warming at about 1.7°C to 1.8°C around mid-century and bring temperatures back to 1.5°C by 2100. That lowest-overshoot path requires global emissions to fall 18 percent by 2030, 45 percent by 2035 and 69 percent by 2040 compared with 2025 — while countries' current pledges, even if fully delivered, would cut emissions by only 5 to 8 percent by 2030. On current policies, warming is headed for roughly 2.6°C to 2.8°C this century, and global emissions in 2025 stayed near record levels at an estimated 58.4 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent. 'Overshoot is not surrender,' Secretary-General António Guterres said, urging governments to keep the exceedance as small and as short as possible ahead of November's COP31 summit.

Russia to Send Its First Diesel Cargo to the United States on Thursday, Novak Says

Russia will dispatch what it describes as its first diesel cargo to the United States on Thursday, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said, in a striking turn for energy ties between the two countries. The vessel carries about 40,000 metric tons of diesel, and Novak said the paperwork governing the supply was nearing completion. The shipment follows Washington's recent decision to lift sanctions on sales of Russian diesel fuel — a step Moscow publicly welcomed — and a reported US deal to buy Russian diesel as American fuel needs reshape policy toward Moscow. Direct Russian fuel deliveries to the United States had been frozen out by the sanctions imposed after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, so even a single cargo carries weight as a signal: it lands while negotiators pursue the Ukraine peace track that President Vladimir Putin agreed in principle this week to resume in trilateral form with Kyiv and Washington.
