Latest · Europe · France25 October 2026

France Arrests Nearly 1,000 as Cost-of-Living Marches Turn Violent for a Second Weekend

Hundreds of thousands of people marched across France on Saturday against rising living costs and inequality, in a second consecutive weekend of unrest that again ended in clashes. The Interior Ministry put nationwide turnout at 410,000, while the organising CGT union said the real figure was twice that; the main Paris march drew at least 110,000 people. After the rallies ended, riot police fired tear gas and water cannon at hooded youths in the capital, while in Marseille protesters set fire to rubbish bins, hurled rocks and bottles at security forces and ransacked several shops, including two jewellery stores. Police arrested nearly 1,000 people and two were seriously injured, in Toulouse and Clermont-Ferrand. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez blamed black-clad “ultra-left” extremists for an “extremely elevated level of violence,” despite 70,000 police and gendarmes being deployed. The unrest comes six months before France votes on a successor to President Emmanuel Macron.

Protesters Waving Palestinian Flags Enter Rome's Colosseum as Unrest Spreads to Italy

Hundreds of young protesters entered Rome's Colosseum on Saturday evening, cheering and chanting inside the 2,000-year-old arena before security forces pushed them out, as the protest wave sweeping France spread across Italy. Thousands marched in Rome, Milan and Turin against the war in Gaza, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government and the rising cost of living. Police used tear gas and water cannon against demonstrators near Rome's Piazza della Repubblica, and clashes were also reported in Turin and Bologna. In Milan, protesters dumped manure outside the national energy regulator and burned an oversized electricity bill, while many shops boarded up their windows fearing a repeat of last week's looting in France. Meloni sent about 500 soldiers to reinforce 5,000 officers in Milan; the Colosseum itself had been declared a security “red zone” last month after repeated incidents at the site.

“Maybe Tomorrow”: Trump Hints at Fresh Iran Strikes While Floating a Deal

President Donald Trump refused to say on Saturday whether the United States would resume strikes on Iran, telling reporters in Florida that an attack could come as early as Sunday — or that Washington might instead reach what he called a “nice, big, beautiful agreement” with Tehran. Speaking after casting his midterm ballot, Trump questioned whether Iran would honour any deal, a day after warning at a Florida rally that the US could launch “another little Epic Fury,” the name of the campaign that opened the war on February 28. Tehran has denied reports that it offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for lifting the US naval blockade. The eight-month conflict remains at a stalemate, with anxiety growing that strikes could resume around Israel's October 27 election or the November 3 US midterms.

Latest · Middle East · Israel25 October 2026

Israel to Close All West Bank Crossings on Election Day as Hebron Operation Widens

Israel said on Sunday that every land crossing with the occupied West Bank will close on Tuesday, October 27, when the country votes, and that Palestinian workers will be barred from settlements and industrial zones through election day. The lockdown follows a Friday checkpoint attack near Hebron in which gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three, and a military push into Hebron that has displaced about 2,000 residents. Defence Minister Israel Katz said those forced out would not be allowed back until Israel had finished “dealing with all the Hamas issues in Hebron,” while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu promised an operation on a scale not seen since 1967. Netanyahu faces a tight race for a new term, his security-first reputation badly damaged by the October 7, 2023 attack. The Palestinian Authority called for international pressure to protect residents caught up in the operation.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine25 October 2026

Ukraine's Starlink Speeds Restored as Musk Admits SpaceX Throttled the Service

Ukraine's access to the Starlink and Starshield satellite networks has been restored to full speed after restrictions disrupted front-line operations for more than a week, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Saturday, thanking US officials who intervened. Elon Musk then admitted that his company SpaceX had throttled Starlink, writing that using the civilian service “for offensive drone strikes to kill people has never been allowed” and that Ukraine's military had violated its terms — a “loophole” he said was closed last week. Musk stressed that Starshield, the military-grade system that underpins Ukraine's drone operations, is controlled by the US government, and warned that switching Starlink off entirely would collapse Ukraine's front line immediately — “NOT a threat,” he wrote, “simply a statement of fact.” The restoration follows a renewed US diplomatic push: Russia agreed on Wednesday to resume three-way talks with Washington and Kyiv next month, and the two sides accepted a localised ceasefire around the occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson region.

Twelve Injured, Six of Them Children, in Shooting Near Florida Stadium

At least 12 people were injured in a shooting late on Saturday near Florida A&M University's Bragg Memorial Stadium in Tallahassee, during the university's homecoming weekend. Police said 11 of the injured had gunshot wounds; six were adults and six were juveniles, the youngest aged 11, and a twelfth person — a victim's grandmother — was hurt while fleeing. One victim was in critical condition and two others seriously injured. The gunfire broke out just before 8 p.m. near Perry and Manatee streets after a fight between two or three people, which Police Chief Lawrence Revell called an isolated incident. No arrests had been made. Officers carried victims to hospital through the crowds, and Revell appealed for witnesses to come forward.

Latest · Africa · Uganda25 October 2026

Uganda Orders 21-Day Quarantine for Citizens Returning From Congo as Ebola Fears Spread

Ugandan citizens coming home after travelling to the Democratic Republic of Congo will now be quarantined for 21 days, Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi announced on Sunday, as the country tightens Ebola precautions while insisting it remains free of the virus. Kampala has also barred travellers with a recent Congo history from transiting through Uganda at all, and reinstated passenger locator forms for everyone entering by air, land or water, with screening stepped up at Entebbe airport and land borders. The measures follow the case of a Nigerian woman who spent two weeks in Uganda before testing positive for Bundibugyo Ebola in Abuja, where she was diagnosed on October 20; genomic analysis has linked Nigeria's case to the strain found in Kenya's index case. Baryomunsi said all 60 contacts traced from a Kenyan case had completed their monitoring period and been discharged. Congo, where the current outbreak began, is battling a fast-spreading epidemic, and Uganda has set up three treatment units across the border with 240 beds.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria24 October 2026

Nigeria's Tinubu Launches Re-Election Campaign as Cost-of-Living Crisis Tests His Support

President Bola Tinubu formally opened his campaign for a second term on Saturday, telling thousands of supporters in the northern city of Kaduna that his painful economic reforms are starting to pay off. “Come 2027, January 16, you must go out massively and elect me again,” the 74-year-old told the crowd at Ranchers Bees Stadium ahead of a presidential election expected to be dominated by the economy and security. Since taking office in 2023, Tinubu has scrapped fuel subsidies and devalued the naira — moves credited with strengthening public finances and reserves but blamed for Nigeria's worst cost-of-living crisis in a generation, with petrol selling at about 1,400 naira a litre in Lagos and Abuja in September. Tinubu dismissed opposition complaints about hunger and poverty — “They created the hunger. They created the poverty. We are trying to eradicate it” — drawing a sharp reply from the campaign of his main challenger, former vice president Atiku Abubakar, whose spokesman called the remark “delusion or a staggering case of political amnesia.” Peter Obi, who finished third in 2023, is also running again.

Cyclone Arnab Bears Down on India's East Coast; Odisha Cancels Leave and Braces for Landfall

India's east coast is preparing for Cyclone Arnab, which the India Meteorological Department expects to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Tuesday morning, October 28, as a very severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h and gusts to 140 km/h. Neighbouring Odisha has cancelled leave for district officials, positioned rescue teams and shut schools and colleges in five southern districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri — for October 28 and 29. Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said the state would take no chances despite the forecast landfall in Andhra Pradesh, restating Odisha's zero-casualty goal. Heavy rain and winds of 70–90 km/h are expected across Odisha's southern and coastal districts, and President Droupadi Murmu has postponed a planned visit to the state.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate25 October 2026

El Niño Strengthens Toward a December Peak That Could Rank Among the Strongest Since 1950

The El Niño now under way in the Pacific is intensifying fast and is expected to peak in December, the World Meteorological Organization says in its latest seasonal outlook, with sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific forecast to reach record levels. The agency's Niño 3.4 index is projected to average about 3.8°C above the reference level from November to January, up from 2.8°C in September, and forecasters see a near-100% likelihood that the event persists through February 2027 — potentially one of the strongest since at least 1950. Above-normal temperatures are likely across much of Africa, southern Europe, the Arabian Peninsula, India, East Asia and parts of North America, while shifting rainfall patterns raise the risk of drought in some regions and flooding in others. WMO chief Celeste Saulo noted that El Niño releases heat stored in the ocean rather than creating it: “The ocean it's drawing from is the warmest we've ever measured it. There is simply more heat available to release.”
