Latest · Asia-Pacific · Diplomacy — A Possible Three-Way Summit9 November 2026

Trump, Xi and Putin in One Room? Beijing Confirms Talks on a Rare Trilateral Meeting at APEC

President Donald Trump says he will likely sit down with both Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ gathering in Shenzhen on 18–19 November — a three-way encounter with no recent precedent. Beijing said on Monday it was “in communication with relevant parties” and did not rule the meeting out. Trump told reporters on Sunday that the war in Ukraine, the Iran conflict and nuclear arms control would be on the agenda. The Shenzhen trip would also give Trump and Xi their third bilateral meeting this year, after visits to Beijing in May and Washington in September, as the two sides try to hold a fragile trade truce that expires in January. Analysts caution that clashing interests may limit the session to symbolism and a carefully staged photograph, with Putin — isolated by much of the West — arguably gaining most simply by appearing alongside the other two leaders.

Latest · Europe · Diplomacy — G7 Meets in Paris9 November 2026

“Europe Must Have a Place at the Table”: G7 Ministers Press Rubio on Ukraine and Iran

Foreign ministers from the G7 nations opened two days of talks in Paris on Monday, the first such meeting since the Republican defeat in the US midterms, with European governments set on two messages for Secretary of State Marco Rubio: any Ukraine settlement must be fair to Kyiv, and Washington should steer clear of fresh military escalation against Iran. The gathering — moved from Marseille to the capital after weeks of protests across France — comes a day before US-mediated talks between Ukraine, Russia and the United States open in Abu Dhabi, and French foreign minister Jean-Noël Barrot has insisted Europeans will be represented there. Germany’s Johann Wadephul said Europe did not need a new security architecture designed over its head and warned the G7 “cannot afford any military escalation” with Iran. Rubio, on his first trip abroad since the midterms, countered that there had been “no progress” with Tehran, whose demands he called unacceptable. Ukraine, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea and Brazil were also invited to parts of the meeting.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Blackouts Before the Talks9 November 2026

600,000 Left in the Dark: Russia Hammers Ukraine’s Power Grid on the Eve of Abu Dhabi

Russian strikes on Ukraine’s energy network have cut electricity to hundreds of thousands of homes as Kyiv heads into another wartime winter. The power company DTEK said more than 600,000 residents of the central Dnipropetrovsk region lost supply after a night attack damaged key facilities, with Kharkiv region also hit and hospitals in Dnipro forced onto diesel generators while electric transport ground to a halt. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said outages spread across the country — in Zhytomyr region alone, 140 apartment buildings lost heating — and accused Moscow of deliberately intensifying the bombardment to weaken Kyiv’s hand at the US-brokered trilateral talks opening in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. Ukraine’s energy minister says the grid has been struck more than 350 times since September. Secretary of State Marco Rubio says work has advanced on a postwar “security infrastructure” for Ukraine, a question that will dominate both the G7 meeting in Paris and the Abu Dhabi session, where France will also sit in.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Aramco Tower Hit9 November 2026

Shrapnel Rains on Riyadh’s Financial District as Houthis Target the Aramco Tower

Saudi civil defence told Riyadh residents the danger had passed on Monday, a day after a Houthi missile and drone attack damaged the Aramco Tower in the capital’s King Abdullah Financial District. Debris from the attempted strike blew out windows across several floors of the 36-storey building; the energy ministry described the damage as limited and said there were no casualties, while the Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted two ballistic missiles over the capital. The Houthis claimed four operations in all, listing the financial district, Riyadh’s airport, Aramco oil storage and the eastern city of Abqaiq among their targets. Fighting between the kingdom and the Iran-aligned group resumed in July after a 2022 truce collapsed, adding a Saudi–Yemeni front to the wider regional war, and Houthi strikes on Saudi airports, ports and oil sites have stepped up in recent months — one attack on the capital’s airport last month killed 13 people.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Winter Rains Flood the Camps9 November 2026

“Tents Don’t Protect Us”: Storm Floods 70,000 Shelters and Leaves 350,000 Exposed in Gaza

The first heavy winter storm of the season has turned Gaza’s tent camps into mud, flooding more than 70,000 tents within about 12 hours of the rain starting on Friday, according to the territory’s government media office, and leaving an estimated 350,000 displaced people without effective shelter. Families described water rushing in at night, soaking mattresses, blankets and stored food; in Deir al-Balah, a collapsed sand embankment killed a woman and four children, and civil defence teams said fuel and equipment shortages meant they could answer only a fraction of thousands of distress calls. The UN humanitarian office estimates Gaza needs roughly 121,000 tents and 561,000 tarpaulins, while only about 20,000 tents have been allowed in and tens of thousands of mobile homes remain waiting on the Egyptian side of the border. Pakistan’s prime minister Shehbaz Sharif said on Monday he had ordered his foreign ministry to urgently escalate humanitarian assistance. For most of Gaza’s displaced, this is a fourth winter living under canvas.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Eisenkot Gets the Mandate8 November 2026

A General’s “National Emergency Government”: Eisenkot Opens the Door Even to Netanyahu

Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, formally handed the task of forming Israel’s next government by President Isaac Herzog on Sunday, has said he is open to including Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud — and even far-right finance minister Bezalel Smotrich — in a broad coalition. “We are in a state of emergency, approaching a point of no return for the State of Israel,” Eisenkot said, promising creative and unconventional steps. The arithmetic is unforgiving: the 27 October election gave Eisenkot’s four-party alliance 48 seats and Netanyahu’s bloc 53, both short of the 61 needed in the 120-seat Knesset, although 67 lawmakers recommended Eisenkot for the mandate, including Arab-led parties he has ruled out governing with. His routes to power run through the ultra-Orthodox parties, a minority government, or unity with the rival he set out to replace. He has 28 days, extendable by 14; if he fails, Netanyahu — still caretaker prime minister — is next in line.

Latest · Americas · Technology — Nvidia’s Spy Scare9 November 2026

Nvidia Investigates Its Own Chief: Was Jensen Huang the Target of a Foreign Intelligence Operation?

Nvidia’s board has opened an independent investigation into whether chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, after reporting linked him to a Chinese businesswoman whom US authorities concluded was passing information to Beijing. In a message to staff on Sunday, Huang acknowledged he had been unfaithful to his wife and took full responsibility, while the company stressed it had “no reason to believe” US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and that Huang, 63, will keep leading the company and cooperate fully. The woman, Alice Huang — no relation — is a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer that US officials have investigated since 2024 on suspicion of helping China obtain advanced AI chips barred from direct shipment; a Malaysian affiliate bought at least $4.8 billion of Nvidia-based equipment in under two years. Senior House Republicans have demanded briefings, and China’s foreign ministry on Monday dismissed the spy narrative as unsubstantiated. Nvidia’s shares slid as the story widened.

Latest · Africa · Egypt — Deadly Train Collision in Giza8 November 2026

Seven Dead, 112 Injured as Two Passenger Trains Collide South of Cairo

Two passenger trains collided on Sunday in the Al-Ayyat area of Giza governorate, about 76 kilometres south of Cairo, killing seven people and injuring 112, Egypt’s health ministry said. A train travelling from Giza to Fayoum ran into the rear of a Cairo–Aswan service between Kafr Ammar and Al-Raqqa stations, derailing several carriages; the train’s conductor was among the dead. Forty-five of the injured had been discharged by Monday as hospitals across Giza treated the rest. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said he was following the accident “with great concern” and ordered an urgent investigation with accountability for anyone found negligent, while the public prosecutor’s office is examining the technical causes. Al-Ayyat carries a grim history: it was also the site of Egypt’s worst-ever rail disaster in 2002, on a network — the oldest in Africa and the Middle East — long plagued by deadly crashes.

Latest · Africa · Senegal — Atlantic Route Tragedy8 November 2026

34 Dead as a Migrant Pirogue Capsizes off Senegal on the Crossing to the Canaries

At least 34 people have died after a small wooden migrant boat capsized off Kébémer, on Senegal’s northwestern coast between Dakar and Saint-Louis, as it headed for Spain’s Canary Islands. The interior ministry first reported 21 bodies recovered and a single survivor, stressing the toll was provisional; the Senegalese Press Agency later put the confirmed dead at 34, with an unknown number still missing and search operations continuing. Aid group Boza Fii said all the bodies recovered so far were Senegalese, many of them from districts near the departure point. The Atlantic route remains one of the world’s deadliest: the Spanish group Walking Borders counted 635 deaths on it between January and May alone, roughly 21 deaths for every 100 people who reached the Canaries. Senegalese authorities intercepted another boat carrying 142 people on Friday, while El Hierro in the Canaries declared an emergency after more than 3,300 arrivals on some 30 boats since the start of November.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi’s Worst Diwali Air in Five Years9 November 2026

Severe Smog Over Delhi: AQI Past 400 in Places, and Questions Over Monitors That Went Dark

Delhi woke on Monday to its most polluted post-Diwali morning since 2021, the citywide average Air Quality Index hovering near 370 — deep in the “very poor” band — with eight monitoring stations above 400, the “severe” threshold, led by Mundka at 425. Diwali day itself averaged 376, and hourly fine-particle readings at one North Campus station peaked near 999 micrograms per cubic metre, about 66 times the WHO’s 24-hour guideline. Stage III emergency restrictions remain in force across the capital region. The episode has also become a data dispute: analysts found more than 20 of the city’s 59 monitoring stations stopped reporting during the peak firecracker hours around midnight, private platforms showed far worse readings than the official ones, and the opposition AAP accused the ruling BJP of data manipulation — charges the BJP returned with claims about stubble burning in Punjab. The Supreme Court had allowed only approved “green” firecrackers in short windows over the weekend.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Magnitude 5.7 Earthquake8 November 2026

Night Quake in Central Japan Injures at Least 22 and Puts the Nankai Coast on Alert

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck western Shizuoka prefecture at 1:04 a.m. on Sunday, about 40 kilometres underground, shaking a broad swathe of central Japan from Hamamatsu to Kyoto and Nara and leaving at least 22 people injured, local media reported. The strongest shaking — upper 5 on Japan’s seven-level intensity scale, the fiercest felt in Chita, Aichi prefecture, in roughly three decades — shattered shop windows and sent people fleeing buildings in the night; the US Geological Survey measured the quake at 5.5. There was no tsunami threat and no abnormality at the Hamaoka nuclear plant. The Japan Meteorological Agency warned residents to expect further strong shaking over the coming week, especially the next two or three days, and to watch for mudslides and falling rocks. The quake’s location inside the broader source region of a feared future Nankai Trough megathrust drew attention, though the agency said it showed no unusual change along the plate boundary.

Norris Masters the Rain in São Paulo as Mercedes Complete a Title Double

Lando Norris won a wet São Paulo Grand Prix from pole position for the second straight year on Sunday, but the bigger prize went to Mercedes, whose fourth- and fifth-place finishes sealed their ninth Formula One constructors’ championship — their first since 2021, and a title double a week after 20-year-old Kimi Antonelli became the youngest drivers’ champion in history. Norris led 47 of 71 laps at Interlagos and took the chequered flag, waved by Neymar, 14.6 seconds ahead of Max Verstappen, who charged from 21st on the grid to second and was voted Driver of the Day; Verstappen’s teammate Isack Hadjar completed the podium. It was Norris’s 14th career win and 50th podium. Mercedes, whose engines also power Norris’s McLaren, now sit level with Williams on nine team titles, uncatchable on 652 points to Ferrari’s 510, with Charles Leclerc seventh and Lewis Hamilton ninth in his 400th race.
