Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Tankers Ablaze8 November 2026

Two Tankers, Then Three: Fires Multiply in Hormuz as Both Sides Claim the Waterway

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that oil tankers using what it called an unauthorised channel in the Strait of Hormuz had exploded and caught fire — first reporting two vessels that struck naval mines with their navigation systems switched off, then raising the count to three tankers engulfed in severe flames since the early hours. The Guards named none of the ships and gave no flags or casualties. British maritime body UKMTO separately reported a tanker on fire off Khasab, Oman, with its crew evacuated. The claims, which have not been independently verified, collide with a recent US Central Command statement that all mines have been cleared and that American forces have helped Gulf states move 1.5 billion barrels of oil through the strait since the summer. Iranian media also reported explosion-like sounds overnight in Qeshm, Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas, without attributing them to an attack. The strait normally carries about a fifth of global oil consumption.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Nuclear Safety8 November 2026

16th Blackout of the Year: Zaporizhzhia Runs on Diesel Again, With Two Weeks of Fuel

Europe’s largest nuclear plant lost its last outside power line at 02:36 on Sunday and is again relying on emergency diesel generators to cool its reactors. Ukraine’s operator Energoatom said the 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 line — the plant’s sole remaining grid link after the main Dniprovska line was knocked out — disconnected and an automatic reconnection attempt failed, counting this as the 16th complete loss of external power in 2026. The Russian-installed management said automatic protection shut the damaged line down. The UN watchdog, the IAEA, said the cause and location of the break are not yet known, that radiation levels are normal, and that the site holds enough fuel to run a limited set of generators for about two weeks. All six reactors are in cold shutdown and produce no electricity, but they still need uninterrupted power for cooling water to protect fuel in the reactors and spent-fuel pools.

A Play on Words, Says Tehran: Washington Extends the Blockade to Land and the Caspian

The United States has widened its effort to isolate Iran beyond the sea. Central Command has stood up Task Force Iron Gate — a team of about 20 personnel based in Tampa — to disrupt Iranian movement of advanced weapons, dual-use technology and sanctioned goods across land borders and the Caspian Sea, alongside the naval blockade that Washington says has already blocked more than $25 billion in crude exports. Tehran dismissed the expansion as “just a play on words” and, in the same news cycle, announced it was lifting limits on its ballistic missile ranges and warning that any fresh attack would bring retaliation without geographical limits. Israel’s defence minister added that the mission against Iran is “not complete,” repeating claims Tehran is pursuing nuclear weapons, which Iran denies. On the ground, the UAE confirmed the 22 Arab crew rescued from a burning cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman are all in stable condition.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Victims Named8 November 2026

His Parents and His Sister: Poland Begins Mourning as the Kraków Case Takes Shape

Poland’s national mourning began at midnight on Sunday and runs through Monday, with Kraków mourning until 11 November, after Saturday’s shooting in the Main Market Square killed six people — four women and two men, including a British national — and wounded 16, among them two police officers. Police confirmed on Sunday morning that the three people found dead at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski are his parents and his 21-year-old sister. Prosecutors named the detained man as Michał O., 24, who was shot by police and remains in hospital; earlier reporting put his age at 25. No motive has been established, though the interior minister has pointed to radical ultra-nationalist views and the defence minister has called the attack an act of terror while stressing the gunman was never a soldier. It remains Poland’s deadliest shooting since the fall of communism in 1989.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Ex-President Jailed7 November 2026

Removed on Friday, Jailed on Saturday: Paraguay’s Peña Is Held Before Trial Over Ueno

A Paraguayan judge has ordered former President Santiago Peña into pretrial detention at a military facility, a day after the Senate removed him from office in a unanimous vote of the 44 senators present. Judge Humberto Otazú said detention was needed to keep Peña in the country while prosecutors investigate his ties to digital lender Ueno, whose collapse after regulators seized it on 3 November cut nearly three million customers — almost half the population — off from savings and cards. Peña is charged with breach of trust and administration for personal gain; prosecutors allege he used the presidency to steer public money to a lender he had financial ties to, and say each charge can carry up to 10 years. His lawyer Ricardo Preda says he pleads not guilty. Former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo was also ordered held, 27 people have been charged in total, and Vice President Pedro Alliana has been sworn in to serve until August 2028 amid protests in Asunción.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens Monday9 November 2026

An Implementation COP With Two Presidents: Antalya Braces for 100,000 Delegates

The UN climate summit COP31 opens on Monday 9 November in Antalya, Türkiye, and runs to 20 November in a first-of-its-kind shared presidency: Turkish environment minister Murat Kurum holds the presidency while Australian climate minister Chris Bowen leads the negotiations, an arrangement settled after rival hosting bids at COP30 in Belém. Organisers bill it as an “implementation COP” — turning pledges into bankable projects — with five priorities covering faster electrification, climate finance, adaptation, oceans and food security, and a World Leaders’ Summit on 11–12 November. The backdrop is stark: a UN synthesis of new national plans points to emissions only about 6 per cent below 2019 levels by 2035, far short of the roughly 60 per cent cut scientists link to holding warming to 1.5°C, and the long-promised $100 billion-a-year finance goal remains the central fight over who pays for damage in countries that did least to cause it.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Remembrance Sunday8 November 2026

Two Minutes of Silence at the Cenotaph: the King Leads Remembrance Sunday

Britain fell silent at 11 a.m. on Sunday as King Charles III led the National Service of Remembrance at the Cenotaph on Whitehall. Wearing the uniform of a Marshal of the Royal Air Force, the King laid the first wreath — 41 paper poppy petals on black leaves, modelled on one made for George VI — and saluted beneath the words “The Glorious Dead,” followed by the Prince of Wales, the Princess Royal and the Duke of Edinburgh. The Queen and the Princess of Wales watched from the Foreign Office balcony, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and senior politicians laid wreaths, and about 10,000 veterans from more than 300 armed-forces and civilian organisations marched past. A gun from the King’s Troop Royal Horse Artillery marked the silence, and the Last Post was sounded by Royal Marines buglers. Services were held across the country, with a further silence due on 11 November itself.

Missiles Stopped Over Riyadh, Shrapnel at Aramco: a Day of Alerts in the Kingdom

Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and at the oil-rich Eastern Province on Sunday, the Saudi-led coalition said, after residents heard loud explosions over the capital. Civil defence alerts covered Dammam, Dhahran, Abqaiq and Al-Ahsa before an all-clear was given. Etihad has cancelled passenger and cargo flights between Abu Dhabi and both Dammam and Riyadh until 19 November, while services to Jeddah have resumed. The coalition says at least 15 missiles and drones have been intercepted across the kingdom so far in November, as Houthi fire intensifies alongside the government’s offensive in Yemen.

Latest · Americas · United States — Attorneys General Flip8 November 2026

Five State Law Offices Change Hands — and Trump’s Legal Map Changes With Them

Beyond Congress, Tuesday’s midterms flipped state attorney general offices in five Republican-leaning states — Georgia, Iowa, Ohio, Kansas and Texas — a shift Reuters says could reshape the country’s biggest court fights. Incoming Democratic attorneys general can withdraw their states from conservative challenges, halt cooperation with federal immigration operations, defend abortion access and launch multistate suits over environmental and gun policy. Texas is pivotal: Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson, who called the office “the private law firm of the MAGA movement” under Ken Paxton, says he will review pending cases — including the multi-state suit against the FDA over the abortion drug mifepristone, which would be left to Florida alone if Texas pulls out. Kansas’s Chris Mann could similarly exit a second mifepristone case, and Georgia’s Tanya Miller could affect the state’s defence of its six-week abortion ban. Florida, where Republican James Uthmeier held on, becomes the conservative legal movement’s anchor.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Earthquake8 November 2026

Strongest Shaking in Aichi Since 1997: Night Quake Injures 22 in Central Japan

An earthquake measured at magnitude 5.7 by the Japan Meteorological Agency — 5.5 by the US Geological Survey — struck western Shizuoka Prefecture at 1:04 a.m. on Sunday, about 40 kilometres underground. The strongest shaking, upper 5 on Japan’s intensity scale, was recorded in Chita, Aichi — the first tremor that strong in Aichi since March 1997 — with lower 5 shaking in parts of Aichi, Nagano and Gifu. At least 22 people were injured in Aichi and Shizuoka as residents rushed outside; shop windows broke and goods were scattered. The tremor was felt from Hamamatsu toward Kyoto, Nara and Yamanashi. No tsunami warning was issued. The agency has urged people in the hardest-hit districts to stay alert for up to a week — especially the next two to three days — and to watch for rockfalls and landslides if rain follows.
