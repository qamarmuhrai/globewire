CENTCOM Chief Briefs Trump on Options for Renewed Iran Strikes as Tehran Insists on Seven Conditions for Talks

Admiral Brad Cooper, who leads US Central Command, gave President Donald Trump an in-person briefing at the White House on Thursday on military options for Iran, joined by Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman General Dan Caine, according to US officials cited by the Wall Street Journal. The meeting, a day after the same officers met Vice President JD Vance, covered the continuing US blockade as Washington weighs resuming major combat operations after the 3 November midterms. The Journal has reported that Gulf Arab states have been told to prepare for a possible short, intense campaign, and that Washington has been adding air defences and munitions and moving troops away from bases seen as vulnerable to retaliation. Tehran, for its part, says progress depends on acceptance of its seven conditions: Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said a carrot-and-stick approach would not sway Iran. Iranian agencies reported Tehran had received a letter from Vance aimed at restarting negotiations but had not yet replied, while fresh US strikes were reported on 29 October against an IRGC launcher on Qeshm Island and the naval base at Jask.

Trump Cancels Iowa and Kansas Rallies for a 72-Hour Sprint of Phone Rallies in Nearly 20 States

President Donald Trump has cancelled planned weekend rallies in Iowa and Kansas and will instead lead a series of telephone rallies reaching voters in nearly 20 states, the Republican National Committee said on Thursday, with only his Friday event in Daytona Beach, Florida, still going ahead in person. RNC Chairman Joe Gruters said the virtual format would be more efficient in the final stretch. Close Senate contests are also being watched in Texas, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa, Alaska and Mississippi, while Democrats hope former senator Sherrod Brown can unseat Republican Jon Husted in Ohio. Republicans currently hold the House 218 to 214 and the Senate 53 to 47, with all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats decided on Tuesday.

Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash30 October 2026

Spain Demands Answers as Mourning Begins for 69 Dead and Investigators Focus on a Signal Decision

Spain entered three days of national mourning on Thursday as relatives gave DNA samples in Toledo and rescuers finished searching the wreckage near Urda, where a Renfe high-speed train ran into the back of a stationary Iryo train on Wednesday morning. The toll stands at 69 dead, with 98 people treated for injuries, 50 still in hospital in Castilla-La Mancha and four in intensive care; Toledo's morgue, with capacity for 30, was overwhelmed after receiving 52 bodies, and the army set up an improvised facility. Transport Minister Óscar Puente said an automatic control system failure had stopped traffic across 69km of track, after which the Renfe service was authorised to proceed despite a red signal — a decision officials called inexplicable and highly unlikely to have been safe. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said the state was mobilised to identify victims, establish the cause and compensate families. It is Europe's deadliest rail disaster since Santiago de Compostela in 2013, and the second fatal crash on the same Madrid–Seville line this year after January's collision killed 46. Train drivers struck on Thursday for greater safety investment, and low-cost operator Ouigo cancelled all services on Friday after a court suspended minimum-service rules.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War30 October 2026

Ukraine Pauses Refinery Strikes After US Pressure, Reports Say, as Russian Attacks Kill at Least 20

Ukraine's long-range strike units were told in mid-October to hold their campaign against Russian oil refineries once missions already under way were complete, the Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, in an unofficial pause expected to last until after the US midterms and possibly deeper into November. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the account. Kyiv had previously resisted halting the strikes, calling them its only viable reply to years of Russian bombardment, while Washington has blamed them for pushing diesel prices to record highs — a claim energy experts largely attribute instead to the war with Iran. Russian fuel shortages have eased as the strikes slowed, with free sales resuming in 15 regions in October. The reported pause has not stopped Russian attacks: officials said at least 20 people were killed in the latest wave, including at least 14 in Thursday's drone strike on the crowded platform at Dnipro's main station. The news comes amid renewed US-backed efforts to revive peace talks, with relations between Presidents Trump and Zelenskyy described as sharply deteriorated.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China Politics29 October 2026

China Formalises Record Expulsion of 33 Senior Officials and Lifts a Military Discipline Chief Into the Politburo

The Chinese Communist Party's fifth plenum, a four-day closed-door meeting that ended in Beijing on Thursday, formally expelled 33 senior officials — a record for the current era, with nearly half of them military officers — and described the purge as eliminating serious hidden dangers by turning the blade inward. Among those removed were former Politburo member Ma Xingrui and former Central Military Commission vice-chairman Zhang Youxia, taking the number of sitting Politburo members ousted this term to three, the most since 1980. Only 279 full and alternate Central Committee members attended, down from 376 named in 2022, the lowest turnout since the post-Mao era began. Five of the seven generals appointed to the Central Military Commission in 2022 have now been purged, leaving only President Xi Jinping and Vice-Chairman Zhang Shengmin, a discipline enforcer who was this week elevated to the Politburo. The plenum adopted a decision on full and rigorous party governance and set the next party congress for the second half of 2027, when Xi is expected to seek another term. Official figures say 105 officials at provincial or ministerial level were sanctioned in the first nine months of this year alone.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab30 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab Leaves ₹4,600 Crore of Damage in Andhra Pradesh, Seven Dead and Tens of Thousands Evacuated in Odisha

Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu put preliminary losses from very severe Cyclone Arnab at about ₹4,600 crore after an aerial survey of the Krishna, Godavari and Konaseema districts, with central assessment teams due in the state on Friday. Seven people died despite the mobilisation of some 30,000 officials; at least ₹500 crore of damage was done to electricity infrastructure alone, including around 8,000 poles damaged in Konaseema and 3,700 uprooted in West Godavari. In neighbouring Odisha, more than 300 disaster-response teams were deployed across nine districts and about 59,000 people were evacuated, including nearly 11,900 from Ganjam, where some 2,000 residents were cut off by floodwater and more than 200 villages were badly hit. Flooding on the Bahuda river forced the cancellation of 36 trains and diversion of 30 more on the Howrah–Chennai route. The storm has since weakened into a depression, bringing heavy rain to West Bengal, Jharkhand and Madhya Pradesh, where farmers in Chhattisgarh report 25–30% damage to nearly ripe paddy.

Latest · Europe · Türkiye — Cappadocia29 October 2026

Three Killed When a Rain-Loosened 'Fairy Chimney' Collapses at Cappadocia's Göreme Open-Air Museum

Three people, including one foreign national, were killed and five injured on Thursday when heavy rain triggered a landslide that brought down a volcanic rock spire in Türkiye's UNESCO-listed Cappadocia region. The collapse happened at 3:15pm local time in the town of Göreme, near the entrance to the open-air museum, Nevşehir Governor Hüseyin Kok said. Justice Minister Akın Gürlek initially reported two dead and five injured; rescue teams later recovered a third body, and the governor confirmed the other two victims were Turkish citizens. Cappadocia's cone-shaped formations, known as fairy chimneys, were shaped by ancient volcanic activity and draw tourists from around the world. Prosecutors will open an investigation on Friday alongside expert assessments. A previous rockfall in the region in 2024 caused no casualties.

Latest · Global Affairs · Biodiversity COP1729 October 2026

Four Countries Accused of Watering Down Science as UN Nature Talks in Yerevan Reach Their Final Day

Diplomats at the COP17 biodiversity summit in Yerevan accused Saudi Arabia, Russia, Argentina and, to a lesser extent, Brazil of trying to weaken how two scientific assessments from the UN-backed IPBES panel are recognised in the final agreement — turning on whether the text should welcome the findings or merely take note of them, a weaker formula in UN language. A French delegate, speaking anonymously, compared the dispute to parallel efforts to delay IPCC climate reports, warning that without a scientific basis everything discussed becomes a matter of opinion. IPBES chair David Obura declined to comment while talks continued. The conference, running from 19 to 30 October under the theme Taking Action for Nature, is conducting the first global review of the Kunming-Montreal framework; negotiators adopted 14 decisions in the first week covering biosafety, invasive species and gender equality, but finance remains deeply contested — the Cali Fund for sharing benefits from digital genetic information had received just $6,000 from two companies by October, and Indigenous groups continue to press for direct access to funding.

Putin Tells African Leaders Russia Is Ready for War With Europe But Does Not Want One, as Summit Adopts Cooperation Declarations

Closing the third Russia–Africa summit in Moscow on Thursday, President Vladimir Putin dismissed talk of escalation into Europe, saying Russia was prepared for any development but did not want a war in which, if things went too far, there would be no winners. He pointed to recent calls with Norway's prime minister and Finland's president as evidence Moscow supports restoring full relations with European countries. Putin said Russia supplied 13 million tonnes of agricultural products to Africa in the first nine months of 2026, hosts about 40,000 African students, and now settles 85% of mutual trade in national currencies; bilateral trade exceeded $27 billion in 2025. The summit proceeded under pressure from rights groups and families over the recruitment of Africans into Russia's armed forces for the Ukraine war, an issue Amnesty International urged leaders to raise directly with the Kremlin.

Latest · Global Affairs · World Health Organization29 October 2026

Seven Candidates Nominated to Succeed Tedros as WHO Chief, With a Decision Due at the May 2027 Assembly

The World Health Organization said on Thursday that member states have proposed seven candidates for director-general, with the winner to be appointed at the 80th World Health Assembly in May 2027 and to take office on 16 August 2027, succeeding Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus at the end of his second term. The nominees are Mohammed Al-Kuwari, Hanan Balkhy, Hans Henri P. Kluge, María P. Neira, Budi Gunadi Sadikin, Li Song and Gevorg Tamamyan. The race unfolds after the United States, formerly the organisation's largest donor, formally withdrew in January under President Trump, who has long accused the WHO of mishandling the Covid-19 pandemic — a backdrop that gives candidates seen as bridge-builders particular weight in the contest. China's nominee, Li Song, is a senior official leading work on women and child health at the National Health Commission, while Indonesia's Sadikin, a former banker who led his country's vast Covid vaccination programme, is the only non-physician in the field.
