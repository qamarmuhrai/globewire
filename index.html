Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Election Day3 November 2026

58.7 Million Ballots Already In, 1,000 Federal Monitors Watching: America Votes, but Nothing Counts Until 6pm Eastern

Voting is under way across the United States for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships, and by late Monday the University of Florida Election Lab put early voting at 58,677,062 ballots — a record for a midterm and well above the roughly 57 million reported earlier in the day. Republicans go into the count holding the House 218 to 214 and the Senate 53 to 47, so Democrats need a net gain of four in each chamber; a 50–50 Senate would still be decided by the Republican vice president. About 40 House districts are rated competitive. The Justice Department has deployed around 1,000 election monitors, a record number, and on the eve of polling the Supreme Court turned down an emergency bid by the Trump administration to require California to separate up to 20,000 mail ballots from voters it described as likely noncitizens. Campaign-finance tracker OpenSecrets projects total cycle spending near $12 billion, the most expensive midterm on record. No votes had been reported by early Tuesday morning Eastern time: the first polls close at 6pm Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky, the last at 1am Eastern in Alaska’s Aleutian Islands, and officials warn the country may go to bed without knowing who controls either chamber, with certification still weeks away.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Qeshm and Oil3 November 2026

A Missile Launcher for a Downed Drone: New Detail on the Qeshm Strike Sends Oil Back Above $105 as Qatar Says It Is Still “Hopeful”

More of Monday night’s exchange in the Strait of Hormuz is now clear. US officials say an Iranian surface-to-air missile, fired by the Revolutionary Guard, brought down an American drone over waters east of the strait, and a US fighter jet then destroyed the launcher on Qeshm Island in what Washington calls self-defence — the latest in a series of small, deliberately low-profile strikes the Wall Street Journal first reported. Iranian state media reported several explosions on Qeshm, the strait’s largest island. Markets reversed Monday’s plunge: after Brent settled down more than 7% at $104.77 on hopes of a deal, it traded about 2% higher near $107 on Tuesday, with US crude around $98, as a senior Iranian official told Reuters the two sides remain far apart and Tehran will not accept what it calls excessive demands. Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf said Tehran has given Washington a “strong and rational response” and that the ball is now in the US court. Qatar, a mediator alongside Pakistan, said on Tuesday it remains hopeful both sides can accept the proposals on the table. The war, running since late February, has left Hormuz — which once carried about a fifth of world oil and gas — largely blocked, and Saudi Aramco on Tuesday reported third-quarter net profit up 34.6% on the higher prices the conflict has sustained.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Hospital and Power Grid3 November 2026

73 Injured at One Hospital, 180,000 Without Power: Ukraine Counts the Cost of 289 Drones and 76 Missiles in a Single Night

Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian ballistic missiles and aerial bombs directly struck a city clinical hospital in Kharkiv overnight, injuring at least 73 people and damaging the cardiology department where patients were being treated; the city’s mayor said more than 1,000 windows were shattered and about 200 patients had to be moved. Zelenskyy put the wider barrage at 289 attack drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles, aimed mainly at energy — generation plants and substations across nine regions including Odesa, Cherkasy, Zhytomyr, Vinnytsia, Dnipro and Chernihiv. In Zhytomyr region alone more than 180,000 people woke without power, and Poland again activated air defences along its border. Russia’s defence ministry said it targeted electricity facilities in Vinnytsia, Dnipropetrovsk and Kharkiv, while Moscow’s mayor said air defences destroyed nearly 500 drones heading for the capital region and the ministry claimed 856 interceptions in total — figures neither side’s claims could be independently verified. The night followed Monday’s strike on a school distributing humanitarian aid at Komyshuvakha in Zaporizhzhia region, where the toll has risen to at least 29 killed with 48 still missing. US envoy Steve Witkoff said trilateral talks resume in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, focused on post-war security, de-escalation and humanitarian steps, and Latvia — holding the UN Security Council presidency for the first time — has called an emergency session over the aid-centre strike.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — The Moving Line3 November 2026

Trapped Behind the Yellow Line: A Year Into the Ceasefire, Gaza’s Families Are Being Squeezed Into Ever-Smaller Ground

The ceasefire that took effect in October 2025 has lowered the scale of fighting in Gaza without ending displacement or giving most families a way home, the UN Human Rights Office warned on 30 October. Its head in the occupied Palestinian territory, Ajith Sunghay, said Israeli operations continue to push people away from the “Yellow Line” marking Israeli-controlled ground — originally about 53% of the Strip, now assessed at roughly two-thirds — with reports of shootings, fires and new concrete markers placed closer to where civilians shelter. Many displaced families cannot return because their neighbourhoods now lie beyond the line. The warning has been underlined by continued killing: at least eight Palestinians died in strikes on 1 November, including five police officers in western Khan Younis and three people in Gaza City, and four more were killed on 2 November in Khan Younis, al-Musaddar and Tel al-Hawa. Gaza’s Government Media Office counts 5,989 ceasefire violations since October 2025, with 1,593 people killed and 5,748 wounded, and says only 77,657 of 232,800 agreed aid trucks — about a third — have been allowed in, while the World Food Programme puts about 1.4 million people, two-thirds of the population, at crisis levels of food insecurity or worse. Israel says it targets fighters and accuses Hamas of rearming; negotiators remain deadlocked over disarmament and withdrawal.

Latest · Americas · Haiti — The Gang Letter3 November 2026

“I Have Raised the White Flag”: Haiti’s 400 Mawozo Leader Writes to the UN Asking for Mediation as Salvadoran Troops Deploy

Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò Sanjou and leader of Haiti’s 400 Mawozo gang, has confirmed to the Associated Press that he wrote to UN Secretary-General António Guterres asking the organisation to send a delegation for dialogue and mediation — his first interview with a major international outlet. In the two-page letter dated 1 November, Joseph says he “officially raised the white flag” on 20 August, describes 400 Mawozo as available for any meeting aimed at a peaceful solution and a lasting stabilisation process, and says dialogue must prevail over force. The immediate trigger is fear of reprisals: four Salvadoran aviation technicians were attacked on 18 October in territory held by the rival Chen Mechan gang and remain missing, prompting President Nayib Bukele to deploy more than 2,000 Salvadoran troops to Haiti. Joseph told AP the technicians are dead, a claim Haiti’s government disputes — it says they were abducted and El Salvador believes they are alive — and offered no evidence. The UN confirmed receiving the letter but stressed receipt implies no recognition or support; most Salvadoran personnel, its spokesperson noted, operate outside the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force. Joseph has been under UN sanctions since December 2023 and the US offers $1 million for information leading to his capture. The UN estimates more than 5,700 people were killed or injured across Haiti between January and mid-September.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Day Four3 November 2026

Paramilitary Troops and Drones Over Gazipur: Garment Workers Defy the Wage Board and Hold the Streets for a Fourth Day

Bangladesh’s garment protests stretched into a fourth day on Tuesday and hardened. Paramilitary Border Guard Bangladesh units deployed in Gazipur and Narayanganj, monitoring the unrest with drones, as workers again blocked highways — including the Dhaka–Tangail road at Naojor, where debris was set alight before police cleared it — and clashed with officers in Ashulia, Gazipur and Dhaka’s Mirpur district. In Ashulia police fired tear gas and sound grenades after bricks and stones were thrown, its industrial police chief said. Police say 58 officers have been injured since Saturday and cases have been filed against thousands of unidentified protesters over torched vehicles and damage; Dhaka police also announced 33 arrests in the past 24 hours of people they link to the banned Awami League. The demand has not moved: a minimum wage of 30,000 taka a month, about $245, against the current 12,500 taka set in December 2023. The six-member wage board formed on 1 November has not calmed the streets, and after nearly 700 factories shut on Monday, dozens more suspended work or declared holidays on Tuesday — 50 in Gazipur district alone — in the peak November-to-February order season for the world’s second-largest apparel exporter.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal — Everest Collision3 November 2026

Both Pilots Hurt as Well: What Is Known About the 8:11am Rotor Strike at Pheriche, 150 Kilometres From Kathmandu

Nepal’s Civil Aviation Authority has opened an investigation into Tuesday morning’s collision at the Pheriche helipad, identifying the aircraft as a Fishtail Air helicopter, registration 9N-AJR, and an Air Dynasty helicopter, 9N-AOD — both Airbus AS350-family machines that had flown from Lukla, the gateway to Everest. Police put the time at about 8:11am local time at roughly 4,200 metres in Ward 4 of Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality. The Fishtail aircraft was landing with passengers rescued from the Everest region when the two machines’ rotors struck. Vasanth Kumar Balaramaramadu, 47, from Chennai, was killed. Shailesh Krishna Patel, a New Zealand citizen of Indian origin travelling on the same helicopter, was injured and rescued, and both pilots — Rabindra Prajapati of Fishtail Air and Prajwal KC of Air Dynasty — were also hurt and taken to Kathmandu for treatment. The Dynasty helicopter was carrying only cargo. Footage circulating online appears to show the rotors making contact moments before both aircraft came down on snow-covered ground; the cause has not been established. The crash falls in peak trekking season, when helicopters are the lifeline for climbers, hikers and remote villages across the high Himalaya.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — Weakening3 November 2026

No Deaths Reported, 198mm of Rain in a Night: Murasu Weakens Inland, but Flood Warnings Run Until Late Tonight

Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu is weakening after crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, between 3:30 and 5:30am on Tuesday with sustained winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph, the India Meteorological Department said. The state disaster authority told Reuters no deaths or injuries had been reported so far — a marked contrast with Cyclone Arnab a week earlier. The damage is still extensive across Prakasam and Bapatla districts: thousands of trees felled, hundreds of power poles damaged, fishing boats wrecked and crops flattened. Naguluppalapadu in Prakasam recorded 198mm of rain between Monday morning and Tuesday morning, with Kothapatnam at 182.3mm and several other stations above 115mm. Nineteen domestic flights were cancelled at Vijayawada, Tirupati and Visakhapatnam. Forecasters expect the system to ease to a cyclonic storm by noon and a deep depression by midnight, but heavy to extremely heavy rain, a 1 to 1.5-metre storm surge on the falling tide and a moderate flash-flood risk across Bapatla, Guntur, Krishna, Palnadu and Prakasam districts remain in force into Tuesday night, with relief teams from the NDRF and SDRF already deployed.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Korea — The Leak3 November 2026

New Passports for the President and First Lady: Seoul Reissues Documents for All 168 People Exposed in the June File

South Korea is reissuing passports to everyone caught in the presidential travel leak — all 168 members of President Lee Jae Myung’s June overseas entourage, including Lee himself and First Lady Kim Hye-kyung. The file, a passenger list for the Korea–Belgium leg of a trip that also took in Italy and France, sat on a publicly accessible website for about five months from June and reportedly contained names, dates of birth and passport numbers with issue and expiry dates for the president, the first lady, National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac, ministers, security personnel and journalists. Presidential chief of staff Lim Kwang-hyun, chairing an emergency meeting on Tuesday, called it a grave matter that cannot be taken lightly and ordered a full accounting of how the file escaped, how far it spread, whether anyone was harmed and who was responsible for its handling, plus a complete review of travel security procedures. Foreign ministry spokesperson Park Doo-soon said the file has been taken down, computers and email accounts linked to the trip are under technical inspection, the privacy regulator has been notified, and the incident is being treated as a leak rather than a hack — with no sign so far of intrusion into the ministry’s email system. Because the list was public for five months, officials cannot yet say who downloaded it.

Latest · Europe · Cyberattack — The Freezers3 November 2026

Set to 50°C in the Night, Emptied by Morning: Europe’s Second Freezer Attack in Three Weeks Points at One Supplier

Staff across at least eight European countries spent Monday clearing fridges and freezers that attackers had remotely overheated overnight — in several stores to 50°C — in the second coordinated refrigeration attack in three weeks. Belgium saw the widest impact: Delhaize described an “IT incident” at several dozen stores in Belgium and a few in Luxembourg, Carrefour confirmed a cyberattack at 15 of its more than 700 Belgian stores, and Intermarché reported problems at 10 to 15. In Sweden, Coop confirmed six or seven of its roughly 800 stores were tampered with and ICA a limited number, including its Sjöbo store where units were set to 50°C; single stores or small clusters were hit in Estonia, Finland, Ireland — 13 SuperValu stores — the Netherlands, where at least 17 businesses including butchers were affected, and Portugal, with seven Intermarché stores. Belgian contractor Sabcobel said attackers switched units into defrost mode and that about 10% of its installations were still unsecured after the 12 October attack on hundreds of French stores. Investigators keep converging on the same link: internet-connected controls from Danish maker Danfoss, which has said it identified flaws last year and had long urged customers to install updates; its advisories page carried no new notice when checked on Tuesday. Belgian federal prosecutors have opened an investigation, the EU food commissioner called the attacks a deliberate attempt to disrupt the food chain, and no group has claimed either wave.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shipping — Struck off Oman3 November 2026

New Delhi Condemns the Strike on MSV Al Jusufi: 13 Crew Coming Home, One Seafarer’s Remains to Be Repatriated

India’s external affairs ministry on Tuesday condemned the attack on the Indian-flagged MSV Al Jusufi near Limah, Oman, and said its embassy is working with Omani authorities on early repatriation of the 13 surviving crew and the remains of the seafarer who died. The vessel, carrying 14 Indian nationals, was struck by an unidentified aerial object on Monday evening; the 13 survivors evacuated and were rescued by the Omani Coast Guard. Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal named the dead man as Gandhar Nahim Ajij, from Gujarat, who succumbed to his injuries, and said the ministry and the embassy are in direct contact with local authorities to assist the survivors. Who fired the object, and what it was, remains unknown. The strike fits a pattern that has made Gulf waters among the most dangerous for merchant shipping since the US–Iran conflict began in February: more than 120 attacks on commercial vessels have been reported in that period, and Indian officials say at least 22 Indians have now been killed in attacks on ships and tankers in the region. India is the world’s second-largest supplier of seafarers, with more than 310,000 maritime professionals.

Latest · Africa · Sudan–Ethiopia — Competing Claims3 November 2026

162 Attacks or 64? Sudan’s Own Briefing Slides Undercut Its Headline Charge Against Ethiopia — and Neither Figure Is Verified

Sudan’s accusation that drones are being launched from Ethiopia against its territory now comes with two different numbers from Khartoum itself. At a press conference on 28 October, officials said 162 attacks had killed more than 400 civilians and 102 military personnel — yet the intelligence slides shown at the same briefing, a joint product of the General Intelligence Service and Military Intelligence, list 64 launch incidents between 15 February and 27 October, according to Janes reporting on the briefing. Sudan says the drones support the Rapid Support Forces, which have fought the army since April 2023, and points to two Akinci aircraft it says it shot down on 17 and 23 May after flights from Bahir Dar. The dispute builds on an earlier rupture: in May, Sudan recalled its ambassador from Addis Ababa and blamed Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates for a drone strike that closed Khartoum International Airport for three days, days after its first flight since the war began. Ethiopia has repeatedly called the allegations baseless and counter-accuses Sudan of backing Tigray-linked fighters; the UAE denies funding the RSF. No outside party has publicly confirmed the launch sites, flight paths or casualty counts, and reporting reviewed for this edition found no link established between specific wreckage and specific airfields — so both Khartoum’s figures are best read as claims by one side in a contested regional dispute, in a war the UN calls the world’s worst humanitarian crisis, with more than 150,000 estimated dead and about 14 million displaced.
