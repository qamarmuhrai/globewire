Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — The Count Keeps Rising16 November 2026

Six Tankers in Two Days, Tehran Claims — and Two Strikes Are Now Confirmed by Maritime Authorities

The Hormuz crisis deepened through Monday as Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two more oil tankers had struck naval mines in the strait’s southern corridor, bringing their claimed total since Sunday to six vessels caught fire or sunk — a figure Iranian army statements pushed to seven with a further tanker disabled east of the strait. What changed on Monday is that parts of the picture are now independently anchored. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, citing Indian shipping authorities, confirmed that a tanker approaching the strait on 15 November was attacked in the Arabian Gulf; maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified it as the Thenamaris-operated supertanker Al Dhannah, hit in the steering-gear room at the stern before turning back toward Dubai, its crew safe. UKMTO also reported a second confirmed incident at 05:58 UTC on Monday: a vessel outbound from the strait, identified by Vanguard as the Panama-flagged On Phoenix, was struck by an unknown projectile about three nautical miles off Oman, suffering an engine-room fire with no casualties. Both ships appear on Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority blacklist of “non-compliant” vessels. Tehran’s claimed sinking of a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels remains unverified — the Guards’ own video shows fire and smoke on open water with no discernible vessel — and Iran named none of the ships it says hit mines. Tehran insists the northern corridor through its territorial waters is the only authorised route, and warns that ships switching off their trackers or using the southern route face “serious” consequences.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 1,336 Drones in One Night16 November 2026

Ukraine’s Biggest Drone Barrage of the War Hits the Crimea Bridge — as Europe Says No Talks Without a Ceasefire First

Ukraine launched its largest overnight drone attack of the war, and its most symbolic target was the bridge Vladimir Putin built to annexed Crimea. Russia’s defence ministry said its air defences downed 1,336 Ukrainian drones across 16 regions, Crimea and the Black and Azov seas — the second-highest daily figure this year — while Moscow’s mayor said about 600 drones headed for the capital, 191 of them neutralised on approach. Sergey Aksyonov, the Russian-installed head of Crimea, confirmed the 19-kilometre Kerch Bridge had been hit; monitoring channels reported a strike on an arch of the navigable span around dawn, Russian channels claimed an arch had visibly shifted, and roughly 2,000 vehicles backed up as the crossing closed from the early hours. The Kremlin called the damage minor, road traffic was suspended for repairs while trains kept running, and Kyiv had not officially commented by Monday afternoon. The barrage landed days before Putin meets Trump and Xi in Shenzhen, and as the diplomacy hardens on every side: EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said on Monday that Europe will need to see a ceasefire and Russian concessions before serious peace talks can begin — a direct counter to the US-floated idea, revealed by President Zelenskyy this weekend, of Ukraine trading Donbas for NATO membership.

Latest · Europe · Estonia — Border Shuts 1 December15 November 2026

A Foiled Arson Attack on a Drone Maker, Blamed on the GRU — and Estonia Closes Every Crossing With Russia

Estonia will close all its border crossing points with Russia from 1 December, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna announced on Sunday, after an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, a maker of reconnaissance and combat drones supplied to Ukraine. Attackers poured flammable liquid through a smashed window at the company’s premises in Viimsi, near Tallinn, in the early hours of Saturday; the security service KAPO said the building would have suffered considerable damage had the plan succeeded. Eight people, all resident in Latvia, were detained. Tsahkna said there was “clear evidence” the operation was ordered by Russia’s GRU military intelligence — the second GRU-attributed attack on an Estonian defence firm since a fire at Milrem Robotics in August — though Tallinn has not published the underlying evidence. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Russian intelligence exploits the border crossings to exert influence and that the risk had to be reduced; no end date for the closure has been given. The three crossings at Narva, Koidula and Luhamaa carry pedestrians and freight, and the shutdown will fall hardest on Narva, whose residents often have family across the river in Ivangorod. Moscow’s foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova dismissed the move as “Russophobia”.

Latest · Europe · Czechia — Letná Fills Again15 November 2026

350,000 People on the Plain Where Communism Fell: Prague Rallies Against the Government’s Public-Media Bill

Hundreds of thousands of Czechs filled Prague’s Letná Park on Sunday — the ground where the Velvet Revolution gathered in 1989, two days before its anniversary — to protest Prime Minister Andrej Babiš’s plan to overhaul how public broadcasters are funded. Organisers from the Million Moments for Democracy movement put the crowd at 350,000 to 450,000, potentially the largest demonstration since the fall of communism; police published no estimate. The bill, pushed through its first reading at 4:36 a.m. on 24 October after the coalition limited opposition speaking time — the rally was named “Letná Against 4:36” in its honour — would abolish the household licence fees that fund Czech Television and Czech Radio (150 and 55 koruna a month) and pay both broadcasters from the state budget instead, at roughly 15 percent less than they planned to collect. Protesters and the European Commission warn that whoever controls the budget line controls the broadcaster; public-media staff have been on strike since 3 November, backed by unions, universities and artists. Babiš rejects claims the reform is meant to exert political control. The bill still faces two more parliamentary readings, and organisers have invited the government to a public debate on 2 December.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 17 Dead in One Night16 November 2026

Nine in a Shack Near Pretoria, Eight in a Bullet-Riddled Vehicle: Two Mass Shootings, No Motive, No Arrests

South African police are investigating two mass shootings that left 17 people dead on Sunday night, about 536 kilometres apart, with no suspects identified and no motive established in either case. In the Jeffsville informal settlement at Atteridgeville, west of Pretoria, officers found nine people — six men and three women — shot dead inside a four-room shack, believed killed between 10 and 11 p.m.; Gauteng’s serious and violent crimes unit is investigating, and provincial police commissioner Tommy Mthombeni said the killings could be linked to a shooting in nearby Saulsville last year in which 12 people died. In Sweetwaters, outside Pietermaritzburg in KwaZulu-Natal, eight men were found dead inside a white seven-seater vehicle riddled with bullet holes; police had not identified the victims by Monday. Police say the two attacks appear unrelated. They are the latest in a series of mass shootings this year — 28 people were killed in two attacks in September — in a country where the latest police statistics record 5,427 murders between April and June, an average of about 60 killings a day.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Thailand — Phuket Fire16 November 2026

Eleven Die in Choking Smoke at Patong: A Transformer, a Fuel Pump, and 100 Motorcycles Waiting to Be Rented

Eleven people were killed and 16 injured when fire tore through four shophouses in Patong, on Thailand’s resort island of Phuket, in the early hours of Monday. The blaze began at about 3:15 a.m. on Phang Muang Sai Kor Road when, investigators say, a roadside transformer exploded during a storm and the flames reached a coin-operated fuel dispenser in front of a motorcycle rental business — where about 100 motorcycles were stored alongside petrol, engine oil, plastic and tyres. Firefighters took roughly two hours to bring the fire under control. Eight women and three men died, most from smoke, in rooms above the shops; the injured were Thai nationals and one person from Myanmar, and police said no tourists were among the dead or injured. Phuket’s police commander said CCTV footage and witnesses had been secured and arson ruled out; the governor said an initial investigation pointed to a short circuit on a substation pole. Thailand’s prime minister directed assistance for the injured and a full investigation into the cause — and into how a fire in a shop became so deadly for the people sleeping above it.

Latest · Technology · Space — Vulcan’s First Failure15 November 2026

Ten Minutes Into Flight, Twenty Years of Reliability Ended: A Vulcan Rocket Dies With 40 Amazon Satellites Aboard

United Launch Alliance lost a rocket on Sunday for the first time in the company’s 20-year history. Its Vulcan launcher — only the type’s fifth flight, and its first in nine months — lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 12:40 p.m. Eastern carrying 40 satellites for Amazon Leo, the company’s planned rival to SpaceX’s Starlink, then suffered an anomaly about ten minutes into flight during the Centaur upper stage’s burn and failed to reach orbit. The six solid boosters, the source of nozzle problems on two earlier Vulcan flights, performed normally this time; ULA, the Boeing–Lockheed Martin joint venture, said it did not yet know what went wrong and would investigate with Amazon before flying again. The loss is a schedule blow on both sides: it was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has bought to carry more than 1,500 Leo satellites, and Amazon has about 396 satellites in orbit against Starlink’s more than 11,100, with a regulatory deadline to deploy half its 3,232-satellite constellation by July 2028. Its next launch, an Ariane 6 carrying 36 satellites, is targeted for 23 November at the earliest. A lengthy investigation is expected to ground Vulcan for months.

Latest · Africa · Cape Verde — Neves Wins Again16 November 2026

63.8 Percent in the First Round: Cape Verde Hands José Maria Neves a Second Term

Cape Verde’s President José Maria Neves has won a second five-year term in the first round of Sunday’s presidential election, according to provisional results from 97 percent of polling stations announced on state television. Neves took 63.8 percent of the vote — comfortably above the 50 percent needed to avoid a runoff — against 31.6 percent for his main challenger, former justice minister Joana Rosa, the archipelago’s first female presidential candidate, who conceded defeat; four other candidates each took less than 1 percent. The campaign centred on jobs for young people, and turnout was low. Neves, 66, was prime minister for 15 years before becoming president in 2021 and belongs to the centre-left PAICV, the country’s oldest party, which also won last May’s parliamentary elections. The national electoral commission is expected to announce official results within the week. Cape Verde, a Portuguese-speaking island nation of about half a million people off West Africa, is widely regarded as one of the continent’s most stable democracies — and Sunday’s orderly concession kept that reputation intact.

Latest · Africa · South Sudan — First Vote Slips Again14 November 2026

Fifteen Years After Independence, Still No Election: South Sudan Pushes Its First Polls to February 2027

South Sudan has postponed its first national elections since independence in 2011 yet again, moving polling day from 22 December 2026 to 11 February 2027. The National Elections Commission published the revised timetable on Saturday — the same day voter registration was due to begin at 3,161 centres — without stating a reason; its spokesperson said the 51-day shift followed consultations with stakeholders and was intended to give more time for voter registration and logistics. Campaigning will now run from 27 December to 9 February, with final results expected on 15 February, eleven days before the transitional period expires. The money tells part of the story: the UN Security Council heard this week that the commission has received only $21 million of the $276 million it requested. The politics tell the rest — the 2018 peace deal between President Salva Kiir and Riek Machar, which ended a civil war that killed an estimated 400,000 people, has effectively collapsed, and Kiir has dissolved the unity government for a caretaker administration. The vote has now been delayed from 2022, to 2024, to December 2026, and to February 2027.

Latest · Europe · Migration — The Vienna Announcement16 November 2026

Unveiled in Vienna: Five EU States Confirm the Rwanda Return Hub, a Pilot of About 100 Places

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands confirmed in Vienna on Monday what diplomats had briefed for days: a deal to establish a migrant return hub in Rwanda, the first deportation facility run by EU states outside the bloc. Rwanda, which said in August it had begun preliminary discussions with several European governments, sent a delegation to the Vienna migration conference for the announcement. The scheme starts as a pilot of roughly 100 places, each country sending around 20 people, with a target opening in 2027. Its legal basis is the EU’s new Returns Regulation, which speeds deportations and expressly permits return hubs in third countries. The design differs in one crucial respect from Britain’s abandoned Rwanda scheme, ruled unlawful by the UK Supreme Court in 2023: the European hub would take only people who have exhausted every legal avenue to remain in the EU, not asylum seekers with claims still pending. Rights groups have raised concerns about the model, which caps an EU push since 2024 to raise deportation numbers as arrivals fall — the bloc has struck departure-stemming deals across Africa and West Asia, and Monday’s announcement extends that logic to returns.
