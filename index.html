Latest · Americas · Space — First Ship Catch14 November 2026

SpaceX Catches a Starship Back From Orbit for the First Time After 14 Laps of Earth

It took 22 hours, and it worked. SpaceX’s Starship upper stage returned to Starbase, Texas, on Saturday afternoon and settled into the launch tower’s mechanical arms — the first time a ship that has been to orbit has been caught in mid-air rather than splashed down or landed on legs. Flight 15 lifted off late on Friday, deployed 26 of the newer Starlink V3 satellites and then circled the planet 14 times at roughly 265 kilometres up, far beyond the one or two laps of earlier tests, before braving re-entry heat and descending upright to the pad it had left. The planned double catch did not happen: minutes after launch the Super Heavy booster, Booster 22, cut its boostback burn short — SpaceX now points to a low fuel-mass alarm, correcting its first account of a guidance fault — diverted over the Gulf of Mexico and was destroyed just above the water. Company officials present the ship catch as proof the full system can be reused for Moon and Mars missions, and say Florida launches from Kennedy Space Center should follow within months.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Chaos Fallout14 November 2026

4,500 More US Flights Cancelled on Saturday as a Faulty Router Is Blamed and Hochul Tells Duffy to Resign

The numbers got worse before they got better. Airlines cancelled more than 4,500 flights across the United States on Saturday and delayed thousands more, a day after Friday’s nationwide ground stop — nearly five hours with almost all departures held — forced roughly 10,000 cancellations on Friday alone and left aircraft and crews scattered. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on Saturday the failure began during routine work by contractor L3Harris at a remote FAA site, where copper lines were being switched to fibre: a replacement router began flooding the wider network with connection attempts, overwhelming communications and radar feeds at all 22 en-route centres. Investigators have not yet settled whether the cause was installer error or defective equipment; Duffy ruled out a cyberattack, and the White House held an emergency Situation Room meeting on Friday while that question was open. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford apologised, called the system stable and said operations should be largely normal by late Sunday. The politics turned sharp: New York Governor Kathy Hochul demanded Duffy resign or be removed, called the lapses a pattern on his watch and pressed Congress for a full investigation, while Duffy renewed his call for Congress to fund an entirely new air-traffic system, warning the ageing one will keep failing.

Latest · Americas · Illinois — Peoria All-Clear14 November 2026

Peoria Lifts Mile-Wide Evacuation After a Full Search Finds No Second Device at the Ethanol Plant

Downtown Peoria can go home. Police lifted the evacuation order at about 1 p.m. on Saturday, roughly a day after a device found at the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant forced more than 5,000 residents and workers out of a one-mile zone covering downtown, the Warehouse District and the Southside, and reaching across the Illinois River to homes and Caterpillar buildings in East Peoria. The sequence began just after 1 p.m. on Friday when the plant reported a bomb threat; a suspicious package was found about an hour later, judged a credible threat by early evening and rendered safe by bomb technicians at 9:12 p.m. The one-mile radius reflected not just the device but what surrounded it — ethanol and other highly combustible chemicals across a site the size of some 44 football fields. A Saturday search by Peoria police with the FBI, ATF, state police and Coast Guard found no further devices and no one was injured. Police Chief Brad Dixon declined on Saturday to confirm the device’s exact composition while analysis continues, and officials would not say whether they are treating the case as terrorism. No arrests have been made; the hunt for whoever planted it continues.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — UN Toll14 November 2026

UN Counts 25 Seafarers Killed and 136 Incidents in Hormuz as Trump Weighs Holding Off on New Iran Strikes

The human cost of the Hormuz standoff now has an official tally. Briefing the Security Council, UN officials said at least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the strait since the US–Israeli war on Iran began in February, with the International Maritime Organization logging 136 security incidents between 1 March and 11 November — October alone produced 35, the highest monthly count of the war, and at least 10 more vessels were hit in the first 10 days of November. That backdrop framed Saturday’s competing claims: Iran’s Revolutionary Guards and regular navy said three tankers that had switched off tracking and used unauthorised or restricted routes struck naval mines and caught fire, one of them a supertanker, and Tehran’s navy warned any tanker entering the restricted zone east of the strait will meet the same fate. No names, flags or casualty figures were offered and the claims remain unverified; Washington maintains it has cleared the main routes and the strait is open, though UK maritime data put traffic about 65 percent below pre-war levels. In Washington, meanwhile, President Donald Trump has told advisers he may hold off on fresh attacks on Iran absent a new provocation from Tehran or the Houthis, as regional allies urge restraint — a pause that leaves the shipping crisis unresolved rather than ended.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — After Abu Dhabi14 November 2026

After Abu Dhabi, Moscow Wants a New European Order — and Romania Scrambles Jets Again Over Snake Island

The talks ended on Wednesday; their meaning is still being fought over. Analysis of the 10–11 November Abu Dhabi sessions — the first direct Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months, mediated by Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner — converges on one point: Moscow used them to reach beyond Ukraine, reviving the core of its December 2021 demands for a halt to NATO enlargement and a thinner alliance presence in central and eastern Europe, while again refusing any ceasefire before a comprehensive deal and insisting on the whole of Donetsk region. The only firm outcome remains an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange, expected within two to three weeks. Kyiv’s negotiators call the talks among the hardest since 2022, held as Russian strikes keep damaging industry and infrastructure, though both sides speak of momentum toward a settlement before year’s end, and Witkoff says Vladimir Putin agreed Europeans may join the next round. On the alliance’s edge the war stayed visible: Romania scrambled two F-16s from Fetești twice overnight into Saturday after radar picked up targets near Ukraine’s Snake Island, sending RO-Alert warnings to Tulcea County at 04:45 and 05:39 before the all-clear at 05:52 — the third alert episode in a week, with no confirmed entry into Romanian airspace and no debris reported.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Summit Opens15 November 2026

ASEAN Opens in Manila: Myanmar, a Sea Code and Energy Costs Top the Philippines’ Final Summit as Chair

The summit itself starts today. Nine Southeast Asian leaders join host President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. at the Philippine International Convention Center in Pasay City on Sunday for the opening, plenary and retreat sessions of the 49th ASEAN Summit, running 15 to 17 November, with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and the IMF addressing the plenary as guests of the chair, and UN Secretary-General António Guterres in attendance. Myanmar stays on the agenda without its junta chief: leaders will review compliance with the 2021 Five-Point Consensus, and Myanmar is represented only by a foreign-ministry director-general, as it has been since the 2021 coup. The Plus One summits with dialogue partners follow on 16–17 November, closing with a special ASEAN–US summit at which President Donald Trump is expected on the final day, before Marcos ceremonially hands the rotating chairmanship to Singapore’s Lawrence Wong.

Kremlin Confirms the Shenzhen Three-Way: Trump, Xi and Putin Meet Thursday With Nuclear Cuts and Iran on the Table

Now both Washington and Moscow have said it on the record. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed on Saturday that Presidents Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump will meet on Thursday 19 November in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ meeting on 18–19 November — the first trilateral encounter of the three largest nuclear powers. The White House, which confirmed the plan on Friday, says Trump will raise reducing nuclear stockpiles nine months after the last US–Russia arms treaty lapsed, and will discuss the war with Iran; a senior US official says Ukraine is deliberately not on this meeting’s agenda and no agreement is expected, and no separate Trump–Putin bilateral is scheduled. Analysts expect symbolism to outweigh substance, but the image alone — three nuclear leaders at one table in China’s tech showcase city — is the point each capital is managing.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Winter Aid Fight14 November 2026

EU, Board of Peace and Britain All Press Israel on Gaza Winter Aid as 3,900 Households Are Left Without Shelter

Three separate pressures landed on the same problem this week: Gaza is wet, cold and short of tents. On Saturday the European Union demanded Israel allow immediate, unimpeded aid at scale and reopen every crossing, a day after more than 50 countries and organisations sent a joint letter on winter supplies. The US-backed Board of Peace, created under the October 2025 ceasefire to oversee reconstruction, had already blamed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s government directly for the stagnation and urged it to authorise shelter, water, sanitation and drainage materials under the Sharm El-Sheikh Humanitarian Protocol. In London, minister Stephen Doughty told the Commons that flooding had affected more than 152,000 people, destroyed over 6,000 shelters and left nearly 4,000 households without shelter — UN OCHA puts the figure at 3,900 households, with 17,400 shelters damaged and about 1.4 million people living in tents or makeshift cover — and announced a further £5 million for UNICEF’s winter response. He also said more than 150,000 donor-bought tents sit outside Gaza awaiting entry and that timber remains blocked. Israel’s position is that the aid is flowing: the IDF and COGAT cite more than 91,000–93,000 tents and over 8,600 truckloads of winter supplies entered, with 850-plus requests approved, and blame Hamas and UN logistics for hold-ups. The gap between those two accounts is now the central argument of Gaza’s fourth winter of war.

Latest · Africa · South Sudan — Election Delayed Again14 November 2026

South Sudan Pushes Its First-Ever Election Back Again, From December to 11 February, With $21 Million of $276 Million in Hand

Fifteen years after independence, the wait goes on. South Sudan’s National Elections Commission published a revised timetable on Saturday moving the country’s first national elections since 2011 from 22 December 2026 to Thursday 11 February 2027 — a 51-day shift announced, pointedly, on the very day voter registration was due to begin. The commission gave no reason; a spokesperson insisted the vote had not been postponed but merely adjusted under the law to let other processes be completed. The new calendar keeps registration from 14 to 28 November at 3,161 centres across the 10 states and three administrative areas, sets a 45-day campaign from 27 December to 9 February, a silence day on 10 February, counting on 11–12 February and final results around 15–16 February. The context argues louder than the statement: the commission has received only $21 million of the $276 million it requested, the UN and African Union have warned the conditions for a peaceful, credible vote are not in place, former vice-president Riek Machar has been under house arrest since March 2025, and the poll has already slipped from 2022 to 2024 to 2026. Whether February holds will depend less on the calendar than on money and security arriving in the next 12 weeks.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola, Six Months On14 November 2026

Congo’s Deadliest Ebola Outbreak Holds Near 11,200 Cases as WHO Says Violence Is Still Slowing the Response

The totals have barely moved in three days, and that is not the same as control. Congo’s Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak, declared on 15 May, stands at 11,197 confirmed cases and 5,471 deaths in the health-ministry series carried by UN channels to 11 November — a fatality rate of about 49 percent — with a parallel South African NICD tally to 10 November at 11,114 cases and 5,433 deaths; the two cut-offs are reported side by side by trackers rather than merged. The outbreak spans eight provinces and 68 health zones, concentrated in Ituri and North Kivu, and is still adding roughly 400 to 500 infections a week, which the WHO calls the largest Ebola emergency in Congo’s history and the second-largest ever recorded. WHO regional emergency director Marie Roseline Belizaire repeated this week that insecurity is the binding constraint: the 5 November attack on Cantine-Aloya killed 66 civilians including three health workers and burned three facilities, and daily contact follow-up remains near 81 percent against a 95 percent target. There is still no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo species; candidate-vaccine trials that began enrolling in Bunia in October are not expected to report before early 2027. Kenya, with nine linked cases, has discharged its last patient and is counting 42 days toward an Ebola-free declaration.

Latest · Global Affairs · COP31 — Into Week Two14 November 2026

COP31 Enters Its Decisive Week in Antalya With Finance Unresolved and the US Chair Empty for a Second Year

Antalya is halfway, and the hard text is still ahead. The COP31 climate summit, running 9 to 20 November with close to 200 countries and some 90,000 participants, moves into its second week under an arrangement no previous COP has tried: Türkiye’s Murat Kurum holds the presidency and the action agenda of voluntary pledges, while Australia’s Chris Bowen, as president of negotiations, controls the formal texts. UN Secretary-General António Guterres set the ambition at the leaders’ summit — phase out unabated coal by 2040, cut oil and gas use by a third, and reduce emissions about 6 percent a year — against a UNEP adaptation assessment that developing countries will need roughly $305 billion a year by 2035. The fights that will decide the outcome are familiar and unresolved: whether developed countries put numbers and a timetable behind adaptation finance and the $1.3 trillion roadmap now that the United States, which has withdrawn from the Paris Agreement and sent no official negotiating delegation for a second year, is absent from the finance table; and whether the closing text names a transition away from fossil fuels at all, which major producers and consumers continue to resist while host Türkiye promotes electrification — raising electricity’s share of final energy from about 23 percent toward 35 percent by 2035. No landmark treaty is expected; delivery, as negotiators keep putting it, is the test.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Outage Closed14 November 2026

Microsoft Closes the Books on Its 24-Hour Outage: Backlog Cleared, Full Report Still to Come

The incident is formally over; the explanation is not finished. Microsoft’s final updates on Saturday confirmed that services across all previously affected environments had fully recovered and the backlog of delayed email had been processed, closing incidents EX1550543 for Exchange Online and MO1550821 for Microsoft 365, with the public status page reading that all products are operational. The company records the impact window as running from Thursday morning GMT on 12 November to Friday afternoon on 13 November — just under 24 hours in its own accounting, longer in the experience of offices that lost most of a working day across at least 74 countries as Outlook, Teams and Copilot failed for businesses, governments, hospitals and airlines. Microsoft’s one-sentence cause stands: a flaw introduced during an earlier network-capacity upgrade, surfaced by a later network change, then amplified by cascading failures and surging retry traffic at the network perimeter. It has stated plainly the outage was neither a cyberattack nor an AI-related failure, apologised to customers, and promised a full post-incident report that has not yet appeared. For administrators, one caution survives the all-clear: restoration does not by itself prove every message sent during the outage reached its recipient.
