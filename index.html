Latest · Europe · France — Iran Envoy Summoned10 November 2026

France Summons Iran’s Ambassador Over the Fridge Hack and Warns It Could Strike Back in Cyberspace

France summoned Iran’s ambassador on Tuesday morning, a day after publicly blaming the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps for October’s supermarket refrigeration attacks, and Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot warned Paris could answer with cyber measures of its own alongside a push for European Union sanctions. Investigators say hackers linked to the IRGC’s Shahid Kaveh unit seized remote controls for refrigeration systems at roughly 700 stores on 11–12 October — including Intermarché, Coopérative U, E. Leclerc, Carrefour and Monoprix — forcing units into defrost cycles that pushed temperatures as high as 50°C overnight, spoiling tonnes of food, causing fires and tens of millions of euros in damage. Paris prosecutors have sought international arrest warrants for the unit’s chief and two deputies, citing Farsi-language traces and IP addresses tied to devices in Iran, while a second wave on 2 November hit more than 100 stores in seven European countries and cold stores used by France’s blood service. Tehran has not formally responded and routinely denies carrying out malicious cyber operations; France says it has not published the intelligence behind the attribution.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi, Day One10 November 2026

Day One in Abu Dhabi: Security Guarantees Take Centre Stage as the First Trilateral Talks Since February Get Under Way

Ukraine, Russia and the United States opened two days of trilateral negotiations in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday — their first such meeting since Geneva in February — with security guarantees at the top of the first day’s agenda and territorial questions expected on Wednesday. Ukraine’s delegation chief Rustem Umerov confirmed the move to a full trilateral format after opening talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner and with representatives of Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission. Kushner said Washington has identified where the remaining gaps lie and has ideas to bridge them, while Kyiv is pressing for prisoner exchanges and possible energy and maritime ceasefires to lay groundwork for a leaders’ summit. Moscow’s team, led by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky, arrived as Russia fired 162 drones and a ballistic missile at Ukraine overnight, and the Kremlin said European representatives would not join the trilateral sessions. The sides remain far apart: Russia still demands Ukrainian withdrawal from Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, which Kyiv rejects.

Latest · Middle East · Türkiye — Steel Plant Blast10 November 2026

Death Toll Reaches 17 at the HABAS Steel Plant as Six Are Arrested and Families Wait in Aliaga

Six people were arrested overnight after Monday’s explosion at the HABAS iron and steel plant in Aliaga, near Izmir, killed 17 workers and injured about a dozen more, at least one critically, as grieving families waited on Tuesday to receive bodies for burial. The blast, shortly before midday, was felt like an earthquake across the industrial district 60 kilometres north of Izmir; Izmir’s governor said a crane carrying a replacement boiler collapsed into an arc furnace during liquid-steel casting, while labour ministry findings later pointed to a falling object damaging gas and oxygen pipes during maintenance. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan said judicial and administrative investigations would establish negligence and responsibility, prosecutors in Menemen assigned four prosecutors to the case, and unions described the disaster as the plant’s third fatal incident in three weeks — after deaths on 21 October and 5 November. HABAS, operating in Aliaga since 1987 with capacity of 4.5 million tonnes a year, is one of Türkiye’s largest steel producers.

Latest · Europe · Books — Booker Prize at 819 November 2026

At 81, M. John Harrison Becomes the Booker Prize’s Oldest Winner With “The End of Everything”

British novelist M. John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize on Monday night for “The End of Everything”, becoming at 81 the oldest winner in the award’s history and, on his first-ever nomination, taking home £50,000 at a ceremony at London’s Old Billingsgate. The 198-page novel — the shortest on this year’s six-book shortlist, chosen unanimously from 163 submissions by a panel chaired by historian Mary Beard — follows beachcomber Phillip Tennent and his aunt Marnie on England’s south coast years after social order frayed and mysterious entities known as the iGhetti arrived. Harrison, whose six-decade career has moved between science fiction, fantasy and literary fiction, surpasses Margaret Atwood, who was 79 when she shared the prize in 2019; judges stressed the award was for this book, not a lifetime honour, and the win is widely read as the first Booker victory for a work rooted in science fiction. The novel, his 13th, is published by independent imprint Serpent’s Tail.

Latest · Americas · Courts — Big Bend Wall Cleared9 November 2026

Supreme Court Lets the Big Bend Border Wall Resume, Saying Congress Favours “Expeditious” Construction

The US Supreme Court on Monday granted the Trump administration’s emergency request to lift a federal judge’s halt on border wall construction across West Texas’s Big Bend region, including Big Bend National Park, while the underlying lawsuit continues. In an unsigned order, the court said Congress has expressed a clear preference for expeditious border construction that outweighs the aesthetic, recreational and environmental harm alleged, and that the government was exceedingly likely to prevail on its argument that the 1996 immigration law limits lower-court review of barrier waivers. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice to publicly dissent. District Judge Kathleen Cardone had blocked the project in October, noting the 517-mile Big Bend sector spans more than a quarter of the US–Mexico border yet records the lowest share of apprehensions of any Southwest sector. The challenge was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity, a local river guide and Friends of the Ruidosa Church, who say Homeland Security waivers sidestepped dozens of environmental protections.

Latest · Americas · California — Billionaire Tax Rejected9 November 2026

California Says No: Voters Reject the One-Time 5% Billionaire Tax After a $378 Million Campaign Against It

California voters have rejected Proposition 40, a one-time 5% tax on residents worth more than $1 billion, with the Associated Press calling the result for the “no” side on Monday at 52.8% to 47.2% with about 83% of ballots counted — nearly a week after the 3 November midterms in which the same electorate delivered sweeping Democratic wins. The measure, sponsored by healthcare union SEIU-UHW and designed with economists Gabriel Zucman and Emmanuel Saez, would have applied to anyone living in the state on 1 January 2026 and was expected to raise about $100 billion, mostly for healthcare. Opponents raised roughly ten times the yes campaign’s $33.5 million, including more than $100 million from Google co-founder Sergey Brin, and both outgoing Governor Gavin Newsom and governor-elect Xavier Becerra opposed it. A companion measure, Proposition 42, which would bar new wealth taxes, was leading 51.6% but had not been called; backers of the tax say they may return in 2028.

Latest · Americas · Business — Boeing Cuts 2,500 Jobs10 November 2026

Boeing to Cut About 5% of Its Commercial Airplane Workforce as the 737 Ramp Slips Into 2027

Boeing will reduce the workforce of its Commercial Airplanes division by about 5% — roughly 2,500 roles from a unit that employed 50,494 people at the end of 2025 — through a mix of attrition and involuntary layoffs, division chief Stephanie Pope told staff in an internal memo on Monday, with notices expected from December. The company says slower production has left it overstaffed: chief executive Kelly Ortberg acknowledged last month that Boeing will miss this year’s 737 production and delivery targets, and the target of 47 jets a month has slipped from summer 2026 to early 2027, with the step-up for the 787 wide-body also pushed back. It is Boeing’s first major workforce reduction since 2024, when it cut 17,000 jobs, and it lands despite a full order book — a sign, analysts say, that stabilising factories and suppliers now matters more than chasing volume. Free-cash-flow goals have likewise shifted a year later, to $10 billion in 2029.

Latest · Africa / Europe · Migration — Shipwrecks Off Senegal and Crete9 November 2026

At Least 56 Dead in Weekend Shipwrecks: Senegal Toll Climbs to 39 as Two Boats Are Lost South of Crete

At least 56 people died in migrant boat disasters off Senegal and Greece over the weekend, the International Organization for Migration said on Monday, with Senegalese authorities later raising the toll from the single worst wreck to 39. A wooden boat that left Saint-Louis for Spain’s Canary Islands capsized near Loumpoul, between Dakar and Saint-Louis, on Friday; only one survivor has been pulled from the water and searches continue with the toll still provisional. South of Crete, two boats that departed eastern Libya got into distress: Greek authorities rescued 28 Sudanese migrants and recovered eight bodies in one incident, and 18 survivors were rescued with 14 bodies recovered in a second, after a cargo ship raised the alarm. IOM director-general Amy Pope said the victims had fallen prey to smugglers exploiting desperation, and the agency has recorded at least 454 deaths and disappearances on the Western African Atlantic route alone this year to 26 October — a figure it warns understates losses from boats that vanish without trace.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — The $1 Trillion Surplus10 November 2026

$1.04 Trillion in Ten Months: China’s Surplus Crosses the Trillion Mark a Month Early, Powered by Chips

China’s trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, customs data released on Tuesday showed — past $1 trillion for a second straight year and a month earlier than in 2025, putting Beijing on course to beat last year’s $1.2 trillion record. October exports jumped 35.2% year on year to $412.3 billion on surging global demand for AI hardware, with integrated-circuit exports up 137% to $39.6 billion in the month, car exports up 37.6% and high-tech exports up 47.9% across the ten months. Imports climbed even faster, up 41.6% to $307.1 billion — the fastest pace since May 2021 — narrowing October’s monthly surplus to $105.3 billion from $133.5 billion in September. Exports to the United States rose 32.9% and the bilateral surplus eased to $32.71 billion, while the surplus with the European Union widened to $310.7 billion. The figures land days before the APEC summit in Shenzhen and an expected Xi–Trump meeting, and analysts warn the imbalance will sharpen trade tensions even as it offsets weak consumption at home.

CPI Day in Washington: No Annual Rate in the Report, and a Fed Governor Already Leaning Towards a December Hike

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released October’s consumer price index on Tuesday morning in Washington — the last inflation reading before the Federal Reserve’s 8–9 December meeting — without any 12-month figure, because price collection was suspended during last autumn’s 43-day government shutdown and no October 2025 index exists. Economists polled by Dow Jones and Reuters expected headline prices up 0.3% on the month, slowing from September’s 0.6%, and core prices up 0.2%, with gasoline down more than 4% easing pressure; Bank of America estimated annual inflation at 3.8% headline and 2.7% core, still well above the Fed’s 2% goal. Ahead of the data, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in London he is leaning towards a quarter-point increase in December and would consider a half-point move if inflation worsens, and markets price a better-than-80% chance of a hike after the Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% in October. Growth remains firm, with third-quarter GDP at 3.4%.

“Get Serious”: Money Fight Opens COP31’s Second Day as Leaders Stream Into Antalya

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya entered its second day on Tuesday with finance already the central fight, after UN Secretary-General António Guterres urged leaders on Monday to get serious about the crisis following a year of European heatwaves, Indonesian wildfires and catastrophic floods in Nepal, amplified by an exceptionally strong El Niño. Leaders continued arriving on Tuesday, including Pakistan’s Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who addresses the summit on Wednesday with a call for predictable climate finance for developing countries, and Bangladesh’s Tarique Rahman and Mauritania’s president. Hosts Türkiye and Australia have set a goal of lifting electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035, from about 20% today, while the World Meteorological Organization reports 2026 has continued the streak of exceptional temperatures, melting ice and rising seas.
