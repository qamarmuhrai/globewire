Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — No Deal22 November 2026

COP31 Ends Without a Deal as the EU and Others Reject a Final Text That Never Mentions Fossil Fuels

The climate summit in Antalya has broken down. After more than two weeks of talks that were meant to finish on Friday and instead ran 44 hours into overtime, COP31 President Murat Kurum told the closing plenary on Sunday that there was no consensus and the cover decision had not been adopted. The 94-paragraph draft at the centre of the dispute recalled the 2023 Dubai agreement “in its entirety” but did not contain the words fossil fuels, oil, coal or phase-out anywhere in its text. Ireland’s climate minister Darragh O’Brien, speaking for the EU presidency, said the bloc would not endorse an Antalya pact that failed to name the main driver of warming, and Panama also formally objected. The draft would have been the first COP outcome to concede that warming will overshoot the 1.5°C Paris limit — Berkeley Earth put October 2026 at 1.8°C above mid-1800s levels — while setting a goal for electricity to reach 35 per cent of final energy use by 2035, with no requirement that the extra power be renewable, and reaffirming a pledge to triple adaptation finance by 2035 without a baseline to measure it from. The failure does not cancel commitments made at earlier summits, but it leaves the next conference, in Addis Ababa, to inherit a stalled process.

Latest · Middle East · Manama — Two Injured Confirmed22 November 2026

Bahrain Now Says Two People Were Hurt in the Iranian Strike on Sitra, as Attacks on Hormuz Tankers Climb

Bahrain has revised its account of Saturday’s strike. The interior ministry said on Sunday that two people were injured when an Iranian attack hit a civilian industrial facility and started a fire — a Bahraini citizen in stable condition and a Pakistani resident in critical condition — after initially reporting damage only. Saudi Arabia and the Arab League both condemned the attack and declared solidarity with Manama. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards say the operation targeted US positions at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases and destroyed two Patriot radars, and Tehran says its forces have also downed US drones over the Strait of Hormuz, including what it calls a fourth MQ-1 in 24 hours. The wider pattern is intensifying: about 25 tankers have been hit in and around Hormuz in the past week, ship transits through the strait fell 15 per cent between 12 and 18 November, and President Masoud Pezeshkian has described Iran as facing a “silent economic war” under sanctions and the US counter-blockade, with rolling power cuts starting Sunday. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said regional talks, including a Qatari delegation’s two hours in Tehran on Friday, should be given a chance and the blockade lifted.

Latest · Europe · Dymer, Kyiv Region — 14 Unaccounted For22 November 2026

Rescuers Hunt for 14 People Under a Collapsed Apartment Block Near Kyiv, as Zelenskyy Counts 2,700 Drones in a Week

A search-and-rescue operation ran through Sunday in Dymer, in the Vyshhorod district north of Kyiv, after a Russian drone partially collapsed the entrance of a five-storey apartment building early in the morning. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said nine residents had been evacuated and the fire contained, but 14 people remained unaccounted for and might still be under the rubble. Regional officials said two people were injured — a 16-year-old boy hospitalised with shrapnel wounds and a 22-year-old woman treated at the scene — and that the blast also damaged a nearby kindergarten and shop. Zelenskyy put Russia’s barrage over the past week at roughly 2,700 strike drones, nearly 1,460 glide bombs and 165 missiles across 20 regions, and renewed his complaint that US deliveries of Patriot interceptors are stalling: Ukraine has received an average of 12 a month during 2026 through the NATO-funded PURL programme, he said, even as ballistic missiles, many supplied by North Korea, lead the attacks on energy infrastructure heading into winter. Separately, one body was recovered from the rubble of another building hit in the district, and a man was killed by shelling in Zaporizhzhia.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Serang, Indonesia — Search Enters Second Day22 November 2026

Search Teams Comb the Ruins of the Banten Candy Factory as Families Wait for News of the Missing

The search at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia plant in Cikande, Serang, continued on Sunday, a day after fire swept through the three-storey factory that makes Milkita candies. Officials have confirmed three deaths, 15 people rescued alive — four of them taken to hospital — and 36 workers still unaccounted for, though police statements carried locally spoke of seven body bags recovered and around 50 people missing as identification work began, a sign of how fluid the lists remain. A company representative said about 300 workers were inside when the blaze started on Saturday afternoon; firefighters arrived to find the building already engulfed, sections of roof later collapsed, and hotspots were still smouldering on Sunday, slowing crews working section by section. Banten’s governor put the number inside at about 60 in the affected area. The cause has not been established and remains under investigation, and authorities have not yet confirmed whether everyone listed as missing was in the building at the time.

Latest · Americas · Guasave, Sinaloa — 11 Dead in the Capture21 November 2026

Mexico Captures “El Chapo Isidro,” a Cartel Boss on the FBI’s Most-Wanted List, in a Raid That Left 11 Dead

Mexican special forces have taken one of the FBI’s ten most wanted fugitives. Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, 44, known as “El Chapo Isidro” — no relation to Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán — was seized on Friday night in Guasave, Sinaloa, along with his second-in-command and the group’s alleged finance operator, and flown to Mexico City. The defence ministry said troops used stealth and surprise to surround a house, came under fire and explosives, and entered to make the arrests, in an operation supported by US intelligence. Four soldiers and seven alleged gunmen were killed, and the fighting spilled across the city of 290,000: burning roadblocks were set up, and fans fled a professional baseball game as gunfire echoed nearby. Washington had offered $5 million for Meza Flores, indicted in the US since 2012, and accuses his organisation, rooted in the Beltrán Leyva cartel and allied with a Sinaloa faction, of smuggling heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, marijuana and fentanyl north for more than two decades. Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch called the capture an important step toward pacifying Sinaloa, though analysts warn that removing a capo can trigger fresh turf fighting.

Washington’s Governor Calls ICE “Out of Control” and Demands Its Abolition After Two Travellers Are Seized at Sea-Tac

A boarding gate has become the latest flashpoint in America’s immigration fight. Masked Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers detained two people on the jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport at about 8 a.m. on Saturday as passengers boarded Alaska Airlines Flight 1306 to Los Angeles, in an incident filmed by fellow travellers and widely shared online. Saudi Defences Shoot Down Three Houthi Missiles Aimed at Riyadh, Days After a Strike Damaged the Capital’s Tallest Tower

Riyadh has come under missile fire for the second time in days. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles heading for the capital early on Saturday, then a drone aimed at the Riyadh region and another toward the south on Sunday, after an AFP journalist in the city heard two explosions and civil defence issued — then lifted — an alert that the capital was under hostile aerial threat. Latest · Global Affairs · Geneva — Two More Years of Talks21 November 2026

UN Talks Give the World Two Years to Draft “Killer Robot” Rules — Without Promising They Will Be Binding

Diplomats in Geneva have agreed to keep talking about lethal autonomous weapons, but not to be bound by the outcome. After five days of negotiations under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons that ran until about 4:45 a.m. on Saturday, the 128 states party to the convention agreed to spend the next two years elaborating an international instrument on systems that can select and attack targets without human intervention — and only then decide whether it should be a treaty and how far it should bind. More than 95 states had backed a treaty, and UN Secretary-General António Guterres, who leaves office at the end of the year, had set 2026 as the deadline to start negotiating one. The United States, Russia and Israel were among the major military powers resisting binding rules; Russia delayed the opening by objecting to the agenda, and Washington had proposed five years of non-binding guidelines instead. The Red Cross said it regretted the outcome and renewed its call for a binding treaty within a year, while the Stop Killer Robots coalition of more than 300 organisations called the mandate watered down. UN disarmament chief Izumi Nakamitsu called the result significant but “glass half full or half empty,” noting that two years is a compressed timetable for such an instrument.

Latest · Global Affairs · Brussels — A Decade in the Making21 November 2026

The EU and Indonesia Sign a Trade Deal After Ten Years of Talks, Scrapping Tariffs on More Than 90 Per Cent of Goods

Brussels and Jakarta have finally put signatures to paper. The European Union and Indonesia signed their Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement and a separate Investment Protection Agreement in Brussels on Saturday, closing negotiations that began a decade ago and were concluded in September 2025. The pact will remove duties on more than 90 per cent of products on both sides: tariffs on over 98 per cent of EU exports to the world’s fourth most populous country will go, including on food, farm and industrial goods, saving European companies more than €600 million a year according to the Commission, while Indonesia’s duties on EU cars, currently up to 50 per cent, will fall, and about 90 per cent of Indonesian exports — including palm oil, footwear, textiles and fisheries — will enter the EU tariff-free. Both sides say the deal opens investment in electric vehicles, electronics and pharmaceuticals, as the EU works to diversify its trade ties. It is not law yet: the European Parliament must approve it and both sides must complete ratification, with entry into force expected in early 2027.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · The Hague — After the Verdict22 November 2026

Rohingya Groups Demand Action to Enforce the World Court’s Genocide Ruling Against Myanmar

The verdict is in; the argument now is over enforcement. Rohingya organisations around the world, in a joint statement endorsed by 64 groups, have demanded immediate international action to give effect to Thursday’s International Court of Justice ruling, which found by nine votes to six that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State during the 2017 military crackdown. It was the first time in its 80-year history that the court has held a state responsible for genocide. Presiding Judge Yuji Iwasawa said the evidence showed Myanmar’s forces killed at least 10,000 Rohingya, alongside widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and that more than 730,000 people fled to Bangladesh. The court ordered Myanmar to stop acts of violence, investigate and prosecute those responsible, and provide restitution and reparations — including facilitating the safe, voluntary return of the Rohingya, restoring property, and searching for the disappeared — with a report on restitution measures due within two years. Myanmar’s military-backed government has dismissed the ruling as unfair and not unanimous. The court’s judgments are final and binding but it has no means of enforcing them, which is why campaigners are pressing governments and the UN to act on it.
