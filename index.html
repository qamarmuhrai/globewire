Latest · Asia-Pacific · Serang, Indonesia — 24 Body Bags Recovered22 November 2026

Police Remove 24 Body Bags From the Banten Candy Factory as 29 Workers Remain Missing and the Search Pauses Until Monday

The toll from Saturday’s fire at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy plant in Cikande, Serang, has darkened sharply. Police said on Sunday evening that 24 body bags containing human remains had been removed from the gutted building, while firefighters were still tackling hotspots and the search was suspended overnight, to resume on Monday morning. Banten police chief Hendra Kurniawan confirmed the pause, and officials stressed that the bag count does not equal a confirmed death toll, because a single bag may hold remains of more than one person. The company, part of United Family Food, said about 300 people were on the Saturday shift and gave a narrower accounting of 65 workers it was tracking: 24 had returned home, four were in medical care, 29 were still missing and the whereabouts of eight more had yet to be confirmed. Disaster victim identification officer Wahyu Hidayati said the intensity of the blaze had made visual identification impossible, so teams are collecting DNA samples and personal belongings from families — a process expected to take two to three weeks. The cause of the fire, which broke out on Saturday evening in the office and warehouse area, remains under investigation.

Latest · Africa · Khartoum — Four Killed22 November 2026

Drone Strikes Kill Four Around Khartoum, Damage a UN Building, as Sudan’s Army Says the Aircraft Were Launched From Ethiopia

Khartoum endured a pre-dawn drone barrage on Sunday. Explosions were heard across the capital from about 2 a.m. to 4:30 a.m. as air defences tried to intercept aircraft that witnesses and officials said were aimed at residential neighbourhoods, government buildings and Khartoum International Airport. Local reports put the dead at four: three people were killed and nine wounded when a drone hit a home in northern Omdurman, across the Nile, while another strike completely destroyed a house in the capital and a United Nations building was damaged. A Sudanese army source told AFP the drones had been launched from Bahir Dar airport in neighbouring Ethiopia and called the attack a continuation of Ethiopian aggression — an allegation that could not be independently verified and that Ethiopia has denied in response to similar accusations before. Reporting on the ground blamed the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, which have fought the army since April 2023. The UN has repeatedly criticised both sides for drone warfare, which it says killed more than 1,100 people in the first half of 2026 alone, and Sudan has previously taken complaints about cross-border drone operations to the Security Council and the African Union.

Latest · Middle East · West Bank — Villages Attacked22 November 2026

Israel’s Premier-Designate Calls Settler Arson in the West Bank “Jewish Terrorism” as Police Open an Investigation

Palestinian villages near Ramallah woke on Sunday to burned-out vehicles and walls daubed with slogans such as “Revenge,” after militant settlers set fires and sprayed racist graffiti in the early hours. No injuries were reported and no group claimed responsibility, but the political reaction was immediate. Gadi Eisenkot, the centre-right Yashar leader tasked by President Isaac Herzog with trying to form a government, labelled the attacks “Jewish terrorism,” saying extremist groups that torch vehicles and homes were undermining regional stability, endangering lives and damaging Israel’s security and foreign relations. He blamed the policies of ultra-nationalist ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich for bringing the area to the brink of anarchy. Ben-Gvir shot back on X that the condemnation amounted to selling out to Arab parties, whose support Eisenkot may need. Police said an investigation had been opened and the military, which also condemned the violence, said soldiers and police had been sent to Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha’al, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani. The attacks come nearly a month after the 27 October election stripped caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of his majority without giving anyone else a clear path to power; if no government can be formed, Israel faces another election next year.

Latest · Europe · Kryvyi Rih & Gulf of Finland — Two Killed, 60 Wounded22 November 2026

A Missile Strike on Zelenskyy’s Hometown Kills a Child and a Young Woman, While a Russian Cargo Ship Burns After a Drone Hit in the Baltic

The war reached from central Ukraine to the Baltic in a single weekend. In Kryvyi Rih, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s hometown, a Friday-night ballistic strike killed a 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman and wounded 60 people, including six children, according to city head Oleksandr Vilkul. He said two missiles hit first and three more rockets struck once doctors, rescuers and utility workers were at the scene, sparking four fires and damaging about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial site. Hours later, the Russian cargo ship Sparta II, sailing toward Kaliningrad, caught fire in the Gulf of Finland after two drones attacked it in neutral waters about three nautical miles from the Russian border; it drifted into Finnish waters, where border guards mounted a rescue and prepared for possible pollution, and Finland’s prime minister Petteri Orpo wrote that the war was spilling into the immediate vicinity. Finnish officials also suspected a Russian helicopter of briefly violating Finnish airspace during the response. The Kryvyi Rih attack followed Russian missiles and a suspected drone coming down in Poland during European military exercises there, which NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called dangerous, pledging that the alliance would defend every inch of Polish territory.

Latest · Americas · Washington — “Not at All”22 November 2026

America’s Intelligence Chief Says Putin Is “Not America’s Friend” in Ukraine, Days After Trump Called Him One

Washington’s top intelligence official has publicly drawn a line under the president’s warm words for Moscow. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told ABC’s This Week on Sunday that Vladimir Putin is not a friend when it comes to Ukraine — “Not at all” — days after President Donald Trump described the Russian leader as a friend at last week’s three-power summit in China with Xi Jinping. Clayton said the word friend was entirely appropriate in the narrower context of talks among the leaders of the world’s three leading nuclear powers, but Trump himself, pressed again on Friday, declined to say whether Putin is a friend to the United States as a whole, replying that he could not speak for America. Trump had defended the label on Thursday by saying it is better to have someone with 4,000 nuclear weapons as a friend than as an enemy. European allies reacted sharply — Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Putin is not our friend and never will be — and Zelenskyy, asked about the remarks on Thursday, shrugged and walked away. Russia, which launched its full-scale invasion in 2022 and holds about a fifth of Ukraine, has shown no sign of dropping its demand that Kyiv surrender all of the eastern Donbas in exchange for peace.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Seoul — Minister Resigns22 November 2026

South Korea’s Education Minister Resigns Over Last-Minute Anti-Cheating Chaos Before the Suneung Exam

South Korea’s education minister has fallen on his sword over the exam that shapes the country’s futures. Choi Kyo-jin stepped down on Sunday and President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation within about an hour, after the ministry was criticised for changing anti-cheating rules right up to the eve of Thursday’s College Scholastic Ability Test, taken by roughly 500,000 students. The scramble followed a confession last Sunday by Han Won-seok, the top scorer in this year’s certified tax accountant exam, that he had photographed questions with a miniature camera and checked answers with a smartphone and generative AI. In response the ministry rushed out measures including pencil-only exam rooms, stickers placed over clothing buttons to block hidden cameras, blocked public Wi-Fi in testing areas, and — for the first time — three nationwide emergency alerts during the exam to flush out phones that had not been handed in. Even so, officials detected 111 suspected cheating cases involving electronic devices. Choi said he could not escape criticism that the ministry had failed to prepare in advance. Lee instructed the prime minister to oversee follow-up measures and wrote that exam fairness is a principle that can never be compromised, as concerns about AI-assisted cheating spread to essay admissions and school tests.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Database Breach Probed22 November 2026

The FBI Investigates an Apparent Hack of Its National Crime Database as Trump Calls the Breach “Not Good”

The system American police use to check warrants, fugitives and backgrounds is at the centre of a security scare. The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, the shared database used by federal, state and local agencies for criminal histories, missing persons, stolen property and gun background checks, according to people briefed on the matter. The bureau has declined to comment, and what was accessed, taken or altered — and by whom — remains unknown. Asked about it in a Fox News interview on Saturday, President Donald Trump said officials had only just heard about it and that it was not good, while suggesting investigators might learn things they would never otherwise have learned. The reported intrusion is the second to hit the bureau this year, after the ShinyHunters group breached an FBI jobs portal in September seeking employment records. It also lands after a week of government and private-sector outages that administration officials have attributed to routine upgrades rather than attack, though there is no indication the NCIC incident is connected to them.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shanghai — Margin Rules Eased22 November 2026

China’s Exchanges Loosen Margin Rules and Cut Fees to Steady a Market Down Sharply From Its June Peak

Beijing is reaching for the levers to calm its stock markets. China’s three main exchanges — Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing — announced a package on Sunday that lets investors borrow more against the same collateral, cutting the minimum margin ratio to 80 per cent from 100 per cent starting 30 November, a reversal of a tightening announced in January. Shanghai and Shenzhen also unveiled fee reductions to lower trading costs, and Shenzhen published draft rules for a market-making mechanism on its ChiNext startup board intended to curb swings and improve pricing. The moves follow early-November steps to encourage share purchases, as equities struggle with sluggish domestic growth, a global sell-off in AI stocks and rising geopolitical tension. The Shanghai Composite fell nearly 3 per cent last week to a 16-month low, while the tech-heavy STAR Market and ChiNext have both tumbled more than 40 per cent from their June peaks. Outstanding margin loans have dropped by more than a fifth, to roughly 2.2 trillion yuan (about $329 billion) from a 3 trillion yuan peak in late June, which the exchanges cited in saying overall leverage is low and that easing would improve liquidity and support long-term stability. South Korea and Taiwan have made similar volatility-steadying changes in recent days.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — Summit on 26–27 November22 November 2026

France and Germany Reject the EU Budget Compromise From Opposite Sides, Five Days Before a Leaders’ Summit

The European Union’s next seven-year budget has been rejected by its two biggest members — for opposite reasons. European Council President António Costa circulated a compromise for the 2028–2034 framework on Saturday that trims the European Commission’s original proposal by roughly €180–204 billion, to around €1.58–1.78 trillion depending on the baseline used, mainly by cutting the Competitiveness Fund, defence and external spending, and floated new revenue including a digital tax on large technology firms. France’s presidency called the draft very disappointing and said it does not constitute a basis for an agreement, arguing it falls well short on the Common Agricultural Policy and on guaranteeing farmers’ incomes. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, whose country is the budget’s biggest net contributor, called it a first step but said the total is still too high, warning that a budget growing by more than 40 per cent does not match national efforts to consolidate, and calling for fairer burden-sharing on the revenue side. The European Parliament’s chief budget negotiator also called the numbers a step backwards. Leaders will debate the plan at a special summit on 26–27 November; the budget needs unanimity among all 27 members, and governments want a political deal before the end of 2026, ahead of elections next year in seven member states.

Latest · Africa · Bayelsa, Nigeria — At Least 24 Dead21 November 2026

At Least 24 Mourners Die as an Overloaded Funeral Boat Capsizes in Nigeria’s Bayelsa State

A journey to a burial ceremony has ended in one of Nigeria’s deadliest river accidents of the year. A boat travelling from Ayama to Ukubie in Bayelsa State capsized on Friday at the river junction between the Oweikorogha and Otuan communities in Southern Ijaw, after what police described as a preliminary mechanical failure. The National Emergency Management Agency said 24 bodies had been recovered by Saturday while search and rescue continued with local divers; another 24 people were rescued, with 19 treated at a clinic in Ayama and five taken to hospital in the state capital, Yenagoa. Two children were among the first four bodies recovered. Police did not confirm how many people were aboard, but residents told Reuters there may have been at least 300, and state officials said the vessel was overloaded, carried no passenger manifest and that most passengers were not wearing life jackets, contrary to marine safety rules. President Bola Tinubu described the sinking as a tragic incident, and the boat’s manager was reported to have been taken into custody while the operator fled. Boat accidents are a recurring hazard in Nigeria’s riverine south, where overloading and weak enforcement are routinely cited after fatal capsizes.
