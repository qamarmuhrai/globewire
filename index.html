Latest · Europe · France24 October 2026

Nearly 1,000 Arrested as 410,000 Protest Across France and Unrest Spreads in Europe

France saw a second weekend of nationwide protests over living costs and school funding on Saturday, with the interior ministry putting turnout at about 410,000 people, including roughly 110,000 in Paris. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said police made nearly 1,000 arrests and faced what he described as an extremely elevated level of violence from black-clad groups who threw projectiles and aimed fireworks at officers. Two people were seriously hurt, in Toulouse and Clermont-Ferrand. The government deployed about 70,000 police and gendarmes, extended night curfews for unaccompanied minors in several northern cities, and used tear gas and water cannon in cities including Lyon, Strasbourg, Marseille, Nice and Nantes. The movement began last month over teacher shortages and has widened to students, workers and unions. Related demonstrations were also reported the same day in Italy, Spain, Belgium and Albania, where students demanded more education funding.

Latest · Europe · Serbia25 October 2026

Serbia Votes in Snap Election as Student Movement Seeks to Unseat Vučić

Polls opened across Serbia on Sunday in a snap parliamentary election that pits the long-dominant Serbian Progressive Party against a student-led list born out of almost two years of anti-corruption protests. The demonstrations began after a concrete canopy collapsed at Novi Sad railway station in November 2024, killing 16 people. Aleksandar Vučić, who has led Serbian politics since 2014 first as prime minister and then as president, resigned the presidency in September to run again for prime minister. His opponents are fielding about 250 candidates, most with no previous time in office, and drew a rally of some 150,000 supporters in Belgrade on Thursday. Opinion polls point to a tight race. Voting runs from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. local time, with first indications expected about two hours after polls close. A student victory would mark a major political shift in the Balkan state of about six million people.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria25 October 2026

Bulgaria Chooses a President After Deadly Drone Strikes Off Its Black Sea Coast

Bulgarians are voting for president on Sunday in an election overshadowed by a series of drone attacks on merchant ships off the country's Black Sea coast that are believed to have killed around two dozen international seafarers. The presidency is mostly ceremonial, but the attacks have pushed maritime security and the war in Ukraine to the centre of the campaign and prompted Sofia to seek help from allies. Polls put two candidates ahead in a field of 27: acting President Iliana Iotova, 61, who stepped up from vice president in January, and Andrey Gyurov, 50, a former caretaker prime minister backed by pro-Western reformist parties who supports Ukraine and signed a security cooperation deal with Kyiv while in office. Iotova, backed by former president Rumen Radev, favours a diplomatic end to the war. Bulgaria has not directly accused Russia of the ship attacks, though the prime minister has said their outcome benefits Moscow. A win for Iotova would make her Bulgaria's first elected female president.

Latest · Americas · Brazil25 October 2026

Brazil Decides Between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro in Presidential Runoff

Brazilians vote on Sunday in a presidential runoff between incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro. In the first round on October 4, Bolsonaro took 47.03% of valid votes to Lula's 45.16%, with neither reaching the 50% needed to win outright. A Quaest poll published on Wednesday put Bolsonaro on 45% and Lula on 44%, a gap inside the survey's two-point margin of error, after interviewing 2,154 people between October 17 and 20. Both camps have spent the final weeks courting supporters of eliminated candidates and the more than one in five voters who stayed home in round one. Bolsonaro has gathered endorsements from several centre-right and right-wing parties and defeated rivals, while Lula has leaned on a stepped-up digital campaign aimed at younger voters. The result will set policy for Latin America's largest economy, home to about 214 million people, for the next four years.

Latest · Middle East · Iran24 October 2026

Iran Warns of “Apocalyptic War” and Bars US Warships From the Gulf

Senior Iranian military figures issued their starkest warnings of the eight-month war on Saturday, saying any renewed attack by the United States and Israel would bring consequences well beyond Iran. Revolutionary Guard spokesman Brigadier General Hossein Mohebbi said defending the country's territory and its national and religious identity was now among the nation's most important duties, and claimed Iran would achieve absolute victory in what he called an apocalyptic war that would decide the fate of other states in the region. In a separate statement, armed forces chief Major General Ali Abdollahi said American naval forces could no longer approach the Persian Gulf and would never be allowed to bring their vessels back in. The remarks came amid reports that Washington is considering resuming air strikes, a day after a US envoy suggested a strike could come this weekend. In earlier phases of the war, Iran retaliated against US bases and energy sites in Gulf states.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia25 October 2026

Riyadh Airport Reopens After Houthi Attacks as Both Sides Trade Missile Claims

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh resumed operations on Sunday after a closure of about two weeks linked to Houthi attacks, with Terminal 1 handling domestic flights and Terminal 2 handling international services by national carriers. Passengers were urged to confirm bookings with their airlines before travelling. The reopening followed a fresh round of Houthi strikes on Saturday that the group said targeted three Saudi airports, energy installations, a pipeline and an air base, in retaliation for strikes it blamed on Riyadh that it said killed 17 people. Saudi civil defence said debris fell in the Mecca and Medina regions after two ballistic missiles were intercepted, damaging some buildings. The Houthis denied aiming at Islam's two holiest cities, calling the Saudi account a huge lie. Indian carriers have extended suspensions on several Saudi routes into late October and November, offering rebooking or refunds. The attacks mark a further breakdown of a years-long truce as the wider regional war continues.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine24 October 2026

Starlink Speeds Restored in Ukraine as Musk Says He Ordered the Throttling

Ukraine says its Starlink and Starshield satellite links are back to full speed after more than a week of restrictions that disrupted front-line operations. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said restoration began overnight and that by Saturday morning speeds were sufficient for logistics and for defence against Russia's fast attack drones, with the defence ministry reporting stable connections above 400 km/h. The curbs, first reported on October 14, had limited connectivity for equipment moving faster than about 100 mph and, Ukrainian officials said, forced some drone missions to be cancelled. Hours after Kyiv announced the fix, SpaceX chief Elon Musk said his company had imposed the throttling, arguing Ukraine's military had used the civilian Starlink service for offensive drone strikes in breach of its terms. He said Starshield, by contrast, is controlled by the US government. The episode has added strain to Kyiv's ties with Washington, already tense over US policy on Russian fuel and the future of negotiations to end the war.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia25 October 2026

Sydney Opens New 24-Hour International Airport After a Decade of Planning

Western Sydney International Airport welcomed its first passengers on Sunday, ending a wait of more than a decade of planning and seven years of construction. The A$5.6 billion facility at Badgerys Creek, about 60 kilometres west of central Sydney, will operate around the clock — unlike the existing airport closer to the city centre, which cannot take off or land between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the opening a milestone, noting Australia had not opened a major new airport in more than 50 years. Qantas budget carrier Jetstar was set to operate the first outbound flight, to the Gold Coast. The airport, built on a 1,780-hectare site bought by the federal government in the 1980s and completed in July 2025, says it can handle more than 80 million passenger movements a year. Cargo flights began there in July, ahead of Sunday's passenger launch.

Latest · Europe · Germany24 October 2026

Fire Guts Hitler's “Eagle's Nest” Retreat in Bavaria; Suspect Detained

A fire has devastated the Kehlsteinhaus, the mountaintop retreat built for Adolf Hitler and known as the Eagle's Nest, high in the Bavarian Alps above Berchtesgaden. The alarm was raised at about 9:15 p.m. on Friday, and crews arriving up the narrow mountain road found the building completely ablaze. About 200 firefighters took part, but rain turning to the season's first significant snow, plus strong winds, forced them to pull back overnight, and by Saturday they still could not enter because of falling material. No one was injured. Police stopped a man on a motorcycle heading down the road away from the scene and arrested him on suspicion of arson. Prosecutors said the 33-year-old German, who has ties to the area but does not live there, showed signs of mental illness and was being sent to a psychiatric facility, while investigators also examine a possible political motive given the site's history. Built in 1938 as a guest house for Nazi officials, the monument draws about 350,000 visitors a year.

Latest · Americas · Business23 October 2026

American Express to Cut 3,500 Jobs in a Week of Layoffs at Big Employers

American Express says it will cut about 3,500 jobs, roughly 5% of its global workforce, as it tries to rein in rising costs and simplify its structure — even after reporting a strong quarter. The company posted third-quarter net profit of $3.71 billion, up 28% year on year, with revenue rising 9% to about $20.06 billion, and it booked a $336 million pre-tax restructuring charge. Chief executive Stephen Squeri told analysts the aim was to reduce management layers, speed up decisions and free up investment for priorities, including preparing the business for wider use of artificial intelligence. The announcement capped a week of cuts elsewhere: Barclays said it would shed about 5,000 jobs, eBay about 1,000, and Volvo Cars another 2,500 on top of 3,000 reductions earlier this year, as the Swedish carmaker targets a further $500 million in savings amid a tough Chinese market. Amex shares fell after the news. The company has not said which countries or teams will be affected.
