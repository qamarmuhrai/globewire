Latest · Americas · US Midterms — The Court Ruling3 November 2026

No Segregation, No Delay: Supreme Court Rejects Trump Bid to Set Aside 20,000 California Ballots on Election Eve

The Supreme Court closed the door, in a single sentence and with no public dissent, on the Trump administration’s last attempt to intervene in how California counts its votes. On Monday the justices denied an emergency request filed only hours earlier by the Justice Department, which wanted the state ordered to separate and hold back mail-in ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters that federal officials, using a Department of Homeland Security list, say are likely not US citizens. California argued the list is unreliable and could sweep in thousands of eligible citizens, and that the state already has safeguards in place. Lower courts had already said no: District Judge David Carter on 28 October called it unprecedented for the federal government, on the eve of an election, to try to coerce a state into abandoning its own constitutional election procedures, and the 9th Circuit refused the request on Friday, stressing state power over elections. The administration warned that once challenged ballots are mixed with lawful ones they cannot be retrieved; the Court, which did not even ask California for a response, let the state’s existing procedures stand as voting for control of Congress got under way. It was the second election defeat for the administration at the Court this autumn, after September’s rejection of a plan to change how mail ballots are sent.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Election Day3 November 2026

Polls Open With Democrats Favoured for the House and the Senate on a Knife Edge: Forecasts Put a Sweep Within Reach

Americans are voting today for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships, in a midterm that will decide who controls Congress for the final two years of Donald Trump’s presidency. The final evidence pointed one way nationally and more narrowly in the states that matter. The RealClearPolling generic-ballot average closed at Democrats 52.0% to Republicans 43.1%, with the last NBC News poll at 52% to 45%, Reuters/Ipsos at 53% to 44% and the New York Times/Siena national sample giving Democrats an 11-point edge. Forecasters translated that into a strong House expectation — Decision Desk HQ gave Democrats an 84% chance with 241 seats projected, other models 92% to 95% — and a much tighter Senate call, at 64% to 76% for a Democratic majority of 52 or 53 seats. Republicans start from 53 Senate seats, so Democrats need a net gain of four; in the House they need only about three. Prediction markets agreed on the shape: Polymarket on Monday morning put Democrats at 95% for the House and 73% for the Senate. More than 51 million people had already voted early or by mail before today, and the first polls close at 6pm Eastern, the last in Alaska at 1am Eastern.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Toll Rises, Talks Set3 November 2026

26 Dead at the Aid Centre Now, and a Date for Talks: Witkoff Sets Abu Dhabi for 10–11 November as Kyiv Mourns Komyshuvakha

Ukraine raised the toll from Monday’s bombing of the aid-distribution school in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia, to at least 26 killed, with five injured and search work continuing where people had sheltered from the first bombs before a second wave hit. Acting regional governor Mykhailo Semikin said the dead include two rescue workers and four police officers. The strike came hours after the six-day Oleshky humanitarian ceasefire — the longest pause of the war, brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump to allow an evacuation mission — expired on Sunday with no violations reported and, Kyiv says, a Ukrainian offer to extend it refused by Moscow. Hours after the bombing, US special envoy Steve Witkoff announced that Ukrainian and Russian delegations will meet in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November to discuss post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian measures and ending the war — the first US-mediated round since February, before the US-Israeli war on Iran froze the track. President Putin agreed to the round after meeting Witkoff in St Petersburg on 21 October, negotiators say, as Washington pushes for a breakthrough before winter.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Warning3 November 2026

“Tomorrow the World Will Be Watching the American Midterms”: Zelensky Warns of a Massive Russian Attack Timed to the Vote

President Volodymyr Zelensky used his evening address to warn that Ukrainian intelligence expects a new large-scale Russian attack aimed mainly at energy and logistics — and that Moscow may be timing it for Election Day in the United States, when attention and headlines are elsewhere. “We have intel that Russia is preparing a new massive attack on our country,” he said, arguing that Kyiv needs unmistakable signals that, in his words, terror will bring no gains and that Russian oil revenues must be squeezed until Moscow has no alternative but to end the war. The warning followed a month Zelensky presented as a turning point on the ground: he said Ukrainian forces retook 96.2 square kilometres in October, more than Russia captured, and inflicted 54,400 Russian casualties — killed, wounded or missing — the highest monthly figure of the full-scale war, a pattern independent tracking by the Institute for the Study of War broadly echoed with different figures showing a second straight month of net Russian ground loss. Overnight attacks have continued at scale, including a 433-drone barrage under cloud cover in recent days that killed a 13-year-old girl at a dormitory for displaced families in Vinnytsia region.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Strikes Continue3 November 2026

Five More Killed in Gaza on Monday as Israel Steps Up Strikes, Pulls Out of Hebron, and Jordan’s King Restates the Statehood Condition

Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians across Gaza on Monday, health officials there said — three in two strikes in Khan Younis in the south, one in al-Mussadar in the centre, and one when a vehicle was hit in Gaza City’s Tel al-Hawa neighbourhood — following nine killed on Sunday, including five police officers in al-Mawasi and a four-year-old boy in Gaza City’s Rimal district. Israel says Hamas is rearming and violating the year-old US-brokered ceasefire, and the military says it has killed six Hamas militants in separate strikes over the past two weeks; Hamas denies rebuilding its arsenal and calls the attacks a deliberate undermining of the deal. Gaza officials count more than 1,580 Palestinians killed since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025, while negotiators remain stuck on Hamas disarmament and an Israeli withdrawal from the roughly two-thirds of the territory its troops still control. In the West Bank, Israeli forces withdrew from a Hebron neighbourhood after a ten-day operation that had emptied more than 3,000 residents following a 23 October checkpoint attack that killed two soldiers — an operation Israeli officials themselves judged to have become ineffective. Jordan’s King Abdullah II said on Monday there will be no security or stability in the region until Palestinian statehood is fulfilled.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — The Red Sea Port3 November 2026

Seven Wounded at Ras Issa: Saudi Strikes Hit the Houthis’ Biggest Port and an Oil Facility as the Yemen War Reignites

Saudi-led coalition strikes on Sunday and Monday hit Ras Issa port and an oil facility in Bajil district, in Yemen’s Hodeidah province, wounding seven people, the Houthi health ministry said — four civilians at the port and three at the oil site. Houthi channel Al-Masirah said a pier and a commercial ship were attacked at Ras Issa, the oil and fuel terminal about 55 kilometres north of Hodeidah city, and reported strikes near Hodeidah University’s faculty of medicine, where residents told AFP they heard an explosion. The coalition described its action as a precision operation to neutralise vital resources the Houthis use to support their war effort, after continued attacks on civilians and threats to Red Sea navigation, and said on Monday it had intercepted and destroyed a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Saudi Arabia’s south, after briefly activating emergency alerts in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Taif and Najran. Hodeidah is the largest seaport under Houthi control and the main entry point for food and aid to the north; the Houthis called the port strike a war crime. The exchange marks a further unravelling of the fragile calm in a civil war that reignited between the Iran-backed group and the coalition backing Yemen’s recognised government.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — Landfall Done3 November 2026

Landfall Complete at Ongole: Murasu Downs Thousands of Trees, Floods Districts and Cuts a District’s Rain Deficit in a Single Night

Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu finished crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast near Ongole in the early hours of Tuesday — the India Meteorological Department put the crossing between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, at 3:30 to 5:30am, with winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph after the landfall process began around 1am near Kothapatnam. By daylight the damage was visible across Bapatla and Prakasam districts: thousands of trees uprooted, hundreds of electric poles damaged, fishing boats wrecked, thatched houses collapsed in Ongole’s Samatha Nagar with residents evacuated, crops flattened and many areas under sheets of water, with vehicles stranded on highways in near-zero visibility. About 200 mm of rain fell in six hours at some central and south-coastal stations; in normally parched Prakasam, one night cut the season’s rainfall deficit from over 64% to 58.6%. Nineteen flights were cancelled and, by live-report estimates, more than 200,000 people were moved to safety overall, including some 400 families from a single coastal village. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu, who reviewed the storm by teleconference at 3:30am, ordered that roads stay closed until fallen wires and debris are cleared — a lesson from Cyclone Arnab a week earlier, when at least five people died touching live wires. Heavy rain continues today across ten districts under red warnings, and fishermen are told to stay ashore until Wednesday morning.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Day Two on the Streets3 November 2026

Paramilitary Deployed as Garment Protests Turn Violent for a Second Day: Around 670 Factories Shut Over the 30,000-Taka Demand

Bangladesh’s garment shutdown entered a second day on Monday with the state putting more force on the street. Workers demanding a minimum monthly wage of 30,000 taka — against the current 12,500 taka set in December 2023 — blocked roads and clashed with police across Dhaka, Gazipur, Savar, Ashulia and Mirpur, and authorities deployed additional paramilitary forces after reports of vehicle vandalism and road blockades; roughly 670 factories were reported shut in the latest counts, close to Sunday’s peak, with a police vehicle set on fire in the first wave and 20 to 25 factories vandalised. The government’s answer so far is procedural: a six-member wage board formed on Sunday under Senior District and Sessions Judge Sheikh Ashfaqur Rahman, and an order from Prime Minister Tarique Rahman to protect workers while keeping factories running. The industry’s exposure is the calendar as much as the wage bill — November to February is peak order season for a sector that employs about 4 million people, earns over 80% of Bangladesh’s export income, supplies H&M, Zara, Walmart and Primark, and books roughly 40% of its annual orders in these months.

Latest · Global Affairs · Crypto — The $6 Billion Denial3 November 2026

“Absurd Rhetoric” and a “Trite Provocation”: Kim Yo Jong Rejects the FBI’s Claim That North Korea Stole $6 Billion From Binance

North Korea answered the largest cryptocurrency accusation ever made against it with a flat denial from the top of the regime. Kim Yo Jong, sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said on Tuesday through state agency KCNA that FBI allegations linking Pyongyang to the theft of more than $6 billion from Binance are “absurd rhetoric” and a “trite provocation” meant to brand her country a cybercriminal state. The FBI said at the weekend that the TraderTraitor group — the same North Korean state-sponsored unit blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft last year — drained the funds on or about 29 October in a breach that unfolded in roughly 20 minutes, and that the actors are converting the haul mainly into Ethereum for laundering through decentralised finance and instant-swap services; analysts at Elliptic count the proceeds scattered across some 12,000 wallets, with about $2.85 billion frozen or seized. Binance says it has covered about 95% of the $6.3 billion stolen using company money to buy replacement tokens, resumed withdrawals on Sunday, and still saw customers pull a net $3.64 billion in the first nine and a half hours. Chainalysis estimates 89% of crypto hack losses in 2026 are linked to North Korea — a sum the Seoul government’s GDP figures put at roughly a sixth of the North’s entire annual output. President Trump has said the US is still not certain North Korea was involved.

Latest · Global Affairs · The Hague — A President by Video3 November 2026

Barred From New York by Sanctions, the ICC President Tells All 193 UN Members to Defend Her Court — and Vows It Will Not Be Dismantled

The International Criminal Court’s president delivered her annual report to the UN General Assembly by video on Monday because US sanctions made it difficult for her to enter the country — and used the address to accuse the Trump administration of trying to obstruct international justice. Judge Tomoko Akane of Japan called the measures against the court “extraordinary” and “unprecedented,” saying they threaten judges, prosecutors and the institution itself, and she asked all 193 UN member states, not only the 125 parties to the court, to take concrete action in its defence. Washington first sanctioned individual officials, including Akane herself in August, and then, on 9 October — the day former ICC judge Navi Pillay received the Nobel Peace Prize — targeted the entire court; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US would ban transactions with what he called a rogue court and cripple its ability to operate. Akane noted that 13 judges and prosecutors have been sanctioned, that six states notified withdrawals during the reporting year, and that arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former defence minister Yoav Gallant remain outstanding. “The court will continue to fully discharge its mandate with independence and impartiality,” she said. The State Department offered no immediate comment; the General Assembly had voted 106 to 8, with the US among the noes, to allow the video presentation.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine Support — The Rehearsal3 November 2026

2,800 Troops, 10 Countries, No Tanks: Europe’s Post-Ceasefire Force for Ukraine Holds Its First Exercise in Poland This Month

Europe is rehearsing for a peace it does not yet have. Some 2,800 troops from 10 European countries will stage the first exercise of the Multinational Force – Ukraine in southeast Poland from 9 to 22 November, London and Paris announced on Tuesday, around the town of Nowa Deba, about 100 kilometres west of the Ukrainian border. France provides the largest contingent, 1,100 troops, and the UK 300, alongside soldiers from Belgium, Estonia, Germany, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Poland, Spain and Sweden, working on air and land operations with light equipment only — drones for anti-drone and self-defence drills rather than heavy armour, and no tanks. The force, created in 2025 with an operational headquarters already running in Paris and led by France and the UK, is explicitly not a NATO operation and has little US involvement; it has no offensive mission and would deploy inside Ukraine only once a ceasefire ends the war. French General Armel Dirou, who will co-lead the exercise with Britain’s Lieutenant General Tom Bateman, called it a key milestone and a concrete demonstration that the force could deploy as soon as hostilities end.

Latest · Africa · Sudan–Ethiopia — The Ambassador Recalled3 November 2026

Khartoum Recalls Its Ambassador From Addis Ababa and Accuses Ethiopia and the UAE Over the Airport Attack That Closed Khartoum’s Skies

Sudan’s quarrel with Ethiopia escalated from paperwork to diplomacy on Tuesday. Khartoum recalled its ambassador from Addis Ababa and accused both Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates of being behind drone attacks on Sudan, including a strike on Khartoum International Airport that forced operations to suspend for three days — days after the airport had received its first flight since the war began in April 2023, in what had looked like a return of calm to the capital. The Sudanese military says it has evidence that four drone attacks have been launched from Ethiopian territory since early March with UAE-supplied drones, an allegation Ethiopia’s foreign ministry dismissed as baseless, counter-accusing Khartoum of funding Tigray-linked rebels. The recall follows Sunday’s formal complaints to the UN Security Council and the African Union, backed by a Transitional Sovereignty Council report documenting 70 alleged attacks from Ethiopian soil between 15 February and 27 October — 187 one-way attack drones and 82 drone-dropped munitions, killing or wounding 484 civilians and killing 176 soldiers — and Monday’s fresh strikes on power stations in Kassala and near the Merowe dam, in a week when about 14 stations in eight states were reported destroyed. The UN describes Sudan’s war, with more than 150,000 estimated dead and about 14 million displaced, as the world’s worst humanitarian crisis.
