Latest · Asia-Pacific · Odisha Coast — Landfall Under Way20 November 2026

Landfall Has Begun: A Weaker Akvan Grinds Into the Coast Near Gopalpur

Cyclone Akvan is coming ashore. The India Meteorological Department said on Friday morning that the landfall process had started, with the storm’s sheared cloud mass already pushing into the north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts, and the crossing near Gopalpur expected to take around three hours. Akvan weakened overnight from a severe cyclonic storm into a cyclonic storm over the west-central Bay of Bengal, so the winds at landfall are now put at 70–80 km/h gusting to 90 — down from the 90–100 gusting to 110 forecast on Thursday — but the rain threat remains severe. At 2:30 a.m. the centre sat about 170 km south of Gopalpur, moving north-northwest at 21 km/h. Heavy rain and gusty winds hit Bhubaneswar from about 1 a.m. Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi spent more than two hours overnight at the Special Relief Commissioner’s office pressing for a zero-casualty outcome; the state’s final pre-landfall count put more than three lakh — over 300,000 — people evacuated and 423 rescue teams deployed, with red alerts in Srikakulam and Vizianagaram districts.

Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — The Tower Strike20 November 2026

Riyadh’s Tallest Tower Is Hit, Then the Missiles Keep Coming

Saudi Arabia is absorbing a second front in the Iran war. On Thursday a Houthi projectile struck the 80-storey PIF Tower — Riyadh’s tallest building and home to the kingdom’s sovereign wealth fund — blowing out windows across seven to eight floors and bending its metal facade, civil defence confirmed, with damage to nearby buildings in the King Abdullah Financial District but no injuries. It was the district’s second hit in under two weeks, after Aramco’s building was damaged on 8 November, and the financial district was closed to visitors. The attacks did not stop: early on Friday the Saudi-led coalition said air defences had intercepted and destroyed another ballistic missile aimed at the kingdom’s south, and early warnings sounded in Abha and Khamis Mushait. Riyadh warned it would not tolerate attacks on its territory or national assets, and Qatar, Kuwait and the GCC condemned the strikes. In Washington, a Pentagon official put the cost of the US war effort at more than $48 billion as Senate Republicans blocked a bid to limit the president’s war powers, while Donald Trump again said only that Washington would “see what happens” after 1 January.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Closing Day20 November 2026

The Word “Fossil” Has Vanished: COP31 Heads Into Its Final Hours

The climate summit in Antalya is due to close on Friday with its central promise missing. A revised draft of the COP31 cover decision, published on Thursday after presidency consultations, was cut from 144 paragraphs to 80 — and every reference to fossil fuels was removed. Monday’s first draft had gone further than any previous summit text, restating the COP28 pledge to transition away from fossil fuels and adding a call to accelerate their phase-out immediately; the new text still acknowledges warming is projected to pass 1.5°C and calls for deep, rapid and sustained emissions cuts, but names no fuel and sets no phase-out date. What survived is the “35 by 35” goal — lifting electricity’s share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035 — and a call to at least triple adaptation finance by 2035, though the earlier $120 billion-a-year figure was dropped even as assessed needs stand near $305 billion a year. Panama has threatened to withhold consensus unless the final text addresses fossil fuel production and finance, the United States government is absent from the talks, and negotiators expect the wording to change again — summits of this kind routinely overrun their deadline.

Latest · Global Affairs · New York — No Successor20 November 2026

Eight Ballots, No Leader: The UN Could Start 2027 With an Empty Chair

With less than two months left of António Guterres’s term, the United Nations still has no agreed successor — and officials are now drafting contingency plans for the once-unthinkable case that nobody is in charge when he steps down on 31 December. The Security Council has held eight rounds of secret straw polls since July and is expected to vote again this week, but none of the candidates has won the backing of all five veto-wielding members. Costa Rica’s Rebeca Grynspan, who heads UN Trade and Development, leads the field — 12 “encourage” votes in the 5 November poll — ahead of Argentina’s Rafael Grossi on nine and Guyana’s Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett on eight, yet the one colour-coded ballot that reveals permanent-member positions, on 16 October, showed each of the top three carrying two red “discourage” votes, any one of which can become a veto. The UAE’s Lana Nusseibeh, fourth in the polling, faces pushback over her country’s alleged backing of Sudan’s Rapid Support Forces. Options now being discussed include temporarily extending Guterres’s term or naming an acting chief — a deadlock analysts say reflects the permanent members’ deepening divisions and a US administration that has cut UN funding and increasingly works around the institution.

Latest · Middle East · Central Gaza — Operation Over20 November 2026

Tanks In, Tanks Out: Israel Ends Its First Central Gaza Ground Operation of the Ceasefire

Israel’s military says the ground operation it launched into central Gaza on Wednesday — the first broad incursion since the US-brokered ceasefire took effect in October 2025 — is over. Tanks had moved into three blocks around the Bureij and Nuseirat refugee camps, residents were ordered out, and a stretch of Salah al-Din road, the territory’s main north-south highway, was closed. The army said the target was a tunnel west of the Yellow Line that threatened its troops; COGAT, the defence body handling civilian affairs, announced the activity had ended and the road reopened, with Palestinians free to move in the affected blocks again. Residents told reporters the route still felt dangerous, with sporadic gunfire, and correspondents reported demolished homes and damaged land north of Nuseirat. Hamas called the incursion a blatant violation of the truce. The wider picture is deteriorating with it: four governments — Australia, Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom — jointly called on Israel to grant immediate, unrestricted aid access across Gaza, saying recent flooding has deepened the suffering there, while Gaza health authorities put Palestinian deaths since the ceasefire began at more than 1,650.

Latest · Europe · Germany — Strike Day20 November 2026

Production Stops Today: IG Metall’s 24-Hour Walkout Reaches the Factory Gates

The warning has become the walkout. IG Metall’s 24-hour strikes are under way on Friday at hundreds of auto and steel plants across Germany, in a pay dispute covering 3.7 million workers. BMW expects output to halt for the day at its Munich, Dingolfing and Landshut plants, with the Munich walkout beginning late on Thursday evening, and Audi’s main Ingolstadt plant is stopping for a full 24 hours; Mercedes-Benz, Tata Steel and big suppliers such as Bosch and ZF are also in the action, backed by local ballots that returned majorities of roughly 89 to 98%. The union wants a 5% rise over 12 months, a €110 increase for trainees, job and site guarantees, and profit-sharing where firms are doing well. Employers have offered a conditional one-off payment and 1.3% — and only after a long delay — which Bavarian employer chief Bertram Brossardt defended while calling the day-long strikes a breach of the tariff partnership. These are the first all-day warning strikes since 2018, they are the union’s last step before a ballot on an open-ended strike, and no fourth round of talks has been scheduled.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Tokyo — The December Decision20 November 2026

Softer Than Expected, Still Above Target: Japan’s Prices Keep a Rate Rise Alive

Japan’s inflation came in a touch cooler than forecast on Friday — but not cool enough to change the Bank of Japan’s course. Core consumer prices, excluding fresh food, rose 2.2% in October from a year earlier, up from 2.1% in September and above the bank’s 2% target for a second straight month, though below the 2.3% economists expected; headline inflation rose to 2.3%, and the measure that also strips out fuel slowed to 2.2% from 2.4%. Goods prices climbed 3.2% while services rose just 1.4%, a pattern analysts read as cost pressure from raw materials and a weak yen rather than wage-driven demand. The policy rate already stands at 1.25% after rises in June and September, and the bank declared last month that underlying inflation had reached its 2% target for the first time since that target was set in 2013. Markets now price roughly an 86% chance of a further rise to 1.50% when the board meets on 17–18 December, and Governor Kazuo Ueda said on Thursday the bank expects to keep raising rates because conditions remain accommodative; one research house now sees the rate reaching 2.5% by the middle of next year.

Latest · Global Affairs · Washington — The Chip Licences20 November 2026

879,000 Chips: Washington Approved a Flood of AI Sales to China, a House Panel Reveals

The scale of America’s licensed AI chip trade with China is now on the record. The House Foreign Affairs Committee said on Thursday that the Commerce Department approved shipments of 879,000 advanced chips made by Nvidia and AMD to Chinese companies in just six months, from December 2025 through May 2026 — including 150,000 for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD, all of them on the Pentagon’s list of alleged Chinese military-backed companies. The chips include Nvidia’s H200 and AMD’s MI325X, and companies had sought permission to ship 3.4 million in the period; how many approved units actually reached China is not clear. Committee chair Brian Mast called the release the most detailed public disclosure in the history of the Export Control Reform Act. The Commerce Department says H200 licensing follows a January 2026 rule imposing extra national-security requirements for case-by-case approval rather than a presumption of denial. The disclosure landed the same week the committee aired scrutiny of a former Nvidia chief executive’s alleged links to a Chinese spy — and days after the Shenzhen summit put US–China technology rivalry at the centre of world politics.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Measles Is Endemic Again20 November 2026

26 Years Undone: The United States Officially Loses Its Measles-Free Status

A public-health milestone that took decades to win has been formally withdrawn. The Pan American Health Organization announced on Tuesday that the United States has lost its measles elimination status — held since 2000 — after its expert panel concluded that a single strain of the virus, first transmitted on 20 January 2025, has circulated without interruption for more than twelve months. PAHO reached the same finding for Bolivia and Mexico, while US rubella elimination remains unchanged. The numbers behind the decision are the worst in a generation: more than 5,400 confirmed US cases this year by CDC counts — over 5,700 infections in some tallies — and 10 deaths, the deadliest episode in more than three decades, with most cases among unvaccinated people. Across the Americas, confirmed cases reached 58,213 by 13 November, against 12,596 in the same period of 2025. Washington must now submit a plan to win the status back, which requires stopping transmission and then proving a full year without it. Canada, Spain and the United Kingdom have also recently lost elimination status.

22 Militants Killed in Three Districts as Pakistan’s Northwest Campaign Rolls On

Pakistan’s military says its forces killed 22 militants in a series of operations across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa earlier this week, in the latest round of a counter-terrorism campaign that has made the northwest the country’s most violent region this year. According to the Inter-Services Public Relations statement issued on Thursday, an intelligence-based operation in South Waziristan killed 15 fighters after an intense exchange of fire, two separate engagements in Bannu district killed six, and one more was killed in Tank district; the operations took place on 16 and 17 November, with sanitisation sweeps continuing afterwards. The military describes those killed as members of the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan under its official designation, and says the campaign will continue under the Azm-e-Istehkam framework approved by the federal apex committee. President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif both praised the forces. The toll follows a heavier week: on 13 November the military reported 74 militants killed in operations across Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and research groups rank the province as the country’s worst-affected by violence through 2026.
