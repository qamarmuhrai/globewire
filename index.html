Latest · Europe · Ukraine War21 October 2026

Putin Agrees in Principle to Trilateral Peace Talks With Ukraine and the US, Kremlin Says

Russian President Vladimir Putin has agreed in principle to resume trilateral negotiations with the United States and Ukraine to end the war, Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov told reporters on Wednesday after US special envoy Steve Witkoff met Putin at the Constantine Palace in St Petersburg. Ushakov said the talks would continue after 10 November and would focus on building a security system for Russia and Ukraine once the fighting ends, a subject that could include Ukraine's path toward European Union membership. Witkoff arrived in Russia after a day of talks in Oslo with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European leaders, where a proposal covering Kyiv's security guarantees and economic recovery funds was drawn up for him to present to Putin. The shift follows recent US steps welcomed in Moscow, including Washington's decision to lift sanctions on sales of Russian diesel fuel. Zelenskyy emerged from the Oslo meeting describing the proposal's security guarantees as strong, though the two sides remain far apart on territory and other core questions.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz21 October 2026

Iran Says Its Naval Mines Hit Three Oil Tankers in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Tuesday that naval mines laid by their forces struck three oil tankers attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz by a southern route Tehran considers illegal. In a statement on their Sepah News outlet, the Guards said two tankers were set ablaze and halted after mines exploded at what they called an illegal crossing point in the southern part of the strait, and that a third tanker, carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil with its radar and navigation systems switched off, exploded and caught fire after striking another mine. The Guards also said their air defences shot down a US MQ-9 Reaper drone over the strait on Monday night. Iran has sought to control passage through the waterway, which in peacetime carried about a fifth of the world's oil, since the war that began with US and Israeli attacks on 28 February, while the United States maintains a counter-blockade on Iranian ports. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei warned on Tuesday that any attack on Iran would be answered with equal or greater force.

Latest · Europe · Italy21 October 2026

Meloni Approves Tougher Anti-Riot Laws and Sends 500 Troops to Milan After Weekend Violence

Italy's government has approved a package of security measures creating a new offence of violent participation in a demonstration, punishable by up to five years in prison and a fine of up to €20,000, after unrest swept northern Italian cities at the weekend. In Milan, gangs made up largely of young people smashed windows, looted shops and attacked police during cost-of-living protests, injuring more than 100 officers over two days and leading to over 200 detentions. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said there was nothing revolutionary about vandalism and theft, calling them criminality rather than protest. The decree, which takes immediate effect but must be converted into law by parliament within 60 days, makes parents financially liable for damage caused by their children, raises penalties when faces are concealed or people are hurt, and allows police to detain youths aged 14 to 17 who are judged to pose a danger even if they have not committed a crime. The government will also deploy 500 soldiers alongside police in Milan until the end of 2026 to guard sensitive sites.

Australian Lawmaker Loses Both Legs After 'Targeted' Car Attack as Accused Driver Faces Court

Australian independent MP Helen Haines has had both legs amputated after being struck by a car outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria, on Monday in what police describe as a targeted attack. Her husband Phil told reporters outside a Melbourne hospital on Wednesday that she is alive and stable, her left leg having been amputated on Monday and her right on Wednesday. Police say the 65-year-old was getting items from the boot of her car when a Toyota Land Cruiser drove past, turned around and veered into her, pinning her against her vehicle. Brendan Nugent, 43, appeared in Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and the federal offence of harming a public official, which carries up to 10 years in prison; he did not apply for bail and returns to court on 4 February. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the attack a test of the country's character, while the federal police commissioner warned that online extremism and a rising volume of threats against parliamentarians are fuelling political polarisation in a country where such violence has been rare.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate21 October 2026

UN Warns a Record-Strength El Niño 'Wrecking Ball' Could Push Pacific Heat Beyond Anything Recorded

The World Meteorological Organization warned on Wednesday that the developing El Niño could become the strongest on record, with temperatures in the central Pacific forecast to rise about 3.8 degrees Celsius above normal between November and January — beyond the roughly 3-degree anomalies seen in past record events. WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said the agency had never recorded a figure of 3.8, describing an event that could roll around the world like a wrecking ball. The outlook points to flooding risks in southern Africa from November, warmer and drier conditions that could strain agriculture and water supplies in Southeast Asia, and heavier rainfall across parts of Latin America. El Niño is a natural warming phase of the Pacific climate cycle rather than a product of climate change, but scientists say global warming can intensify its effects. Saulo and UN humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher jointly called for urgent preparations to protect housing, water, sanitation and health services in the regions most exposed.

US Says More Than 300,000 Visas Have Been Revoked Since Trump Returned to Office

The United States has revoked more than 300,000 visas since President Donald Trump returned to office, a senior State Department official said on Tuesday. Assistant Secretary of State for Consular Affairs Mora Namdar wrote on X that the revocations were continuing and told visa holders to respect US laws or lose the privilege of travelling to the country, adding that safeguarding the United States comes first under Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. The White House said the administration had revoked visas at a faster pace than any in history. State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott said the cancellations targeted foreign nationals who engage in illegal activity or pose a threat to national security, saying there is zero tolerance for visa holders who break the law, commit fraud or undermine national security. The figure was announced as Rubio travelled in India, where he reassured officials that tightened US visa rules are not aimed at Indian professionals.

Super Typhoon Rosal Begins to Weaken Off Northern Luzon but Heavy Rain Warnings Stay in Force

Super Typhoon Rosal, known internationally as Champi, started to weaken on Wednesday afternoon as it moved offshore east of northern Luzon, the Philippine weather bureau PAGASA said, with maximum sustained winds easing to 205 kilometres per hour from a peak of 215 kph and gusts dropping to 250 kph. The storm was last located 725 kilometres east of Aparri, Cagayan, at 4 p.m., moving west-northwest at 25 kph on a track that keeps it over water. No Philippine landfall is expected, but Rosal will pass closest to extreme northern Luzon between Thursday afternoon and Friday morning, and Wind Signal No. 2 remains raised over Batanes with Signal No. 1 over parts of Cagayan, the Babuyan Islands, Isabela, Apayao, Ilocos Norte and Catanduanes. PAGASA expects moderate to heavy rain of 50 to 100 millimetres over Batanes, Cagayan and Apayao in the next 24 hours, rising to 100 to 200 millimetres in Batanes into Friday, with waves of up to eight metres possible off the far northern coasts. The storm is forecast to be downgraded to a typhoon by Friday and to leave Philippine waters by Friday night or early Saturday as it turns north toward Taiwan.

Rubio Says Ukraine's Starlink Access Is Elon Musk's Decision, Not the White House's

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday that decisions about Ukraine's access to the Starlink satellite service rest with SpaceX and Elon Musk rather than the White House, remarks that appeared to acknowledge Musk may have restricted Kyiv's access. The Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday that Musk had ordered a slowdown of a military-grade version of Starlink that Ukraine relies on to defend against Russian missile attacks; the Journal cited one source saying the matter was discussed with Trump and White House officials beforehand, while two US officials and a Western official briefed on the matter told Reuters the decision ultimately came from the White House — a version asserted by President Zelenskyy and denied by the administration. Speaking to reporters during a trip to India, Rubio said he was not aware the administration knew of the decision in advance, but that it was governed by a private commercial contract covering how the service can be used and at what speeds. Trump has said publicly that neither he nor Musk deliberately ordered a slowdown, and Rubio added that his understanding was that the reduced speed had been restored.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shipping21 October 2026

Container Lines Accelerate Their Return to the Suez Canal as Red Sea Traffic Recovers

Container shipping lines are moving back to the Red Sea and Suez Canal route between Asia and Europe, with industry analysis published on Wednesday showing almost two-thirds of container capacity on Far East–Europe trades now sailing through the Red Sea. Alphaliner data covering 15 September to 15 October found 63 percent of capacity on those trades using the route, with 24 of 46 direct services transiting the Red Sea, though some operators still send vessels around the Cape of Good Hope in one direction while using Suez in the other. Figures from BIMCO, based on Clarksons Research data, put weekly container-ship capacity through the canal at about 753,000 TEUs in the first two weeks of October — more than half the 1.457 million TEUs recorded in the same period of 2023, before Houthi attacks on shipping from late 2023 forced widespread diversions around southern Africa. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd's Gemini Cooperation is among the alliances restoring services, with several Asia–Europe and Middle East services scheduled to resume Suez transits from late October.
