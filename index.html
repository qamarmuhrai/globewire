Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Four Tankers Hit Mines, Traffic Halted24 November 2026

Iran Says Four Tankers Struck Mines in Hormuz, Demands $290 Billion From the US as Shipping Stops

The Hormuz crisis deepened as Iran said four oil tankers in total had struck naval mines in the strait, including two sailing with support from US Central Command, while two others ran into trouble on a southern route Tehran says vessels should not use without its coordination. The Revolutionary Guard also said it had brought down a fourth US unmanned aircraft in the area within 24 hours, after earlier claims against an MQ-1 and a low-cost LUCAS drone east of the strait; US Central Command has not confirmed the tanker or drone claims, and they could not be independently verified. A senior Iranian justice official said Iranian courts have ruled Washington must pay $290 billion in compensation for conflict damage, with lawsuits also filed against Gulf states accused of letting US forces use their territory. Shipping data cited by Reuters showed the practical effect: only five cargo ships transited the strait on 22 November, down from 31 the previous weekend, and none were recorded on 23 November, though some vessels may have switched off their trackers.

Latest · Europe · New York and Kyiv — UN Clash as Death Toll Near Kyiv Rises to 1524 November 2026

Ukraine Warns at the UN That Russia Is Preparing New Strikes and Pleads for Patriot Missiles

Ukraine confronted Russia at a high-level UN Security Council meeting chaired by Latvia’s president, demanding Moscow explain what military purpose could justify hits on hospitals, schools, kindergartens and homes. Kyiv’s deputy envoy said every Ukrainian power plant has now been damaged and more than 800 pieces of energy equipment were hit in November alone, as Ukraine pressed for immediate deliveries of PAC-3 Patriot interceptors and stronger protection for Black Sea shipping. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said intelligence shows Russia is preparing another wave of strikes and urged people to heed air-raid warnings, while the European Union demanded an immediate, unconditional ceasefire and warned that attacks on commercial shipping threaten global food supplies. On the ground, rescue work finished in Dymer, north of Kyiv, where a strike on a five-storey block killed at least 11 people; officials put the wider Kyiv-region toll from the weekend attacks at at least 15, including three killed at a supermarket in Vyshneve.

Latest · Global Affairs · Berlin and Moscow — ‘Sputnik-S’ Said Ready Near Moscow24 November 2026

Western Intelligence Says Russia Could Launch a Nuclear-Armed Satellite as Early as December

Russia may put a satellite carrying a nuclear explosive into orbit as early as December, according to Western intelligence assessments reported jointly by German broadcasters WDR and NDR and the newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung. The spacecraft, named Sputnik-S in the reporting, is said to have been completed years ago and kept launch-ready near Moscow, with warnings shared among NATO allies in early November that it could fly within 60 days; Germany’s military Space Command assesses a December launch is possible. A US Defense Intelligence Agency assessment in October likewise said Russia is developing a satellite designed to carry a nuclear weapon, with few effective countermeasures once it is in orbit. US Space Command chief General Stephen Whiting has warned a nuclear blast in space could disable about a third of satellites in low Earth orbit within hours or days. Placing such a weapon in orbit would breach the 1967 Outer Space Treaty. Russia’s embassy in Germany rejected the reports as unfounded, and no documents or imagery confirming the claims have been made public.

Latest · Europe · Moscow and Tallinn — Border Crossings to Close From 1 December24 November 2026

Moscow Accuses Estonia of Actions ‘On the Verge of Triggering Open War’ Over Border Closure

Russia’s Foreign Ministry summoned Estonia’s chargé d’affaires and accused Tallinn of a string of hostile acts it said sit on the verge of triggering open war, sharply raising its rhetoric toward the NATO and EU member state. The protest followed Estonia’s plan to close its land border crossings with Russia from 1 December for at least six months, a step Tallinn links to an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence company that it blames on Russian security services — an allegation Moscow denies. Russia also accused Estonia of quietly letting Ukraine use its airspace against Russian targets, obstructing Russian cargo ships in the Baltic, blocking Russian and Belarusian grain transit, and discriminating against Russian speakers and the Russian Orthodox Church, and said it reserves the right to respond. Estonia replied that it has never allowed, and will never allow, its territory to be used for another country’s military operations, and otherwise declined to engage with what it called propaganda narratives.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya, Turkiye — Longest COP Ends Without a Pact24 November 2026

COP31 Climate Summit Collapses Without a Deal After EU Rejects a Text Silent on Fossil Fuels

The COP31 climate conference in Antalya collapsed on Sunday, the first failure of UN climate talks to reach an overarching agreement since Copenhagen in 2009 and, after deep overtime, the longest COP on record. The draft Antalya Pact, presented under the Turkish presidency with Australia leading negotiations, contained no mention of fossil fuels at all and dropped a $120 billion target for helping poorer countries adapt. The European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands, Norway and other vulnerable states refused to endorse it; Ireland’s climate minister, speaking for the EU side, said a deal that does not even mention fossil fuels would greenwash the main driver of warming, while Russia’s delegate said Moscow could accept no formulation on fossil fuels. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was deeply disappointed and accused powerful fossil-fuel interests of obstructing action. The talks were held without the United States, which has withdrawn from international climate action, and the failed text would for the first time have acknowledged that warming is on course to pass the 1.5°C Paris threshold.

Latest · Americas · Washington — At Least 258 House Seats, Four Races Still Open24 November 2026

US Democrats Clinch Their Largest House Majority Since 1992 After a North Carolina Recount

US Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the House of Representatives, their largest majority since 1992, after a recount in North Carolina’s 5th district confirmed Democrat Chuck Hubbard’s defeat of Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx, who had served 11 terms. The result lifts the party’s midterm gains to roughly 44 to 47 seats from its outgoing 214, with four House races still undecided, and Democrats will also hold 56 Senate seats when the new Congress is sworn in on 3 January — their biggest Senate majority in more than a decade. The sweep sets up divided government opposite a Republican president and gives Democrats the votes both to block the White House agenda and to open investigations, while also raising expectations that they deliver on the affordability promises at the centre of their campaign. The 258-seat tally matches the majority Democrats held when Bill Clinton was first elected in 1992 and edges past the 257 they won alongside Barack Obama in 2008.

Manchester Arena Probe Continues After ‘Unknown Substance’ Forces 12,000 From a Rap Concert

Police in Manchester are still testing items recovered from the Co-op Live arena to identify the substance that forced more than 12,000 people out of US rapper Don Toliver’s concert on Sunday night and to establish whether a crime was committed. Greater Manchester Police said 89 people showed symptoms linked to exposure; one woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary and the rest underwent dry decontamination at the scene, with no arrests made so far. Officers initially said the substance was believed to be tear gas let off in the crowd, and one report attributed it to pepper spray used in an isolated altercation between concertgoers, which police have not confirmed. Toliver sent his prayers to those affected and, citing production delays after the incident, rescheduled his London O2 show to 15 December, with original tickets still valid and Manchester ticketholders to receive automatic refunds. City leaders praised emergency services for a safe evacuation and said those affected deserve answers.

Drone Attacks Kill Four Around Khartoum and Damage a UN Building in a Civilian Neighbourhood

Drone attacks blamed on Sudan’s paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed four people in and around Khartoum early on Sunday and damaged a United Nations building while staff were inside it, according to local reports and UN officials. Explosions echoed across the capital from about 2 a.m. to 4:30 a.m. as air defences engaged drones reportedly aimed at government buildings and the airport. Three people were killed and nine wounded when a home in northern Omdurman, across the Nile, was struck, and a child was killed in a separate attack south of the capital; in Jebel Aulia, about 45 kilometres away, a drone hit the home of a mosque’s muezzin. The UN humanitarian office condemned exposing its personnel in a civilian neighbourhood as unacceptable, and Saudi Arabia also condemned the strikes. A Sudanese army source alleged the drones were launched from Ethiopia’s Bahir Dar airport, a claim that could not be independently verified. The UN has said drone warfare by both sides killed more than 1,100 people in Sudan in the first half of 2026 alone.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Jakarta — Parliament to Elect a Vice President on 11 December24 November 2026

Indonesia’s Prabowo Names a Loyalist and a Tycoon’s Cousin as Vice-Presidential Candidates

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto has named two cabinet ministers — Coordinating Minister Sugiono and Agriculture Minister Andi Amran Sulaiman — as candidates to replace former vice president Gibran Rakabuming Raka, setting up a parliamentary vote that could reshape power in Southeast Asia’s largest economy. Gibran, the eldest son of former president Joko Widodo, resigned on 12 November after the Supreme Court invalidated recognition of his overseas high-school qualifications. Under the constitution the People’s Consultative Assembly, parliament’s joint houses, must elect one of the president’s two nominees, and it is scheduled to vote on 11 December, with the winner sworn in the same day for the remainder of the term to 2029. Sugiono, 47, is a former special-forces officer, ex-foreign minister and long-time Gerindra loyalist described as Prabowo’s ideological heir; Amran, 58, is a nonpartisan businessman-turned-minister retained from the previous administration and a cousin of tycoon Haji Isam. Notably absent is deputy House speaker Sufmi Dasco Ahmad, a Gerindra heavyweight whose exclusion analysts read as Prabowo preventing a rival power centre.

Israel Investigates West Bank Arson as Eisenkot Calls Settler Attacks ‘Jewish Terrorism’

Israeli police and soldiers have opened an investigation after Palestinian vehicles and property were torched and walls daubed with slogans such as “Revenge” and “Death to Arabs” in four occupied West Bank villages — Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha’al, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani — in overnight attacks residents blame on Israeli settlers. Talks between Eisenkot’s Yashar and Netanyahu’s Likud on a possible unity government are continuing; if no coalition emerges, Israel faces another election next year.
