Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shanghai — Fourth Politburo Member Probed Since 202524 November 2026

China Places Politburo Member Chen Jining Under Investigation, Xinhua Says

Chen Jining, Shanghai’s Communist Party chief and a member of China’s elite Politburo, is under investigation, the state-run Xinhua news agency said on Tuesday. The brief announcement makes Chen the fourth sitting member of the top leadership body to face a probe since 2025, underlining the reach of Beijing’s continuing anti-corruption campaign into the highest ranks of the party. Xinhua gave no details of the allegations, following the usual pattern for such announcements, in which a formal statement that a senior official is suspected of serious violations precedes any public account of the case. Chen, an environmental scientist by training who previously served as environment minister and as mayor of Beijing, had been widely seen as a technocrat on a rising trajectory after taking over Shanghai, China’s financial hub, in 2022.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Gazipur — Dozens of Factories Suspend Operations24 November 2026

Bangladesh Garment Workers Clash With Police as Pay Protests Shut Factories Again

Garment workers blocked roads across the Gazipur industrial belt on the outskirts of Dhaka on Tuesday, prompting police to fire tear gas and sound grenades as renewed pay protests forced dozens of factories to suspend operations. Police and witnesses said workers walked out in the morning, blocked the Dhaka–Mymensingh highway at several points and set fires after rumours spread that a worker had died — a claim Gazipur Industrial Police Superintendent Mohammad Amjad Hossain said was untrue. Workers are demanding that the minimum monthly wage more than double, from 12,500 taka to 30,000 taka (about $241), citing sharply higher food, housing and transport costs. The unrest has triggered repeated shutdowns and clashes since early November in Bangladesh, the world’s second-largest garment exporter after China, and comes while a wage board is still deliberating a new rate.

Latest · Global Affairs · Bangkok — 6,929 Casualties Recorded in 202524 November 2026

Global Landmine Casualties Hit a Five-Year High as Myanmar and Syria Lead the Toll

Landmines and unexploded ordnance killed or wounded 6,929 people in 2025, the highest total in five years and a rise of about 10 percent on 2024, according to the Landmine Monitor 2026 report released on Tuesday by the International Campaign to Ban Landmines. Civilians accounted for some 84 percent of casualties, and half of the victims were children. Myanmar recorded 1,891 casualties, the most of any country for a third consecutive year, as both the military and the armed groups fighting it make widespread use of mines in the civil war — an average of about five people killed or wounded every day. Syria was second with 1,602 casualties, up more than 50 percent, as large numbers of people returned to contaminated land after the fall of the Assad government at the end of 2024. Southeast Asia accounted for 27 percent of the global total despite holding just over 8 percent of the world’s population.

Latest · Africa · Benghazi — Tripoli Condemns the Strike on Libyan Soil24 November 2026

Ukraine’s Sinking of a Russian Warship Off Libya Widens the War and Angers Tripoli

Libya’s internationally recognised government in Tripoli has condemned Ukraine’s drone attack on a Russian warship docked in Benghazi, after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed that seven Ukrainian-made naval drones sank the corvette Merkury on Sunday and hit the cargo vessel MV Angara, which Kyiv says Moscow used to transport weapons. A Russian Ka-27 helicopter was also destroyed, and local authorities said at least 10 Libyan and Russian crew members were killed; satellite imagery showed most of the Merkury capsized, with only its bow above water. The 1,800-tonne corvette, one of Russia’s newest warships, commissioned in 2023, had docked to refuel under a cooperation deal with Libya’s eastern authorities. Ukraine’s SBU security service called both vessels legitimate military targets, but the strike — carried out some 2,400 kilometres from the front line — has raised fears that the war is spreading well beyond Europe.

Latest · Europe · Berlin — Rutte Pledges Defence ‘in Space Too’24 November 2026

NATO Vows to Defend Against Space Threats After Reports of a Russian Nuclear Satellite

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said on Monday that the alliance would defend every inch of its territory, including against threats in space, after German media reported that Russia may be preparing to launch a nuclear-armed satellite within weeks. The investigation by public broadcasters WDR and NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung, citing unnamed military and intelligence sources, said a satellite dubbed Sputnik-S, stored for years near Moscow and fitted with a nuclear warhead, could be launched within about 60 days and used to disrupt or destroy communications and observation satellites. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, speaking alongside Rutte in Berlin, called the reports “not new, yet no less shocking” but neither confirmed nor denied them. The Kremlin and Russia’s embassy in Berlin dismissed the reporting as propaganda and said Moscow’s space activities are peaceful. The head of the European Space Agency described nuclear weapons in orbit as “the ultimate nightmare scenario.”

Latest · Europe · Yerevan — Pashinyan Stakes His Office on a Referendum24 November 2026

Armenia Unveils a Draft Constitution That Drops the Karabakh Claim to Unlock Peace With Azerbaijan

Armenia published a draft new constitution on Monday that rewrites the preamble and removes the reference to the 1990 Declaration of Independence, which in turn invokes reunification with Nagorno-Karabakh — the wording Azerbaijan has long demanded be deleted before it will sign a formal peace treaty. Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, who won a new term in June in a vote widely read as a referendum on the peace process, said adoption would let the two countries sign and ratify a deal delivering “institutional, lasting and irreversible peace,” and he has pledged to resign if voters reject the text. The two neighbours have been in conflict since the late 1980s, mostly over Karabakh, which Azerbaijan retook in a lightning offensive in 2023, prompting almost all of its ethnic Armenian population to flee. They signed a US-backed joint peace declaration last August, but Pashinyan still needs at least six opposition votes to reach the two-thirds majority required to call a referendum, and the main opposition parties have so far rejected the draft, branding it a capitulation.

Latest · Middle East · Baghdad and Erbil — Move Follows an Oil Smuggling Probe24 November 2026

Iraqi Prime Minister Orders a Federal Takeover of Kurdistan Border Crossings and Oilfields

Iraq’s prime minister, Ali al-Zaidi, has ordered the federal border crossings authority to take over all crossings in the semi-autonomous Kurdistan region and the federal oil ministry to manage oilfields in areas under Kurdish security control, the state news agency said on Tuesday. The order covers ministry fields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh provinces and follows an investigation into oil smuggling at the state-run North Oil Company. The move is likely to deepen long-running tensions between Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government over who controls the north’s oil revenues and border trade — a dispute that has repeatedly stalled exports and federal budget transfers. Neither the Kurdish authorities nor the oil ministry had publicly detailed how or when the takeover would be carried out.

Latest · Middle East · Khan Younis and Netzarim — Two Police Officers Among the Dead24 November 2026

Israeli Airstrikes Kill at Least Six Palestinians in Gaza on Tuesday, Medics Say

Israeli airstrikes killed at least six Palestinians in the Gaza Strip on Tuesday, including two police officers, local health and police officials said. Medics said three people were killed and five wounded in a strike on a police post in Khan Younis in the south, with the Hamas-run interior ministry confirming that two of the dead were officers. Later on Tuesday, a second strike near the Netzarim corridor further north killed another three people. Gaza’s civil defence agency separately told AFP that the dead included a 17-year-old killed near Gaza City and three people killed at a police station in the al-Mawasi area. Israel’s military said it had carried out at least one airstrike in Gaza on Tuesday and was looking into reports of another. The killings come a day after a strike in Jabalia killed a UNRWA staff member, underlining how fragile the calm in the strip remains.

Latest · Africa · Ituri — More Than 11,700 Ebola Cases Confirmed24 November 2026

Fighting Near a Congo Hospital Traps Ebola Responders and Halts the Outbreak Response in Ituri

Heavy fighting near a hospital in Ituri province in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has disrupted the Ebola response and forced World Health Organization staff and partners to shelter in the facility’s maternity ward for more than 24 hours, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said. Tedros said the clashes, close to where the outbreak first emerged, had put patients, families and health workers at grave risk, and that the WHO was working with partners to secure safe passage out while calling for hospitals to be protected. The UN says the WHO has verified more than 87 attacks on healthcare in Congo since 1 January. Ituri remains the epicentre of the Bundibugyo virus outbreak and accounts for more than two-thirds of cases: figures released on 20 November put the outbreak at 11,762 confirmed cases and 5,775 deaths across 68 health zones in eight provinces, with Ituri alone recording more than 8,000 cases and 3,791 deaths.

Latest · Americas · Richmond and Salem — First States to Sue Over Grok24 November 2026

Virginia and Oregon Sue Musk’s Companies Over Sexualised Images Created by the Grok Chatbot

Virginia and Oregon on Monday became the first US states to sue Elon Musk’s companies over the Grok artificial intelligence chatbot, accusing them of misleading consumers about safety and failing to install safeguards while promoting a “spicy mode” that lets users turn an ordinary photograph of a person into a sexual or nude image or video. The separate suits, filed in state courts under consumer protection laws against defendants including SpaceX, the social platform X and the AI unit now called SpaceX Super Intelligence, seek damages, penalties and court orders to stop the alleged violations. Virginia’s filing cites research by the Center for Countering Digital Hate estimating that between December 2025 and January 2026 Grok generated and posted millions of sexualised images on X, including more than 20,000 depicting children. Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said the companies put their business model ahead of the safety of millions of people. The AI unit said sexual content involving minors is abhorrent, and the companies already face more than a dozen individual lawsuits from people who say nonconsensual images of them were generated.
