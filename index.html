Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Europe Joins Next Talks13 November 2026

Putin Agrees to Let Europe Join the Next Ukraine Talks, as Washington Works Toward the War’s Largest Prisoner Exchange

The diplomacy around Ukraine widened on Friday. US envoy Jared Kushner said President Vladimir Putin had agreed to allow European representatives into the next round of negotiations, and that Washington was working toward what could become the largest prisoner exchange of the war, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the prospect of European participation after meeting Kushner and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff near Dublin, following their talks with Putin in Moscow. The shift builds on this week’s meeting in Abu Dhabi — the first direct Russian–Ukrainian session in nine months. Territory remains the central obstacle: Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine and Kyiv refuses to cede the rest of the eastern Donbas, while questions of ceasefire monitoring and security guarantees are expected to dominate an expanded agenda that now formally includes Europe.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Hormuz and Diplomacy13 November 2026

Trump Says the US Does Not Need a Deal With Iran, as a Tanker Explosion Keeps Hormuz at the Centre of the Crisis

Washington hardened its line on Iran even as shipping risk in the Gulf stayed high. President Donald Trump said the United States does not need an agreement with Tehran and directed Vice President JD Vance to refocus on domestic priorities, while Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy reported that a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday after using what it called an unauthorised and dangerous route. No vessel details were released. Iranian media reported four oil tankers hit by explosions in the waterway on Thursday after allegedly ignoring warnings about restricted areas. Analyst counts cited by Reuters put at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October — the highest monthly total since the conflict began in late February. Separately, Yemen’s Houthi forces claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia’s East–West oil pipeline and a facility at the Red Sea port of Yanbu, adding a second energy front to the day’s tensions.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Nasser Hospital Toll Rises13 November 2026

Death Toll From the Strike on Gaza’s Nasser Hospital Rises to Seven, as Gulf States Condemn Continued Attacks

The human cost of Thursday’s strike on southern Gaza’s main hospital grew overnight. The toll at Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis rose to seven after another Palestinian died of injuries sustained in the attack, the hospital said — up from the six deaths first reported, which included a surgeon and a 13-year-old boy. The secretary-general of the Gulf Cooperation Council, Jassim Mohammed Al-Budaiwi, condemned what he described as continued Israeli violations against Palestinians in Gaza, including the hospital strike. In parallel, the UN Security Council unanimously renewed financial and travel sanctions on individuals deemed to threaten peace in Yemen and called on the Houthi movement to halt military escalation and attacks on civilians. For families in Khan Younis, the immediate reality remains a damaged surgical wing serving a vast population, and a ceasefire that has not stopped near-daily strikes.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Recovery13 November 2026

Microsoft Restores Outlook and Teams in Phases After a Near 10-Hour Global Failure, With Mail Backlogs Still Clearing

More than 12 hours after the failure began, Microsoft’s recovery remained uneven on Friday. The company said recovery actions had been completed across parts of the affected infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific region, with traffic gradually restored and improvements in mailbox access and delayed email delivery, while warning that some users would still face intermittent problems. In the United States, thousands of outage reports were still being logged on Friday morning, down from a peak above 46,000 for Microsoft 365 and far higher counts for Outlook the day before. Microsoft says a large number of Exchange Online servers became unavailable after failing automated health checks, straining the remaining capacity; it says there is no evidence of malicious activity and no surge in traffic behind the collapse. To protect delayed messages, the email timeout window has been extended to 48 hours while queues clear, and a preliminary post-incident review is expected within two business days.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Nvidia Fallout Widens13 November 2026

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick Comes Under Scrutiny as the Nvidia Crisis Reaches the White House

The political fallout from Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia chief executive widened to the Commerce Department. The White House said President Trump continues to back Secretary Howard Lutnick, even as lawmakers from both parties pressed him over delayed action on chip smuggling and oversight of advanced chip exports to China. Reporting cited by The National says Commerce did not act against Singapore-based Megaspeed, a firm at the heart of the scandal, and has not added a company to its Entity List since October 2025 — leaving more than 100 entities, including Chinese AI and memory-chip firms, off the blacklist despite security concerns. Senate figures including Tom Cotton and Chuck Schumer pushed for tighter controls and new legislation, while House panels prepared December testimony from Lutnick and others. Nvidia shares, which fell sharply on the resignation, steadied only partly ahead of next week’s earnings.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China — Pacific Drills13 November 2026

China’s Unannounced Pacific Drills Drew All Three Theatre Commands, Taipei Says, in a Rehearsal Beyond a Taiwan Blockade

Fresh detail emerged on the scale of China’s early-November exercises. A senior Taiwan security official told Reuters the deployment at its peak massed more than 100 naval and coast guard vessels — larger than Beijing’s last formally named anti-Taiwan drills nearly a year ago — and involved coordinated forces from the eastern, northern and southern theatre commands, suggesting rehearsal not only for a blockade but for broader western Pacific operations, including simulated attacks. A Chinese submarine also launched an intercontinental ballistic missile from the Yellow Sea into the mainland during the exercise. The fleet has since eased to about 70 vessels within the first island chain. President Xi Jinping gave a major Taiwan policy speech on Thursday urging peaceful reunification, while China’s defence ministry did not respond to requests for comment and Taipei’s defence ministry pointed to earlier remarks about area-denial operations.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · APEC — Shenzhen Summit Preview13 November 2026

All Eyes on Shenzhen: Trump, Xi and Putin Are Expected at APEC, With a Possible First Three-Way Meeting

Next week’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen is shaping up as the year’s most watched diplomatic gathering. The leaders’ meeting runs 18–19 November, preceded by a chief executives’ summit on 17–18 November, in China’s third hosting of APEC after Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014. President Xi Jinping will chair the meeting; Presidents Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin and Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. are among those expected, while Taiwan has named Acer founder Stan Shih as its envoy. Analysts expect the headline questions to be whether Trump and Xi extend their trade truce beyond its January deadline, whether a bilateral channel on AI incidents and military crisis communication advances, and whether an unprecedented trilateral Trump–Xi–Putin encounter takes place on the margins. Beijing has said only that it is in communication with relevant parties about such a meeting, and has insisted Ukraine is not on the APEC table.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Myanmar Dilemma13 November 2026

ASEAN Faces Pressure to Ease Its Five-Year Ban on Myanmar’s Leaders as the Manila Summit Opens This Weekend

Southeast Asia’s bloc meets in Manila from 15 to 17 November with Myanmar at the top of its hardest agenda item. ASEAN is under growing pressure to ease the ban that has barred Myanmar’s military-backed political leadership from summits and foreign ministers’ meetings since the 2021 coup, over failure to implement the Five-Point Consensus peace plan. Thailand is now openly calling for restrictions to be eased, and nearly half the bloc’s 11 members — including Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia and Malaysia — have stepped up bilateral engagement this year, with Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim pledging to work toward Myanmar’s reintegration. Myanmar’s war has killed more than 100,000 people, and its parliament formally rejected the consensus in July. Leaders are expected to issue a negotiated review document in Manila; Myanmar itself will again be represented only by a foreign ministry director-general, while US President Trump is expected on the final day for a special ASEAN–US session.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Vice Presidency Vacancy13 November 2026

Indonesia’s Upper House Will Choose a New Vice President on 2 December After Gibran’s Resignation

Indonesia set a date to fill its vacant vice presidency. Speaker Ahmad Muzani said on Friday the upper house of parliament will pick the new vice president on 2 December, following the resignation this week of Gibran Rakabuming Raka, President Prabowo Subianto’s deputy and the son of former President Joko Widodo. The house will write to Prabowo asking him to submit two names, and has formed a 36-lawmaker team to verify the candidates’ credentials. Gibran, 39, elected alongside Prabowo in 2024, said his decision was personal and unrelated to education credentials — the issue on which the country’s top courts had ruled he lacked adequate qualifications, after a month of mounting pressure from lawmakers. Analysts say the departure could widen the distance between Prabowo and his predecessor, adding a new political test early in the president’s term.

Latest · Americas · Economy — Fed and AI13 November 2026

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Takes the AI Debate to San Francisco, as the Boom Lifts Prices Before It Cuts Them

The Federal Reserve is confronting the economics of the AI buildout head-on. Chair Kevin Warsh will lead a public forum in San Francisco on Monday — the only scheduled public event of the five task forces he created this year — with three panels on what the technology means for inflation, jobs and growth. Officials expect AI to reshape the economy over time, but the immediate effect has run the other way: frenetic spending on data centres and infrastructure is driving up prices, with no clarity on when the pressure eases, and some policymakers arguing rates may need to stay more restrictive. The forum lands after midterm elections that amplified local backlash against data-centre construction, and amid wider public unease spanning land use, children’s wellbeing, financial-market risks and national security. Warsh, who has called this a hinge point in history, will hear stakeholders at the centre of the industry he must now help steer policy around.

Latest · Africa · Cape Verde — Presidential Election13 November 2026

Cape Verde’s President José Maria Neves Is Favourite for a Second Term in Sunday’s Vote

Cape Verde votes on Sunday with its president expected to consolidate his party’s hold on power. José Maria Neves is tipped to win a second five-year term, an outcome that would give the African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde full control of government after it won 37 of 72 seats in May’s legislative elections and Francisco Carvalho became prime minister. Neves, who served as prime minister for 15 years from 2001, campaigns on inclusive growth and better wages. His main challenger is Joana Rosa, a former justice minister and the country’s first woman presidential candidate, from the Movement for Democracy, which governed for a decade before this year’s legislative vote. For the archipelago of about 500,000 people, more than 600 kilometres off mainland Africa, the race has centred on employment and stemming outward migration — in a year when the islands also drew global attention for their men’s football team and a hantavirus scare aboard a cruise ship.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi Air Stays Severe13 November 2026

Delhi’s Air Stays in the Severe Band Into the Evening, With Readings Above 400 Around AIIMS and Rajokri

Delhi’s pollution emergency did not lift as Friday wore on. Live monitoring through the day kept the capital in the severe category, with the air quality index touching 418 around AIIMS and 402 at Rajokri Border, while areas such as the Dwarka Expressway recorded 369 in the very poor band. The readings extend the morning pattern that briefly placed Delhi at the top of global most-polluted-city tables. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, speaking on Friday, said the conflict around Iran and the Gulf had underlined the importance of energy security, and that steps taken over the past decade had helped India face a difficult situation with resilience — a reminder of how the smog season now overlaps with wider energy and health pressures. For residents, the practical picture was unchanged: haze, reduced visibility and fine-particle levels far above safe thresholds, driven by the seasonal mix of crop-residue burning, traffic emissions and cooling, still air over the Indo-Gangetic plain.
