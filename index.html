Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — No Deal Without Blockade Lifted2 November 2026

Iran Sets Its Price: No Hormuz Reopening Without an End to the US Naval Blockade, as Oil Falls Nearly 4% and Trump Gives Diplomacy More Time

The diplomacy now has a stated condition. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said on Monday that responsibility for progress lies entirely with Washington and that Tehran's starting demand — an end to the US naval blockade — must be met before the Strait of Hormuz can be reopened or secured. Iran says it is considering a two-day plan to reopen the waterway if its conditions are accepted. The statement followed a weekend in which the Revolutionary Guard said two tankers, one a supertanker, exploded after striking mines on an unauthorised route, and UK Maritime Trade Operations reported vessels struck by projectiles in Hormuz and east of Ras Al Khafji, Saudi Arabia. Markets chose the talks over the explosions: Brent fell 3.7% to $109.23 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate dropped 3.8% to $100.14, after reports that President Donald Trump wants to give diplomacy more time before deciding on renewed strikes, and not before Tuesday's US midterm elections. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said no decision on fresh strikes has been made and that Trump prefers a diplomatic resolution. Goldman Sachs has raised its December Brent forecast to $105, citing geopolitical supply risk, and analysts warn the reprieve could reverse quickly once the US vote is out of the way.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Final Rallies2 November 2026

Final Rallies, 54 Million Votes Already Cast: Democrats Near-Certain for the House on Eve of Polls, With the Senate Now Rated Even

America's campaign ended the way its polls have pointed for weeks — with one party defending, the other advancing. Democrats and President Trump held closing get-out-the-vote rallies on Monday ahead of Tuesday's vote for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships, with a record 54 million ballots already cast early. The final New York Times/Siena surveys of 5,708 likely voters across seven Senate battlegrounds put Democrats ahead in every one: Michigan, where Abdul El-Sayed leads Mike Rogers 53% to 42%; Texas, James Talarico over Ken Paxton 50% to 43%; Kansas, 49% to 44% over Senator Roger Marshall; Maine, 51% to 45% over Susan Collins; Ohio, Sherrod Brown over Jon Husted 49% to 44%; and narrow leads in Alaska and Iowa. Forecasters have moved accordingly: the Cook Political Report projects Democrats flipping 12 to 25 House seats and two to six Senate seats — they need four — while Harvard's ElectIt model gives Democrats a 96% chance of the House and 79% of the Senate. Republicans need a polling miss to hold either chamber. In a late twist in Michigan, El-Sayed's defeated primary rival Haley Stevens endorsed the Republican, Rogers, on Sunday.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Truce Used to Reinforce, Kyiv Says2 November 2026

Kyiv Says Russia Used the Oleshky Truce to Rotate Troops and Fortify — Shelling Resumes Across 16 Kherson Settlements Within Hours of Its End

The argument over the truce has become the story of what follows it. Ukraine's General Staff said on Monday that Russian forces used last week's six-day humanitarian ceasefire around Russian-held Oleshky — brokered with US First Lady Melania Trump's involvement and the participation of Pope Leo — to rotate units, fortify positions, expand anti-drone defences along roads and bring in ammunition, and warned of intensified fighting ahead. The pause, the longest local truce since the 2022 invasion, allowed nearly 800 people to be evacuated. By early Monday, Kherson's governor said Russian forces had shelled and attacked with drones and aircraft 16 settlements in Ukrainian-held parts of the region, including the city of Kherson, where a drone struck the centre with no casualties reported. The recriminations extended to cyberspace: Russia said its emergency-warning app suffered a massive cyberattack posting false information, hours after Ukraine confirmed its air-raid alert app had been hacked with fake radiation warnings. Elsewhere Russia said it struck a military facility in the Odesa region and a cargo vessel 58 km south of the port, while Ukrainian strikes hit the Kstovo refinery east of Moscow and a missile-components factory in Rostov. President Zelenskiy has repeated his offer to stop refinery strikes, as Trump demands, if Washington supplies Patriot interceptors. US-mediated talks, frozen since February, are expected to resume this month.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — Midnight Landfall2 November 2026

Landfall Tonight Near Ongole: Severe Cyclone Murasu Drives Evacuations in Two States, Cancels More Than 20 Flights and Puts 300 Rescue Teams in Place

India's east coast is spending Monday night braced. Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu, over the west-central Bay of Bengal and moving northwest at about 25 kmph, was centred roughly 200 km east-northeast of Chennai on Monday afternoon and is expected to cross the southern Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, between 11:30pm Monday and 4am Tuesday, with sustained winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph. Odisha, still recovering from Cyclone Arnab, expedited a second round of evacuations from low-lying and landslide-prone areas, identifying at least 150 vulnerable locations in Gajapati district alone and placing Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri on high alert, with more than 300 NDRF, ODRAF and Fire Service teams pre-positioned. Heavy rain was already lashing Konaseema in Andhra Pradesh by afternoon. At least 21 flights were cancelled across Chennai, Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam, schools and colleges shut in Chennai and Tiruvallur, rail services were disrupted, fishermen were ordered to stay ashore until 4 November, and officials estimate winds could damage some 15,000 electricity poles across 12 districts. Extremely heavy rain is forecast for coastal Andhra Pradesh, Yanam and Rayalaseema on 2–3 November and for Telangana on Tuesday.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria — Final Count at 100%2 November 2026

100% Counted: Iotova Takes 1,093,852 Votes and 57.56% to Become Bulgaria's Seventh President — and the First Woman Elected to the Post

The result is now official in full. With 100% of polling-station protocols processed, Bulgaria's Central Election Commission gives Iliana Iotova and running mate Kiril Valchev 1,093,852 votes, 57.56%, against 750,251 votes, 39.48%, for Andrey Gyurov and Georgi Kandev. Iotova carried 28 electoral districts; Gyurov took three — two in Sofia and Kardzhali — and won among Bulgarians abroad with 56.23%. Turnout stood at about 27%, up from the first round's record low of 23%, which still means the winner was chosen by roughly one registered voter in six. Gyurov has conceded, saying the result must be accepted as it stands, and Iotova has pledged to work for national consensus. She became acting president in January when Rumen Radev resigned to run for prime minister, a post he won when his Progressive Bulgaria took an outright parliamentary majority in April, and she takes office in her own right on 22 January 2027. Backed by Radev's camp, she favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine; European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has congratulated her. The campaign closed under the shadow of October drone attacks on commercial shipping off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast.

Latest · Americas · Florida Jet Crash — NTSB Briefing Day2 November 2026

Descent Exceeded 32,000 Feet a Minute: NTSB Holds Its West Palm Beach Briefing as the Learjet's Final Minutes Come Into Focus

Federal investigators spent Monday at the crater in Greenacres, and the flight data has filled in the jet's last minute. Flightradar24 data shows the Learjet 60, registration N722BD, at one point descending at more than 32,000 feet per minute before it struck Military Trail near Cresthaven shortly before 1pm on Sunday, killing all nine aboard and forcing about 600 residents of an adjoining mobile-home community into temporary shelter. No one on the ground was killed. Cockpit recordings reported by CNN and Al Jazeera have the crew describing icing as manageable near 35,000 feet, then reporting rapid altitude loss and declaring the aircraft was going down; the crew had also reported turbulence and precipitation. The National Transportation Safety Board's go-team, which arrived Sunday and Monday, scheduled a media briefing in West Palm Beach on Monday, with the FAA supporting the investigation and manufacturer Bombardier pledging full cooperation. Governor Ron DeSantis called the damage cataclysmic. The crash is the second-deadliest US aviation accident of 2026, after the June Missouri skydiving-plane crash that killed 12. Officials have still not identified the nine, nor said how many were crew, and the board has given no cause: its preliminary report is expected within weeks, the final determination considerably later.

Latest · Europe · Black Sea — The Crew Comes Home2 November 2026

Two to Three Hours in the Water: The Contship Jet's 14 Filipino Seafarers Fly Home as Bulgaria Counts the Ninth Ship Struck in a Month

The human aftermath of the Black Sea attacks arrived at an airport on Monday. The 14 Filipino crew of the Liberian-flagged container ship Contship Jet were due at Clark International Airport after a journey home from Varna, Bulgaria, where Bulgarian warships brought all 18 crew — 14 Filipinos and four Ukrainians — after three sea drones struck their vessel on 28 October about 17 nautical miles east of Cape Kaliakra. The crew abandoned ship within 12 minutes; the Filipinos told labour officials they spent two to three hours in the water before rescue. The 1,276-TEU ship, carrying 421 containers from Haifa to Constanta, listed 20 to 30 degrees and sank overnight on 29 October, losing about 20 containers overboard, some holding lithium-ion batteries and other hazardous materials, though navy teams found no pollution. No one has claimed the attack; a French patrol aircraft spotted the drones, and maritime risk firm Ambrey says video suggests a craft derived from Russia's Murena-300S design. By the count of The Maritime Executive, the Contship Jet was the ninth vessel struck in the western Black Sea since early October, with 34 sailors killed or missing across the attacks. The European Commission has called the strikes a dangerous and unacceptable escalation, and Bulgaria has asked allies for more Black Sea support.

New Zealand's election, on Saturday 7 November, is being decided at the checkout and the petrol pump. Latest · Europe · Britain — Settlement Overhaul Day2 November 2026

Mahmood Unveils the Settlement Overhaul: Ten Years Becomes the Standard Route, Up to Fifteen for Lower-Paid Workers, After Months of Argument

Britain's migration argument reaches its decision point on Monday. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood sets out the government's reforms to settlement, replacing the current rule — under which most migrants can apply for indefinite leave to remain after five years, gaining access to some welfare benefits — with an earned-settlement system that makes ten years the standard wait and up to fifteen for some lower-paid workers, including care workers in earlier drafts. The overhaul follows the post-Brexit surge in arrivals, particularly healthcare workers and students, and months of consultation that drew more than 200,000 responses. The government frames it as balancing voter demands for lower immigration against business warnings that tougher rules could deter workers the country still needs; junior business minister Chris McDonald said before the statement that immigration levels under the previous Conservative government had become unsustainable. New analysis by the Institute for Public Policy Research, using the government's own methodology, argues that skilled migrants and their dependants due to settle under the current rules would contribute £115 billion more over their lifetimes than they cost — against a Home Office estimate that settling some 350,000 lower-skilled care-route workers and dependants would cost £10 billion. Whether people already in Britain keep the five-year route they started on, and whether care workers already here are exempted, are the questions the statement must answer.
