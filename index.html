Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia26 October 2026

Riyadh Airport Reopens — Then the Houthis Say They Hit It Again, as Cancellations Reach 86%

Saudi Arabia partly reopened King Khalid International Airport on Sunday, two weeks after a Houthi attack on 10 October killed 13 people in the deadliest strike in the kingdom in more than a decade. Terminal 1 was assigned to domestic flights and Terminal 2 to international services by national carriers. By Monday the reopening already looked fragile: Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had targeted the airport again with a ballistic missile and had also sent drones against crude-oil pumping stations in eastern and western Saudi Arabia, while the Saudi-led coalition said its air defences had intercepted and destroyed a missile launched toward Riyadh and civil defence told residents the danger had passed. The scale of the disruption is now clear in aviation data cited by Reuters: about 86% of scheduled flights at Riyadh — 4,071 out of 4,736 — were cancelled between 8 and 25 October, international airlines have suspended services into November, and Delta has pushed back the launch of its Riyadh route until next year. Saree had warned before the reopening that the airport would “never be safe” and urged airlines and travellers to stay away; flight-tracking data showed little traffic even on Sunday.

Latest · Europe · France26 October 2026

1,314 Arrests, 75 Fires, 24 Shops Looted: France's Weekend of Protest — and Le Pen's Campaign Launch in Orléans

France woke on Monday to the tally from another weekend of unrest: police arrested 1,314 people, 53 officers were injured, responders dealt with 75 fires and 24 shops were looted, the Interior Ministry said. More than 400,000 people marched on Saturday — 410,000 by the ministry's count, nearly 800,000 by the CGT union's — in protests that began with students demanding better school funding and have widened into anger over living costs. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez blamed much of the violence on “ultra-left” extremists; clashes and tear gas were reported in Paris, Lyon, Nantes, Marseille and other cities. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu was due to meet student leaders at Matignon on Monday. Into that febrile moment stepped Marine Le Pen, who launched her campaign for next year's presidential election before 8,500 supporters in Orléans on Sunday, vowing to “restore the rule of law,” promising tougher immigration laws and border controls, and accusing President Emmanuel Macron's government of lacking authority. An Ipsos poll puts her at 32–34% in the first round, ahead of the April–May vote to choose Macron's successor; thousands of counter-protesters had marched against her party in Orléans the day before.

Final Day Before Israel Votes: Defence Minister Denies Any Rift Over Iran, Trump Tells Voters to Back “Bibi”

Israel's election campaign reached its last full day on Monday with the government closing ranks over Iran. Defence Minister Israel Katz dismissed reports of a split between the political leadership and the military over a possible pre-election strike, writing that accounts of gaps between the two levels were unfounded and that there was no intention to initiate an attack on Iran before the vote. The denial followed days in which Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned Tehran against trying to disrupt Tuesday's polling, opposition leader Yair Lapid accused him of seeking escalation to delay the election — a charge Netanyahu denies — and President Donald Trump intervened directly, urging Israelis at the polls to remember that “Bibi has been an outstanding Warrior Prime Minister” while saying there would be no US strike that day. Iran, for its part, is preparing to host senior Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials for regional security talks as the war, now in its ninth month, continues to shadow the vote.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza26 October 2026

Three Killed at Gaza's Islamic University, Eight Dead Across the Strip on Sunday, Hospital and Officials Say

An Israeli strike at the Islamic University in Gaza City killed three people on Sunday, according to Shifa Hospital, which said it received three bodies and treated eight wounded; two university sources said those killed were administrative staff and the injured were students. Gaza officials said the campus was hit twice within hours, an earlier strike wounding seven female students with shrapnel. The Israeli military said it had targeted a Hamas military-wing operative, that after identifying uninvolved people nearby the munition was diverted to reduce harm, and that the incident was under review; it was not clear whether the intended target was among the dead. Civil defence spokesman Mahmoud Bassal said five more people were killed elsewhere on Sunday — three near Khan Younis in the south, one in Deir al-Balah in central Gaza and one northwest of Gaza City — bringing the day's reported toll to at least eight, including, in other accounts, an eight-year-old girl who died of earlier wounds. Gaza's education ministry condemned the university strike, saying 95% of academic buildings have been damaged in the war.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India26 October 2026

More Than 4,800 Fishing Boats Back Ashore at Kakinada as Cyclone Arnab Strengthens Toward a Wednesday Landfall

Cyclone Arnab, which formed over the Bay of Bengal early on Monday, is expected to become a severe cyclonic storm by midnight and a very severe one on Tuesday before crossing the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Wednesday forenoon, the India Meteorological Department said, with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h gusting to 140 km/h. At 5:30am the storm lay near 15.4°N, 88.9°E, about 650 km east-southeast of Visakhapatnam, moving west at 15 km/h, and the IMD's red message remains in force for the Andhra Pradesh and adjoining south Odisha coasts. On the ground, preparations accelerated through Monday: more than 4,800 fishing boats working the Kakinada coast had returned and anchored by Monday, sea activity was suspended, and neighbouring Odisha — bracing mainly for extreme rain — kept disaster-response and fire-service teams on alert with schools in the most exposed southern districts to stay shut on 28 and 29 October. Bangladesh's meteorological department, tracking the same system, advised its four maritime ports to hoist distant warning signal No. 2 and told boats in the north bay to stay close to shore.

Latest · Americas · Brazil26 October 2026

“Brazil Is No One's Backyard”: Lula Aims His Victory Speech at Washington as Bolsonaro Seeks Annulment

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva used his runoff victory to send a blunt message north: “Brazil is no one's backyard.” The 80-year-old, who defeated Flávio Bolsonaro by about 51.8% to 48.2% — overturning a first-round deficit of more than two million votes to win by roughly 4.3 million — said his campaign had “defeated foreign intervention through digital platforms” and promised to make people participate more in decision-making and to regulate digital platforms. His remarks followed a Reuters report, citing ten current and former US officials, that two State Department officials had pushed for sanctions intended to help return the Bolsonaro family to power; the State Department has previously denied election-interference accusations. Bolsonaro, meanwhile, has refused to concede and is demanding the result be annulled over a two-hour voting extension that a Supreme Court justice granted after both campaigns requested more time. Lula's win makes him the first candidate since direct elections resumed in 1989 to win the presidency after trailing in the first round, and breaks a run of right-wing victories across Latin America; his new term starts in January 2027.

Kyiv Ready to Freeze the Front Line but Not to Cede Land, as Abu Dhabi Talks Are Set for Early November

Ukraine, Russia and the United States plan to meet at technical level in Abu Dhabi in the first half of November, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, after US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met President Vladimir Putin earlier in the week; the Kremlin's Dmitry Peskov confirmed the trilateral format and the timing. Zelenskyy said he hoped a leaders' meeting would follow, that any settlement would need strong security guarantees and ten years of funding to sustain Ukraine's army, and that Europe should be at the table — while warning that a similar October meeting never materialised because, in his words, Moscow found another excuse to pull out. In a separate interview he said Kyiv was ready to freeze fighting along the current front lines but rejected giving up a quarter of Ukrainian territory, and he complained that Ukraine had received only 24 Patriot interceptor missiles from the US in three months against a monthly need he put at 350, and that speed limits on Starlink and Starshield — which Washington says have been lifted — were in practice still restricting his forces.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria26 October 2026

Record-Low 22% Turnout Sends Bulgaria to a Presidential Runoff: Iotova 34.8%, Gyurov 31.9%

Bulgaria will choose its president in a runoff on 1 November after Sunday's first round produced no outright winner and the lowest turnout in the country's presidential history — about 22%. With 99.94% of precinct protocols processed, incumbent Iliana Iotova led with 34.76%, just ahead of former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov on 31.94%, in a field of 27 candidates; another 4.4% of voters actively chose “I do not support any candidate.” The close result was well below pre-vote polling that had put Iotova as high as 47%, and analysts described it as a punishment for the government amid anger over energy-driven living costs. The runoff draws a sharp foreign-policy line: Iotova, who took over in January when Rumen Radev stepped aside, favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine, while Gyurov is backed by the pro-Western opposition. The vote was held under the shadow of drone attacks on shipping off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast this month, in which vessels were struck and seafarers left missing; both leading candidates condemned the strikes. Iotova said it was “an illusion” to think staying home changes anything.

Two Dan Sullivans, One Senate Seat: Trump's Justice Department Sues to Block Alaska Certifying a Republican Loss

The US Justice Department has taken the extraordinary step of suing, before a single general-election vote is counted, to block Alaska from certifying its Senate race if the Republican incumbent loses narrowly. The suit, filed on 22 October, argues that a second candidate — retired teacher Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., whose name is nearly identical to Senator Dan S. Sullivan's — could confuse voters, and asks a federal judge to halt certification pending an investigation into whether votes for one Sullivan were meant for the other. President Donald Trump took personal credit: “I'm the one that said do it.” Alaska's election director had initially ruled the second Sullivan's candidacy was filed “with a purpose to confuse or mislead,” but state courts, up to the Alaska Supreme Court, put him back on the ballot. The Democrat in the race, former representative Mary Peltola, called the lawsuit “desperate” and “clearly corruption,” and a Notre Dame election-law professor described the department's legal theory — drawn from the stated aims of the National Voter Registration Act — as “strange and unusual.” Attorney General Todd Blanche said the department coordinated with no campaign. The 3 November midterms will decide control of Congress, and Alaska could decide the Senate.

$40 Billion for Chips: Musk Says SpaceX Has Closed Its GPU Debt Deal — and Predicts “Guppy-Level Death” for 200+ Neoclouds

Elon Musk said on Sunday that SpaceX had closed $40 billion in debt financing to buy Nvidia graphics processors, in what would be one of the largest single borrowings yet for AI computing hardware. The debt is mainly callable — repayable early — and tied to GPU deliveries expected in the first and second quarters of next year, Musk said, crediting the raise to SpaceX's investment-grade rating and to revenue from Starlink and rocket launches that does not depend on AI data centres. He paired the announcement with a warning: debt markets, he said, “have almost entirely shut off access to money for any company that doesn't have substantial revenue independent of AI data centers,” so the more than 200 so-called neoclouds — firms that rent out AI computing power as their whole business — face “guppy-level death and consolidation,” with only a few surviving; SpaceX, he added, would not buy any of them, calling that “pointless.” People familiar with the discussions told Bloomberg that pricing talks were still continuing late on Sunday, leaving exactly what “closed” means not yet settled. SpaceX expects $35–40 billion of capital spending in the fourth quarter alone.
