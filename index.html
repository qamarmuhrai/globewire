Latest · Global Affairs · Quad and G20 Atlanta31 October 2026

Rubio Hosts the Quad, Then Closes a Divided G20 as Leaders Look to Manila and Florida

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio is spending Saturday on two tracks in Atlanta: first chairing a meeting of the Quad foreign ministers from Australia, India and Japan — their third gathering this year — and then wrapping up the two-day G20 foreign ministers' meeting. The Quad talks are meant to prepare for a leaders' summit planned alongside the ASEAN meetings in Manila on 15–18 November, which would be the first Quad summit since 2024 and the first for President Donald Trump. A planned summit in India late last year was cancelled amid trade and tariff friction with New Delhi. The G20, by contrast, is ending without a joint communique after deep splits on Friday over Ukraine and the Middle East war; organisers cancelled the traditional family photo after European ministers refused to be photographed with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov. Rubio, who used a reception at the Georgia Aquarium on Friday evening to urge cooperation where possible, is due to hold a closing press conference and has invited ministers to a college football game. Diplomats say the aim now is to lay groundwork for the G20 leaders' summit on 14–15 December at Trump's Doral resort in Florida.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine Air Defence31 October 2026

US Clears 500 Patriot Missiles for Ukraine in a $2.1 Billion Sale Paid for by Allies

The US State Department said on Friday it has approved the possible sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptor missiles to Ukraine for an estimated $2.1 billion, one of the largest defensive-weapons steps for Kyiv since President Trump returned to office. The missiles are intended to help Ukraine intercept Russian ballistic missiles as attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure intensify, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had said Kyiv had waited almost a month for the decision. The catch is timing: the interceptors have not yet been built, and officials briefed on the deal say delivery is not expected until a couple of years into the 2030s, as weapons orders of this scale typically take years to fulfil. No US government money is involved — Ukraine will pay using contributions from Canada, Germany and Norway channelled through the US JUMPSTART programme, together with national funds financed by the European Union's Ukraine Support Loan. Lockheed Martin is the principal contractor, and the US Army awarded the company a production contract worth up to $58.6 billion in July.

Latest · Middle East · Iran War Diplomacy31 October 2026

Egypt Warns a Second Round of the Iran War Would Be 'More Destructive' and Lead Nowhere

Egypt's Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty told the IISS Manama Dialogue in Bahrain on Saturday that he is extremely worried a renewed escalation between the United States and Iran would be more destructive than the first round and would achieve nothing, urging both sides to draw lessons from what has already happened. His warning comes as diplomacy remains stuck: an interim agreement reached in June did not produce a lasting ceasefire or restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran has since proposed that Washington lift its blockade of Iranian ports in exchange for reopening the waterway within seven days, and Qatari mediators returned to Tehran on Friday in a fresh attempt to broker a deal. The two sides still disagree on sequencing — whether nuclear talks or relief from the blockade and oil sanctions should come first — while a separate reported draft framework has floated transferring Iran's highly enriched uranium to a third country or diluting it, alongside a Hormuz reopening.

Latest · Global · Crypto — Record Theft30 October 2026

$6.36 Billion Drained From Binance in 20 Minutes — Investigators Blame North Korea's TraderTraitor

Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs says about $6.36 billion was moved out of wallets belonging to Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, across ten blockchains on the evening of 29 October, most of it in roughly 20 minutes — about four times the previous record, the $1.5 billion Bybit theft of February 2025. TRM attributes the attack with high confidence to TraderTraitor, described as North Korea's elite hacking unit, and Chainalysis chief executive Jonathan Levin said his firm reached the same assessment; the FBI previously named the same group for the Bybit hack. Binance has confirmed a security incident, suspended withdrawals on several networks and said an initial inquiry pointed to a compromised engineer's laptop, with further points of breach found later. Roughly $3 billion of the affected assets have been frozen with partners including Tether, Circle and Ripple, though TRM estimates about $2.4 billion in harder-to-freeze assets, including bitcoin, may remain with the attackers. Binance says deposits were unaffected and that it will cover losses.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — California31 October 2026

ICE Raids in a California Toss-Up District Spark Accusations of Voter Intimidation Days Before the Midterms

Federal immigration agents have stepped up operations across California's Central Valley in recent weeks, drawing fear in Latino communities and accusations of voter intimidation in a House race that could help decide control of Congress on Tuesday. Reuters reports the surge was announced to agents in early October in an internal email and, according to a US official speaking anonymously, was directed by the White House. The United Farm Workers union says it knows of hundreds of farmworkers detained, most, it says, without criminal records and some with legal work authorisation. In Bakersfield, arrests rose from about one a day for much of the year to nearly ten a day in mid-October, according to preliminary Homeland Security data; in nearby Wasco, a town of 26,000, at least seven people were arrested within days. The focus is California's 22nd district, which is 73% Latino, where Democrat Randy Villegas holds a slight polling edge over six-term Republican David Valadao. Villegas called the arrests a clear attempt to intimidate communities before the election. The White House said President Trump's agenda had made Hispanic Americans more prosperous and safer and referred questions on the operations to Homeland Security.

Latest · Americas · Canada–EU Summit30 October 2026

Canada and the EU Launch an 'Alliance for the Future' in Montreal — and Talk of Associate Membership

Canada and the European Union closed a two-day summit in Montreal on Friday by launching what they call an Alliance for the Future, with Prime Minister Mark Carney hosting European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa. Carney said Canada and the EU would pursue the closest possible partnership and described Canada as on a path to becoming the bloc's first associate member — a term with no legal basis in EU treaties and one contested by some member states. The summit produced more than twenty measures, including a digital trade agreement and a deal allowing the European Investment Bank to invest in Canadian critical minerals and raw materials, plus closer work on artificial intelligence and supply chains. On Ukraine, Carney announced Canada will join the EU's €90 billion support loan for Kyiv with an initial contribution of €500 million, a step that would also let Ukraine buy equipment from Canadian defence firms using EU funds. Both sides framed the push as a response to worsening global uncertainty and to US tariff measures that have hit Canada and Europe alike.

Latest · Global Affairs · Biodiversity COP1731 October 2026

Nearly 200 Nations Agree in Yerevan to Get Nature Goals Back on Track — but Admit They Are Off Course

Nearly 200 countries adopted hard-fought agreements late on Friday at the UN biodiversity summit, COP17, in Yerevan, Armenia, after two weeks of talks that ran into overtime, in a bid to revive the 2022 Kunming-Montreal pledge to halt and reverse nature loss by 2030. Delegates cheered as COP17 president Hambardzum Matevosyan gaveled through two key texts, though the final versions were not immediately published. The UN had warned before the summit that most targets are falling short — including protecting 30% of land and oceans, curbing pollution and invasive species, and raising conservation finance, where an estimated $700 billion a year more is needed. Disputes over finance, harmful subsidies for fossil fuels, intensive agriculture and overfishing, and the role of science, were among the hardest issues, and France's Ecological Transition Minister Monique Barbut said the outcome was not commensurate with the scale of the crisis. Ecuador was chosen to host the next summit in 2028, defeating Panama in a secret ballot.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Health31 October 2026

Bangladesh Dengue Deaths Hit 418 as October Becomes the Deadliest Month of the Year

Bangladesh recorded its highest monthly dengue death toll of 2026 in October, and experts warn the outbreak is unlikely to ease significantly before December. Health ministry data up to 30 October show 418 deaths and more than 133,000 hospital admissions this year, with October alone accounting for 173 deaths, ahead of September's 148. The surge comes on top of what officials describe as the world's worst measles outbreak, which has killed more than 1,200 people, leaving hospitals doubly stretched. At Dhaka's Shaheed Suhrawardy Medical College and Hospital, 279 dengue patients were being treated after 67 new admissions in a single day, its director said. Bangladesh has not yet introduced a dengue vaccine, though authorities are considering one for the longer term. Entomologist Kabirul Bashar of Jahangirnagar University said transmission would probably continue through November, with only modest relief expected before December, as a changing climate extends the dengue season.

Latest · Europe · Britain — Commonwealth31 October 2026

Burnham Says He Is Open to a Formal Slavery Apology — but Rules Out Reparations

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said he is open to calls for a formal apology for Britain's role in slavery, ahead of next week's Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda, where Caribbean leaders are expected to press demands on reparations. Writing in the Guardian, Burnham said Britain is offering a structured dialogue with those affected by the transatlantic slave trade, in which at least 12.5 million Africans were abducted and sold, with Britain transporting an estimated 3.2 million people. He rejected financial reparations, arguing that people in Britain today cannot be held liable for wrongs committed in a different age under different laws. Caribbean governments have often framed their claims in terms of debt relief, development finance, education, healthcare and climate resilience. Antigua and Barbuda Prime Minister Gaston Browne welcomed Burnham's comments and proposals for practical assistance, while noting reservations in a Facebook post. King Charles, who is touring the Caribbean, is also expected to face the issue at the summit.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China–Germany Trade31 October 2026

China Tells Visiting German President It Wants More German Goods and Open Trade

China's Premier Li Qiang told visiting German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier on Friday that Beijing is willing to import more high-quality German products, as the two countries try to steady trade ties amid rising protectionism between Europe and China. In a readout released late on Friday by China's foreign ministry, Li urged both sides to firmly uphold free trade and open cooperation, and said intergovernmental consultation mechanisms should be used to expand work together in autos, chemicals, artificial intelligence, digital industries and green technology. President Xi Jinping also met Steinmeier on Friday and urged Germany to pursue a constructive approach in its relations with China. The visit underlines how Europe's largest economy is balancing pressure over trade imbalances and industrial competition with the value of the Chinese market for its exporters.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan Border31 October 2026

Jordan Says Border Guards Stopped 14 Armed Men Trying to Cross From Syria Under Fire

Jordan's armed forces said on Saturday that border guards stopped a group of 14 armed individuals who tried to infiltrate northern Jordan from Syria on Friday. According to the military statement, the group attempted the crossing under heavy fire and in direct engagement with Jordanian forces, causing numerous casualties among the armed group, while the remaining members fled back into Syria. No details were given on Jordanian casualties or on the identity of the group. Jordan has repeatedly tightened security along its long border with Syria in recent years, citing smuggling and armed crossings, and Saturday's announcement is a reminder of how fragile security remains along that frontier even as regional diplomacy focuses on larger wars elsewhere.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan Pride31 October 2026

Taipei Mayor Gets an Angry Reception at Pride After a Police Raid on a Gay Bar

Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an received a hostile reception when he briefly appeared at Taiwan's annual Pride parade on Saturday, weeks after police raided a gay bar in the capital. More than 170,000 people joined the parade, east Asia's largest Pride event, in a country that legalised same-sex marriage in 2019 — a first for Asia — and prizes its reputation for LGBTQ+ rights. The raid on the Commander D bar in Ximen, the city's main gay neighbourhood, saw 51 people taken in for questioning and the owner arrested, and organisers said it revived historical trauma from decades when sexual and gender minorities faced state oppression; this year's parade theme was revised in response. Chiang, of the opposition Kuomintang and in a close re-election race, has previously expressed support for the community. Vice-President Hsiao Bi-khim led the ruling Democratic Progressive Party's delegation at the event, and DPP lawmaker Puma Shen was among those critical of the raid.
