Magnitude 7 Earthquake Strikes Northern Tibet, Strongest to Hit China Since January 2024

A powerful earthquake measuring magnitude 7 struck Baqen County in Nagqu, northern Tibet, at 10:33 p.m. local time on Thursday, China's Earthquake Networks Centre said, with no immediate reports of casualties or damage. The tremor occurred at a shallow depth of 10 kilometres, with its epicentre recorded at 32.40 degrees north latitude and 93.79 degrees east longitude — in high-altitude terrain where the average elevation within five kilometres is about 4,864 metres, roughly 400 kilometres from the Tibetan capital Lhasa. The centre said the shaking was clearly felt locally. China's Earthquake Administration activated a Level II emergency response, state news agency Xinhua reported. The Tibetan Plateau is highly prone to major quakes because it lies where the Eurasian and Indian tectonic plates collide. The quake is the strongest recorded in China since January 2024; a magnitude 7.1 tremor in Yushu, in neighbouring Qinghai province about 280 kilometres away, killed at least 2,698 people in 2010, while a quake in Tingri county in January 2025 killed at least 126.

Global Scramble to Patch Critical PHP Flaws Found With AI as Hackers Race to Exploit Them

Website operators, governments and cloud providers around the world have spent a second day racing to patch two critical vulnerabilities in PHP, the programming language that underpins a large share of the public web, after the flaws were disclosed on Wednesday. Tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and dubbed PHP2Shell, the remote-code-execution bugs each carry a severity rating of 9.2 out of 10 and affect releases dating back two decades. They lay undetected for more than a decade until researchers using artificial intelligence models located and verified them, working with the PHP team and security firm Wiz. PHP powers WordPress, Drupal, Joomla and many other systems, and the Drupal Association warned that all servers running any PHP version from the last 20 years are exposed. Several European governments took sites offline as a precaution, Japan's prime minister urged owners to check their sites, and Mitsubishi UFJ temporarily suspended some online services. Cloudflare said exploitation attempts jumped from thousands in the hours before disclosure to millions afterwards, while the US National Security Agency took the rare step of advising organisations that could not patch immediately to take systems offline. South Korea on Thursday raised its private-sector alert and convened an emergency meeting of intelligence, defence and science officials.

Zelenskyy Backs Freezing Ukraine's Front Lines as a First Step to End the War

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Wednesday that he is prepared to halt fighting along the current front lines as a starting point for ending the war with Russia, while insisting that any settlement must be followed by firm security guarantees from the United States and Europe. Asked on Fox & Friends whether he would accept a deal that left Ukraine holding 75 percent or more of its territory, Zelenskyy replied: "Yes, right now, we have to freeze. And I think this is [a] real way to end this war. We call it ceasefire or 'stop killing' like President Trump called it." He described a freeze as the minimum Kyiv needs from Moscow today, and rejected surrendering the Donbas as unfair, arguing Russian forces are not winning on the battlefield and are seeking through pressure what they have not taken by force. His remarks came just before the Kremlin said President Vladimir Putin had agreed in principle, after meeting US envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg, to resume three-way talks with Washington and Kyiv after 10 November, likely in Abu Dhabi. Russia also released retired American teacher Stephen Hubbard, detained since 2022, whose freedom President Donald Trump had requested.

Saudi Arabia Downs Four Drones Over Riyadh and Medina as India Condemns Deadly Houthi Attacks

Saudi Arabia said on Thursday it had intercepted and destroyed four drones in the regions of Riyadh and the holy city of Medina, as Houthi attacks on the kingdom continued for another day. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Malki announced the interceptions on X. The latest defensive action followed Wednesday's twin strikes in the Eastern Province: an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed a Saudi citizen and an Indian national and wounded eight others, including two Indians, while falling shrapnel from a drone intercepted over Aramco's Shaybah oil field killed a Filipino and a Nepali resident and injured an Indian and a Pakistani, according to the energy ministry, which said field operations were unaffected. India on Thursday strongly condemned the attacks on civilian and energy infrastructure as "completely unacceptable," expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia, and said it was working to help Indian pilgrims stranded by flight suspensions return home. New Delhi noted it also lost a citizen in an earlier strike on Riyadh's airport this month, part of a run of Houthi attacks in October that Saudi officials say has killed at least 17 people in the capital.

UAE and Greece Announce €12.75 Billion in Deals as Abu Dhabi Plans a Further €15 Billion

The United Arab Emirates and Greece on Thursday unveiled 18 memoranda of understanding, agreements and commercial partnerships worth €12.75 billion (about $14.3 billion), while Abu Dhabi said it intends to invest a further €15 billion in Greece. The announcements were made at the UAE–Greece Strategic Forum in Athens, attended by Abu Dhabi Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan and Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, the Abu Dhabi Media Office said. Sheikh Khaled also held talks with Mitsotakis on widening cooperation in energy, infrastructure and healthcare. The planned new investment builds on an existing joint initiative between the two countries and would add to €4 billion committed in 2022. Last year, Abu Dhabi renewable-energy company Masdar completed its full acquisition of Greek firm Terna Energy, after first buying a 70 percent stake in 2024 at an enterprise value of €3.2 billion.

Australian MP Helen Haines Loses Both Legs After Targeted Car Attack Outside Her Office

Independent Australian lawmaker Helen Haines has had both legs amputated after being struck by a car outside her office in Wangaratta, Victoria, in what police described as a targeted attack and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called a deliberate act. The 65-year-old was hit on Monday while retrieving items from the back of her car and was flown to a Melbourne hospital, where her left leg was amputated that day and her right leg on Wednesday. Her husband Phil Haines told reporters she is alive and stable, saying her heart and mind — "the two most important parts of Helen for us" — are in good shape, and calling her a fighter facing a long recovery. Police have charged 43-year-old Brendan Nugent with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and the federal offence of harming a public official, which carries up to 10 years in prison; he did not seek bail and is due back in court on 4 February. The attack has prompted national soul-searching in a country where political violence is rare, as police warn of rising online extremism and a steady increase in threats and abuse directed at elected officials.

Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia Sign 'Badr' Pact to Deepen Defence-Industry Cooperation

Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia signed the Badr Memorandum of Understanding in Doha on Wednesday to expand cooperation on defence manufacturing, joint production, technology transfer, technical training and research and development. The signing brought together Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Muhammad Asif, Türkiye's Defence Minister Yaşar Güler, Qatar's Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of State for Defence Sheikh Saud bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, and Indonesia's Deputy Defence Minister Yusuf Jauhari, alongside the head of Türkiye's Presidency of Defence Industries, Haluk Görgün. Türkiye's defence ministry said the accord holds substantial economic importance, pooling each country's distinct production strengths to strengthen industrial capacity and help meet the needs of allied and friendly nations. Unlike the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signed in August by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye — under which an attack on one party is treated as an attack on all — the Badr memorandum is focused on industrial collaboration and does not itself create a collective-defence obligation. Analysts say Türkiye contributes systems integration and technology, Pakistan and Indonesia bring manufacturing scale and large domestic markets, and Qatar brings investment capacity and demand.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology & Security22 October 2026

UN Launches Push for Oversight of AI in Warfare as Pakistan Flags New Risks From Emerging Technologies

The United Nations is launching a new forum on artificial intelligence, peace and security through its Institute for Disarmament Research (UNIDIR) — described as the first and only forum of its kind in the UN system — amid warnings that AI is shrinking the space for human judgment in war, including in the nuclear domain. UNIDIR director Robin Geiss cautioned that the opacity of many AI systems heightens the risk of errors, miscalculation and unintended escalation, and noted that AI is already used for intelligence analysis, target identification, drone operations and cyberattacks. The forum, run through UNIDIR's Centre of Excellence on AI, Peace and Security, will work on voluntary guidelines for responsible industry practice, a shared lexicon for terms such as autonomy and safety, and the risk of non-state groups misusing AI for planning and logistics. Geiss pointed to growing cross-divide concern, including a US proposal in September for an AI notification mechanism with China, and to support for Secretary-General António Guterres's calls for stronger regulation and a legally binding treaty on lethal autonomous weapons. Separately at the UN General Assembly's First Committee, Pakistan's Counsellor Syed Atif Raza warned that AI converging with synthetic biology and life sciences is creating novel risks, saying: "Science is moving faster than governance."

Poland Detains Second Suspect in Murder of Russian Artist Who Criticised Putin

Polish authorities have detained a second suspect in the June murder of a Russian artist known for criticising President Vladimir Putin, counterintelligence chief Tomasz Siemoniak said on Thursday. The artist, Robert Kuzovkov, also known as Semyon Skrepetsky, died after being shot five times in the eastern city of Biała Podlaska. He had staged a protest in Berlin shortly before the killing. Siemoniak wrote on X that the newly detained man, a 22-year-old Georgian national suspected of complicity in the murder, has been placed in pre-trial detention for three months by a court. At the time of the shooting, police detained a 36-year-old man who they said was using a Georgian passport. The case has drawn attention in Poland as an apparent targeted killing on EU soil of a vocal critic of the Kremlin.

South African Police Investigate Killing of Foreign National Beaten by Crowd in Durban

South African police said on Thursday they are investigating the killing of a foreign national who was beaten to death by a crowd of about 50 people in central Durban. The police service said in a post on X that the victim, believed to be in his late thirties or early forties, was declared dead at the scene, after two men who had broken away from a march in the city centre approached him and the wider crowd joined the assault. Police did not disclose the man's nationality and said the motive remains unknown. The killing follows months of anti-migrant protests in South Africa, some of which have turned violent and drawn international criticism, prompting thousands of migrants from elsewhere in Africa to leave affected communities. Earlier this month, anti-migrant demonstrations in Durban and in Johannesburg's Soweto township turned violent, with foreign-owned businesses looted and vehicles set on fire after protests about the processing of asylum applications at a refugee reception centre.
