Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan Strait29 October 2026

Taiwan Flags an "Obvious" Threat From More Than 50 Chinese Vessels Spread From Southern Japan to the Philippines

Taiwan's National Security Council secretary-general Joseph Wu said on Wednesday that more than 50 Chinese navy and other official vessels were operating across a wide stretch of water running from southern Japan, past Taiwan, and toward the Philippines, describing the simultaneous pressure on all three as obvious. Wu published a map showing 53 vessels in total, 34 of them naval warships, including the aircraft carrier Liaoning and its battle group positioned east of the Philippines, other formations off Taiwan's east and south-east coasts, and three vessels off southern Japan. Taiwan's defence minister Wellington Koo told parliament on Thursday that the ministry was keeping close watch through intelligence-sharing with partners and did not welcome attempts to project deterrence that could undermine regional stability. Beijing said earlier this month that the Liaoning was on a routine training deployment not aimed at any specific country, and the defence ministries of China, Japan and the Philippines did not immediately comment. Relations between China and both Japan and the Philippines have deteriorated in recent months, over Japanese support for Taiwan and a bitter stand-off with Manila in the South China Sea.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets & AI Earnings29 October 2026

Alphabet Loses Nearly $400 Billion in After-Hours Value as AI Spending Spooks Investors — Samsung Posts Record Profit and Still Falls

Asian markets gave up early gains on Thursday after a sharply divided set of technology earnings raised fresh questions about whether massive artificial-intelligence spending is producing cash quickly enough. Alphabet shares fell about 10% in after-hours trading, erasing close to $400 billion from its market value, after heavy cash burn and pressure on its traditional advertising business worried investors, pulling Nasdaq futures about 1% lower. Microsoft moved the other way, rising around 3% after stronger-than-expected cash flow and cloud growth, while Oracle had earlier jumped on an upbeat long-term cash-flow outlook. In Seoul, Samsung Electronics reported third-quarter operating profit up nearly ninefold to a record high on strong demand for AI chips, yet its shares still slid more than 4%, dragging South Korea's KOSPI down 2.6% a day after it had surged nearly 7%. The broader Asia-Pacific index outside Japan fell 0.7%, Japan's Nikkei trimmed gains to 0.3%, mainland Chinese blue chips fell 0.9% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng edged up 0.2%. Investors are now waiting for Amazon and Intel results later on Thursday and for the US inflation reading the Federal Reserve watches most closely, after the Fed held rates steady overnight with two members voting for a rise.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab, India28 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab: Damage Count Begins in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha After More Than 180,000 People Were Moved to Safety

Officials in eastern India were assessing damage on Thursday after Cyclone Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh coast early on Wednesday as a very severe cyclonic storm, with sustained winds of 125–135 km/h and gusts to 150 km/h, a storm surge of up to 1.5 metres and torrential rain. More than 180,000 people were evacuated across Andhra Pradesh and Odisha ahead of landfall; Andhra Pradesh alone opened about 290 shelters housing more than 84,000 people, moved nearly 5,000 pregnant women to health facilities, and brought more than 30,000 fishing boats back to shore. Reports of the death toll differed as the clean-up began: the National Disaster Response Force said three people were killed in Andhra Pradesh, while state reporting cited by the Indian Express put the figure at at least seven, from falling trees and electrocution, with no official state total yet published. At least 180 houses were reported destroyed in Andhra Pradesh, hundreds of trees were brought down, National Highway 16 was briefly blocked, power supplies were disrupted and operations at three airports were suspended. Odisha evacuated more than 30,000 people from low-lying and landslide-prone areas and placed six southern districts under a red warning after Gajapati district recorded almost 250 mm of rain; Andhra Pradesh's chief minister has asked the central government to send assessment teams and assistance.

Latest · Americas · Brazil After the Election28 October 2026

Flávio Bolsonaro Lands in Florida After Leaving Brazil Despite a Court Travel Ban — Lula Calls the Flight "Shameful"

Brazil's defeated right-wing presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro has reached the United States after leaving the country despite a Supreme Court order barring his departure and requiring him to surrender his passports in a corruption and money-laundering investigation. Argentine migration authorities said Bolsonaro, 45, crossed by land at the Tancredo Neves bridge late on Tuesday with his wife and two daughters, then boarded a private Baires Fly flight to Guayaquil, Ecuador, which later continued to Miami; Al Jazeera reported the family landed at Opa-Locka Executive Airport near Miami on Wednesday morning. Bolsonaro, the son of jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, lost Sunday's runoff to incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by 51.8% to 48.2% and has refused to concede, criticising a last-minute court decision to extend voting by two hours. The US State Department said it would issue visas to Bolsonaro and his family, and President Donald Trump — who has not acknowledged the result and said without evidence that the vote appeared rigged, while also expressing admiration for Lula — said he would approve their entry. Lula called leaving the country "shameful" and said Bolsonaro should have stayed to face the allegations in court, his own campaign manager said he was shocked and had received only a brief message, and analysts say the flight could trigger a struggle for leadership of Brazil's conservative movement. With the election lost, Bolsonaro is also set to lose his parliamentary immunity.

Latest · Africa · Congo Conflict28 October 2026

US Sanctions Rwanda's Defence Ministry and a Military Bank Over Support for M23 Rebels in Eastern Congo

The United States imposed sanctions on Wednesday on Rwanda's Ministry of Defence and on Zigama Credit and Savings Society, a financial institution serving the security forces, accusing Kigali of continuing to back the M23 rebel group in the mineral-rich east of the Democratic Republic of Congo despite the US-brokered Washington Accords signed by both countries' presidents last December. The Treasury Department said the ministry directed the Rwanda Defence Force — already sanctioned earlier this year along with four senior officials — to deploy thousands of troops across North and South Kivu provinces, operating alongside M23 and supplying equipment, logistics and training, and said Rwanda had introduced advanced weaponry including surface-to-air missiles and jamming equipment that risks civilian aviation. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington was holding Rwanda to its commitments and that it must halt support for M23 and withdraw its troops. Congo's foreign ministry welcomed the move as a message that Rwanda cannot act with impunity. A Rwandan government spokesperson responded that commitments in the accords on neutralising the FDLR, a Hutu militia rooted in the 1994 genocide, remain unmet, and that Rwanda stands ready to implement the deal if Congo does the same; Rwanda's foreign minister, speaking in Moscow, said his country should not be a scapegoat for a fundamentally Congolese problem. Rwanda denies backing M23, while UN experts say it exercises significant control over the group, which seized Goma and Bukavu in early 2025.

Dozens of Somali Soldiers Reported Killed in a Major Battle With Al-Shabab — Both Sides Claim Heavy Losses Inflicted

Somali soldiers who survived a major battle with al-Shabab in the south of the country say dozens of government troops were killed, in fighting neither side's account of which can be independently verified. An officer travelling with the convoy told the Associated Press he saw at least 20 fellow soldiers dead after Monday's fighting near Jameeca village in Burhakaba district, Bay region, as troops advanced from Leego; two soldiers told Reuters dozens were killed, one saying he helped move 40 bodies and more than 100 wounded to hospitals in Mogadishu, while some troops initially listed as missing later reached safety and others remain unaccounted for. Somalia's defence ministry said its forces killed about 75 militants and wounded 56, and acknowledged that explosive-laden vehicles damaged army vehicles, but gave no figure for its own casualties. Al-Shabab, the al-Qaida-linked group that has fought the government for nearly two decades and still holds large areas of central Somalia, claimed it killed 321 soldiers, captured prisoners and seized Turkish-supplied armoured vehicles, pickups, weapons and ammunition after ambushing a convoy entering territory it controls — a toll far above the soldiers' accounts. Türkiye has become a key backer of Somalia's government as the United States has cut funding for the UN logistics mission that supports combat operations.

Israeli Strikes Kill Five in Gaza, Health Officials Say, a Day After Israel's Deadlocked Election

Gaza's civil defence agency and local hospitals said Israeli strikes killed five people and wounded others across the territory on Wednesday, the day after Israelis voted in an election that produced no clear winner. Civil defence spokesperson Mahmoud Bassal said three people were killed and several wounded, some seriously, in a drone attack in Rafah in the south; a local hospital said it received three bodies and 15 wounded, one of whom later died, and civil defence said another person was killed in a separate strike in Khan Younis. Kremlin Aide: Talks Are Under Way With Washington on Lifting Sanctions on Russia's Sovereign Investment Fund

Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said on Wednesday that discussions are under way with the Trump administration on lifting US sanctions on the Russian Direct Investment Fund, Russia's flagship sovereign investment vehicle, according to Russian news agencies. Ushakov said the fund's head, presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev, was actively pursuing the talks, that the conversation had been going on for some time, and that he did not know what the outcome would be. The comments followed a New York Times report the same day that the administration was considering abandoning the sanctions. The fund was sanctioned after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and Washington has already made a series of concessions to Moscow: earlier this month President Trump announced Russia would supply diesel to US and world markets under a temporary sanctions lift — a fourth Russian diesel tanker was due to depart on Wednesday — and he issued an executive order waiving key Russia-related sanctions and tariffs, including duties on Russian goods and penalties for buying its oil and gas, that had been due to take effect under the Graham Act he signed into law last month. The possible investment-fund move comes as separate diplomacy on Ukraine continues, with Nordic leaders having just spoken directly to President Vladimir Putin and trilateral talks planned in Abu Dhabi.

Latest · Americas · Haiti29 October 2026

Haiti Pushes Its First Presidential Election in a Decade Back to July 2027 as Gang Violence Blocks Preparations

Haiti's electoral council has postponed the country's first presidential election in ten years to 18 July 2027, according to a new calendar published on Wednesday, abandoning the previously planned first-round date of 13 December. Haiti has held no elections since 2016 and has had no elected president since Jovenel Moïse was assassinated in 2021; legislative terms have since lapsed one by one, leaving the country without any nationally elected officials. A transitional arrangement created in 2024 now runs the country — its presidential council's mandate expired in February, leaving appointed Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé and his cabinet governing the interim period largely by decree. Heavily armed gangs control large parts of Port-au-Prince and surrounding areas, have displaced roughly 1.5 million people and cut the routes needed to move voting materials, and the council says the timetable must reflect the technical and operational conditions needed to run the vote properly. Voter registration, which began on 20 July, is continuing, but as of Tuesday only about 769,000 voters had registered. Some residents at registration centres say they still want a vote that produces a government able to tackle insecurity, while others question whether an election can be held while armed groups dominate daily life.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms & Voting29 October 2026

US Judge Rejects Justice Department Bid to Block California Ballots From Voters It Calls Likely Noncitizens

A US federal judge on Wednesday rejected a Justice Department request to temporarily bar California from counting mail ballots in the 3 November midterm elections cast by people on a list of more than 20,000 registered voters the Trump administration alleges are likely noncitizens. District Judge David Carter issued the decision after a hearing in Los Angeles on the department's request for a temporary restraining order, in a lawsuit filed on 15 October — an unusual federal intervention in a state-run voting process weeks before an election. California argues its safeguards against noncitizen voting are robust, that the department's claims rest on suspect data, and that complying would risk disenfranchising eligible voters. Studies and state audits have repeatedly found voting by noncitizens to be rare, despite the president's long-running claims to the contrary. The ruling is the latest setback for the administration's effort to expand the federal role in elections as Republicans fight to keep control of both houses of Congress; California, a Democratic stronghold, has more House seats — 52 — than any other state, and has also been a focus of the administration's immigration crackdown. In a separate case in Minnesota, a prosecutor on Wednesday charged conservative activist James O'Keefe and influencer Cam Higby with felony unlawful voting and identity theft, alleging they posed as registered voters at polling places during the August primary for a hidden-camera stunt.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology29 October 2026

Report: China's DeepSeek and Moonshot Relied Heavily on OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra Outputs to Train Their Latest Models

Chinese AI developers DeepSeek and Moonshot AI relied heavily on outputs from OpenAI's powerful GPT-6 Astra model to train their newest systems, The Information reported on Wednesday, citing people with direct knowledge of the matter, in a claim reported by Reuters. The technique, known as distillation — training a smaller or newer model on the answers produced by a more capable one — has become a flashpoint in US–China technology competition, with leading American firms arguing it lets rivals reproduce advanced capabilities more quickly and cheaply. The report said Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro, released last week, and DeepSeek's upgraded V4.1-Flash 1017, launched on 17 October, both benefited from distilling Astra. OpenAI said in a 30 September blog post that it had linked a core part of a distillation campaign that began in July to individuals associated with Moonshot AI. OpenAI, DeepSeek and Moonshot did not immediately respond to Reuters requests for comment. Firms including Anthropic, OpenAI and Google have all argued against the practice when applied to their models, even as distillation remains a widely used training method across the industry, and the dispute is likely to sharpen debate in Washington over export controls and access to frontier US models.
