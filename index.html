Latest · Middle East · Iran21 October 2026

Iran Rules Out Talks With US Until Its Demands Are Met, Pezeshkian Tells Pakistan's Army Chief

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has told Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, that Tehran will not enter talks with the United States while it faces blockade and threats, according to Iranian state media — a day after Munir visited Tehran to discuss mediation efforts. Parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf told Munir that Washington could not regain Iran's trust until it fulfilled seven conditions Tehran set last month for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran had received no reply from Saudi Arabia or Yemen's Houthis to its offer to mediate between them, while the Houthis denied launching drones toward the Saudi holy city of Medina after the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted drones there and over the Eastern Region. Separately, the Financial Times reported that President Trump blocked an Israeli plan to strike Iranian nuclear, regime and energy targets on the weekend of 10 October, unwilling to reignite the conflict ahead of the US midterm elections on 3 November.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War21 October 2026

Four Killed in Russian Strikes on Ukraine as US Envoy Witkoff Meets Putin in St Petersburg

Four people were killed in overnight Russian attacks across Ukraine, officials said on Wednesday, as US special envoy Steve Witkoff travelled to St Petersburg to present President Vladimir Putin with an updated proposal for ending the war. Two people died when a drone struck a 24-storey residential building in Kyiv, Prime Minister Sergiy Koretsky said, and two more — an elderly couple — were killed in the northeastern Kharkiv region. The strikes hit energy facilities in the Kyiv, Volyn, Odesa and Mykolaiv regions, prompting emergency power cuts in the capital. Witkoff had met President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European representatives in Norway a day earlier, after which Zelenskyy called for a trilateral meeting with Putin and Trump — a format Moscow has so far rejected. Ahead of the talks, Russia released US citizen Stephen James Hubbard, who was seized in Ukraine after the 2022 invasion and accused of being a mercenary, in what was described as a goodwill gesture.

Latest · Africa · Sudan20 October 2026

Drone Strike on a Market in Disputed Abyei Kills at Least 153 People

At least 153 people were killed when a drone strike hit Amiet Market in Abyei, the oil-rich border region disputed by Sudan and South Sudan, and ignited a nearby fuel depot, a local official said. Yohanna Akol Ngor, the Abyei administration's caretaker information minister, blamed Sudan's military for Monday's attack and said the death toll kept rising as the fire burned into Tuesday. A senior Sudanese officer said the army had targeted fuel tankers arriving from South Sudan that he claimed were bound for the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, insisting the army does not target civilians — an account the Associated Press said it could not independently verify. Doctors Without Borders said more than 20 wounded people, several in critical condition and some with burns, were treated at Ameth Bek Hospital. South Sudan's foreign ministry summoned Sudan's ambassador to protest and urged Khartoum to settle Abyei's status through diplomacy.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Philippines Weather20 October 2026

Super Typhoon Rosal Bears Down on Northern Luzon With Winds of 185 Kilometres an Hour

Super Typhoon Champi entered the Philippine Area of Responsibility on Tuesday morning and was given the local name Rosal, the state weather bureau PAGASA said, prompting Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal No. 1 over Batanes, the eastern Babuyan Islands, eastern mainland Cagayan, northeastern Isabela and northeastern Catanduanes. The storm packed maximum sustained winds of 185 kilometres per hour with gusts up to 230 kph as it moved west-northwest across the Philippine Sea, and forecasters said further strengthening was possible before it gradually weakens later in the week. Rosal is expected to skirt northern Luzon, bringing heavy rain to the far north and rain over Central Luzon and Metro Manila, before turning north and leaving Philippine waters by Friday night or early Saturday. The social welfare department said more than 4.5 million family food packs had been prepositioned in relief warehouses nationwide ahead of the storm.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Space20 October 2026

Japan Launches First-Ever Mission to Land on a Mars Moon and Bring Samples Home

Japan's Martian Moons eXploration spacecraft, known as MMX, is on its way to Phobos after lifting off aboard an H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center, with the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency confirming separation about 71 minutes after launch. The probe is due to reach Mars in August 2027 and spend roughly three years surveying both of the planet's moons, Phobos and Deimos, before landing on Phobos to collect at least 10 grams of surface material from below the top layer. If all goes to plan, a return capsule carrying the samples will land in Australia in 2031 — the first material ever brought back from a Martian moon. Scientists hope the samples will settle whether Phobos formed from debris blasted off Mars by a giant impact or is an asteroid captured by the planet's gravity, and shed light on how water and organic material reached the early terrestrial planets. NASA is contributing instruments and scientists to the mission, which also carries a small European rover.

Latest · Americas · Canada20 October 2026

Alberta Voters Reject the Path to an Independence Referendum by a Wide Margin

Voters in the Canadian province of Alberta have decisively rejected starting the legal process toward a binding referendum on separating from Canada, with about 76 percent choosing to remain part of the country and 24 percent backing the separatist path in preliminary results. The non-binding vote asked whether Alberta should stay a province or begin the constitutional steps needed for a full independence referendum. Prime Minister Mark Carney, who campaigned for unity as Canada faces a trade war and annexation talk from the United States, called it a great day and said Albertans had made a clear choice to build together. Premier Danielle Smith said she personally voted against independence but had called the vote to settle a question that has divided the province for years. Leaders of the Alberta Prosperity Project, the main separatist group backed by allies of President Trump, conceded the defeat but vowed their campaign would continue.

Latest · Africa · Burkina Faso21 October 2026

Burkina Faso's Parliament Votes Unanimously to Restore the Death Penalty

Burkina Faso's transitional parliament has adopted a revised penal code that brings back the death penalty, nearly a decade after the West African country abolished capital punishment in 2018. All 71 members of the assembly voted for the change, which makes terrorism, espionage and treason punishable by death, along with importing expired or banned products that cause someone's death. Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala argued that the previous code's relative leniency had helped armed groups recruit, and the military government presents the reform as part of its fight against an Islamist insurgency linked to al Qaeda and Islamic State that has killed thousands and displaced millions. Human Rights Watch has warned that restoring executions could deepen repression under the junta and chill criticism of the authorities. The country's last known judicial execution took place in 1988, according to Amnesty International.

Latest · Europe · UK Energy21 October 2026

UK Approves Jackdaw Gas Field in the North Sea on Energy Security Grounds

The British government has approved production at the Jackdaw gas field, about 150 miles east of Aberdeen, saying gas could begin flowing to homes and businesses as early as the end of this month. Ministers estimate the field — the UK's largest known untapped gas reservoir, first approved in 2022 but held up by years of litigation over its climate impact — will lift domestic gas production by around 6 percent at its peak, operate for up to 11 years, protect hundreds of jobs and add an estimated £2.9 billion to the economy. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the decision was compatible with Britain's legally binding climate commitments and its net zero target for 2050. Alongside the approval, the government announced a new Energy Wealth Fund, seeded with £2 billion, to channel North Sea tax revenues into renewable and low-carbon projects. Environmental groups sharply criticised the decision, which was announced on the same day the UN warned the world will unavoidably overshoot the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C warming limit.

Latest · Americas · Brazil Election20 October 2026

Meta Tells Brazil's Electoral Court It Now Treats Lula and Bolsonaro Equally Before Sunday's Runoff

Meta has told Brazil's Superior Electoral Court that it has put in place measures guaranteeing equal treatment for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and his challenger, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp ahead of Sunday's presidential runoff. The filing followed an order giving the company 24 hours to prove compliance, after Lula's campaign complained on 15 October that Meta's algorithms and AI restrictions were suppressing its content while favouring Bolsonaro; the court had raised the daily fine for non-compliance to $200,000. In a nine-page submission, Meta listed 17 steps it has taken, including human review within two hours of any automatic block or limit placed on either campaign's advertising accounts, a system that will stay in place until the vote. The company denied any political bias, blaming technical problems, and said the court's restrictions do not affect organic posts. Bolsonaro led the first round on 4 October with 47 percent to Lula's 45 percent, and polls point to a tight finish.

Latest · Global Affairs · Sport21 October 2026

Dakar Rally Will Return to Africa in 2027 as Fighting Forces It Out of Saudi Arabia

The Dakar Rally will leave Saudi Arabia and return to Africa for its 49th edition next January, according to three people involved in the World Rally-Raid Championship who spoke to Reuters. The race, staged in Saudi Arabia since 2020, is now expected to open with a prologue in Spain or Portugal before crossing to Morocco for around 12 stages between 9 and 22 January. Organisers had unveiled a Saudi route in May starting and finishing in King Abdullah Economic City, and later worked on a fallback route in the north of the kingdom, but the worsening conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi movement — including a strike on Riyadh's international airport on 10 October that killed at least 12 people and wounded more than 300 — forced the change. The rally began in 1978 as a Paris-to-Dakar race across the Sahara, left Africa for security reasons and ran in South America from 2009 to 2019. The Amaury Sport Organisation and the FIA and FIM governing bodies did not immediately comment.

Source: The Straits Times / Reuters
