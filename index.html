Latest · Middle East · Iran — Warning to Washington11 November 2026

Iran’s Security Chief Warns US Bases in Europe Could Be Struck, and Says Hormuz Stays Shut Until Tehran’s Seven Conditions Are Met

Iran’s top security official, Mohsen Rezaei, widened Tehran’s threats on Wednesday, telling Lebanon’s Al-Mayadeen TV that if American aircraft take off from a US base in Europe or any other distant region to bomb Iran, Tehran will strike that base as well. He said a renewed US attack would bring retaliation against distant installations and even islands Washington assumes are beyond Iranian missile range, insisted that no talks with the United States are currently taking place, and ruled out allowing even a particle of enriched nuclear material to leave the country. Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until Washington meets seven Iranian conditions, reported to include lifting sanctions imposed since the Islamabad memorandum, ending the US naval blockade, unfreezing restricted assets and drawing down the American military footprint in the region. The remarks followed Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s comment in Paris that US economic pressure is now damaging enough that major combat operations are not needed for the moment.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Talks Close11 November 2026

Abu Dhabi Swap Could Exceed 1,000 Prisoners on Each Side Within Two to Three Weeks, but the Land Dispute That Blocks a Peace Deal Has Not Moved

Fresh detail emerged on Wednesday night from the first direct Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months, held over two days in Abu Dhabi with US mediation. Russian Lieutenant-General Alexander Zorin said the aim is to go past 1,000 prisoners on each side, and Russia’s TASS agency reported the exchange could take place within two to three weeks, with exact numbers to be finalised on Thursday. US envoy Jared Kushner called the sessions productive and said progress had been made on post-war security guarantees, but was clear that no settlement can be signed until the territorial dispute is resolved. Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky restated Moscow’s position in uncompromising terms, and Russia continues to demand a Ukrainian withdrawal from the part of the Donbas Kyiv still holds and from two other partly occupied regions. Kushner and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff are due in Moscow on Thursday to meet President Vladimir Putin, and then in Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 in Antalya11 November 2026

At COP31, Guterres Sets Hard Numbers: End Unabated Coal by 2040, Cut Oil and Gas by a Third, and Reduce Emissions 6% Every Year

UN Secretary-General António Guterres used his address to the COP31 leaders’ summit in Antalya on Wednesday to put dates and figures on the transition he is demanding: phase out unabated coal by 2040, cut oil and gas consumption by a third, and bring global greenhouse gas emissions down by about 6 percent a year. He warned that current national plans deliver at best a third of that pace and said fossil fuels must go the way of the dinosaurs. A UNEP adaptation report released on 4 November estimates developing countries will need roughly $305 billion a year by 2035 to cope with climate impacts. Host president Recep Tayyip Erdoğan called for fairer climate finance and pointed to Türkiye’s 2053 net-zero target and its renewables build-out. The summit, running 9–20 November with Türkiye holding the presidency and Australia’s Chris Bowen chairing the negotiations, is pushing a voluntary pledge to lift electricity to 35 percent of global energy use by 2035. The United States is absent for a second straight year, and the leaders of China and India also stayed away, sending deputies instead.

Latest · Europe · France — Supermarket Cyberattack11 November 2026

France Seeks Arrest Warrants for Three Iranian Suspects Over the Fridge Cyberattack That Hit 700 Supermarkets and Started at Least 15 Fires

France’s confrontation with Iran over last month’s supermarket sabotage sharpened this week. Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau has opened a formal inquiry into the Shahid Kaveh cyber unit, which France links to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, and prosecutors have requested international arrest warrants for its alleged leader Hamid Reza Lashgarian and two deputies, Mahdi Lashgarian and Reza Salarvand. Investigators say hackers remotely triggered defrost cycles overnight on 11–12 October, driving refrigeration temperatures up across roughly 700 of France’s 10,000 supermarkets, spoiling entire inventories of perishable food and sparking at least 15 fires. Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called the operation unprecedented in scale and aggression, summoned Iran’s ambassador, and said Paris will seek EU-level sanctions and rules nothing out in response. Tehran’s foreign ministry categorically rejects the accusation as baseless, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi mocked France as unable to manage its own fridges. Analysts say similar incidents reported in at least 11 other European countries suggest a widening shadow campaign.

Latest · Europe · NATO — Baltic Drone Alerts11 November 2026

NATO Jets Scrambled Twice Overnight as Drones From Belarus Cross Lithuania Into Latvia and Two Airports Shut

The Baltic states endured a difficult night on Tuesday into Wednesday as suspected drones approaching from Belarus triggered repeated air alerts. Lithuania’s National Crisis Management Centre said NATO Baltic Air Policing jets were scrambled twice: in the first incident three drones were tracked inside Belarus and none crossed the border, but in the second a drone entered near Druskininkai in the south, flew north across several regions and left Lithuanian airspace near Pasvalys, with at least one aircraft continuing into Latvia. Alerts escalated from yellow to red, urging residents in areas including Kaunas to take shelter, and Vilnius and Kaunas airports were temporarily closed, disrupting three flights and more than 400 passengers at the capital’s airport alone. Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevičius said four drones were spotted across the region and that an attempt was made to bring one down over northern Lithuania, with missile debris later found near Biržai. Latvia activated drone-interceptor and air-defence units along its eastern border before lifting its alert in the early hours. Officials have not established who launched the drones.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria — Day of Mourning11 November 2026

Bulgaria Mourns 12 Killed in Fog Pile-Ups Involving up to 86 Vehicles on the Trakia Motorway; EU Flags Fly at Half-Mast

Bulgaria observed a national day of mourning on Wednesday for the 12 people killed and around 50 injured in chain-reaction crashes on the Trakia motorway near Yambol on Tuesday morning. Officials say a grain truck blocked a passenger bus and triggered the first pile-up in fog so dense that visibility fell to about 30 metres, with a second crash following minutes later on the opposite carriageway; estimates of vehicles involved range from more than 50 to as many as 86. Ten people died at the scene and two on the way to hospital, according to the health minister, and the motorway linking Sofia with the Black Sea port of Burgas was closed in both directions between Zimnitsa and Kabile for rescue and investigation work. Prime Minister Rumen Radev called it a dark day for the whole country, and EU flags at the European Commission and its representations were flown at half-mast in solidarity. Bulgaria recorded 71 road deaths per million inhabitants last year, the highest rate in the European Union.

Saudi Air Defences Destroy Three Houthi Cruise Missiles Aimed at Riyadh, the Second Day of Interceptions Over the Capital

Saudi Arabia intercepted missiles over its capital for a second consecutive day on Wednesday, with the Saudi-led coalition saying air defences destroyed three cruise missiles launched by Yemen’s Houthi movement toward the Riyadh region and reporting no casualties or damage from the latest salvo. On Tuesday the coalition had downed a ballistic missile and a cruise missile also aimed at Riyadh, and further projectiles toward the kingdom’s south and a drone over the oil-rich east. The exchanges follow a Houthi strike on Sunday that damaged an Aramco tower in Riyadh’s financial district, and mark a sustained escalation since the autumn in a conflict the World Health Organization says has killed at least 1,771 people and displaced more than 300,000 in Yemen since August.

Latest · Americas · US — Iran-Linked Plots Charged10 November 2026

US Charges Three Men Over Nearly 20 Iran-Backed Plots, Including at RAF Fairford, as Treasury Secretary Bessent Moves Onto a Military Base

The US Justice Department unsealed an eight-count indictment on Tuesday charging three men — dual Iranian-Iraqi national Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, Iranian national Mohsen Mohajeri and Iraqi national Mohammad Ali al-Hassani — with terrorism offences for allegedly acting for Kata’ib Hezbollah and Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps across nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in the United States and Europe. Prosecutors say al-Hassani organised surveillance and logistics for a planned September attack on RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, the British base hosting US aircraft and personnel, and that al-Saadi, already jailed in New York, passed coded instructions in the days before it. Some charges carry up to life in prison; the allegations are unproven and Iran has denied involvement. The case landed as the US moved B-1 bombers out of Fairford, removed aircraft from RAF Mildenhall on Monday, and — according to the New York Times — relocated Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent from his home to a military base following Iranian threats against his life.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — Ebola Outbreak11 November 2026

Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Passes 11,042 Cases and 5,387 Deaths, Now the Second-Largest and Second-Deadliest on Record

Government data released on Tuesday put the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s Ebola outbreak at 11,042 confirmed cases and at least 5,387 deaths, making it the country’s deadliest outbreak and the second-largest ever recorded, behind only the 2014–2016 West African epidemic. The outbreak, declared in mid-May and caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, has spread to eight of Congo’s 26 provinces, concentrated in Ituri and North Kivu, and cases have also been reported in Kenya and Nigeria in the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak to date. There is no approved vaccine or specific treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, many deaths have occurred outside treatment centres, and health authorities say armed conflict, poor roads, a strained health system and community mistrust are hampering the response. Congolese scientists have sequenced more than 1,500 viral genomes in under six months, and experimental vaccine candidates are being studied in a WHO-supported trial that began enrolling in Bunia in October.
