Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Close to a Deal22 November 2026

COP31 Presidents Say a Final Climate Package Is Ready for Approval After Talks Ran Through the Night

The climate summit in Antalya has moved from deadlock to the edge of a deal. COP31 President Murat Kurum told a late-night plenary that the presidency had put forward a text it believed every party could accept, and that delegates would reconvene on Sunday morning to begin approving it. Negotiators had already blown past a Friday deadline, worked through Saturday and continued into the early hours of Sunday, after intensive consultations led jointly by Australia, which is steering the talks, and Turkey, the host. Delegations were given until 03:00 GMT to raise objections. The central fight remained the same one that stalled the summit a day earlier: whether the final decision names a transition away from fossil fuels. A loose coalition of about 130 countries has pushed for that wording, while major producers have resisted it, and separate disputes over carbon capture language and an electricity-share goal also held up agreement. This is the first COP since the UN confirmed global warming is set to pass 1.5°C, the threshold countries pledged a decade ago to try to hold.

Latest · Middle East · Tehran — Power Cuts and Tankers22 November 2026

Iran Orders Rolling Power Cuts, Vows to Break the US Blockade, and Keeps Talking Through Qatar

Iran is feeling the war at home and signalling it will push back at sea. The head of the national electricity company, Mohammad Allahdad, told state television that distribution restrictions would start on Sunday because of fuel shortages and damage to energy infrastructure, with outages expected to last about two hours every two days, or up to two hours a day depending on the area. President Masoud Pezeshkian warned that strikes on gas infrastructure had made the coming winter harder. At the same time, Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran would take any action necessary to lift what it calls an American economic blockade — described by Iran as an act of war — and reports said 14 Iranian oil tankers were being prepared to challenge it. Gharibabadi also confirmed a Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on Friday night. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said reopening the Strait of Hormuz was not difficult in itself, but required the United States to accept what Tehran calls reasonable conditions, and he insisted Iran’s enriched uranium stockpiles would not leave the country.

Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — Third Night of Alerts21 November 2026

Saudi-Led Coalition Says It Downed Three Houthi Missiles Headed for Riyadh

Riyadh spent another night under emergency alerts. The Saudi-led coalition said early on Saturday it intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles fired by Yemen’s Houthis toward the capital, after residents heard explosions, and later said it destroyed a drone headed for the kingdom’s south. It was the third day in a row that authorities issued and then lifted warnings for Riyadh, with civil defence sending eight public alerts in a single 24-hour period starting Friday morning. The barrage came two days after a Houthi strike damaged the Public Investment Fund tower — the headquarters of Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund and the city’s tallest skyscraper — blowing out windows across several floors but causing no injuries. The Houthis have threatened further attacks on Jeddah and other Red Sea ports, warned airlines that Saudi airspace is unsafe, and named Riyadh’s financial district and a power station in Al-Shuqaiq as targets. Pakistan’s foreign minister condemned the attack on the financial district, and the World Health Organization has put the toll from the wider escalation since August at 2,260 dead and 10,519 wounded as of 16 November.

Latest · Americas · Guasave, Mexico — A Most-Wanted Capture21 November 2026

Mexico Captures “El Chapo Isidro,” a Cartel Boss on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted List, in a Raid That Left 11 Dead

Mexican special forces have captured Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” in a night operation in Guasave, in the northwestern state of Sinaloa. Mexico’s defence ministry said troops using stealth and surprise surrounded a house and came under fire and explosives before arresting Meza-Flores along with his alleged second-in-command, known as “Serafin,” and a financial operator; the three were flown to Mexico City. The fighting was heavy: the ministry said four soldiers and seven gunmen were killed, and other accounts described flaming roadblocks across the area and fans fleeing a nearby baseball game. Meza-Flores, 44, is not related to Joaquin “El Chapo” Guzman despite the shared nickname. Authorities say he leads a Beltran Leyva-linked organisation allied with a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel, faces organised-crime and drug charges in Mexico and an extradition order to the United States, where the FBI had listed him among its ten most wanted fugitives with a $5 million reward, accusing his group of moving fentanyl, cocaine, heroin and other drugs into the US over two decades.

Latest · Africa · Bayelsa, Nigeria — A Funeral Journey21 November 2026

At Least 24 Mourners Die When an Overcrowded Boat Capsizes on the Way to a Burial in Southern Nigeria

A boat carrying people to a funeral has capsized in Nigeria’s Bayelsa State, killing at least 24 people, with search and rescue work still under way. The vessel was travelling on Friday from Ayama toward Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it overturned at a river junction between the Oweikorogha and Otuan communities in the Southern Ijaw district. Police said preliminary reports pointed to a mechanical failure; the National Emergency Management Agency said 24 bodies had been recovered by Saturday. Another 24 people were rescued and needed medical help — 19 were treated at a local clinic and five were taken to hospital in the state capital. Two children were among the first four bodies recovered on Friday. Police said they were coordinating with local divers and other responders, and later reporting said the boat’s operator had fled while its manager was taken into custody. Authorities have not said how many people were on board in total, describing the boat as overcrowded and believed to have been carrying hundreds.

Latest · Americas · Tupancireta, Brazil — “We Lost Everything”21 November 2026

A Supercell Storm Tears Through a Brazilian Town in 40 Minutes, Killing Four and Damaging Thousands of Homes

Residents of Tupancireta, an agricultural town of about 20,000 people in Brazil’s southern state of Rio Grande do Sul, spent Saturday picking through rubble and patching roofs after a violent storm killed four people and injured 162. The storm hit before dawn on Friday and lasted less than an hour, with gusts reaching 114 kilometres per hour, according to the national meteorological institute Inmet. Civil defence classified it as a supercell — a rotating thunderstorm that can spawn tornadoes — and did not rule out that a tornado had formed. Officials gave differing damage counts as assessments continued, from about 5,000 homes damaged to some 8,000 buildings hit, along with schools and the local hospital, whose roof was destroyed and 11 patients had to be moved. Thousands of electricity poles came down, leaving the town without power or running water, and almost all residents were forced from their homes, though only about 30 used public shelters. Mayor Gustavo Herter said “the town is gone,” while bricklayer Ivair Prieto, standing in his ruined house, said: “We lost everything. But nobody was hurt, and life goes on.” Severe weather has battered around 30 municipalities in the state since 16 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · Mercury — Arrival After Eight Years21 November 2026

BepiColombo Reaches Mercury: Two European and Japanese Probes Enter Orbit Around the Innermost Planet

After eight years travelling through the inner Solar System, the joint European-Japanese BepiColombo mission has arrived at Mercury. The European Space Agency announced on 21 November that the stacked spacecraft had been captured into orbit, beginning months of manoeuvres — only the second mission ever to orbit Mercury, after NASA’s MESSENGER in 2011–2015. BepiColombo launched in October 2018 and completed nine planetary flybys — one of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury — to slow itself enough to be caught by the small planet’s gravity, on a route worked out from the mathematics of Giuseppe “Bepi” Colombo. Its transfer module separated on 3 September. For now the two science orbiters remain joined in an initial elliptical orbit of about 590 by 11,640 kilometres. On 9–10 December, ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter and JAXA’s Mio will separate: Mio will swing out to study Mercury’s magnetic field and its interaction with the solar wind, while the ESA orbiter descends closer to map the surface. Science operations are due to begin in April 2027.

Latest · Europe · Paris — Sixth Weekend in a Row21 November 2026

France Marches for a Sixth Weekend Over Living Costs and Public Services, as Turnout Falls but Anger Holds

Tens of thousands of people marched across France on Saturday for the sixth weekend in a row, demanding better public services and protesting the rising cost of living and inequality. The Interior Ministry counted 82,500 demonstrators nationwide, including 21,500 in Paris — down from 106,000 the previous Saturday — while organisers put the figure at 150,000, down from 200,000 a week earlier and 800,000 at the first rally on 17 October. The movement began in mid-September with high school students demanding better learning conditions, then widened to take in soaring prices, strained services and allegations of police brutality, drawing in unions and Yellow Vest activists. In Paris, thousands gathered at Place de la Republique in a festive mood, with musicians, food trucks and homemade signs, before marching east; rallies also took place from Lille to Marseille and Lyon. The protests were largely peaceful, though clashes on the sidelines led to 144 arrests by early evening, and an AFP count based on testimony, lawyers and NGOs says at least 31 people have been seriously injured by security forces since the movement began. Unions and activists have called further protests in the coming weeks.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Ulsan, South Korea — “Speed Is Vital”21 November 2026

South Korea’s President Tells the Country Speed Will Decide the AI Race, on a Tour of a $5.2 Billion Data Centre

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung used a visit to a vast data centre rising in the southeastern city of Ulsan to make a blunt argument: in artificial intelligence, speed is decisive. “The whole world is competing right now, and speed is vital,” Lee said in a public video of Saturday’s tour. The Ulsan project is being built by SK Group, the country’s second-largest conglomerate, jointly with Amazon Web Services, at an estimated cost of 7 trillion won — about $5.2 billion — through 2029, with operations scheduled to begin in the second half of 2027. Data centres are a major driver of demand for semiconductors, and Lee said he hoped the project’s success would draw further investment from global AI companies. He also framed Ulsan as proof that AI investment need not concentrate around Seoul, pointing to regional growth, and signalled the government would look at labour shortages created by the simultaneous build-out of chip factories and data centres across the country.
