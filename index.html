Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Voting Day3 November 2026

Voting Under Way for Control of Congress: 57 Million Voted Early as Democrats Defend a Clear Polling Lead Into Election Day

Polls are open across the United States for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships, in the vote that decides who controls Congress for the last two years of Donald Trump’s presidency. Turnout was already historic before Tuesday began: about 57 million early and mail ballots had been cast, well beyond the roughly 45.9 million early votes recorded across the whole of the 2022 midterms. Democrats start the day ahead in every final national average — RealClearPolling closed at 52.0% to 43.1% on the generic ballot, NBC News at 52% to 45% and Reuters/Ipsos at 53% to 44% — and forecasters give them an 84% to 95% chance in the House, where they need a net gain of about three seats, against a tighter 64% to 76% in the Senate, where they need four. The New York Times/Siena battleground polling put Democrats ahead in six of seven key Senate states and tied in the seventh, Iowa, with only 38% of likely voters in those states approving of Trump’s performance. No results are due until the first polls close at 6pm Eastern; the last, in Alaska, shut at 1am Eastern.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Strike and Oil3 November 2026

Explosions on Qeshm, a Deal “Could Happen”: US Reported to Have Struck Iran’s Hormuz Island as Oil Falls More Than 7%

The United States struck Iran’s Qeshm Island on Monday, the Wall Street Journal reported, in what officials described as the latest of a series of small, unannounced operations Washington has run for weeks to stop Iran attacking shipping in the Strait of Hormuz — a waterway largely blocked since the war began in late February. Iran’s state agency IRNA reported five explosions in quick succession on Qeshm, the strait’s largest island, with residents seeing two aircraft overhead, and further blasts were reported at Bandar Lengeh; Tehran said its forces had downed three US drones near the strait. The Pentagon referred questions to Central Command, which did not comment. The military pressure ran alongside an open diplomatic signal: President Trump posted that “Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible. It could happen!” and pointed to oil’s fall of nearly $10 a barrel. Crude duly plunged more than 7% on Monday as talks between Iranian and Qatari officials lifted hopes of an end to the eight-month conflict, then steadied in Asia on Tuesday, with Brent around $104.72, while Iran’s foreign minister described intensive shuttle diplomacy through Qatari and Pakistani mediators.

Nearly 500 Drones Downed Over Russia, 25 Wounded at a Kharkiv Hospital: The Truce Is Over and the Air War Is Back at Full Scale

The fighting that paused for six days in Kherson has returned everywhere at once. Moscow’s mayor Sergei Sobyanin said Russian air defences destroyed nearly 500 drones heading for the capital on Tuesday, while Ukrainian authorities reported a fresh string of overnight Russian strikes — including one that wounded 25 people at a medical facility in Kharkiv and forced patients to evacuate, with damage to buildings, windows and cars — and ballistic-missile warnings for Kharkiv, Kyiv, Dnipro and Sumy, as air defences were also triggered in neighbouring Poland. The escalation lands a week before US-mediated talks resume in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, and days after Monday’s bombing of the Komyshuvakha aid centre killed 26 people, among them two rescue workers and four police officers.

Latest · Americas · Florida Crash — The Investigation3 November 2026

It May Have Broken Up in the Air: Investigators Hunt for the Black Box of the Learjet That Killed Nine in Palm Beach County

Federal investigators say the Bahamas-bound Learjet 60 that crashed into a South Florida neighbourhood on Sunday may have started breaking apart before it hit the ground, scattering debris across a wide area — and they do not yet know whether its cockpit voice recorder was even on board. The jet left Austin, Texas, for North Eleuthera in the Bahamas and came down at about 12:50pm Eastern on 1 November near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in Palm Beach County, less than ten miles from Mar-a-Lago, killing both crew members and all seven passengers and injuring one man on the ground in the leg. Pilots had reported icing, turbulence and precipitation shortly before the steep descent, and heavy rain and thunderstorms were in the area. NTSB member Thomas Chapman said records show a recorder was installed at some point, and investigators are “very hopeful” of recovering it; the medical examiner has not yet formally identified the victims, though families have named several, including 20-year-old co-pilot Nathalia Filgueiras and a family of four. The crash is the deadliest involving a Learjet 60 on record and the second-deadliest US aviation incident this year.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Nepal — Everest Collision3 November 2026

Rotors Clipped on a Snowy Helipad at 4,200 Metres: Two Helicopters Collide Below Everest Base Camp, Killing an Indian Tourist

Two helicopters collided as they tried to land at the Pheriche helipad in Nepal’s Solukhumbu district on Tuesday morning, killing a 47-year-old Indian tourist and injuring a New Zealand passenger and crew. Police put the collision at about 8:11am local time in Ward 4 of Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality, at roughly 4,200 metres on the trail to Everest Base Camp, about 150 kilometres northeast of Kathmandu. The dead man, Vasanth Kumar Balaramaramadu, was aboard a Fishtail Air helicopter that was carrying passengers rescued from the Everest region; Shailesh Krishna Patel, a New Zealand citizen of Indian origin on the same aircraft, was injured and rescued. The second aircraft, operated by Dynasty Air, was carrying only cargo, the company said. Video circulating online appears to show the two aircraft’s rotors striking each other moments before both came down on the icy ground, though the footage has not been independently verified. The crash comes in peak trekking season, when helicopters ferry climbers and hikers throughout a country that holds eight of the world’s fourteen highest peaks.

Five Dead on Monday, 1,593 Since the “Ceasefire” Began: Gaza’s Toll Keeps Rising as Winter Shelter Becomes the Next Emergency

Israeli attacks killed four Palestinians across Gaza on Monday and a fifth man died of wounds from Sunday’s strike on a police post in al-Mawasi, medical sources and the Wafa news agency said: two killed in Khan Younis in the south, one in a drone attack on al-Musaddar in the centre, and Hani Hamed Kahil killed, with five others wounded, when a vehicle was hit in Gaza City’s Tel al-Hawa neighbourhood. Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — After Landfall3 November 2026

The Morning After Murasu: Fallen Trees, Flooded Streets and a Parched District Whose Rain Deficit Shrank in One Night

Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu finished crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast near Ongole in the early hours of Tuesday, the India Meteorological Department placing the crossing between Nellore and Narsapur at 3:30 to 5:30am, with winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph after the landfall process began around 1am near Kothapatnam. Daylight revealed the cost across Bapatla and Prakasam districts: thousands of trees uprooted, hundreds of electric poles damaged, fishing boats wrecked, thatched houses collapsed in Ongole’s Samatha Nagar, crops flattened and roads under water with vehicles stranded in near-zero visibility. Some stations recorded about 200 mm of rain in six hours; in normally dry Prakasam the season’s rainfall deficit fell from over 64% to 58.6% overnight. Nineteen flights were cancelled and live-report estimates put total evacuations above 200,000 people, including some 400 families from a single coastal village. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu, who reviewed the storm at 3:30am, ordered roads kept closed until fallen wires and debris are cleared — a precaution shaped by Cyclone Arnab a week earlier, when at least five people died touching live wires. Heavy rain continues across ten districts under red warnings today, and fishermen are told to stay ashore until Wednesday morning.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Day Four3 November 2026

Tear Gas on Day Four: Garment Workers Hold Out for 30,000 Taka as Nearly 700 Factories Stay Shut in Peak Order Season

Bangladesh’s garment unrest entered a fourth day on Tuesday, with police firing tear gas to disperse workers who again tried to block highways across the industrial belts around Dhaka — Ashulia, Gazipur, Savar and the capital’s Mirpur area — and several factories suspending operations or declaring holidays all over again. The wave began on Saturday over a single number: workers want the minimum monthly wage raised from 12,500 taka, set in December 2023, to 30,000 taka, about $245, arguing inflation has eaten their pay. On Monday the factories had briefly reopened after Sunday’s shutdown, then shut again as clashes resumed; industry and wire counts put 670 to 679 factories halted, with 20 to 25 vandalised, a police vehicle set on fire and at least 29 protesters arrested. The government’s response remains the six-member wage board formed on Sunday under Senior District and Sessions Judge Sheikh Ashfaqur Rahman, plus an order from Prime Minister Tarique Rahman to protect workers while keeping factories running — a formula that has not yet calmed the streets. The stakes are the export calendar: the sector employs about 4 million people, earns over 80% of export income, supplies H&M, Zara, Walmart and Primark, and books roughly 40% of annual orders between November and February.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Korea — The Leak3 November 2026

The President’s Passport Number Sat Online for Five Months: Seoul Holds an Emergency Meeting Over a Leak of 168 Travellers’ Details

South Korea’s presidential office convened an emergency meeting on Tuesday after it emerged that a file listing the personal details of President Lee Jae Myung’s entire overseas-travel entourage — 168 people — had been sitting on a publicly accessible website since June. The file, titled as a passenger list for the Korea–Belgium leg of Lee’s June trip to Belgium, Italy and France, reportedly contains Korean and English names, affiliations and titles, plus dates of birth and passport numbers, issue and expiry dates, for the president, First Lady Kim Hye-kyung, then policy chief Kim Yong-beom, National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac, ministers, presidential security personnel and accompanying journalists. Chief of staff Lim Kwang-hyun called the leak a grave matter that cannot be taken lightly and ordered a full investigation into how it happened, how far it spread, whether anyone was harmed and who was responsible for managing the data. The foreign ministry said it is inspecting the computers and email accounts linked to the trip, has had the file removed, and is arranging replacement passports for everyone affected, stressing the incident is being treated as a data leak rather than a hack. Because the file was online for five months, officials cannot yet say who downloaded it.

Latest · Europe · Cyberattack — The Freezers3 November 2026

Freezers Set to 50°C at 2am: A Second Refrigeration Cyberattack Empties Supermarket Shelves From Sweden to Portugal

Hackers remotely seized supermarket refrigeration controls across at least eight European countries overnight into Monday, pushing fridges and freezers as high as 50°C and forcing stores to throw away spoiled food — the second such wave in three weeks, after an October attack hit hundreds of French stores. Belgium was hardest hit: Delhaize reported an “IT incident” at several dozen stores in Belgium and a few in Luxembourg, Carrefour confirmed a cyberattack at 15 of its 700-plus Belgian stores, and contractor Sabcobel said attackers had switched units into defrost mode, with about 10% of its installations still unsecured after the French incident. In Sweden, Coop confirmed a cyber incident at six of its roughly 800 stores and ICA reported problems at a limited number; an ICA store in Sjöbo said its units had been set to 50°C. Single or small numbers of stores were hit in Estonia, Finland, Ireland — 13 SuperValu stores — the Netherlands and Portugal, where seven Intermarché stores were affected. Belgian federal prosecutors have opened an investigation, the EU’s food commissioner called it a deliberate attempt to disrupt the bloc’s food chain, and investigators keep returning to one link: many of the compromised units run internet-connected controls from Danish maker Danfoss, the same supplier involved in France. No group has claimed responsibility for either attack.

Latest · Global Affairs · Shipping — Struck off Oman3 November 2026

One Seafarer Dead, 13 Rescued: An Unidentified Aerial Object Hits an Indian-Flagged Cargo Vessel Near Limah, Oman

An Indian-flagged cargo vessel carrying 14 Indian seafarers was struck by an unidentified aerial object near Limah, off the Omani coast, on Monday evening, killing one crew member, New Delhi confirmed on Tuesday. Thirteen sailors evacuated safely and were picked up by the Omani Coast Guard; the man who died was named by Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal as Gandhar Nahim Ajij, from Gujarat, who succumbed to his injuries. India’s external affairs ministry condemned the attack on the MSV Al Jusufi, said its embassy in Oman is working with local authorities to assist the survivors and repatriate both the crew and Ajij’s remains, and called for de-escalation and a diplomatic settlement in West Asia. Who fired, and with what, is not yet known. The strike is part of a pattern that has made the waters around the Strait of Hormuz among the most dangerous in commercial shipping: more than 120 attacks on merchant vessels have been reported since the US–Israel–Iran conflict began in February, and New Delhi says at least 22 Indians have now been killed in attacks on ships and tankers in Gulf waters.

Latest · Africa · Sudan–Ethiopia — Ambassador Recalled3 November 2026

Khartoum Pulls Its Ambassador and Names Two Countries: Sudan Accuses Ethiopia and the UAE Over the Airport Drone Attack

Sudan recalled its ambassador from Addis Ababa on Tuesday and formally accused Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates of responsibility for drone attacks on Sudan, including the strike on Khartoum International Airport that suspended operations for three days — just after the airport had received its first flight since the war began in April 2023. Khartoum says it has evidence that four attacks were launched from Ethiopian territory with UAE-supplied drones, and warns the aggression will not be met with silence. Ethiopia’s foreign ministry dismissed the charges as baseless and counter-accused Sudan of funding Tigray-linked rebels. The recall follows formal complaints to the UN Security Council and the African Union backed by a Transitional Sovereignty Council report documenting 70 alleged attacks from Ethiopian soil between 15 February and 27 October — 187 one-way attack drones and 82 drone-dropped munitions — which it says killed or wounded 484 civilians and killed 176 soldiers, and a fresh week in which about 14 power stations in eight states were reported destroyed, including strikes near the Merowe dam and in Kassala. The UN describes Sudan’s war — more than 150,000 estimated dead, about 14 million displaced — as the world’s worst humanitarian crisis.
