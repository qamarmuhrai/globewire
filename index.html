Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Summit Opens in Manila15 November 2026

Marcos Opens the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila With Energy, Trade and the Sea Code on the Table

The summit is now under way. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. formally opened the 49th ASEAN Summit on Sunday morning at the Philippine International Convention Center in Pasay City, leading the traditional handshake and family photo with leaders and representatives of the bloc’s 11 member states before chairing the plenary and retreat sessions. In his opening remarks Marcos pointed to more than 100 outcome documents spanning peace and security, the economy and people’s welfare, and argued that ASEAN’s institutions must work better across those pillars as the Middle East energy shock and rapid change from artificial intelligence reshape the region. The hard items have not moved: Myanmar remains represented below leader level as leaders review the stalled Five-Point Consensus, transboundary haze is back on the agenda, and a binding South China Sea code of conduct is being discussed with no final deal expected this week. Dialogue partners fill the next two days — separate meetings with Australia, India, Japan, South Korea and the United States, an East Asia Summit on Tuesday, and a closing ceremony at which the Philippines hands the rotating chairmanship to Singapore.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Recovery Timeline15 November 2026

US Flight Chaos Runs Into Sunday: More Than 6,100 Saturday Cancellations, Normal Schedules Only by Late Sunday

Sunday will not start clean. FlightAware counts cited by officials and outlets put Saturday’s damage at more than 6,150 cancellations and over 7,300 delays by early evening US time, after a Friday that the FAA itself puts at roughly 12,000 cancelled flights — about a quarter of the day’s schedule — and other tallies put near 9,200, with more than 530 of Sunday’s flights already cancelled by Saturday night. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford apologised to passengers, said the system is stable, and set expectations plainly: schedules are unlikely to be normal until late Sunday, with a full resumption hoped for by close of business. The cause, as the administration now tells it, sits in an upgrade job: contractor L3Harris was replacing ageing copper communications lines with fibre at a remote facility when a newly installed router overloaded the wider network, knocking out communications and radar feeds and forcing Friday’s nationwide ground stop of nearly five hours — the longest since the September 11 attacks and the first nationwide halt since January 2023. Officials rule out a cyberattack. For travellers the practical advice is unchanged: expect knock-on delays all day Sunday even where the weather is fine, because aircraft and crews are still out of place.

Zelensky Says US Floated Donbas Withdrawal for NATO Membership as Macron Warns Rationing Aid Means an Unjust Peace

A new element of the talks is now public, and Europe is arguing about what it means. President Volodymyr Zelensky said in remarks released Saturday that US negotiators for the first time asked whether Ukraine would withdraw from the eastern Donbas in exchange for joining NATO — an idea Kyiv rejects, along with Moscow’s demand for a wider European security settlement before any deal on ending the war itself, and a proposed local humanitarian ceasefire limited to Kostiantynivka when Ukraine has named seven or eight other trapped settlements. US envoy Jared Kushner, meanwhile, said Washington’s focus is now on getting Moscow and European capitals talking directly and preparing the next trilateral round, after the Abu Dhabi sessions ended without a breakthrough beyond agreement in principle on a prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people. Europe’s five designated representatives — Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — are expected at the next table.

Trump Leans Toward Holding Off on Iran Strikes as Allies Urge Restraint and Mines Hit Three Ships in Hormuz in Two Days

Restraint is being argued in Washington while the strait keeps producing incidents. Three people familiar with recent discussions say President Donald Trump has told senior advisers he may hold off on further attacks on Iran if neither Tehran nor the Houthis stage a new provocation — a leaning, not a decision, after intelligence briefings assessed that the naval blockade and sanctions are already squeezing Iran’s economy and that fresh strikes would be unlikely to move its negotiating demands. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan have both urged him in recent calls not to resume the war, and Trump said publicly this week he could end it immediately but is taking a longer, more civil route at the request of the nations he is protecting, while also promising to strike without hesitation if US allies or vessels are threatened. In the Strait of Hormuz itself, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards say three vessels in two days — including a supertanker and a liquefied-gas carrier filmed with smoke rising — struck naval mines after switching off tracking and using unauthorised routes; the claims are unverified and no casualty figures have been given. At the United Nations, the head of the UN Office for Project Services urged both sides to let fertiliser shipments through immediately, warning the blockage is hurting vulnerable countries far from the strait.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Winter Aid Argument Deepens15 November 2026

Israel Calls the EU Statement on Gaza Completely Unbalanced as Singapore Expands Its Winter Aid Partnerships

The argument over Gaza’s winter has sharpened into a direct diplomatic clash. Israel on Sunday rejected the European Council’s Saturday statement — which voiced grave concern after heavy rain and flooding, and called for every crossing to reopen with immediate, unimpeded aid at scale — as completely unbalanced, restating the IDF position that more than 93,000 tents and over 8,600 truckloads of winter supplies have entered since the season began, that medical deliveries including dialysis fluids, incubators and defibrillators continue, and that Hamas, not access approvals, is the main obstacle to rehabilitation. The UN humanitarian team’s counter-figure remains stark: more than 150,000 tents sit outside Gaza awaiting dispatch, held up by layered approvals, limited crossing capacity and narrow delivery windows. Away from the statements, practical aid is being organised: Singapore, which says it has contributed over S$25 million since 2023, is deepening work through the Egyptian Red Crescent and four local charities, with a new fundraising campaign targeting at least S$1.5 million for community kitchens, sanitation and winter shelter, hot meals and food parcels for 7,800 people through February, and winterised blankets for displaced patients in Egypt — while noting that of an agreed 600 aid trucks a day, NGOs report only about 200 actually crossing.

The midpoint of COP31 arrived with noise outside the hall and a warning inside it. Latest · Asia-Pacific · Trade — Canada Deals in Manila14 November 2026

Canada Closes Two Trade Deals in Manila: A Philippines Pact and a Substantially Finished ASEAN Agreement, Both Aimed at 2027

Canada used its guest-of-the-chair seat in Manila to sign off on trade, not just attend. Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. announced on Saturday that negotiations for a Canada–Philippines free trade agreement have concluded, just a year after talks were launched, with signature targeted for 2027. It would be the Philippines’ first bilateral trade deal with a North American country, covering goods, services, investment, digital trade and small businesses, eliminating tariffs on more than 90 percent of tariff lines on the Canadian account, and with ambitions to triple bilateral trade to more than $10 billion a year by 2035. Alongside it, Carney announced the substantial conclusion of the broader ASEAN–Canada free trade agreement, opening a market of more than 700 million people and a combined economy near $6 trillion, with $5 million pledged to help every ASEAN economy ratify and benefit, and a further $26 million in Canada–Philippines investments. Ottawa is explicit about the strategy: diversify partnerships and reduce reliance on a single trading partner, starting in one of the world’s fastest-growing regions.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Hasina Extradition14 November 2026

Bangladesh Says Its Legal Work for Hasina’s Return Is Complete and the Next Move Is India’s, as Delhi Insists on the Legal Route

The extradition file is now, by Dhaka’s account, sitting entirely in New Delhi. Law Minister Md Asaduzzaman said on Saturday that Bangladesh has completed everything required on its side to secure the return of ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina under the bilateral treaty, and that what India does next is up to India. Hasina, 79, fled to India aboard a Bangladesh military aircraft on 5 August 2024 as a student-led uprising toppled her government, and was convicted in absentia in November 2025 of crimes against humanity over the July–August 2024 crackdown, receiving a death sentence on one charge and life imprisonment on another; she rejects the proceedings and has said she intends to return in December. Dhaka has formally requested extradition twice, in December 2024 and again after sentencing. A BBC Bangla report this week said India has explored a return on two conditions — that the Bangladesh Army take her back, by aircraft or at a border point, mirroring how she arrived, and that she be guaranteed an appeal and a fresh trial — neither confirmed by either capital. India’s foreign ministry spokesman Randhir Jaiswal declined to address the reported conditions on Friday, saying only that extradition is a legal matter and will be dealt with accordingly; Asaduzzaman, for his part, said a person convicted of crimes against humanity cannot claim fundamental rights under the 1973 law, and that no appeal opportunity exists under it.

South Sudan Opens Voter Registration for an Election Just Moved to February, With Equipment Still Not at the Centres

Registration has begun for an election whose date changed the same morning. South Sudan’s National Elections Commission confirmed on Saturday that its first national vote since independence in 2011 moves from 22 December to Thursday 11 February 2027 — a 51-day shift given without a stated reason — while voter registration runs as scheduled from 14 to 28 November across 3,161 centres in the 10 states and three administrative areas. The commission says 22,127 officials have been recruited, but also that electoral materials and equipment have yet to reach individual registration centres, a gap that sits alongside the deeper constraints: only $21 million received of the $276 million requested, former vice-president Riek Machar under house arrest since March 2025, renewed clashes between his supporters and forces loyal to President Salva Kiir, and a caretaker administration in place since Kiir dissolved the transitional government in September. The revised calendar sets candidate nominations for 14–20 December, a 45-day campaign from 27 December to 9 February, a silence day on 10 February, and final results on 15 February — just a week before the transitional period itself expires on 22 February, leaving almost no room for the disputes process the same calendar allows to run into July.

Latest · Global Affairs · Space — Voyager Milestone18 November 2026

On Wednesday Voyager 1 Becomes the First Object One Light-Day From Earth, and NASA Is Asking Everyone to Shine a Light Back

Forty-nine years after launch, the farthest machine is about to pass a distance that can be said in one phrase. On Wednesday 18 November, at 05:16 Eastern time, Voyager 1 will be one light-day from Earth — 16.094 billion miles, or 25.9 billion kilometres, the distance light itself needs 24 hours to cross. Nothing on board will change at that moment; the milestone is symbolic, but its practical meaning is real for the small Jet Propulsion Laboratory team still flying the probe: every command now takes a full day to arrive and every answer a full day to return, so a single exchange costs 48 hours, from a transmitter of about 23 watts whose signal arrives carried by only hundreds of photons per bit. The spacecraft, launched on 5 September 1977 for a five-year tour of Jupiter and Saturn, crossed into interstellar space in 2012 and now runs on just two science instruments after power-saving shutdowns in 2025 and April this year. NASA is marking the week publicly: after sunset on Tuesday 17 November, its Shine Bright event invites people worldwide to flash the Morse signal for V-G-R skyward in a wave of light following the sunset line, and on 19 November JPL hosts a public discussion on keeping both Voyagers alive at the edge of the possible. Voyager 2 is not expected to reach the same distance until 2035.

The biggest test yet of the AI boom reaches the market this week. Pricing is set for Wednesday 18 November, with trading due to begin on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday 19 November, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley among more than 20 banks.
