Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — Beijing Confirms the Trilateral16 November 2026

Beijing Makes It Official: Xi, Trump and Putin Meet Thursday in Shenzhen — the First Three-Way Summit of the Nuclear Powers

China’s foreign ministry confirmed on Monday what Washington and Moscow had already announced: Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin will hold their first-ever trilateral meeting on Thursday, 19 November, in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ summit. It is the first time the leaders of the world’s three foremost nuclear powers have sat down in a three-way format. Trump wants the conversation to open on reducing nuclear arsenals — a live question since the last US–Russian arms pact, New START, expired in February, leaving the two largest stockpiles uncapped for the first time in more than half a century — and he also intends to raise the war with Iran. A senior US official has been blunt that no agreement is expected and that Ukraine is not on the trilateral agenda, describing the session as the start of a dialogue rather than a negotiation. The idea reportedly originated with Putin, who proposed it to Trump last month; analysts read the gathering as heavy on symbolism — a showcase for Shenzhen and for China’s first hosting of a major global summit in a decade — with Beijing historically reluctant to cap an arsenal far smaller than Washington’s or Moscow’s. Trump will also hold a bilateral working lunch with Xi, but has no separate one-on-one scheduled with Putin.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Chaos Enters Day Three16 November 2026

17,000 Flights Cancelled and Counting: One Router, a Copper-to-Fibre Swap, and America’s Longest Ground Stop Since 9/11

The disruption from Friday’s nationwide ground stop stretched into a third day on Sunday, with cumulative US cancellations passing 17,000. Airlines scrapped more than 6,600 flights on Saturday and nearly 1,900 more by mid-afternoon Sunday, with over 6,300 delayed, as crews and aircraft remained out of position ahead of the busy Thanksgiving travel week. Investigators are now focused on a single piece of hardware: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says contractor L3Harris was replacing ageing copper telecom lines with fibre near El Dorado, Arkansas, when a newly installed router began flooding the air-traffic control network with connection attempts, destabilising 17 of the FAA’s 22 en-route control centres. The FAA ordered the ground stop at about 3:30 p.m. New York time on Friday and lifted it at 8:15 p.m. — the longest nationwide halt since the September 11 attacks. The suspect router is being tested at an L3Harris facility with FAA and FBI personnel on site; officials say they have found no evidence of a cyberattack, and it remains unclear whether the failure was a faulty unit or an installation error. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford apologised for the shutdown and said airlines expected to return to normal schedules by the end of Sunday, while warning that the ageing system — roughly 30 percent of its copper wiring still awaits replacement, with full conversion targeted for September 2027 — will keep breaking until it is replaced.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Zelenskyy Blames Starshield Limits15 November 2026

“Our Interceptors Lose Contact at Speed”: Zelenskyy Says Satellite Restrictions Are Letting Russian Drones Through to the Power Grid

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy used Sunday to press a pointed complaint against an American supplier: speed restrictions on SpaceX’s Starshield satellite service, he said, are breaking the link between Ukrainian interceptor drones and their operators once the interceptors pass roughly 160 km/h, letting jet-powered Russian Shahed drones reach energy and railway infrastructure in eight regions overnight. In the Black Sea port of Chornomorsk, a strike on a facility run by grid operator DTEK injured six people, damaged a gas pipeline and nearby homes, set two parked cars ablaze and temporarily cut power to about 41,000 customers. Zaporizhzhia reported six more injured, including a two-year-old boy. Zelenskyy put the week’s incoming fire at more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 guided bombs and 15 missiles, and warned the satellite limits could weaken defences around the grid just as winter — the season Moscow has repeatedly targeted power infrastructure — approaches. Kyiv struck back over the same period: Ukraine says it launched nearly 1,200 drones at Russian targets in one of its largest aerial attacks of the war, hitting an oil refinery at Volgograd, while Ukrainian drone strikes on two substations in Russia’s Rostov region killed a chief engineer, according to the regional governor. Moscow claimed its air defences downed 1,198 Ukrainian drones in a single night.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Sirens Over Bahrain Again15 November 2026

Six Drones at a US Radar Site: Iran Strikes Bahrain for the Second Time in Three Days, Ending a Two-Month Lull

Air-raid sirens sounded over Bahrain before dawn on Sunday for the first time since September, and residents reported two waves of explosions, around 4 a.m. and again at 6:30 a.m. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said their aerospace force had sent six drones against the US air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, framing the attack as retaliation for hostile American actions and repeating that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed and any further US action will draw a harsher response. Bahrain’s military said its air defences intercepted several Iranian attacks before they reached their targets, and the foreign ministry condemned the strikes; the UAE also condemned the attack. It was Tehran’s second strike on the kingdom in three days — on Friday, US Central Command said it shot down two Iranian drones bound for Bahrain, with no casualties or damage — and it ended a lull of roughly two months while peace talks with Washington remain stalled. The same weekend brought fire across the Gulf: Saudi Arabia said it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh, and US intelligence assessments cited by the Wall Street Journal estimate Iran still holds between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles despite months of US and Israeli strikes on its weapons infrastructure — a figure that sits behind President Trump’s public claim that Iran’s arsenal was destroyed.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Rafah Brigade Loses Another Commander15 November 2026

Third Commander in Eighteen Months: Israel Kills Hamas’s Rafah Brigade Chief in a Street Strike in Khan Younis

Israel killed the commander of Hamas’s Rafah Brigade in an overnight drone strike in the al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis, both the Israeli military and Hamas confirmed on Sunday — the third man to hold the post to be killed in eighteen months. Samir Abu Younis, 53, a brigade member since 1991 whom Hamas described as a prominent planner of the 7 October 2023 attack, had taken command only in September, after his predecessor Nael Abu Obeid was killed; Abu Obeid had himself replaced Muhammad Shabana, killed in May 2025. The IDF and Shin Bet said Abu Younis directed attacks on Israeli civilians and troops and worked to rebuild the brigade’s capabilities, and that precision munitions and aerial surveillance were used to limit civilian harm; the strike, on a tent area sheltering displaced people, wounded two others. The killing came days after an Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis — aimed, the IDF said, at the Khan Younis Brigade commander — killed at least six people including a doctor and a 13-year-old awaiting surgery. Gaza’s health ministry says Israeli attacks killed 16 people in the past 48 hours and that at least 1,643 Palestinians have been killed since the US-brokered ceasefire took effect on 11 October 2025, with both sides accusing the other of violating it.

Modi Lands in Manila, Trump Arrives Tuesday, and ASEAN Extends Its Ban on Myanmar’s Junta Leaders

The 49th ASEAN Summit entered its second day in Manila on Monday with the guest list widening well beyond Southeast Asia. India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi is in the Philippine capital from Monday to Wednesday for the 23rd ASEAN–India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit, in a year the two sides have designated for maritime cooperation; Japan’s Sanae Takaichi, South Korea’s Lee Jae Myung and Australia’s Anthony Albanese — who is signing a new defence pact with the hosts — are also arriving, alongside China’s Premier Li Qiang, Canada’s Mark Carney and Brazil’s Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. The Philippines’ headline deliverable — a South China Sea code of conduct with China — is not expected to be finished this week.

Latest · Climate · COP31 — Ministers Take Over in Antalya16 November 2026

“Even Tripling Adaptation Finance Meets Less Than a Third of the Need”: Guterres Warns COP31 as Its Final Week Opens

The UN climate summit in Antalya has moved from technical drafting to ministerial decision-making, and the money argument is exactly where it was left. Secretary-General António Guterres, at his final COP press conference before leaving office at the end of the year, warned that climate disasters are erasing development gains and pushing vulnerable countries deeper into debt — and that even tripling adaptation finance would cover less than 30 percent of what is needed. The numbers frame the deadlock: developed countries are committed to mobilising at least $300 billion a year for developing nations by 2035 within a broader $1.3 trillion goal, but an assessment highlighted at the summit suggests annual flows could fall $840 billion short of that larger target on current trends. The EU, UK, Japan and Canada want the final texts tied more tightly to Global Stocktake outcomes; India, China and the Arab and African groups insist on nationally determined action backed by finance. Campaigners are demanding a time-bound plan to triple adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year, in grants rather than loans, plus new pledges to the Loss and Damage Fund. One concrete offer did land on Friday: development finance institutions from Britain, Norway and Canada announced plans to mobilise $10 billion for climate projects in emerging markets by 2030. The summit, run under a split Turkish–Australian presidency, closes on Friday, 20 November.

Latest · Europe · Estonia — Border With Russia to Close 1 December15 November 2026

Estonia Will Seal All Three Russian Border Crossings After a GRU-Ordered Arson Plot Against a Drone Maker

Estonia will close every border crossing point with Russia from 1 December, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna announced on Sunday, after security services foiled an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, a defence company that builds long-range attack and reconnaissance drones used by Ukraine. Eight people were detained overnight on Saturday at the company’s facility in Viimsi; the Internal Security Service, Kapo, said the attackers intended to cause an explosion and set the building on fire, and that the operation was ordered by Russia’s GRU military intelligence. No injuries or damage were reported. The closure covers the three road crossings at Narva in the north and Koidula and Luhamaa in the south, which handle pedestrians and freight; the government has not said how long it will last. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said the step was needed to reduce the risk of further attacks and to send a clear message that attacks on Estonia’s defence industry are unacceptable. Tallinn links the plot to an earlier attack on Milrem Robotics, a maker of unmanned ground vehicles, also attributed to Russian intelligence. Moscow, which routinely denies sabotage allegations, had not commented on the accusation or the closure; its foreign ministry said it would verify the information.

Latest · Americas · Space — Starship Caught From Orbit14 November 2026

Caught by the Chopsticks: SpaceX Brings a Starship Down From Orbit Into the Arms of Its Own Launch Tower

SpaceX has done the thing its whole reusability bet rests on: on Saturday, a Starship spacecraft returning from orbit was caught in mid-descent by the two giant mechanical arms — the “chopsticks” — of its launch tower at Starbase, Texas, the first time an orbital upper stage has been recovered this way. The ship, on the system’s 15th test flight, had spent about 22 hours in space, completed 14 orbits and deployed 26 Starlink satellites before surviving re-entry and descending slowly over the Gulf coast site into the tower’s arms above Launch Pad 2, to cheers from crowds on the nearby beach. Elon Musk posted video of the vehicle hanging in the arms, saying it “looks great.” The flight was not a complete success: the plan had been to catch both stages, but the Super Heavy booster’s boostback burn ended early — SpaceX has pointed to a low-fuel-mass warning — its guidance system diverted it automatically, and it was lost in the Gulf of Mexico. The root cause is still under investigation, and the caught ship’s condition and repair needs have not been detailed. Even so, catching the ship rather than splashing it down moves the mass and cost of recovery onto reusable ground equipment — the core economics behind SpaceX’s pitch for routine, cheaper orbital flight.

Latest · Americas · New Orleans — Steering Failure on the Mississippi15 November 2026

Two Minutes From Failure to Impact: NTSB Says a Stuck Rudder Sent a Tanker Into a Docked Riverboat With 190 People Aboard

Federal investigators have given the first official account of Friday’s New Orleans river collision, and it is a story of two minutes. The National Transportation Safety Board said on Sunday that voyage-data-recorder evidence shows the tanker Atlantic Asphalt suffered a steering failure at 12:26 p.m. near the Crescent City Connection bridge, leaving its rudder stuck fully to starboard; the ship veered toward the east bank and, at about 12:28 p.m., struck the American Heritage, a docked river cruise boat carrying 140 passengers and 50 crew at the Thalia Street Wharf. The riverboat capsized in roughly 22 minutes, throwing passengers, crew and two police officers into the Mississippi; 32 people were taken to hospital, none critically, and remarkably no one was killed. The tanker, bound upriver for an oil facility at Reserve, Louisiana, with 19 crew and a local pilot, is now the subject of parallel tracks: a 16-member NTSB team will spend about a week on scene, with a preliminary report due in roughly 30 days and final findings in 18 to 24 months, while the Port of New Orleans has sued the tanker’s owner and operator for at least $1 million in terminal damage, and a judge has issued a warrant giving federal officials control of the vessel. What caused the steering failure itself remains unknown.

Latest · Europe · Spain — A Record Day on the Atlantic Route15 November 2026

1,488 People, 14 Boats, One Day: The Canary Islands Record Their Busiest Arrivals in 32 Years of Counting

Spain’s maritime rescue service brought 1,488 people ashore in the Canary Islands on Saturday — the highest single-day total since records began 32 years ago, edging past the previous record of 1,461 set on 7 November 2020. Twelve of the 14 wooden boats, known as cayucos or pirogues, reached El Hierro, the archipelago’s smallest island, carrying 1,235 people between them, with the rest landing on Gran Canaria and Tenerife; the provisional count included 108 women and 167 minors, most of them from Senegal, Gambia and Mauritania, after crossings of four to seven days. The weekend total reached 2,331 people in 22 boats. Madrid had already declared a migration emergency for the islands on 10 November, with more than 9,000 arrivals since the start of the month, and Territorial Policy Minister Ángel Víctor Torres travelled to El Hierro on Sunday as the island’s president said repeated arrivals had overwhelmed local reception, health and assistance capacity. The surge is an autumn phenomenon layered on a quieter year: arrivals from January to October were down on 2025, but October alone was the route’s busiest month since 2023. The International Organization for Migration recorded at least 450 deaths on the West African Atlantic route in the first ten months of 2026, and the EU’s new migration and asylum pact, in force since June, has so far not slowed the departures.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan — Death Sentences in the Karak Case15 November 2026

Death by Hanging for the Funeral Shooting: Jordan’s Court Rules on the Attack That Killed Nine at a Village Church

Jordan’s State Security Court sentenced three men to death by hanging on Sunday for the 23 October attack on St George’s Greek Orthodox Church in Hammoud village, in the southern Karak governorate, where gunmen opened fire on mourners at a funeral, killing nine people and wounding 25. The defendants — two brothers and a relative by marriage, arrested on 26 October after a shootout at an abandoned house in Karak — were convicted of carrying out terrorist acts resulting in death, weapons and conspiracy offences, and promoting the ideology of a terrorist organisation; two further defendants received six-year prison terms for selling weapons to the attackers. Church bells rang in Hammoud as the sentences were announced, and churches across the kingdom have set aside Christmas festivities in mourning. The verdict came just 23 days after the shooting, a rare attack on Jordan’s Christian minority. The sentences are not final: they go automatically to review by the Court of Cassation, and the defendants may also appeal.
