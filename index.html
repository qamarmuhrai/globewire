Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Election Day3 November 2026

Election Day in America: Democrats Lead in Five Republican-Held Senate States, and the House Needs Only a Three-Seat Flip

America decides today who controls Congress for the last two years of Donald Trump’s second term. All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are on the ballot, and the final polling points to an opposition wave that has widened beyond the House. The New York Times/Siena surveys of 5,708 voters across seven battlegrounds, taken 23 October to 1 November, put Democrats ahead in six states and tied in the seventh: James Talarico leads Ken Paxton 50% to 43% in Texas, Chris Hamilton leads Senator Roger Marshall 49% to 44% in Kansas, Graham Platner leads Senator Susan Collins 51% to 45% in Maine, Sherrod Brown leads Jon Husted 49% to 44% in Ohio, and Democrats also lead in Alaska and in Michigan, where Abdul El-Sayed is ahead 53% to 42%. Nationally, the RealClearPolling average through 30 October gives Democrats 52% to 43.1% on the generic ballot, NBC News has it 52% to 45%, and Emerson 52% to 42%. Democrats need a net gain of three seats for the House and four for the Senate, where Republicans currently hold 53 seats. Only 38% of likely voters in the battlegrounds approve of Trump’s performance, and Democrats lead by nine points on the cost of living — the issue voters rank alongside the economy at the top.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — The Pardon Promise3 November 2026

“We’ll Have to Protect Them”: Trump Says He Would Pre-Emptively Pardon Musk and His Own Staff If Democrats Take Congress

On the eve of the vote, President Trump added a legal threat to the campaign. Asked on the New York Post’s Pod Force One podcast, released Monday, whether he would pre-emptively pardon Elon Musk and others around him if Democrats win the House and pursue investigations, he answered without hesitation: “I would if I needed to. Sure. I would not even think about it. I’d do it. I did it for J6.” He was referring to the roughly 1,500 people charged over the 6 January 2021 Capitol attack whom he pardoned on his first day back in office, and he pointed to Joe Biden’s own farewell pardons — for Anthony Fauci, Mark Milley, the January 6 committee and members of Biden’s family — as precedent: “That’s what Biden did. He gave everybody a pardon.” The exchange was prompted by talk of Democrats arresting Musk or seizing SpaceX assets, which Trump called talk — “It won’t happen” — while still promising protection. Democrats have been open about oversight plans: Representative Jamie Raskin has spoken of probing what he calls systemic corruption, and Senator Elissa Slotkin has urged “signature cases” against those who enabled Trump. A pardon, however, covers only federal crimes; it does not stop congressional subpoenas, state prosecutions or civil suits — a limit Trump himself seemed to acknowledge when asked about his own exposure: “Well, I don’t know about me.”

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — The Tanker That Was Cleared, Then Hit3 November 2026

Cleared by Tehran Hours Earlier, Then Struck: A Pakistan-Bound LNG Tanker Is Attacked Near Hormuz and Turns Back Into the Gulf

Safe passage, it turns out, is not a guarantee in Hormuz. The Marshall Islands-flagged LNG carrier Al Mayeda, loaded in Qatar and bound for Pakistan, came under fire on Friday as it approached the Strait, according to people with direct knowledge of the matter cited by Bloomberg — just hours after Iranian authorities were understood to have given the voyage a green light that Pakistan had negotiated, as it has for several cargoes before. Ship-tracking data shows the vessel reversing course into the Persian Gulf. Its owner, Qatar Gas Transport Company, known as Nakilat, says the ship remains operational and under assessment, with no crew injured and no environmental damage; India’s shipping minister described a projectile attack and confirmed all 25 crew, including six Indian nationals, are safe. Qatar condemned the attack as a threat to maritime security and global energy supplies and opened an investigation; Saudi Arabia joined the condemnation. In Islamabad on Monday, Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik confirmed a Pakistan-bound LNG ship was hit before entering the Strait, while Information Minister Attaullah Tarar urged against speculation about who fired. The stakes are practical: Pakistan received three Qatari cargoes in October even as LNG flows through Hormuz held at about 30% of pre-war levels, and a lost cargo would mean buying a replacement on a spot market where prices are more than three times pre-war levels — in the same briefing, the government said refineries will absorb Rs15 billion a month so petrol can sell Rs15 cheaper.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Aid Centre3 November 2026

Ten Guided Bombs, Then Nine More on the Shelter: 24 Killed at a Village Aid Centre in Zaporizhzhia, 27 Still Missing

The deadliest single strike of the day came where people had gathered to be helped. Russian forces bombed the village of Komyshuvakha in Ukraine’s southeastern Zaporizhzhia region on Monday, hitting a school used every week to distribute humanitarian aid and then, in what President Volodymyr Zelensky called a repeat strike, dropping nine more bombs directly on the shelter where people had taken cover. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service put the toll at 24 killed and five injured, with 27 people still missing; among the dead were two rescue workers — named as Denys Konovalenko and Oleksii Sushchevskyi, both 44 — and four police officers. The attack destroyed a gymnasium, an outbuilding and a fire truck and badly damaged the school, about 20 kilometres from the front line. “The Russians could not have been unaware that every week residents who have remained in Komyshuvakha were receiving assistance right there,” Zelensky wrote. Ukraine’s Prosecutor General has opened a war-crimes investigation; Russia continues to deny targeting civilians. The timing sharpened the charge in Kyiv: the strike came a day after the six-day Oleshky humanitarian ceasefire expired, and the same morning a Russian drone hit a hospital in central Kherson, damaging rooms but causing no reported injuries.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Summit Offer3 November 2026

“Yes, We Are Ready”: Zelensky Offers to Meet Putin in Türkiye as Technical Talks Head for Abu Dhabi

Even on a day of mass casualties, the diplomatic track moved. President Zelensky told CNN Türk, in an interview published 1 November after his 31 October meeting with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan in Istanbul, that he is ready to meet Vladimir Putin face to face in Türkiye to negotiate an end to the war — and, in a wider format, to sit down with Putin and President Trump together. “This war must end. We must unite to at least move this war from its active phase into a diplomatic one,” he said, arguing that Erdoğan’s channels to both Trump and Putin make Türkiye a practical, realistic mediator that also supports Ukraine’s sovereignty. Kyiv’s proposed division of labour is clear: technical teams, due to meet in Abu Dhabi later in November, would work on a partial energy ceasefire, a maritime ceasefire for the Black Sea and prisoner exchanges, while only leaders can settle the front-line ceasefire, monitoring and territory — on which Zelensky rules out concessions and any deal made without Ukraine. The Kremlin has so far avoided a leaders’ meeting, proposing a phone call instead, though aide Yuri Ushakov said trilateral Russia–US–Ukraine talks will resume after 4 November, possibly in Abu Dhabi. Türkiye hosted three rounds of delegation talks in 2025; whether it now hosts the principals is the open question of the month.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Murasu — Landfall Window3 November 2026

Landfall Under Way South of Ongole: Murasu Crosses the Andhra Coast Between Midnight and Dawn With 120 kmph Gusts

The forecast window is now the event itself. Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu is crossing the south Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, in the India Meteorological Department’s window of 11:30pm Monday to 4am Tuesday, with sustained winds of 100–110 kmph gusting to 120 kmph. At its last daylight fix, at 2:30pm Monday, the storm was about 100 km east of Nellore over the west-central Bay of Bengal, tracked by Doppler radars at Chennai, Machilipatnam and Visakhapatnam, after racing in at up to 27 kmph. The IMD warns of storm surges of 1 to 1.5 metres inundating low-lying coast, with seawater projected up to 330 metres inland in Ongole mandal and 190 metres in Bapatla, and of very heavy to extremely heavy rainfall — 115mm to over 200mm in 24 hours — across coastal Andhra. Fishing and shipping off the Andhra coast and the southwest Bay are suspended until Tuesday afternoon. After crossing, Murasu is expected to weaken into a depression and turn northwest toward Telangana. It is Andhra’s second landfall in under a week: Cyclone Arnab came ashore on 28 October with winds up to 150 kmph, and the state is still clearing that damage as this storm arrives.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — The Garment Shutdown Grows3 November 2026

679 Factories Down on Day Two: Bangladesh Garment Workers Hold Out for 30,000 Taka as the Wage Board Meets a Street It Cannot Calm

Bangladesh’s most important industry stopped again on Monday. Garment factories that had briefly reopened after Sunday’s shutdown closed once more as fresh clashes between workers and police spread across Dhaka, Gazipur, Savar, Ashulia and Mirpur, taking the number of plants halted to 679, Reuters reported. Workers blocked roads, vandalised factories and threw bricks and stones; police answered with tear gas, and a police vehicle was set on fire. The demand is a single number: a minimum monthly wage of 30,000 taka, about $243–245, against the current 12,500 taka set in December 2023, which workers say inflation in food, housing, transport and healthcare has hollowed out. Sunday’s first wave alone shut 529 factories and idled 987,190 workers — 276 factories and 645,360 workers in Gazipur and Mymensingh, 185 factories in Savar, Ashulia and Jirani, and 68 in Mirpur — and the industry association says 20 to 25 factories were vandalised. The government formed a six-member wage board on Sunday, chaired by Senior District and Sessions Judge Sheikh Ashfaqur Rahman with worker, employer, government and neutral members, and Prime Minister Tarique Rahman ordered officials to protect workers and keep factories running. The timing could hardly be worse for exporters: November to February is peak order season, when Bangladesh — whose garment sector employs about 4 million people, earns over 80% of export income and supplies H&M, Zara, Walmart and Primark — books roughly 40% of its annual orders.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — The Climate Milestone3 November 2026

Five Years Early: India Crosses 50% Non-Fossil Power Capacity and Modi Sets the Next Target — Another 500 GW in a Decade

India has met a Paris promise ahead of time, and used the announcement to set a larger one. Inaugurating the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit and Expo 2026 at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on Monday, Prime Minister Narendra Modi said more than half of India’s installed electricity capacity now comes from non-fossil sources — the 50% mark India had pledged to reach only by 2030 — with total non-fossil capacity around 308 GW, up more than 110 GW in two years, according to Renewable Energy Minister Pralhad Joshi. The next goal is to add a further 500 GW of non-fossil capacity over the coming 10 to 11 years, on top of the existing 500 GW-by-2030 trajectory, a build-out the government values at over Rs 20 lakh crore, about $220 billion, in investment opportunities. India now ranks among the world’s top three renewable markets, third in solar and fourth in wind; more than 6.5 million households have joined the PM Surya Ghar rooftop solar scheme, with over 100,000 new installations every six days, and green hydrogen production is targeted at 5 million tonnes a year by decade’s end. The four-day summit, themed “Invest. Innovate. Inspire.”, has drawn participants from more than 130 countries, over 40 ministers, more than 400 exhibitors and 13 Indian states pitching their renewable potential — with Modi framing clean energy plainly as energy security and economic security, not only climate policy.

Latest · Global Affairs · Anthropic — The Japan Tranche3 November 2026

$3.5 Billion From Japan Alone: Anthropic’s IPO Filing Reveals the Scale of the AI Listing Taking Shape on Nasdaq

The largest slice of detail yet in the year’s most anticipated listing came from a filing in Tokyo. Anthropic, maker of the Claude chatbot, will sell up to 552.9 billion yen — about $3.5 billion — of shares to Japanese investors as part of its US initial public offering, a securities filing showed on Monday, with proceeds earmarked for working capital, operating expenses and capital expenditure. Mizuho Securities will manage the Japanese sale, with Goldman Sachs, Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley Securities, Morgan Stanley MUFG Securities, Rakuten Securities and SBI Securities also handling orders; the shares themselves will list only on Nasdaq, under the ticker ANT, with parallel offerings in Australia, Canada, parts of Europe, Switzerland and the UK. The filing gives neither the total size of the IPO nor a valuation, though Reuters reports some investors have discussed a figure around $1.5 trillion, and Nikkei reports expectations of roughly $100 billion raised. The comparisons underline the scale: Japanese investors took $2.2 billion of SpaceX’s $75 billion June IPO, then the world’s largest, and Japan’s entire domestic IPO market raised only about 200 billion yen in April–September — Anthropic’s Japanese tranche alone could be nearly three times that, and the largest foreign-company fundraising in Japan on record.

Latest · Europe · Cyberattack — Counting the Freezers3 November 2026

“Everything Is Destroyed”: 115 to 125 Sites Now Counted in Europe’s Second Freezer Hack, and Investigators Still Cannot Name Who Did It

The clean-up from Monday’s refrigeration hack is becoming an inventory of a new kind of sabotage. Reporters counting company statements put the affected sites at roughly 115 to 125 across seven countries — Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland — including Ahold Delhaize and Colruyt in Belgium and Luxembourg, ICA and Coop in Sweden, and at least one IKEA store. Attackers struck around 2am, switching internet-connected units into defrost mode and driving temperatures to 50°C, and 55°C at an ICA store on the island of Öland; in Estonia, technicians caught one Selver supermarket’s controller set to 50°C within 10 to 15 minutes, limiting the real rise to a few degrees. Elsewhere the food was lost: an ICA sales manager in Stockholm told Reuters “everything is destroyed,” and a Belgian butchery owner south of Antwerp put his ruined meat at about €15,000. Some individual stores in the first reporting put losses above $145,000. The equipment thread runs through both waves — older network-connected controllers, including Danish maker Danfoss units also targeted when some 700 French supermarkets were hit on 12 October, with fires at several. Sweden’s National Cybersecurity Centre is working with affected chains, and Belgium’s Centre for Cybersecurity warned the perpetrator may never be identified; no group has claimed either attack, and Moscow has repeatedly denied involvement in sabotage operations.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia — Health System at the Brink3 November 2026

A Newborn Among the Dead at a Displacement Camp: WHO Says Northern Ethiopia’s Health System Is Being Pushed Toward Collapse

The World Health Organization’s chief used the word collapse for a health system still standing — barely. Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said on Monday that reports of attacks on civilians in Ethiopia’s Amhara and Tigray regions are “deeply disturbing”: children, including a newborn, were among those killed at the Jaari 2 displacement camp in Amhara — rescue sources told Reuters at least 15 people died there in Friday’s drone strikes — and several people were killed in Rama, Tigray, while trying to get food. “The violence in northern Ethiopia is pushing an already fragile health system closer to collapse,” Tedros said. “Hospitals face shortages of health workers, medicines, and equipment. Ambulance services are severely limited. Insecurity is obstructing the delivery of life-saving medical supplies to some areas.” UN Secretary-General António Guterres has warned of escalation and wider destabilisation and urged an immediate cessation of hostilities. The fighting — federal forces against Fano militias in Amhara since 2023, and renewed clashes with Tigrayan rebels since September across Amhara, Tigray and Afar — has effectively shattered the November 2022 peace agreement, and Tedros warned that continued violence will strip more people of emergency, maternal and child care and disease treatment even as WHO teams work around the clock to move supplies in.

Latest · Africa · Sudan — 18 Strikes on the Grid in a Week3 November 2026

Three More Substations on Monday Make 18 in a Week: Sudan’s Grid Campaign Deepens as Khartoum’s Ethiopia Dossier Reaches 70 Attacks

Sudan’s war on electricity is accelerating. Drone strikes on Monday hit three substations — Aroma and New Halfa in eastern Kassala state and al-Ghazali in Northern State — causing widespread outages and bringing the reported total of attacks on the power network to 18 in a single week, Al Jazeera reported, in a campaign spanning Khartoum, Gezira, Sennar, Red Sea, Gedaref, Kassala and River Nile. Khartoum’s military government blames the Rapid Support Forces, which it has fought since April 2023, and says Ethiopia is aiding them — the accusation it formalised on Sunday in complaints to the UN Security Council and the African Union Peace and Security Council. A Transitional Sovereignty Council report released the same day documents 70 alleged attacks launched from Ethiopian territory between 15 February and 27 October, involving 187 one-way attack drones and 82 drone-dropped munitions, killing or wounding 484 civilians and killing 176 soldiers with 200 more wounded; it names Asosa Airport, the Kurmuk-Gizen border area and Bahir Dar Air Base as launch locations, and says Sudan reserves its right to self-defence. Ethiopia, which has denied earlier allegations and in turn accuses Khartoum of backing Tigray-linked rebels, gave no immediate response to the latest filing.
