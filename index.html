Latest · Middle East · Iran Diplomacy31 October 2026

Iranian Envoys Meet Qatari Mediators in Doha as Fears Grow of US Strikes After the Midterms

Iranian negotiators sat down with Qatari mediators in Doha on Saturday, a source briefed on the talks told Reuters, following more than twelve hours of discussions in Tehran on Friday — all of it coordinated through regular contact with Washington. The urgency is timing: regional governments expect President Donald Trump, who has held back from fresh strikes on Iran in the run-up to Tuesday's US midterm elections, may resume attacks once voting ends, and Tehran has repeatedly warned it would answer by hitting US and Israeli targets in neighbouring countries. Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei issued a statement on Saturday ordering the armed forces to be ready to respond to any attack, saying commanders reported high readiness along the Gulf coast, around the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman, and on Iran's northwestern and southeastern borders. The gaps remain wide: Qatar brought a seven-day confidence-building proposal to Tehran a week ago, which a senior Iranian official said on Friday was not satisfactory, while Iran's own seven-day plan — lifting the blockade of its ports, unfreezing assets and approving an Iranian-backed Hormuz route before reopening the strait — has so far been rejected by Trump. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Iran was studying a letter sent earlier this week by Vice President JD Vance to a senior Iranian security official, an initiative Trump has publicly played down.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine Diplomacy31 October 2026

Zelenskyy Meets Erdoğan in Istanbul as Kyiv Eyes Trilateral Talks in Abu Dhabi Next Week

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy travelled to Istanbul on Saturday for talks with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, part of a diplomatic push after more than four and a half years of war with Russia. Announcing the visit in his nightly address on Friday, Zelenskyy called the Turkey discussions important and said Kyiv was working to use every available diplomatic opening, expressing hope that November would bring movement toward the negotiating formats discussed for so long. He has said this week that he hopes a trilateral meeting between Ukrainian, Russian and US negotiators can take place in Abu Dhabi between 6 and 10 November, and that Kyiv wants Europe involved in the process of ending the war. Turkey has repeatedly offered to facilitate talks and has hosted several rounds between Ukrainian and Russian officials since Moscow's 2022 invasion. The Istanbul stop also follows Erdoğan's recent phone call with President Trump, in which the Turkish leader said Ankara stood ready to support efforts to revive diplomacy between Moscow and Kyiv.

Latest · Europe · Russia Security Alert31 October 2026

US Warned Russia of a Possible Attack in Moscow, FSB Says, as a Palace Park Is Closed

Russia's Federal Security Service said on Saturday it had received information from the United States, passed through established partner channels, about preparations for a terrorist attack in Moscow, and that it was taking appropriate measures. Separately, the US Embassy in Moscow said it was aware of a credible threat of a mass-casualty event on Saturday at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve, a palace museum and park complex in the south of the capital, and that the US government was coordinating with Russian authorities. The Tsaritsyno site has been temporarily closed and access to the park restricted, the RIA news agency reported, citing law enforcement agencies. The warning echoes March 2024, when the US Embassy said extremists had imminent plans for an attack in Moscow — days before gunmen stormed the Crocus City Hall concert venue outside the capital, killing more than 140 people.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Court Crisis31 October 2026

Poland's President Accuses Tusk of a Forced Takeover of the Country's Top Court

Polish President Karol Nawrocki accused Prime Minister Donald Tusk on Saturday of staging a forced takeover of the Constitutional Tribunal, after the court's longest-serving judge suspended its president late on Friday and took charge herself. Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska suspended Bogdan Świeczkowski, an appointee of the previous nationalist government, in a move that sharply escalated the long-running battle between Nawrocki — an ally of that former government — and Tusk's pro-European coalition over control of the judiciary. Nawrocki, who cut short a trip to Africa and flew back overnight, said in a statement read by an aide outside the court early on Saturday that there had been a breach of public order and a forcible takeover of a constitutional body, and he called for urgent political consultations. Riot police were deployed as several hundred protesters demanded Świeczkowski's reinstatement. Tusk wrote on X that his government was seeking to restore the rule of law, which the European Union had accused his Law and Justice predecessors of undermining. The dispute over the tribunal dates back to 2015 and has effectively paralysed the institution; last month parliament appointed a Tusk ally, Maciej Berek, to the court.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election31 October 2026

Israel's Deadlock Deepens: Officials Reject 'Stolen Vote' Claims as Opposition Weighs a Direct Vote for Prime Minister

Final results from Israel's 27 October election, released early on Friday, confirmed a hung parliament: Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc holds 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, the opposition bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot has 48, and Arab parties — the Joint List and Ra'am — won a combined 19, a record driven by turnout of nearly 78%, the highest in decades, with especially strong participation among Arab citizens. Likud remains the largest single party with 27 seats, ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar on 21. With neither camp able to reach the 61 seats needed to govern, election officials on Saturday dismissed Netanyahu's allegations of foreign interference, saying an ongoing investigation would not change the results or invalidate any community's votes. Opposition parties are now weighing legislation to introduce direct elections for prime minister as a way out of the impasse — a change that would itself need 61 lawmakers to amend Israel's Basic Law. The results are due to be certified and handed to President Isaac Herzog on Wednesday, after which he will ask a candidate to try to form a government; if the talks fail, another election remains possible.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Early Voting31 October 2026

Early Voting in the US Midterms Passes 48 Million, Smashing the 2022 Record Before Election Day

Nearly 48 million Americans had already voted by midday on Friday ahead of Tuesday's midterm elections — 47,922,669 ballots, according to the University of Florida's Election Lab — surpassing the previous midterm record of 45.9 million early votes set in 2022, with days of voting still to go. Measured five days before Election Day, more than ten million more people had voted early than at the same point in 2022. Texas drove much of the surge, with more than six million early votes by Wednesday and in-person turnout running about 50% ahead of 2022, while Georgia's in-person early voting broke the state's midterm record on Thursday, with women casting 56% of ballots there. In states that record party registration, Democrats made up about 44% of early voters, Republicans 31% and independents or minor-party voters 25%, and Election Lab director Michael McDonald pointed to rising participation among Black and Hispanic voters, notably in heavily Hispanic communities along the Texas–Mexico border. Analysts caution that registration does not reveal how anyone voted, that tens of millions more will vote on Tuesday, and that a record early vote may partly reflect people voting sooner rather than more people voting overall.

Latest · Africa · Dakar 202631 October 2026

Africa Hosts an Olympic Event for the First Time as the Dakar Youth Games Open in Senegal

The 2026 Summer Youth Olympic Games opened in Senegal on Saturday evening with a ceremony in Diamniadio, near Dakar — the first Olympic event ever staged on African soil. Around 2,665 athletes aged 14 to 17 from across the world will compete in 25 sports and 153 medal events through 13 November, across eight venues in Dakar, Diamniadio and the coastal town of Saly, under banners proclaiming that Africa welcomes and Dakar celebrates. Senegal fields 136 athletes, backed by some 6,000 volunteers, and visiting leaders included Rwanda's President Paul Kagame, who joined host President Bassirou Diomaye Faye for the opening. The Games carry two notable returns: Russian athletes compete under their own flag and colours for the first time at an Olympics since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, after the IOC lifted the Russian Olympic Committee's suspension in July, and a refugee Olympic team, drawn largely from athletes based in Africa, also takes part. IOC President Kirsty Coventry, herself a former Zimbabwean swimmer and the first African to lead the organisation, has framed Dakar as a chance to change perceptions of the continent. The Games, originally due in 2022 and postponed because of the Covid pandemic, are the fourth Summer Youth Olympics after Singapore, Nanjing and Buenos Aires.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan Strait31 October 2026

China's Coast Guard Patrols Waters East of Taiwan in What Beijing Calls Routine Law Enforcement

China's coast guard carried out patrols in waters east of Taiwan on Saturday, which it described as routine law-enforcement activity, state broadcaster CCTV reported. A coast guard spokesperson, Jiang Lue, said the service has strengthened control over those waters since the start of October to protect the lawful rights and safety of Chinese people, including compatriots in Taiwan, and that patrols in waters under its jurisdiction would continue to step up. The patrols are part of a broader increase in Chinese activity east of the island since June. Beijing claims Taiwan as its own territory and recognises no sovereignty claims by Taipei, including over surrounding seas; Taiwan's democratically elected government rejects Beijing's claims, and officials in Taipei say they believe the stepped-up activity amounts to rehearsing for a potential future operation against the island.

Quad Foreign Ministers Meet in Atlanta as the G20 Wraps Up Without a Joint Statement

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio hosted the foreign ministers of Australia, India and Japan in Atlanta on Saturday for the Quad's third ministerial meeting of the year, with India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar among those attending to discuss shared Indo-Pacific priorities. The talks prepare the ground for a leaders' summit planned alongside the ASEAN meetings in Manila on 15–18 November — the first Quad summit since 2024 and the first for President Trump, after a summit planned in India late last year was cancelled amid trade and tariff friction with New Delhi. Rubio then closed the two-day G20 foreign ministers' meeting, which laid bare deep divisions over the Ukraine and Middle East wars: organisers cancelled the traditional family photo after European ministers refused to appear alongside Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, and no joint communique was expected. Rubio, who urged cooperation where possible at a Georgia Aquarium reception on Friday evening, invited the visiting ministers to a college football game, with attention now turning to the G20 leaders' summit on 14–15 December at Trump's Doral resort in Florida.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Brazilian Foreign Minister Mauro Vieira spoke briefly on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta and agreed to meet again in the coming weeks, before the G20 leaders' summit in December, a source familiar with the plans said. Latest · Africa · Health — Dakar Games31 October 2026

Senegal Puts Its Health Emergency Teams on High Alert as 206 Delegations Arrive for the Youth Games

Behind the celebrations in Dakar, Senegal's Health Emergency Operations Center has stepped up prevention and response planning for the Youth Olympic Games, which bring more than 2,700 athletes and 206 delegations together for fourteen days across Dakar, Diamniadio and Saly. Its director general, Dr Papa Samba Dièye, said the current disease and environmental picture demands heightened vigilance. The Ebola risk assessment was raised from unlikely to probable after an imported case was confirmed in Nigeria on 22 October, prompting tighter importation precautions. Diphtheria is also under close watch after a recent transmission was reported in the Diamniadio health district — the very area hosting key Olympic venues — even as overall cases trend downward. With the rainy season ending, officials are also monitoring mosquito-borne threats including dengue, malaria and Rift Valley fever as part of a comprehensive plan covering athletes, delegations and the wider public.
