Latest · Europe · Serbia26 October 2026

“It Is Clear That We Have Lost”: Vučić Concedes to Serbia's Student Movement, Ending Twelve Years in Power

Aleksandar Vučić, who has dominated Serbian politics since 2014, conceded defeat on Sunday night to the student-led movement that grew out of nearly two years of anti-corruption protests, telling the nation: “It is clear that we have lost these elections, the Student List has won.” Based on a sample of votes, Vučić said the Student List — its full name is Students Win — was expected to take between 135 and 140 of the 250 seats in parliament, against about 105 for his own list, comfortably above the 126 needed to govern. Turnout reached 64.9% by early evening, the highest since the breakup of Yugoslavia in 1992. The snap election, which Vučić called in September after resigning as president to run for prime minister, traces back to November 2024, when a railway-station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad and killed 16 people; demands for accountability widened into a movement against alleged corruption and repression, which the government denies. Election monitors reported a flawed vote alongside the celebration: the CRTA watchdog recorded pressure on voters or election workers at almost a third of the 500 polling stations it observed, and the CeSID group logged some 260 reports of possible irregularities. None of it dampened the night in Belgrade, where fireworks lit the sky and crowds chanted “Aleksandar is gone.” European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen congratulated the Student List, saying the EU stood ready to support reforms and “to advance Serbia's future in the European Union.”

Election Eve in Israel: Trump Campaigns for Netanyahu as the Iran Warning, and a Rift With the Army Chief, Dominate the Final Hours

Israel goes to the polls on Tuesday with the campaign's final day consumed by Iran. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spent Sunday warning that Tehran and its proxies might attack to disrupt the vote — “Don't test us,” he said at an Arrow anti-missile battery, promising “disproportionate blows” in reply — while President Donald Trump again intervened directly in the campaign, urging Israelis to vote for Netanyahu: “We have to finish the job.” Trump told Fox News there would be no strike on Iran that night, but framed Washington's choice as “Do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off?” Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, answered that resuming the war, now in its ninth month, would not change Tehran's policies. Behind the public messaging, Israeli media reported a clash between Netanyahu and his military chief, Eyal Zamir, over a reported plan to strike Iran in the days before the election — a plan Zamir was said last week to see no operational need for. Opposition figures accuse Netanyahu of manufacturing a security crisis to rescue his campaign: former general Gadi Eisenkot warned that Netanyahu “will stop at nothing to stay in power,” and the final polls before the vote showed the prime minister's bloc trailing.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India26 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab Is Born: The Bay of Bengal Storm Strengthens on Its Run Toward a “Very Severe” Wednesday Landfall

The deep depression over the Bay of Bengal intensified into Cyclonic Storm Arnab at 5:30 on Monday morning, the India Meteorological Department said, and is strengthening fast. At formation the storm sat over the east-central bay near 15.4°N, 88.9°E — about 650 km east-southeast of Visakhapatnam — moving west at 15 km/h. Forecasters expect Arnab to become a severe cyclonic storm by midnight tonight and a very severe one on Tuesday, before crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Wednesday forenoon with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h gusting to 140 km/h. The IMD has issued a red message for the Andhra Pradesh and adjoining south Odisha coasts, and heavy rain is already lashing parts of Tamil Nadu. Odisha, expecting the worst of the rain rather than the wind, has deployed 60 state disaster-response teams and 17 national teams, with 225 fire-service teams on alert and a stated zero-casualty objective; a red warning for extremely heavy rainfall covers five southern districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri — where schools and colleges will remain closed on October 28 and 29, and fishermen have been ordered to stay ashore.

Two Dead, 90,000 Still Without Power: Britain Counts the Cost of Storm Boelo — With More Wind and Rain on the Way

Two men were killed by falling trees as Storm Boelo battered Britain over the weekend with winds of up to 84 mph, cutting power to 800,000 homes at its peak and leaving some 90,000 customers still in the dark by Sunday evening, the Energy Networks Association said. A 60-year-old man was struck by a falling tree near Castle Douglas in Dumfries, and a man in his twenties died when a tree fell on his car in north London; two women were taken to hospital after being hit by another tree in Aberfeldy, Perth and Kinross. Scotland, Northern Ireland and northern England took the worst of it: the equivalent of a third of a month's rain fell in parts of Scotland, the final amber wind alert expired only at 9pm on Sunday, and on Monday morning the Environment Agency had 10 flood warnings and 65 flood alerts in force in England. Boelo was Britain's second storm in three days, following Storm Austen on Friday. Rail operators warned of “significant recovery work” before services normalise, and the Met Office has already issued a yellow warning for strong winds from 3am Tuesday to midnight across Scotland, northern England and north-east Northern Ireland, with another deepening low pressure system on its way.

The World Congratulates Lula on His Fourth Term — While Bolsonaro Demands the Runoff Be Annulled

Congratulations poured in from across the Americas and Europe on Monday after Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's runoff victory secured him a fourth term as Brazil's president — but the man he beat is refusing to accept it. Flávio Bolsonaro has called for Sunday's vote to be annulled, calling the two-hour extension of voting “unacceptable,” even though the Supreme Court justice who granted it, Cristiano Zanin, acted after both campaigns had requested more time to deal with transport chaos and severe weather. With 99.77% of polling sections counted, Lula won 51.76% of valid votes to Bolsonaro's 48.24% — a margin of more than 4.3 million ballots — becoming the first candidate since direct elections were restored in 1989 to win the presidency after trailing in the first round. Mexico's Claudia Sheinbaum wrote “Long live democracy, the people of Brazil won”; Canada's Mark Carney proposed seizing the moment to deepen trade; European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen declared that democracy and multilateralism “have won” in Brazil; and India's Narendra Modi said he looked forward to working closely with Lula. Washington split along partisan lines: Donald Trump Jr attacked the voting extension, while Senator Bernie Sanders hailed an “extraordinary re-election victory” and Congressman Ro Khanna warned that President Trump “must respect the results and not undermine Brazil's elections.” Lula's new term begins on January 5, 2027.

Eight Hours Through the Crowds: Machado's Triumphal Road Journey Ends in Caracas

María Corina Machado completed her return to Venezuela on Sunday night, reaching Caracas after an eight-hour road journey so slowed by cheering supporters that the highway was at times all but blocked. “This country is giving lessons to everyone,” the Nobel Peace Prize laureate shouted, leaning out of her vehicle's sunroof to shake hands, festooned in rosaries handed to her by followers. The journey was the final leg of a carefully guarded return: according to people familiar with her movements, Machado flew by private plane from Panama to Colombia late last week, crossed into Venezuela overland on Sunday under the protection of US security contractors, and was met first at a church in Barquisimeto, in Lara state, where bystanders sang the national anthem and shouted “liberty!” Her team said Washington had warned interim president Delcy Rodríguez's authorities not to interfere with her safety. The politics remain tangled: Secretary of State Marco Rubio credited US-backed reconciliation talks for making the return possible, while President Donald Trump said publicly that he knew nothing about it and that the US would continue backing Rodríguez. Machado's adviser David Smolansky said she would now push “proactively for a date for free and fair elections,” meet families of political prisoners and victims of June's deadly earthquakes, and be joined soon by her movement's former presidential candidate, Edmundo González, who announced he will return from exile in Spain.

Decision Day in Cairo: Parliament Resumes Its Secret Deliberations on Sending Egyptian Forces to Defend Saudi Arabia

Egypt's House of Representatives resumed its closed deliberations on Monday on President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi's request for authority to send armed forces to support Saudi Arabia against the Iran-aligned Houthis — a vote that could draw one of the region's largest militaries directly into the widening conflict. Approval will not be simple arithmetic: under Article 152 of the constitution, sending forces on a combat mission abroad requires a two-thirds majority of the chamber's 596 members — at least 398 votes — in a roll-call ballot, after the National Defence Council has been consulted. Even then, the authorisation would give Sisi a conditional power, exercisable if Riyadh makes a formal request; Cairo has insisted any Egyptian role would be limited to defending Saudi territory and borders, not attacking Yemen. The House has already sat twice on the request, on October 18 and 19, and adjourned without deciding; the deputy speaker said last week the chamber was leaning toward approval, with nearly 500 members asking to speak, though some parties, including the Egyptian Social Democratic Party, have registered reservations. In parallel, Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty continues to press the diplomatic track, demanding a full Houthi withdrawal from territory seized near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait in September — the Red Sea chokepoint on which Egypt's Suez Canal revenues depend.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria26 October 2026

Suspected Cases Test Negative as Nigeria Watches 53 Ebola Contacts — and Pays the Quarantined a Weekly Stipend

Nigeria's Ebola containment effort recorded an encouraging day: the disease-control agency, the NCDC, confirmed that the additional suspected cases under investigation — including one in Rivers State — have all tested negative, leaving the 42-year-old Abuja woman confirmed on October 20 as the country's only case. She is reported to be showing remarkable clinical improvement in isolation. The number of contacts under monitoring stands at 53 — health workers, patients, caregivers and household members — each to be watched for the mandatory 21 days after last exposure. In a notable step to keep people complying with quarantine, the federal government is paying those isolated ₦100,000 per week on top of accommodation and feeding, after contacts complained about the burden on their families. NCDC director-general Jide Idris warned that reluctance to disclose exposure could allow undetected transmission, and noted that genomic sequencing of the virus — the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no licensed vaccine — has been completed with 500 donated test kits added to the national stockpile. Lagos, Nigeria's commercial capital, has tightened passenger screening at Murtala Muhammed International Airport, though it has recorded no suspected case. The patient arrived from Uganda via Addis Ababa on October 3 and fell ill four days later; hers is West Africa's first case of the outbreak devastating the Democratic Republic of Congo.

1.5 Billion Children Now Live With Dangerous Heat: UN Agencies Launch the First Scorecard for Tracking What It Does to Them

An estimated 1.5 billion children worldwide are exposed to heatwaves that are growing more frequent, longer and more severe, the World Health Organization says, as it and three other UN agencies launched the first standard set of indicators for tracking what extreme heat does to pregnant women, newborns and children. The framework, developed with the World Meteorological Organization, UNICEF and the UN Population Fund, asks countries to monitor preterm births, stillbirths, low birthweight, pregnancy hypertension, child deaths and heat-related clinic visits alongside temperature data — and even school attendance, as a measure of how heat disrupts children's lives. The evidence behind it is stark: heat exposure is associated with a 26% increase in the odds of preterm birth, and babies under one year old experienced 389% more heatwave days in 2024 than in the 1986–2005 baseline. “As our world continues to heat up, it creates new risks for health, and older people, pregnant women, newborns and children are among those least able to escape them,” said Bruce Gordon, acting director of the WHO's environment and climate department. “Our health systems need the data to recognize this reality and take action.”
