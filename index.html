Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Donbas-for-NATO Question Raised15 November 2026

“What If Ukraine Joins NATO — Would It Withdraw From Donbas?” Zelenskyy Says US Envoys Raised the Swap, and Offers a Referendum on Any Full Deal

A new and explosive question has entered the peace talks. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told reporters on Saturday that US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff asked whether Ukraine would pull its remaining troops out of the eastern Donbas in exchange for NATO membership — the first time, he said, that alliance membership had been linked to a territorial withdrawal. Zelenskyy repeated that Kyiv will not surrender territory or recognise Russian occupation, but said that if Washington and its partners put forward a concrete package — specific security guarantees, no cap on a strong and well-funded Ukrainian army, and money to rebuild — he would put that package to the Ukrainian people in a national referendum. The sequence matters: the envoys met Vladimir Putin in Moscow, then briefed Zelenskyy in Dublin, after two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi that ended without a breakthrough but with agreement on a prisoner exchange involving more than 1,000 people, and no progress yet on an energy ceasefire. Washington has not confirmed the proposal — a US official told the BBC that many ideas continue to be discussed — and European leaders meeting in Dublin agreed Europe should sit at future talks through Britain, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the EU institutions, a format Moscow has resisted. Russia, for its part, is reported to be demanding NATO return to its pre-1997 posture.

Latest · Middle East · Bahrain — Interceptions Confirmed by Manama15 November 2026

Bahrain Says Its Air Defences Destroyed Several Iranian Attacks Before They Landed — Tehran Still Claims It Hit the US Radar at Al-Riffa

The two sides are now telling opposite versions of the same morning. Bahrain’s Defence Force said on Sunday that its air defences intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets — the first such interceptions since September — after the Interior Ministry issued two shelter alerts within about three hours from around 4:30 a.m. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards maintain that their Aerospace Force sent six drones against the US air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base, in retaliation for what they call hostile US actions, in statements carried by Fars and Press TV. There is still no confirmation from Washington of any hit, and no casualties have been reported. It was the second claimed attack on a US radar in Bahrain in three days: on Friday the Guards said they destroyed an early-warning radar, while US Central Command said it shot down two Iranian drones with no injuries or damage. Saudi Arabia condemned the attacks on Bahrain, and Gulf News’ live coverage put the Bahrain interceptions alongside Riyadh’s own missile defences in a single, widening Sunday of Gulf alerts.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Tankers, Mines and Oil15 November 2026

Three Tankers Reported Ablaze After Striking Mines In and Around Hormuz — Yet the Oil Market, Now in Surplus, Barely Blinks

The war at sea escalated on paper and shrugged in the market. Iran’s navy and Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines on Saturday — two inside the Strait of Hormuz that Tehran says were crossing without authorisation with their tracking systems off, and a third east of the strait in what it calls a prohibited zone; the Guards later claimed a tanker carrying some 2 million barrels had sunk, a claim AFP notes has no independent confirmation, while the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said the crew in its report was safe and Reuters could not verify the incidents. The human ledger is grim regardless: the UN has told the Security Council at least 25 seafarers have been killed around Hormuz since February, the International Maritime Organization counts 136 security incidents to 11 November, and shipping through the strait remains about 65 percent below pre-war levels. And yet Brent closed Friday at $95.84 a barrel, and the International Energy Agency says October brought the market’s first surplus since the war began — Saudi output hit 10.5 million barrels a day in October, Iraq, Kuwait and the UAE pumped more, and the US Energy Information Administration, which put Brent’s October average at $124, expects roughly $100 through November and December. The pain has moved downstream instead: European diesel trades near $170 a barrel.

Latest · Science · SpaceX — First Starship Catch14 November 2026

Caught From Orbit: SpaceX’s Starship Comes Home After 14 Laps of Earth and Slips Into the Tower’s Arms — a First in Rocketry

It looked like science fiction and landed like routine. On Saturday, SpaceX’s Starship spacecraft emerged from thick cloud over Starbase, Texas, reignited its engines, flipped upright and descended slowly into the mechanical “chopstick” arms of its launch tower — the first time a Starship returning from orbit has been caught in mid-air. The 52-metre spacecraft had spent about 22 hours in space, completed 14 orbits at roughly 265 kilometres up and deployed 26 Starlink satellites, on the 15th full-scale Starship flight since 2023 and only its second trip to orbit; its heat shield, the component that decides whether reuse is real, appeared to perform well. The triumph was not complete: the Super Heavy booster’s engines cut out early on its way back on Friday, its guidance system diverted it into the Gulf of Mexico where it exploded, and wreckage sat cordoned off by buoys — so the double catch SpaceX wants for rapid Moon and Mars operations remains undone, and engineers are investigating. NASA welcomed the result, and SpaceX expects to begin Starship launches from Florida’s Kennedy Space Center within months.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Missiles Downed Over Riyadh15 November 2026

Saudi Air Defences Destroy Two Houthi Ballistic Missiles Aimed at Riyadh, and a Third Drone Over the Eastern Region Within Hours

The Yemen front widened to the Saudi capital on Sunday. The Saudi-led coalition says its air defences destroyed two ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis toward Riyadh, after a short emergency alert was sounded in the city, and coalition spokesman Colonel Turki Al Malki also reported a drone intercepted over the Eastern Region — the third such interception there within three hours. No casualties from the interceptions have been reported. The launch also lands a day after the UN Security Council voted to extend sanctions on the Houthis, strongly condemning their renewed military escalation. For Riyadh, Sunday underlined how quickly the region’s separate fronts are bleeding into one another: Iranian drones claimed over Bahrain, missiles over the Saudi capital, an Israeli strike killing a senior Hamas commander in Gaza, and the Strait of Hormuz still largely closed to normal shipping.

Latest · Climate · COP31 — Midpoint in Antalya15 November 2026

7,000 March in the Antalya Rain as COP31 Hits Halfway With Its Hardest Fights — Adaptation Money, Climate Science, Fossil Fuels — Still Unsettled

The summit paused on Sunday with its central arguments unresolved. More than 7,000 people, by police estimate, marched through downtown Antalya on Saturday in the largest protest since COP31 opened — costumes, placards and slogans under close police supervision, organised by environmental groups, unions and rights organisations alongside a parallel People’s Climate Summit — accusing negotiators of failing the people already living with environmental destruction. Inside the talks, the halfway stocktake left countries far apart on financing adaptation in developing nations, on how firmly the texts defend climate science, and on whether any explicit move away from fossil fuels will be named. UN climate chief Simon Stiell called progress so far constructive but urged governments to move swiftly to the hardest issues and avoid tactical delays, as ministers arrive for the final stretch. The context is unforgiving: the world has warmed about 1.4 degrees above pre-industrial levels, a UN report last month put the planet on course to breach 1.5 degrees, and small island states such as Palau are telling the summit that rising seas threaten their people and sovereignty. Talks resume Monday with five days left before the Friday close.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Defence Pact Signed in Manila15 November 2026

Australia and the Philippines Sign a New Defence Pact in Manila as the ASEAN Summit Moves Into Its Second Day

The summit’s most concrete security result so far was signed on Sunday. Australia and the Philippines concluded a new defence agreement on the sidelines of the leaders’ meetings in Manila, updating a decades-old memorandum to fast-track military cooperation and, in Manila’s words, bolster deterrence in a region shaped by China’s growing military presence. Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro said the pact makes coordination “faster, speedier,” strengthens the Philippines’ credible deterrent posture in areas under its jurisdiction, and lets its forces familiarise themselves with Australian equipment ahead of joint training; the two countries have held a visiting forces agreement since 2012 and already run joint maritime patrols in the South China Sea. Australian Defence Minister Richard Marles said the deal deepens practical cooperation — enhanced maritime activity, information-sharing and defence infrastructure — and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese attended the signing. The pact sits inside a crowded summit week: ASEAN leaders held their closed retreat on Myanmar, energy security and transboundary haze — voicing deep concern at the lack of progress on Myanmar’s peace plan and continued attacks on civilians — dialogue-partner meetings follow on Monday and Tuesday, the East Asia Summit meets Tuesday, and the first Quad leaders’ summit since 2024 is expected Tuesday evening after President Donald Trump lands.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Arsenal Estimate15 November 2026

US Intelligence Estimates Iran Still Holds Between 1,500 and 2,500 Missiles Despite Months of Strikes

Months of bombing have not emptied Tehran’s magazines, on Washington’s own numbers. US intelligence assessments reported by the Wall Street Journal on Sunday estimate that Iran retains between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles despite sustained US and Israeli strikes on its weapons infrastructure, alongside significant numbers of drones and short-range missiles — a stockpile that sits uneasily beside President Donald Trump’s claims that Iran’s military capability has been largely destroyed. Officials caution that the estimate carries a low level of confidence, because it is hard to know how many missiles are ready to fire and how many sit in storage, and Israeli military estimates put ballistic missiles alone at more than 1,500. A former US deputy assistant secretary of defence for the Middle East, Simone Ledeen, noted that even the lower figure would mean Iran has rebuilt more than half of its pre-war stockpile, though the recovery is reported to be slower than before the war. The assessment helps explain Washington’s current posture: reporting this weekend says Trump has told advisers he may hold off on further attacks barring a new provocation, favouring sanctions and the naval blockade instead, while Gulf allies warn against renewed fighting and Israel continues to press for more military action.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flights Recover, Blame Game Starts15 November 2026

US Flights Head Back to Normal by Sunday’s Close — 1,200 Already Cancelled — as Washington Fights Over Who Pays for Ageing Systems

The recovery is real, and so is the argument about how the system failed. FlightAware had recorded more than 1,200 cancelled US flights by Sunday morning, after what FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford put at 12,000 cancellations on Friday and more than 6,600 on Saturday; Bedford predicted a full resumption of schedules by close of business on Sunday, and the nationwide ground stop — only the second in US airspace since the 9/11 attacks — had been lifted at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Friday once full functionality was restored. Officials have firmly ruled out a cyberattack. The administration’s account is that a botched upgrade from copper wire to fibre cable flooded routers elsewhere in the air-traffic system; Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy used the failure to appeal for $50 billion to modernise air traffic control and called on Congress to “meet this moment.” Democrats answered in kind: Illinois Governor JB Pritzker pointed out that Republicans control Congress and could have funded the system, New York Governor Kathy Hochul called on Duffy to resign or be fired, and lawmakers from both parties demanded investigations. President Trump, unusually, had made no public statement on the outage by Sunday morning. Britain’s Civil Aviation Authority warned UK–US travellers that follow-on disruption could persist while airlines rebook passengers and reposition aircraft.

Hamas confirmed on Sunday that Samir Abu Younis, 53, who led its Rafah brigade, was killed in an Israeli drone strike on the al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis in southern Gaza; Israel accuses him of involvement in planning the 7 October attack. Israel maintains that more than 93,000 tents and thousands of truckloads of winter supplies have entered since September and blames distribution failures inside Gaza; aid agencies and the EU say crossings and access remain the binding constraint.

Latest · Europe · Portugal — Housing Protests14 November 2026

Tens of Thousands March in More Than 30 Portuguese Cities in the Largest Protest Yet Against Housing Costs Beyond Reach

Europe’s autumn of street protest has a housing chapter. Tens of thousands of people marched through more than 30 cities and towns across Portugal on Saturday in the largest demonstration yet against soaring housing costs that many say have put buying or renting a home beyond their reach. The marches drew inspiration from protests that began in neighbouring Spain last month over similar pressures, and they join a wider season of unrest: in France, a fifth consecutive weekend of cost-of-living protests drew about 106,000 people nationwide on Saturday by the interior ministry’s count — unions claimed 200,000 — with tear gas in Paris, Bordeaux and Nantes and a new strike day called for Tuesday; in Bern, hundreds of Swiss men marched to denounce violence against women and demand men take a larger role in fighting it. The Portuguese turnout underlines how housing, once a local planning issue, has become a continental political fault line, squeezing younger and lower-income households in Lisbon and Porto just as it has in Madrid and Barcelona.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Bus Crash in the West15 November 2026

Twelve Killed and 16 Injured as a Bus Carrying Gas-Refinery Workers Overturns in Western Iran Near the Iraqi Border

A deadly road crash has added a domestic tragedy to Iran’s wartime Sunday. Twelve people were killed and 16 injured after a bus carrying employees of a gas refinery overturned in western Iran, near the border with Iraq, late on Saturday, according to Iranian state media reports carried in live coverage on Sunday. No further official detail on the cause was immediately reported. The crash was among several transport disasters recorded over the same weekend internationally — a bus overturning in southwestern Colombia killed at least six people and injured 11, Colombian police said, and two miners were killed with two more missing after a mine blast in Colombia — a reminder that for all the region’s focus on missiles and drones, the ordinary risks of roads and workplaces continue to claim lives at scale.
