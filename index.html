Latest · Africa · Cairo — 31 Dead, 21 Injured25 November 2026

Fuel Tanker Overturns on Cairo Ring Road, Fire Kills at Least 31

A tanker carrying petroleum products overturned and burst into flames on Cairo’s busy Ring Road in the Warraq district of Giza on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people and injuring 21 others, Egypt’s Health Ministry said. The blaze swept through traffic caught nearby, leaving more than two dozen cars and motorcycles charred, and 39 ambulances were sent to the scene. Officials said the privately operated tanker veered off the road before exploding above Tunnel 10 near Imbaba. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar described the toll as preliminary and warned it could still rise, while prosecutors put the number of vehicles burned at 27. The highway has since been cleared and reopened.

Latest · Americas · United States — Parade Delayed to Saturday25 November 2026

Storms Batter Both US Coasts as Macy’s Postpones Thanksgiving Parade for First Time

Severe weather on both American coasts disrupted the Thanksgiving holiday on Wednesday. In New York, organisers took the unprecedented step of moving the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade from Thursday to Saturday at 8:30 a.m., citing forecasts of intense wind and rain that threatened the parade’s giant balloons, with a high wind warning and coastal flood warning in force and empty tractor-trailers banned from bridges. On the West Coast, king tides — the year’s highest tides, amplified by a strong El Niño — combined with powerful surf to tear away part of the pier at Pacifica, flood beachfront homes and prompt evacuation warnings in Marin County, where breaking waves of 20 to 30 feet were forecast. Forecasters also warned of record water levels in places, with San Diego recording a tide near 8 feet 11 inches, as AAA predicted 81.5 million Americans would travel for the holiday.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Asia Rebounds, Tariffs Still in Focus26 November 2026

Asian Shares Bounce as Wall Street Recovers, but AI and Tariff Worries Linger

Asian markets managed a rare advance on Thursday after Wall Street clawed back most of an early sell-off, even as doubts about artificial-intelligence financing and a new round of US tariffs kept investors cautious. Japan’s Nikkei rose 1.6 percent and South Korea’s main index gained 1.9 percent, though both remain sharply lower for November, while Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan added 0.6 percent. In the United States, third-quarter growth was revised up to 4.3 percent but the Federal Reserve’s preferred core inflation measure came in at 3.3 percent. Nvidia ended Wednesday down 3.9 percent at $149.30, far below its October peak, after S&P placed its rating on watch. Traders noted the proposed US duties on 14 partners including China, Japan and the EU would not stack on top of July’s levies, limiting the added burden, while oil held firm after Houthi claims of an attack on a Saudi refinery and gold eased to about $3,566 an ounce.

Latest · Europe · Nizhny Novgorod — Five Aircraft Hit25 November 2026

Ukraine Says Long-Range Strike Disabled Five Russian Warplanes at Savasleyka Air Base

Ukraine’s General Staff said on Wednesday that its forces struck the Savasleyka air base in Russia’s Nizhny Novgorod region, east of Moscow, damaging five aircraft used to launch missiles and bombs against Ukraine. According to Kyiv, three MiG-31 jets that carry hypersonic Kinzhal missiles and two Su-34 fighter-bombers were put out of action for some time in attacks carried out on 24 November and overnight into 25 November. The General Staff also reported hitting a P-18 radar in occupied Luhansk and fuel and supply sites in occupied Luhansk and Donetsk. Moscow did not immediately comment on the claim, and the full extent of the damage is still being assessed.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bogor, West Java — Search Resumes Thursday26 November 2026

Rescuers Resume Search for 13 Trapped in Flooded Illegal Gold Mine in Indonesia

Rescue teams in Indonesia resumed work on Thursday to reach 13 miners still trapped underground in an illegal gold mine in Bogor regency, West Java, where 13 others have already been confirmed dead. Police said 26 workers were inside the mine on Monday when heavy rain caused the nearby Ciseureuh River to overflow into shafts about 100 metres deep; 11 miners escaped from one section. West Java police chief Hengki said six of the recovered victims had been identified so far and that the search would continue barring bad weather. State miner Antam, which operates legally in the area, stressed the disaster happened at an unauthorised site unrelated to its operations. Officials said reporting was delayed because the mining was illegal, and police have opened an investigation into who organised and financed it.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Tiangong Station — First Foreign Guest Aboard25 November 2026

Pakistan’s First Astronaut Boards China’s Tiangong Station on Shenzhou-24

China’s Shenzhou-24 mission docked with the Tiangong space station on Wednesday, bringing Pakistani Wing Commander Muhammad Zeeshan Ali aboard as the first foreign astronaut to visit the station and the first Pakistani in orbit. The three-person crew — commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi and Ali as payload specialist — lifted off from Jiuquan on a Long March-2F rocket at 8:18 a.m. Beijing time and entered the station about six hours later, where they were greeted by the Shenzhou-23 crew. Ali, a 40-year-old pilot, is expected to stay five to ten days conducting experiments, including biotechnology and materials work under the Pakistan–China programme, before returning on Shenzhou-23. The flight, agreed in February 2025, marks a milestone for Beijing’s space diplomacy in the 75th year of China–Pakistan diplomatic ties.

Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — Judges Question Bribery Case25 November 2026

Israeli Judges Urge Prosecutors to Drop Netanyahu Bribery Case and Rethink Another

Judges in Benjamin Netanyahu’s long-running corruption trial dealt prosecutors a major setback on Wednesday, recommending that the bribery case known as Case 4000 be dropped and that a second case be reconsidered. Presiding Judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman told Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara, who had been summoned to the Jerusalem hearing, that the bribery charge faced considerable difficulties and that there was serious doubt the evidence supported a conviction. In Case 4000 prosecutors allege Netanyahu granted regulatory favours worth hundreds of millions of dollars to Bezeq’s owner in exchange for favourable coverage on the Walla news site. The court also questioned whether Case 2000, over alleged talks for favourable coverage in Yedioth Ahronoth, amounted to a crime, and suggested the gifts case, Case 1000, could be settled by agreement. The judges stressed their remarks were preliminary, not a verdict. Netanyahu, who denies all wrongdoing, said the cases were falling apart and repeated a call for a unity government; Baharav-Miara said she would review the recommendations.

Latest · Americas · Cartagena — All 76 Freed Unharmed25 November 2026

Colombian Forces Storm Supermarket and Free 76 Hostages After 17-Hour Siege

Colombian security forces stormed an Éxito supermarket in Cartagena before dawn on Wednesday and freed all 76 shoppers and staff who had been held hostage for about 17 hours, police said — more than double the roughly 30 first reported. One of the gunmen was killed and four others were arrested after special forces used stun grenades to overwhelm the group. President Abelardo de la Espriella said the siege began after a failed attempt to rob an armoured cash vehicle on Tuesday morning, when the attackers, some disguised as police, fled into the store. Ten hostages, mostly elderly people, had been released during the day. Flights into Cartagena were briefly diverted to Barranquilla during the crisis, and the freed hostages were reunited with their families after medical checks.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pyongyang — Envoy Given 48 Hours to Leave25 November 2026

North Korea Expels Swiss Envoy and Closes Office in Row Over Children’s Camp Sanctions

North Korea has declared the head of Switzerland’s diplomatic office in Pyongyang, Sylvain Astier, persona non grata and ordered him to leave within 48 hours, while demanding the office itself close, state media KCNA said on Wednesday. Pyongyang called Switzerland’s decision earlier this month to sanction the Songdowon International Children’s Camp an overtly hostile act. The camp, near Wonsan, is accused by Western governments of helping to transfer and indoctrinate children from Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine, and is also sanctioned by the EU and the UK. Bern said it regretted the decision and would bring Astier back via its embassy in Beijing, noting the office had only reopened on 19 October after a visit by Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis and that its sanctions policy is separate from its commitment to dialogue.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — One Year On, 168 Remembered26 November 2026

Hong Kong Marks First Anniversary of Wang Fuk Court Fire That Killed 168

Hong Kong on Thursday marked one year since the fire at the Wang Fuk Court housing estate in Tai Po killed 168 people and displaced nearly 5,000, the city’s deadliest blaze since 1948. The fire tore through seven of the estate’s eight towers while renovation work was under way. On Wednesday evening several hundred people attended a requiem mass at the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception, where the names of all 168 victims were read aloud, and visitors laid flowers near the still-vacant, blackened blocks. The government said it would not hold official memorial events but would not block lawful commemorations. Chief Executive John Lee, who apologised last month after an independent report found four departments partly responsible and the housing secretary resigned, has pledged to press ahead with rehousing and reforms, backed by a support fund of HK$630 million. An investigation into individual officials is due to finish by the end of January 2027.
