Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — A Fourth Day Down5 November 2026

Brent Near $93.65: The Oil Sell-Off Runs Into a Fourth Day

Oil kept falling on Thursday morning, extending the week’s rout into a fourth session. Brent futures were down about 97 cents, or 1 per cent, at $93.65 a barrel in early Asian trade, while US West Texas Intermediate slipped about 1.3 per cent to $86.98 — both benchmarks now roughly 15 to 16 per cent lower for the week and at their weakest since the end of August. The logic of the trade has not changed since Wednesday’s near-9 per cent plunge: with Democrats holding both chambers of Congress and the White House publicly conceding that the Iran war drove up fuel prices and cost Republicans the election, traders are betting that political pressure in Washington makes a negotiated end to the eight-month conflict — and an easing of the squeeze on Strait of Hormuz shipping — more likely than fresh escalation. The president’s statement that the United States has no immediate plans for new strikes on Iran reinforced that reading. Analysts cautioned the move could reverse quickly: JPMorgan estimates the market was already in surplus of about 1.1 million barrels a day in September, widening to 1.4 million in October, yet physical disruption through Hormuz persists, and US data showed crude, gasoline and distillate stocks all falling last week as demand hit a one-year high.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — The Early Count5 November 2026

ANC 35%, DA 29% — With Barely One District in Twenty Counted

South Africa’s count moved from a trickle to a stream overnight, but it remains far too early to call the local elections. With roughly 1,100 of 23,696 voting districts reported — under 5 per cent — the Electoral Commission’s figures, tracked by ESP’s results service, put the ANC first nationally on about 35.0 per cent of the combined vote, ahead of the DA on 29.0 per cent, the Patriotic Alliance on 5.7 per cent, the Freedom Front Plus on 5.2 per cent, the EFF on 4.3 per cent and the MK Party on 3.9 per cent. The provincial picture is sharply split. In Gauteng, the economic heartland, the DA leads the ward ballot with about 35.2 per cent against the ANC’s 19.5 per cent; in the Western Cape the DA is well ahead on about 54.4 per cent; in KwaZulu-Natal the MK Party leads with roughly 33.4 per cent to the ANC’s 28.1 per cent; and in the Eastern Cape the ANC holds a clear lead at about 51.6 per cent. Turnout in the districts counted so far sits between roughly 45 and 56 per cent depending on the province. The commission has seven days to declare final results and has indicated they should be known by Sunday, after which councils — and the coalition bargaining expected in the big metros — must be constituted within 14 days.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Final Day of Consultations5 November 2026

Likud Goes Last: Herzog Finishes Asking, and Eisenkot Waits for the Mandate

Israel’s government-formation process reaches its decision point on Thursday. President Isaac Herzog, who formally accepted the certified results of the 27 October election on Wednesday, concludes his consultations at the President’s Residence in Jerusalem with the largest factions — Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar and, last of all, Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud. The arithmetic has barely moved: Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53 of the Knesset’s 120 seats and Eisenkot’s 48, with Arab-led parties taking a record 19 — and nobody at the 61 needed to govern. But recommendations, which are what the president weighs, favour Eisenkot: five opposition parties plus the Arab-led Ra’am put him at about 56 endorsements, after Ra’am’s decision to back him improved his prospects further on Wednesday. The Arab-led Joint List has still not declared its position, and the ultra-Orthodox Degel HaTorah faction has said it will recommend no one. Under Israeli law Herzog must hand the mandate to the lawmaker he judges most likely to succeed, not simply the one with the most seats — and Israeli media report Eisenkot is widely expected to receive it, giving him 28 days, extendable by 14, to assemble a coalition. Herzog has until 11 November to announce his choice; the new Knesset is sworn in on 10 November regardless.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — An Honour, Then a Meeting5 November 2026

Kyiv Gives Melania Trump Its Highest Honour for Women — and Wants Oleshky Repeated

Ukraine has awarded US First Lady Melania Trump the Order of Princess Olga, First Class — its highest state honour for women — for her role in brokering the localised ceasefire around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson region. The six-day truce, which ran from late October into the first days of November, held against most expectations: aid convoys delivered food and medicine, and roughly 750 to 800 people were evacuated from a front-line town where about 2,000 residents remained. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov praised her humanitarian efforts on Wednesday and said Moscow hoped they would continue. On Thursday she hosts the follow-up at the White House, bringing together Ukrainian parliamentary faction leader David Arakhamia and human-rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets with Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev and human-rights official Yana Lantratova — the two ombudsmen having travelled to Oleshky to oversee the evacuation in person. Kyiv’s aim is explicit: to use Oleshky as a template for repatriating civilians trapped in other occupied territories. The meeting is a prelude, not the main event — US-mediated trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and Washington follow in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the first such round since February.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — The Wave Below Congress4 November 2026

Texas Falls, Georgia Makes History: The Democratic Wave Rolled Through the States Too

The midterm earthquake did not stop at Capitol Hill. Tracked by the Associated Press and the National Conference of State Legislatures, Democrats won full control of state governments — governor plus both legislative chambers — in five more states, and now hold 28 governorships to the Republicans’ 20, with two races still uncalled. The biggest single flip was Texas: Gina Hinojosa defeated Governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, becoming the state’s first Latina governor and its first Democratic one since 1995, while Democrats also took a majority in the Texas state House, flipping more than 20 Republican-held seats in a chamber the party last ran in 2003. In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms beat billionaire Rick Jackson to become the first Black woman ever elected governor of a US state. Other Senate results underlined the scale of the night: James Talarico defeated Ken Paxton in Texas despite heavy spending against him, Sherrod Brown returned by ousting Jon Husted in Ohio, and a pastor, Adam Hamilton, beat Roger Marshall in Kansas — that state’s first Democratic senator since 1932. All of these remain projections from AP race calls until states certify their results over the coming weeks.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea — Missiles Again5 November 2026

Pyongyang Shows Off a New Air-Defence Missile and a Cruise-Missile Strike Drill

North Korea has test-fired a newly designed air-defence missile system and staged a long-range strategic cruise-missile strike drill, state media reported on Thursday — the fourth weapons launch in a month. The Korean Central News Agency said Wednesday’s air-defence test, run by the Missile Administration and overseen by Central Military Commission member Kim Jong-sik, confirmed the system’s technical specifications, including new materials, multiple guidance modes and its ability to identify and track targets, though it disclosed no range, launch site or target details. Separately, a firepower sub-unit of the eastern long-range cruise-missile unit struck a virtual airport target, which KCNA said it hit “perfectly”. South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff had earlier reported several missiles fired toward the sea from the Sondok area of South Hamgyong Province, assessed to have flown less than 300 kilometres, and said analysts were still determining whether they were close-range ballistic missiles, surface-to-air missiles or cruise missiles. The display continues a busy few weeks of demonstrations that have included cluster munitions, attack drones, a precision-guided torpedo and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile; cruise-missile tests, unlike ballistic ones, are not banned under UN Security Council sanctions.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — Sentencing Day5 November 2026

Joshua Wong Faces His Sentence: The Collusion Case That Could Mean Life

Joshua Wong, the activist who became the international face of Hong Kong’s pro-democracy movement, was due before the High Court on Thursday to be sentenced for “foreign collusion” under the Beijing-imposed national security law — an offence carrying a maximum of life in prison. Prosecutors alleged that between July and November 2020 Wong conspired with exiled activist Nathan Law and others to ask foreign governments and institutions to impose sanctions or blockades on Hong Kong and China; he pleaded guilty in September, a step that usually reduces a sentence. The central question for Judge William Tam is whether the offence is of a “grave nature” — if so, the law mandates between ten years and life. Wong, who turned 30 last month, has spent almost six years in jail across several convictions and more than 2,000 days behind bars in total; he is already serving four years and eight months for conspiracy to commit subversion over the unofficial 2020 democracy primary, a term that had been expected to end his imprisonment in January or 2027 depending on the calculation in different reports. Rights groups say the second prosecution was brought in June 2025 precisely to keep him in custody. He co-founded the student movement Scholarism as a teenager, led the 2014 Umbrella Movement era protests and disbanded his party Demosisto the day the security law took effect in 2020.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia–Sudan — Tit for Tat5 November 2026

Expulsions Answered With Expulsions: Ethiopia and Sudan’s Border Row Goes Diplomatic

Ethiopia and Sudan have carried out reciprocal diplomatic expulsions, senior officials confirmed, as each accuses the other of arming its enemies. Addis Ababa declared six staff of Sudan’s consulate in Asosa, in the Benishangul-Gumuz region near the border, persona non grata on 2 November and gave them 48 hours to leave; the consulate was ordered closed, Sudan’s ambassador was summoned to be told, and the staff departed on Tuesday evening. Khartoum answered on Wednesday by declaring the staff of Ethiopia’s consulate in Gedaref, also near the border, persona non grata with the same 48 hours to go. Behind the row is Sudan’s civil war: its army-aligned government says it has documented 70 attacks launched this year from inside Ethiopian territory by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, including drone strikes on electricity infrastructure such as the Al-Markhiyat power station near Omdurman, and on Sunday it lodged formal complaints against Ethiopia with both the UN Security Council and the African Union’s Peace and Security Council, alleging the drones were supplied by the United Arab Emirates and launched from Ethiopia. Addis Ababa denies backing the RSF and counters that Sudan supports Tigrayan rebels who attacked government forces in September. The expulsions follow Ethiopia’s separate decision on 1 October to expel ten Eritrean diplomats.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Decision at Noon5 November 2026

The Bank of England Decides at Noon, With a First Rise Since 2023 Almost Fully Priced

At noon in London on Thursday the Bank of England announces whether Bank Rate rises for the first time in more than three years. Going into the decision, markets had priced a roughly 96 to 97 per cent chance that the Monetary Policy Committee lifts the rate by a quarter point, from 3.75 per cent — its level since a cut last December — to 4.00 per cent, alongside a fresh Monetary Policy Report with updated growth and inflation forecasts. The argument for moving is prices: consumer inflation rose 3.5 per cent in the year to September, up from 3.1 per cent in August and the highest in almost a year, against a 2 per cent target, with Middle East-driven energy costs a central factor. The committee was already split 6–3 in September, when three members wanted an immediate rise to 4 per cent; economist surveys point to a 7–2 or 6–3 vote this time. Precisely because the outcome is so heavily priced, traders expect the vote split and the Bank’s forward guidance to matter more for sterling than the quarter point itself. Households will feel it in the refinancing queue: research cited in coverage puts about £62 billion of fixed-rate buy-to-let loans alone maturing in the year to next August, and markets are already pricing several further rises during 2027.

Latest · Global Affairs · Space — Swift Comes Home4 November 2026

Built for Two Years, It Worked for Twenty-Two: NASA’s Swift Observatory Burns Up Over Sumatra

NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory has ended its mission the way it spent it — in a flash. The US Space Force confirmed the 1,452-kilogram telescope re-entered the atmosphere at 7:39 a.m. Eastern Time on Wednesday over Indonesia’s North Sumatra province, at roughly 22 times the speed of sound and about 200 miles north of the equator. NASA expected most of the spacecraft to burn up and said some durable components might survive, with the pre-entry risk of harm to anyone on the ground put at about one in 2,100; no damage or injuries were reported. Swift had been launched from Cape Canaveral on 20 November 2004 for a planned two-year mission and instead worked for nearly 22, detecting more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts — the most violent explosions known — plus hundreds of supernovae, black holes shredding stars, and comets, feeding some 7,000 scientific papers and a public archive of about 12 terabytes. Its end was written by the sun: heightened solar activity thickened the upper atmosphere’s drag, its orbit decayed, and it made its final observation on 20 October from about 171 miles up. A last-ditch rescue — a commercial robotic spacecraft, LINK, launched in July by Arizona start-up Katalyst to latch on and tow Swift higher — failed months before the fall, and the rescuer itself re-entered in September.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — The Brush, Recognised5 November 2026

Shodo Nears the World List: UNESCO Advisers Back Japan’s Art of the Brush

Japanese calligraphy is one step from joining UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list. An advisory body to the UN cultural agency has recommended inscribing shodo — the art of writing kanji and kana with an ink brush, black ink and inkstone, using techniques refined over centuries — Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs announced on Thursday. A formal decision is expected from UNESCO’s intergovernmental committee when it meets in Xiamen, China, from 30 November to 5 December; if approved, shodo would become Japan’s 24th entry on the list, joining items such as kabuki and the traditional knowledge behind sake and shochu brewing. Tokyo selected shodo as its candidate in 2023 and filed the application in 2024, arguing that calligraphy remains woven into everyday life — from New Year writing ceremonies to school classrooms — rather than surviving only as a museum art. It has been registered as a national intangible cultural property since 2021. Culture Minister Seki Yoshihiro welcomed the recommendation in a statement, and the government’s case to UNESCO rested on presenting the practice as evidence of the diversity and depth of Japanese culture.
