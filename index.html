Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — APEC Week Opens17 November 2026

Shenzhen Takes the Stage: Xi Opens the CEO Summit as the World Waits for Thursday’s Three-President Meeting

APEC week is under way in Shenzhen. The CEO Summit of business leaders opened on Tuesday ahead of the Economic Leaders’ Meeting on Wednesday and Thursday, with President Xi Jinping set to host the gathering in China’s southern technology hub. The diplomatic centrepiece remains Thursday’s first-ever trilateral meeting between Xi, US President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, confirmed by Beijing on Monday after earlier announcements from Washington and Moscow. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning said the three leaders will exchange views on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability. US officials say Trump wants nuclear arms reductions at the centre of the talks — the last US–Russia arms pact expired in February — and intends to raise the Iran war, while Ukraine is not expected on the trilateral agenda. Trump and Xi will also hold a separate bilateral meeting and working lunch, their third encounter this year; no separate Trump–Putin meeting is planned. Analysts caution the trilateral may prove heavier on symbolism than on breakthroughs.

Kyiv’s Two Dnipro Bridges Are Hit in the Night Zelenskyy Warned About — and Ukraine Answers at the Crimea Bridge

Russian strikes hit Kyiv’s North and South bridges across the Dnipro River late on Monday, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said, in an overnight attack that also damaged critical infrastructure and disrupted heating and water supplies in parts of the capital. Klitschko posted the preliminary report on Telegram just before midnight, shortly after the air force warned of incoming missiles; Reuters witnesses heard a series of loud explosions. It also coincided with diplomacy: Zelenskyy had spent Monday in talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner and European representatives on the next steps toward negotiations. Ukraine struck back across the map — drones headed toward Russia’s capital region, and Crimea’s Moscow-installed governor said the bridge linking the annexed peninsula to Russia was hit for a second consecutive day, with traffic suspended. Ukrainian officials also confirmed the final toll from an earlier strike on the southern village of Komyshuvakha has reached 72, among the deadliest single bombardments of the war.

Latest · Americas · United States — Four Outages in Five Days17 November 2026

A Bank Bug, an Airline Freeze, a Grounded Country: America’s Systems Keep Failing — and None of It Was a Hack

For the fourth time in five days, a core American system failed on Monday — and for the fourth time, officials said it was not a cyberattack. A software bug in JPMorgan Chase’s on-premise hardware slowed transactions for most of the business day, with more than 5,000 outage reports logged against the country’s largest bank and knock-on delays reported by customers of Bank of America, Wells Fargo, US Bank and users of the Zelle payment service. Chase said transactions were posting normally by early evening and stressed the problem was not systemic. The same afternoon, United Airlines resolved a systemwide outage in its departure and weight-and-balance systems that halted departures for about 90 minutes and delayed more than 700 flights, though aircraft already airborne and United Express services were unaffected. The two failures followed last Thursday’s global Microsoft outage affecting Outlook, Teams and Copilot, and Friday’s FAA network failure, which triggered the first nationwide ground stop in 25 years, more than 18,000 cancellations over the weekend and a funding fight in Washington over a proposed $50 billion fix. Four failures, four technical explanations — and a growing argument about how fragile the underlying infrastructure has become.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Draft on the Table17 November 2026

“Accelerated Action to Phase Out Fossil Fuels”: COP31’s First Draft Sets Up the Fight of Its Final Week

COP31’s endgame now has a text. Australia, which chairs the formal negotiations under the summit’s split Turkey–Australia presidency, released an 18-page first draft of the cover decision on Monday, drawn from more than 500 pages of submissions by over 160 countries. Its most contested line calls for accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, including an immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems — stronger wording than past deals, but with no global timetable attached. The draft also carries the presidency’s 35-by-35 electrification push, raising electricity’s share of final energy demand to 35 percent by 2035, and developing countries’ demand to triple adaptation finance to at least $120 billion. Negotiations president Chris Bowen conceded elements would not survive unchanged: agreement has been reached on 48 items, with around 30 mandates still open. Pacific island states are pressing for firm phase-out language against opposition from Russia and the Arab Group, while more than 60 civil-society organisations accused the presidency of sidelining finance and just-transition questions. Ministers take over for the final stretch to Friday’s deadline, with science discussions recording warming of 1.47°C for January to September 2026.

Nvidia reports third-quarter results after US markets close on Tuesday — its first earnings since Jensen Huang, co-founder and chief executive for 33 years, resigned from all his roles last week. Tonight’s numbers will be read less as a verdict on demand for AI chips than on whether Nvidia can be Nvidia without Huang.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Banda Sea Earthquake17 November 2026

Magnitude 6.6 Under the Banda Sea: A Strong Quake Near Tual, a Green Alert, and No Tsunami Threat

A magnitude 6.6 earthquake struck beneath the Banda Sea late on Monday, about 148 kilometres west-southwest of Tual in Indonesia’s Maluku Islands, the US Geological Survey reported. The quake struck at 21:11 UTC — early Tuesday morning local time — at a depth of about 68 kilometres. USGS issued a Green alert, indicating a low likelihood of casualties or economic damage, and estimated roughly 8,100 people were exposed to moderate shaking, with no population estimated at the strongest intensities. Indonesia’s BMKG agency measured the event differently, at magnitude 7.1 and 115 kilometres deep, and said it carried no tsunami potential; weak shaking was reported across Tual, Saumlaki, Dobo, Sorong, Manokwari, Fakfak and other towns in the region. Sri Lanka’s tsunami warning centre likewise confirmed no threat to its coasts. There were no immediate reports of casualties or damage. Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where offshore quakes of this size are frequent; the depth and distance from shore appear to have limited this one’s impact.

93,000 Tents In or 150,000 Waiting? Gaza’s Flooded Camps Sit Between Two Governments’ Numbers

Gaza’s winter shelter crisis remains a dispute about numbers as much as tents. The US-backed Board of Peace, which oversees President Trump’s Gaza plan, has pressed Israel to authorise far more shelter, sanitation and drainage supplies after the season’s first storms flooded more than 70,000 tents and a collapsed sand embankment near Deir al-Balah killed at least five displaced people. Israel’s account points the other way: the military and COGAT say a winter preparedness plan run with the Board of Peace, the UN and donor countries has brought in more than 91,000 tents — a figure later updated to over 93,000 — plus more than 8,600 truckloads of tarpaulins, blankets and winter clothing, sewage pumping equipment and water-network repairs, with about 600 trucks entering daily. The UN says its in-Gaza stocks of tents and blankets are virtually exhausted while more than 150,000 tents wait outside the territory, and aid groups note Israel still bars mobile homes they consider essential. About 1.4 million people, roughly 65 percent of Gaza’s population, are living in tents or makeshift shelters, many of them worn through three winters.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola at Six Months15 November 2026

400 to 500 New Cases a Week: Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Is the Widest Ever — and Still Spreading

Six months after it was declared, Congo’s Ebola outbreak is still not under control, the Africa CDC said in a joint stocktake with the WHO and UN partners. More than 11,200 infections and about 5,500 deaths have been recorded since 15 May, making this the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, driven by the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no licensed vaccine or treatment. The virus has spread from three health zones in one province to 67 zones across eight provinces, with cases also traced into neighbouring countries — the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak in Africa. Between 400 and 500 new cases are still reported each week, more than 80 percent of them outside known contact lists, and children account for over a quarter of cases and a third of deaths; seven in ten infected children under five have died. Responders cite deadly attacks on health workers — a recent assault in Cantine-Aloya killed 66 civilians, including three health workers — alongside unpaid staff and shortages. About 3,000 people have recovered. Africa CDC director-general Jean Kaseya warned that if the outbreak is not stopped now, it could become the worst the world has seen.
