Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Precision Strike, Confirmed18 November 2026

A Missile Site on Qeshm, a Radar in Bahrain: Washington Names What It Hit

The exchange around the Strait of Hormuz acquired firmer outlines overnight. A United States military official said American fighter jets used precision munitions against a Revolutionary Guards missile-launch site on Qeshm Island, describing the action as self-defence after Iran’s drone operation against a US-operated radar installation at Bahrain’s Sheikh Isa Air Base. Iranian officials, quoted through the state agency IRNA, said five to six explosions were heard on the island on Tuesday evening and insisted residential, commercial and infrastructure areas were untouched, with local services on full alert. Bahrain said its air defences intercepted the incoming projectiles without casualties or damage. Tehran separately said three tankers struck naval mines while trying to transit the strait, and reporting from the scene noted a tanker crew reported missing even as mediators Oman and Qatar remained in contact with Iran. The waterway, which carried roughly a fifth of world oil and liquefied gas before the war, stays partly blocked, and a third American carrier group, led by USS Theodore Roosevelt, is now in the region.

Latest · Europe · Kerch — An Arch Down, by Ukrainian Weapons18 November 2026

Neptune on the Rails, Palianytsia on the Road: Kyiv Details How the Kerch Bridge Fell

Ukraine has filled in the mechanics of its bridge operation. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack on the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to annexed Crimea was carried out entirely with domestically made systems — Neptune missiles and Ruta and FP-5 drones against the railway section, Palianytsia drones against the road section — as the seventh stage of a weeks-long effort to wear down Russian air defences. The Security Service said a metal arch and the road span beneath it were destroyed and the railway arch damaged; road traffic was severed, while Russian officials said rail traffic resumed by Tuesday afternoon and the Crimea governor, Sergei Aksyonov, confirmed a second day of suspension after another attack in which two people in Crimea were reported killed. In Kyiv, seven Russian ballistic missiles hit the Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro, killing at least two people, closing both crossings and leaving long queues on a third. Moscow said it downed 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight. The timing matters: the strike landed on the eve of President Vladimir Putin’s trip to Asia for meetings with Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping.

2,000 Business Leaders First, Three Presidents on Thursday: Shenzhen’s APEC Week Begins

China’s APEC week is now under way in Shenzhen. Latest · Middle East · Jerusalem — 66 to 5218 November 2026

After Six Hours of Filibuster, Kahana Takes the Chair: Netanyahu Loses the Knesset’s Agenda

The vote that was still running at last night’s close has finished. Late on Tuesday the Knesset elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party as its 26th speaker by 66 votes to 52 for Likud’s Ofir Katz, replacing Likud’s Amir Ohana after more than six hours of filibustering by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc. Both candidates abstained in the separate ballots. Kahana’s majority came from Eisenkot’s four-party bloc together with the Arab-majority Ra’am and Joint List — the same 67-seat alignment, minus the candidates themselves, that emerged from the 27 October election, in which Netanyahu’s camp took 53 seats. In his first remarks Kahana promised to work for the whole public regardless of religion, race or gender and said the next government would respect court rulings. The result is the Eisenkot bloc’s first decisive institutional move since the election and gives it control of the legislative timetable while Eisenkot, tasked by President Isaac Herzog with forming a government, still has weeks to complete that negotiation. Separately, the elections chief said a Shin Bet probe into alleged vote-buying had so far found no evidence of activity that could have changed the result, and Foreign Minister Gideon Sa’ar vowed countermeasures after Britain’s settlement sanctions.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — The Decisive Week18 November 2026

Ministers in a Divan, 80 Signatures on AI: COP31 Turns from Technical Text to Political Bargain

COP31 in Antalya has entered its second and final week with the argument moving upstairs. Ministers arriving for Tuesday’s high-level segment inherit an 18-page draft overarching decision circulated late on Monday by Australia’s Chris Bowen, who is leading negotiations under a split presidency with Türkiye — a text some countries dispute in content and others dispute in principle, asking whether such a cover decision should exist at all. Bowen will convene ministers in a Divan, borrowing the Ottoman term for an imperial assembly, to hunt for compromises on finance, fossil-fuel language, adaptation and a just transition, against Friday’s deadline. Two side agreements gathered pace: the Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge, launched with the International Telecommunication Union, reached 80 endorsements and 271 commitments to make AI more sustainable, and governments launched a Green Industrialization Compact aimed at cleaner steel and cement and at getting technology to developing countries. A separate fight continues over timing — vulnerable states want the next IPCC assessment ready in 2028 to feed the second Global Stocktake — while developing countries press, through the new Climate Finance Work Programme, to turn the Baku finance goal and a proposed tripling of adaptation finance into structured obligations rather than aspirations.

Latest · Europe · Kemerovo — 19 Confirmed Dead18 November 2026

Burning in Flight, Five Kilometres Short of the Runway: An An-12 Is Lost over Siberia

Russia’s Defence Ministry has now confirmed the loss that Russian media spent Tuesday piecing together. An Antonov An-12 military transport on a scheduled flight from Novosibirsk to Kansk, carrying spare parts, came down near the village of Yagunovo, south of Kemerovo in western Siberia, with 19 people on board including the crew — all of them killed. The ministry said the aircraft fell in an uninhabited area and that preliminary findings point to a technical malfunction, with an investigation under way involving an air force commission. Earlier accounts, citing the Telegram channels Baza and Mash and the newspaper Izvestia, described the ageing four-engine turboprop catching fire in the air, losing height sharply while attempting an emergency landing, and exploding on impact about five kilometres short of the airfield; a possible fuel leak was mentioned in those reports but has not been established. Kemerovo governor Ilya Seredyuk confirmed an aviation incident near Yagunovo and travelled to the site, where emergency services and investigators are working. The An-12 first flew in the late 1950s and remains in Russian military service.

Latest · Europe · Stara Pazova — A Pilot of 30 Years18 November 2026

Down in a Field at 08:45, No Ejection: Serbia Loses a MiG-29 and Major Bulajić

Serbia’s defence ministry confirmed a second military air loss on Tuesday, this one in Europe’s west. A MiG-29 of the 204th Aviation Brigade from Batajnica air base crashed at about 08:45 local time into a field near Stara Pazova, northwest of Belgrade, killing its pilot, Major Goran Bulajić, 53, who joined the armed forces in 1996 and left two children. Witness accounts reported low flying before the crash; the ministry said the pilot did not eject and reported no injuries on the ground, and it has not stated the purpose of the flight. Two inquiries are running in parallel: a ministry commission examining the circumstances and any organisational responsibility, an expert air force commission tasked with the technical cause, and — according to Serbian reporting — a criminal investigation led by the Sremska Mitrovica High Prosecutor’s Office. The site has been secured and findings are expected to take weeks. Serbia operates 14 MiG-29s built between 1987 and 1991, a Soviet-era fleet whose age is again part of the public discussion after this loss.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shanghai — A Warning Not Passed On18 November 2026

A Monkey Report, a Missing Death, a Nature Paper: Shanghai Sacks Two Gene-Editing Researchers

Shanghai’s municipal investigation into a child’s death in an experimental gene-editing trial has ended careers. The inquiry, published on 13 November, found that lead researcher Zilong Qiu of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine and associate researcher Kan Yang failed to pass a monkey safety report promptly to the clinical lead, left both that report and the child’s death out of their February 2026 Nature paper, and took money and gifts from the family. Both were dismissed, barred for five years from state science projects and awards, and entered in a national database of serious research-integrity violations. The six-year-old girl, who had Snijders Blok–Campeau syndrome, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder that is not itself life-threatening, received a base-editing therapy delivered by viral vector into her spinal fluid at Xinhua Hospital on 24 March 2025 and died on 31 March of thrombotic microangiopathy, a complication the hospital’s ethics committee attributed to the therapy. Her parents had funded the work with more than five million yuan. The clinical lead, Yu, was removed as head of Xinhua’s research department and demoted, and both institutions were reprimanded. The case, first exposed jointly by Science and Retraction Watch in July, was run under China’s fast-track investigator-initiated trial pathway.

540 Kilometres of Coast, One Landfall Window: Cyclone Akvan Aims at Andhra for Friday Noon

India’s east coast has a date to prepare for. The India Meteorological Department said Cyclone Akvan, centred on Tuesday morning about 610 kilometres east of Chennai over the south and west-central Bay of Bengal, will strengthen into a severe cyclonic storm by Tuesday night and a very severe storm by 19 November, then cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts around noon on 20 November anywhere on the 540-kilometre stretch between Nellore and Tuni, with sustained winds of 120–130 kilometres an hour gusting to 145. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu reviewed preparations on Tuesday; the state counts crops on 23.87 lakh hectares in the storm’s path and, as of Monday morning, had brought 30,635 of its 30,808 fishing boats back to shore, with 173 still at sea. Latest · Africa · South Africa — Trace the Guns18 November 2026

Six of Eight Named, Two Firearms in the Car: The Day After South Africa’s Twin Mass Shootings

The investigations into Sunday night’s two mass shootings — 17 dead, about 530 kilometres apart — produced their first details on Tuesday, and a new demand. In Sweetwaters, outside Pietermaritzburg, police said six of the eight men found dead in a seven-seater vehicle in the Mabane section have been identified, believed to be aged 20 to 23 and from around Mariannhill, and that two firearms, 14 rounds of ammunition and a blank gun were recovered inside the vehicle. In Jeffsville, in Atteridgeville west of Pretoria, the nine victims in a four-room shack — six men and three women, shot between about 10 and 11 p.m. on Sunday and found at 7:30 on Monday morning — remain under investigation by Gauteng’s serious and violent crimes unit, which is examining a possible link to the December 2025 hostel shooting in nearby Saulsville that killed 12 and wounded 13. No arrests and no motive have been announced in either case, and police say the two attacks are not connected. Gun Free South Africa urged investigators to go beyond identifying the shooters and trace how the firearms entered criminal hands, calling the killings a crisis; official figures released in August recorded 5,427 murders between April and June, down from 5,770 a year earlier.
