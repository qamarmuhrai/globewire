Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Barrage Before Talks8 November 2026

172 Drones, 36 Missiles, Only Four Stopped: Kyiv’s Grid Is Hit Again Before Abu Dhabi

Ukraine says Russia launched one of its heaviest recent barrages overnight — 172 drones and 36 ballistic missiles aimed mainly at Kyiv and the surrounding region — just days before Russian, Ukrainian and American negotiators meet in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said air defences downed 149 drones but only four ballistic missiles, blaming a shortage of Patriot interceptors, and said some of the missiles were North Korean-made KN-23s. The energy ministry said transmission links carrying power from nuclear plants toward Kyiv and central regions were damaged, along with substations in five other regions, leaving homes and businesses without electricity and water as temperatures near zero. At least two people were killed and around a dozen wounded nationwide, with homes, commercial buildings and a kindergarten reported damaged and the Northern Bridge across the Dnipro hit. Moscow said it struck military-industrial, energy and transport targets. Kyiv also struck back, including a reported hit on an electronics plant in Oryol, while Russia said it downed 411 Ukrainian drones.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Missiles Intercepted8 November 2026

Alerts in Dhahran, Then an All-Clear: Saudi Defences Stop Missiles Aimed at Riyadh and the East

Saudi Arabia spent Sunday morning under air-defence alerts. The Saudi-led coalition said its defences destroyed a ballistic missile launched by Yemen’s Houthis toward the oil-rich Eastern Province, then two more ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh, with Saudi Gazette putting the early-Sunday total at three ballistic and two cruise missiles intercepted. Civil defence warned residents in Dammam, Dhahran — home to Saudi Aramco’s headquarters — Abqaiq and Al-Ahsa, then lifted the alerts and said the danger had passed; no damage from the interceptions was reported. The coalition says at least 15 missiles and drones have been intercepted across the kingdom so far in November, as Houthi fire at airports, ports and energy sites intensifies alongside the government’s Dawn of Yemen offensive. Separately, Houthi health officials said on Saturday that 33 people, including women and children, were killed in coalition strikes near Sanaa — a claim the coalition has not commented on and which could not be independently verified.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf of Oman — Rescue at Sea8 November 2026

22 Sailors Off a Burning Ship: the UAE Coast Guard Answers in the Gulf of Oman

The United Arab Emirates National Guard said on Sunday that its Coast Guard rescued all 22 crew members from a cargo ship that caught fire in the Gulf of Oman. The crew, all Arab nationals, sent a distress report; specialised teams located the vessel, evacuated everyone on board, gave first aid and handed them to the relevant authorities, with all 22 reported in stable condition. The Guard did not name the ship, its flag or the cause of the fire. The rescue came in the same waters and within hours of US Central Command saying American forces had disabled the St Kitts-flagged M/V Huda after it left an Iranian port, part of a months-long blockade that Washington says has blocked more than $25 billion in Iranian crude exports. British maritime security body UKMTO had also reported an incident involving a bulk carrier and military forces east of Khor Fakkan on Saturday. Whether the burning ship and the disabled vessel are connected has not been stated by any authority.

Latest · Global Affairs · US–Iran — Backchannel Diplomacy7 November 2026

While Missiles Fly, Vance Writes to Tehran: a Fortnight of Quiet Talks Emerges

A second track is running alongside the blockade. Axios reports that Vice President JD Vance has spent the past two weeks directly involved in indirect talks with Iran aimed at a new ceasefire and a restart of nuclear negotiations, speaking almost daily with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, Pakistan’s army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and other mediators. Envoy Steve Witkoff and adviser Jared Kushner are also involved. Two weeks ago Vance sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, proposing a return to direct talks, and the sides have discussed a working-level technical meeting that has not yet taken place. President Donald Trump told reporters on Friday, “We are talking to them,” and officials say he is for now willing to let diplomacy run — while US forces remain ready to resume strikes quickly if he orders it. Vance has publicly blamed the Iran war for Republicans’ midterm losses, giving the diplomatic push a domestic logic as well.

Latest · Americas · United States — Miller Takes Over Monday6 November 2026

Defeat, Then a Hardliner Promoted: Stephen Miller Becomes Chief of Staff on Monday

President Donald Trump’s answer to a midterm drubbing is to promote the architect of his immigration crackdown. Stephen Miller, 41, deputy chief of staff and homeland security adviser, will replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff on Monday 9 November; Wiles, the first woman to hold the job and Trump’s longest-serving chief of staff, moves to his political operation. Trump announced the change on Truth Social on Friday, calling Miller one of his most trusted advisers. Democrats have taken both chambers — a net gain of seven Senate seats with Alaska and Mississippi still counting, and 253 House seats with 27 races left — and are expected to open investigations once they take control in January. Senate Democrat Chuck Schumer said Trump had learned nothing from the defeat. Miller’s elevation, urged by MAGA allies including Steve Bannon, signals a more confrontational final two years rather than a softening, and makes comprehensive immigration legislation far less likely.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — Taiz Market Strike6 November 2026

A Market Hit at Its Busiest Hour, Two Stories of Who Fired: Taiz Counts Its Dead

Dozens were killed and wounded when strikes hit the al-Hadara market in al-Waziyah district, Taiz province, on Friday — but almost everything else about the attack is disputed. The Houthi-run health ministry put the toll first at 30 dead and eight wounded, later at 25 dead and 30 injured, and blamed Saudi Arabia, saying an F-15 carried out three strikes at about 7 a.m. as shoppers crowded the bazaar; Houthi-linked reporting has given figures as high as 40 wounded. Yemen’s internationally recognised authorities in Taiz acknowledged an attack on a busy market but blamed the Houthis, saying a ballistic missile — not aircraft — did the killing and that no military aircraft operated over the district that day. The Associated Press could not independently verify either account, and no UN body or neutral hospital has confirmed a toll. The strike follows a similar market attack in the same province days earlier and lands as fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces intensifies across the south-west.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Mourning8 November 2026

Nine Dead in Total, a City in Mourning Until Wednesday: Kraków After the Square Attack

Poland is observing national mourning on Sunday and Monday after Saturday’s attack in Kraków’s Main Market Square, with city mourning declared until 11 November. Officials put the dead in the square at six — four women and two men, including a British national — with 16 wounded, among them two police officers and five foreign nationals. The suspect, a 25-year-old Polish man who drove onto the square at about 3 p.m. and fired at random with an automatic weapon in military-style clothing, was shot by police and remains in hospital after surgery. Searchers later found three more bodies — two women and a man, believed to be close family — at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 200 kilometres away. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the evidence points to radical ultra-nationalist views; the defence minister called the shooting an act of terror while stressing the gunman was never a soldier. It is described as Poland’s deadliest shooting since the fall of communism in 1989.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Earthquake8 November 2026

Strongest Shaking in Aichi Since 1997: Night Quake Injures 22 in Central Japan

An earthquake measured at magnitude 5.7 by the Japan Meteorological Agency — 5.5 by the US Geological Survey — struck western Shizuoka Prefecture at 1:04 a.m. on Sunday, about 40 kilometres underground. The strongest shaking, upper 5 on Japan’s intensity scale, was recorded in Chita, Aichi — the first tremor that strong in Aichi since March 1997 — with lower 5 shaking in parts of Aichi, Nagano and Gifu. At least 22 people were injured in Aichi and Shizuoka as residents rushed outside; shop windows broke and goods were scattered. The tremor was felt from Hamamatsu toward Kyoto, Nara and Yamanashi. No tsunami warning was issued. The agency has urged people in the hardest-hit districts to stay alert for up to a week — especially the next two to three days — and to watch for rockfalls and landslides if rain follows.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Mandate Day8 November 2026

67 Recommendations, 48 Seats of His Own: Eisenkot’s 28 Days Start in Jerusalem

President Isaac Herzog has formally tasked former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, 66, with forming Israel’s next government after the inconclusive 27 October election. The Yashar leader drew recommendations from factions holding 67 of the Knesset’s 120 seats against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — the widest backing for a first nominee since 2015 — but his own Jewish-led bloc holds only 48 seats, and the Arab-led parties whose endorsements lifted him have so far been ruled out as coalition partners by Eisenkot himself. Likud remains the largest party with 27 seats and Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53. Eisenkot now has 28 days, extendable by 14, to reach 61; failure passes the attempt to Netanyahu, and failure there points to another election in about 100 days. He would be Israel’s first prime minister from the Mizrahi community. His first test comes Tuesday, when the new Knesset is sworn in and elects a speaker.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — EU Question Reopens8 November 2026

“In My Lifetime”: Burnham Reopens the EU Door — and Brussels Lists the Price

Prime Minister Andy Burnham travels to Brussels on 20 November for the second UK–EU summit with a bilateral deal reported 90 per cent complete — covering food and drink checks, a youth mobility scheme and closer partnership, worth an estimated 0.4 per cent of GDP — but the politics have outrun the paperwork. Burnham has said he wants Britain back in the EU in his lifetime and may put rejoining in Labour’s next manifesto, while holding this parliament’s red lines against the single market, customs union and free movement. Former EU Brexit negotiator Michel Barnier has warned London there is no single-market access without free movement and no keeping the old budget rebate, though the pound might be negotiable. Polling now shows a majority favouring rejoin — about 55–56 per cent in recent YouGov and Ipsos surveys — but support falls sharply if the euro and Schengen are required, terms Burnham says he would never recommend.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan — Indictment6 November 2026

NT$170 Million and a 14-Year Ask: a Sitting Lawmaker Is Charged With Working for Beijing

Taiwan’s High Prosecutors’ Office has indicted independent legislator Kao Chin Su-mei — also known as May Chin — and three aides on national-security and corruption charges, alleging they built organisations for the Chinese Communist Party with more than NT$170 million (about US$5.2 million) channelled between 2005 and 2016 through relatives’ and friends’ accounts. Prosecutors say the money funded work in Indigenous communities and Kao Chin’s re-election campaigns, and that in return she echoed Beijing’s positions and disrupted the legislature on arms purchases, cross-strait and foreign-policy questions. They are seeking 14 years for Kao Chin and 15 for her former office director Chang Chun-chieh, who is believed to have fled Taiwan after release on bail. Kao Chin denies developing any organisation for the CCP or taking Chinese funding, and accuses prosecutors of rushing the indictment to divert attention from Chang’s escape. A separate June case against her and 23 others, over alleged embezzlement and unapproved COVID test-kit imports, is still pending.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — No Winner Yet8 November 2026

A Majority of One or Two, and Hundreds of Thousands of Votes Still Uncounted

New Zealand still has no confirmed government a day after polling. Saturday’s election left Christopher Luxon’s National and Chris Hipkins’ Labour both below 30 per cent after a campaign dominated by a weak economy and frustration with both major parties. On preliminary results National holds about 42 seats; with ACT’s 13 and New Zealand First’s 12, the incumbent bloc reaches roughly 67 seats in a parliament enlarged by overhangs to about 130–131 — a working majority of one or two. Labour’s 35–36 seats, the Greens’ record 16 and Te Pāti Māori’s five fall short. The centrist Opportunity Party, which cleared the 5 per cent threshold for the first time at about 5.9 per cent and seven seats, could yet hold the balance. Several hundred thousand special votes — cast abroad or outside a home electorate and historically favourable to the left — will not be counted before the official result on 27 November. Luxon governs as caretaker meanwhile; in 2023, coalition talks alone took 41 days.
