Latest · Americas · Canada20 October 2026

Alberta Votes Overwhelmingly to Stay in Canada, Rejecting the Road to Separation

Voters in Canada’s oil-rich province of Alberta have decisively rejected the path toward separation, with unofficial referendum results showing about three-quarters backing remaining part of Canada and roughly a quarter supporting the start of a process that could have led to a binding secession vote. The non-binding ballot, held on Monday, was the first vote of its kind in Canada since Quebec’s razor-thin 1995 referendum, and it came amid a surge of Canadian patriotism fuelled by US President Donald Trump’s tariffs and annexation threats. Prime Minister Mark Carney, who had campaigned hard for unity, said Albertans “chose unity, to build together, and to take care of each other,” while Premier Danielle Smith also welcomed the result. Separatist leaders pointed to the quarter of voters who backed their cause as a sign that a large minority still feels Alberta is treated unfairly within the federation.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza19 October 2026

UN Food Agency Cuts Gaza Assistance by a Third, Leaving Half a Million Without Support

The UN World Food Programme has announced it is reducing food assistance in Gaza by around one-third because donor funding has fallen short, a move that will strip critical support from some 500,000 people just as winter approaches. The agency will now reach about 1.1 million people a month, down from 1.6 million, with rations halved: families will receive one food parcel instead of two, a smaller bag of wheat flour, and daily hot meals will fall from 250,000 to 150,000, with households in hard-to-reach areas near the Israeli military’s “yellow line” getting a meal only every other day. WFP says an estimated 1.4 million Gazans — about 67% of the population — face crisis-level food insecurity or worse, and it urgently needs $450 million to sustain operations in Gaza and the West Bank through March. “It’s not because the needs are gone,” the agency’s country director said. “It’s because we don’t have the funding.”

Rubio Lands in Mumbai Saying a US–India Trade Deal Is “Very Close” to Done

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio arrived in Mumbai on Tuesday for talks with External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar, declaring that Washington and New Delhi are “very close” to finalising a long-negotiated trade agreement. After Mumbai, Rubio travels to Jamnagar in Gujarat, home to one of the world’s largest oil refineries, for business engagements.

Zelenskyy Meets Trump’s Envoys as Kyiv Accuses Russia of Breaking the Energy Ceasefire 76 Times

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has held talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner on the stalled push to end the war, saying Ukraine is ready to work “constructively” and “honestly” and expects the same approach from its partners — while warning NBC that it would be a “historic mistake” for Washington to formally side with Russia. Zelenskyy also thanked the United States for resuming intelligence sharing after a pause that Kyiv says left it exposed to missile strikes.

Latest · Global Affairs · Environment19 October 2026

World’s Nature Targets Face Their First Global Review as COP17 Opens in Yerevan

More than 10,000 delegates have gathered in Armenia’s capital for the UN biodiversity conference COP17, which opened on Monday and runs until 30 October — the halfway checkpoint for the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, the 2022 pact that set 23 targets to halt and reverse nature loss by 2030, including protecting 30% of land and seas. The early evidence is sobering: a UN “State of Biodiversity Action” report released days before the summit found that fewer than half of the national targets assessed are on track, despite nearly 90% of the convention’s 196 parties having set national goals. Negotiators in Yerevan will try to finalise the “Yerevan Pathway,” a package meant to close gaps in biodiversity finance and speed up delivery. “Commitments must be honoured,” said the convention’s executive secretary, Astrid Schomaker.

Latest · Europe · The Hague20 October 2026

Sanctioned by Washington, the International Criminal Court Is Shortlisted for the EU’s Top Rights Prize

The International Criminal Court has been named one of three finalists for the European Parliament’s Sakharov Prize, the EU’s highest human rights honour — a show of European support for the Hague tribunal as the United States moves to sanction it. The court shares the shortlist with Tanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu, who faces a possible death sentence on treason charges, and Iranian sisters Taraneh and Romina Rahimi, sentenced to death and 36 years in prison respectively over anti-government protests. Washington this month announced sanctions aimed at the ICC’s day-to-day operations, and President Donald Trump’s administration has vowed to dismantle the court “brick by brick” over its arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The prize, created in 1988 and named for Soviet dissident Andrei Sakharov, is awarded annually to those who defend human rights and democracy.

Latest · Africa · Development20 October 2026

World Bank Unveils Shared AI Computing Plan for Governments in Africa and Latin America

The World Bank Group has announced an artificial-intelligence computing initiative intended to give governments shared access to powerful GPU infrastructure on a pay-as-you-go basis, with an initial focus on Africa and Latin America. The AI Infrastructure and Readiness Initiative, launched alongside the bank’s first digital strategy and backed by Cassava Technologies and NVIDIA as knowledge partners, is pitched as an alternative to costly standalone national data centres. The strategy emphasises practical, locally adapted tools — described as “small AI” — such as detecting crop disease or assisting health workers, alongside safeguards for privacy and cybersecurity. The announcement, made in Seoul, did not name participating countries or give prices, funding amounts or a start date.

Latest · Africa · Football20 October 2026

Ruling on Senegal’s Appeal Over the Stripped Africa Cup of Nations Title Due at End of November

The Court of Arbitration for Sport says it will issue its full ruling at the end of November on Senegal’s appeal against being stripped of this year’s Africa Cup of Nations title. Senegal beat hosts Morocco 1–0 after extra time in January’s final in Rabat, but the Confederation of African Football later overturned the result and awarded Morocco a 3–0 victory, ruling that Senegal had forfeited the match when its players walked off the pitch for 14 minutes in protest at a late penalty — which Morocco then missed. Senegal, whose case rests on the argument that the referee’s decision was final and he allowed the match to be completed, lodged its appeal in March; arguments from Senegal, Morocco and CAF were heard in Lausanne on 8 October. CAS said it will now deliver a complete award, including its legal reasoning, a process that takes several weeks.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy20 October 2026

Refiners in Asia and Europe Are Losing Money on Every Barrel as Tanker Rates Soar Tenfold

Oil product prices are rising, yet refiners in Asia and Europe are slipping into loss: tanker freight rates have surged to more than ten times normal levels as the Middle East war disrupts shipping, and the cost of hauling crude now outruns the gains from higher fuel prices. According to LSEG data cited by Nikkei, Europe’s benchmark Rotterdam refining margin stood at minus $10.95 a barrel, while Singapore’s Asian benchmark has also fallen below zero — even as diesel “crack spreads” in the US and Europe have approached record highs. Freight for very large crude carriers moving oil from the Middle East to Asia and Europe, and from Africa to Europe, is running at $20–$40 a barrel, swamping the margin refiners earn between buying crude and selling finished products.
