Latest · Middle East · Gulf of Oman — Blockade27 November 2026

US Aircraft Disables Blockade-Runner in Gulf of Oman as Tehran Vows to Defy Embargo

US Central Command said an American aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier M/V Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday, disabling its propulsion after the vessel left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas and tried to run Washington’s naval blockade of Iranian ports. The civilian crew was unharmed. CENTCOM said the ship was bound for Singapore according to tracking data and is at least the sixth vessel disabled since the blockade resumed this summer, with 158 others redirected. An adviser to Iran’s supreme leader responded that Tehran would keep the Strait of Hormuz closed and work to break the embargo, while Iran’s navy says it continues to watch waters from the eastern strait to the northern Indian Ocean. The exchange came alongside Washington’s rejection of Iranian claims about seized tankers, a strike on the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln and a $2.5 million Hormuz transit toll.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Black Sea Ceasefire27 November 2026

Ukraine Offers Black Sea Ceasefire to Protect Grain Exports and Awaits Moscow’s Reply

Ukraine has put forward terms for a ceasefire in the Black Sea focused on restoring freedom of navigation and keeping agricultural exports moving, Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha told the Food from Ukraine conference in Kyiv on Friday. Sybiha said all proposals have been passed to partners and Kyiv is now waiting for Russia’s answer, thanking India, Türkiye and Egypt for mediation work aimed at lowering tensions. Moscow has pressed to fold Russian oil transport into any maritime truce, but Kyiv says oil belongs in a separate, broader discussion about an energy ceasefire and that the Black Sea arrangement should cover food shipments only. The offer would in effect revive the grain corridor that collapsed when Russia left the earlier deal in July 2023, and it comes as attacks on ports and shipping have again choked sailings from Odesa. On the same day, President Vladimir Putin attended a flag-raising for a new Russian nuclear submarine said to carry Zircon hypersonic missiles.

Latest · Europe · Russia — Mediterranean Flotilla27 November 2026

Russian Convoy Bound for Libya Turns Back After Ukrainian Drones Sink Warship in Benghazi

A Russian flotilla carrying military vehicles toward Libya has reversed course in the Mediterranean after Ukrainian maritime drones sank a Russian corvette in Benghazi port on 22 November, according to military and security sources cited by Reuters. A second convoy, escorted by the frigate Admiral Golovko, also turned near the Strait of Sicily on the day of the attack and is now paused off Algeria. The diversions, not previously reported, underline how far Ukraine’s sea-drone campaign now reaches — Benghazi lies roughly 2,000 kilometres from the Ukrainian battlefield — and how Moscow is scrambling to shield naval assets that support its presence in North Africa. A Ukrainian intelligence official confirmed the second flotilla’s halt. Russia has not publicly commented on the sinkings or the change of course.

Latest · Americas · United States — Air Force Cyber Breach27 November 2026

Hackers Breach US Air Force Flagship Cloud One Platform, Freezing Updates and Migrations

Unknown intruders have penetrated Cloud One, the US Air Force’s central cloud platform, in an intrusion discovered within the past month during upgrade work, people familiar with the matter told Reuters. One source said the attackers used a software flaw to reach the control plane — the management layer likened to a universal remote for the system — raising concern for tactical, personnel, pay and logistics data held across scores of applications. The Air Force has paused moving more back-office systems onto Cloud One, blocked updates to applications already running there even as fresh vulnerabilities emerge across the industry, and revoked administrator rights for some users. US Cyber Command has been brought in to assess the damage. Investigators have not identified who was behind the breach, which vulnerability was used, or whether data was taken. The Air Force declined to discuss the security status of specific systems. Cloud One, launched in 2017, combines services from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet and is run by Leidos since 2025.

Latest · Americas · Canada — Supreme Court Ruling27 November 2026

Canada’s Supreme Court Largely Upholds Quebec Ban on Religious Symbols for Public Servants

Canada’s Supreme Court on Friday largely upheld Quebec’s 2019 secularism law, Bill 21, which bars teachers, police officers, judges and other specified public employees from wearing religious symbols such as hijabs, turbans and kippahs at work. In a 520-page decision, Chief Justice Richard Wagner wrote that the law reflects Quebec’s legitimate and historical commitment to state laicity in a free and democratic society, and that the province validly invoked the Charter’s notwithstanding clause — which lets governments keep a law in force despite certain rights conflicts for up to five years at a time — when it passed and renewed the measure. The court made one exception: the rule requiring members of the National Assembly to work with faces uncovered will not apply to legislators, because it infringes democratic rights the override cannot displace. Rights groups condemned the outcome, with the National Council of Canadian Muslims calling it a dark day for constitutional democracy. The four-day hearing in March was among the longest in the court’s history.

Latest · Africa · Kenya — Shakahola Verdict27 November 2026

Kenyan Cult Leader Paul Mackenzie Found Guilty of Manslaughter Over 123 More Starvation Deaths

A court in Mombasa on Friday convicted Paul Mackenzie, founder of the Good News International Church, of manslaughter for the deaths of 123 followers in the Shakahola Forest, along with 29 co-accused including his wife Rhoda Mumbua. Magistrate Alex Ithuku found Mackenzie gave the directions to fast to death, with prosecutors saying he systematically persuaded followers to starve in a set order — children first, then women, then men — between January 2021 and September 2023. The case covered 238 victims in total; post-mortems showed 129 died of starvation while 86 remains were too skeletal to determine a cause. Mackenzie, arrested in April 2023 after bodies began emerging from the 800-acre coastal ranch, was already sentenced on 6 November to 200 years for murdering 191 people, all but one of them children. He pleaded not guilty to manslaughter and his lawyers say he will appeal after sentencing. Kenya’s human rights commission put the overall Shakahola toll at 429 in 2024.

Latest · Europe · Spain — Canary Islands Crossing27 November 2026

Fourteen Found Dead on Migrant Boat Rescued Off El Hierro as Canary Arrivals Hit Record

Fourteen people were found dead aboard a boat carrying about 100 people that was rescued 39 nautical miles south of El Hierro in Spain’s Canary Islands on Friday, the EFE news agency reported. The vessel, said to have left Banjul in The Gambia about ten days earlier, was towed to La Estaca port; six survivors were airlifted to hospital in serious condition. Separately, 84 survivors came ashore at La Restinga, where a reception centre and field hospital handle most arrivals to the island of 11,400 residents. Crossings to the archipelago have surged since October, reaching a record 2,100 arrivals in a single day on 25 November, most of them landing on El Hierro and overwhelming local authorities ahead of Spain’s general election, where migration is a central issue.

Trump Calls Pre-Election GDP Figures ‘Fake’ and Demands Probe After Upward Revision to 4.3 Percent

President Donald Trump on Thanksgiving accused his own Commerce Department of deliberately publishing wrong growth numbers before the 3 November midterms and demanded an immediate investigation. The outburst followed the Bureau of Economic Analysis revising third-quarter growth on Wednesday to an annualised 4.3 percent, up from the 3.4 percent advance estimate released on 29 October — a routine revision the bureau attributes to more complete data on investment and consumer spending. In a Truth Social post offering no evidence, Trump called the earlier figure a massive, purposeful error and asked why the correct number was not issued before the election, in which Republicans lost control of both houses of Congress. He had earlier that morning celebrated the higher figure as proof the economy was roaring. The Commerce Department did not immediately respond. Analysts note advance estimates regularly change as inventory, trade and services data arrive.

Flogging of Singer Parastoo Ahmadi Read as Warning From Iran’s Anxious Hardliners, Insiders Say

The flogging this week of singer Parastoo Ahmadi, 29, and four members of her production team — 74 lashes each for offending public decency — was a show of force by a clerical establishment straining to enforce obedience it once took for granted, Iranian insiders told Reuters. Analysts say the punishment, imposed as the country faces a hard wartime winter, signals anxiety rather than confidence: authorities are leaning on visible penalties for cultural offences to deter wider unrest while the war and the naval blockade squeeze daily life. The sources spoke on condition of anonymity.
