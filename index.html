Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Democrats Take Congress4 November 2026

A Blue Wave, Called: Democrats Win Both the Senate and the House

What looked like a strong Democratic night in the morning edition has hardened into a rout. Projections now give Democrats control of both chambers of Congress: the party cleared the 51-seat threshold in the Senate by flipping Republican seats in North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Kansas and Iowa, and the Associated Press later called the House for them as well after they passed 218 seats. The flips were striking in their geography — a Democratic Senate win in Kansas for the first time since the 1930s, a South Carolina seat held by Republicans since Strom Thurmond switched parties in the 1960s, and in Texas, James Talarico's defeat of Ken Paxton in the most expensive Senate race in US history, alongside Gina Hinojosa unseating three-term Governor Greg Abbott. In Maine, Troy Jackson ousted five-term Republican Susan Collins; in Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed became the first Muslim elected to the Senate. Analysts read the night as a referendum on President Trump — on inflation, tariffs, AI data centres and the Iran war — and the verdict reshapes his remaining term: a Congress able to investigate, block and bargain now faces a White House that has said it will accept the results.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Tankers and Mines4 November 2026

Four Tankers Burning, Two Seized, One Drone Down: Tehran's Hormuz Claim Meets a Wall of Caution

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday that four oil tankers were destroyed after striking naval mines in a prohibited lane south of the Strait of Hormuz and were still ablaze, that two further vessels had been halted, and that their air defences had shot down a US MQ-9 drone — the 56th, by their count. The claim arrived with no ship names, flags or positions attached, and no maritime authority or shipping company has confirmed a vessel lost to a mine; the one attack independently logged in the same waters was different in kind — a tanker hit by two projectiles in its engine room off Oman's Limah coast on Tuesday, reported by the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, with no casualties. The announcement is the latest in a pattern in which Guards' tallies of mined ships have repeatedly gone unsubstantiated by the forces monitoring the strait. It lands in the middle of a negotiation: Tehran has told mediators the US response to its seven conditions for reopening Hormuz — among them lifting sanctions and ending the naval blockade — is "unconvincing", while a two-day reopening plan brokered through Qatar waits on Washington's first move. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf read the US midterm results as Americans voting "no to war"; the mines claim reads as the other half of the message — the strait's price, stated in fire.

Latest · Middle East · US–Israel — The Aid Cutoff3 November 2026

$3.8 Billion a Year, Ending at Year's End: Trump Says US Aid to Israel Stops — Jerusalem Says It Wasn't Told

President Trump used a Fox News phone interview on election day to announce the end of one of the most durable arrangements in American foreign policy: the roughly $3.8 billion a year in military assistance Israel receives under a ten-year memorandum signed in 2016 and running to 2028. Trump said he and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had agreed months ago to stop the payments, that Netanyahu himself raised the idea, and that Israel — "a very thriving country" — no longer needs the money, though Washington would "help Israel if they need it." The Israeli side of the story does not yet match. Reporting in Israel says the Finance Ministry first learned of the plan from the broadcast, a spokesman for Netanyahu told NBC News he knew nothing of the remarks, and the existing agreement still has two years to run — no order, bill or budget request giving effect to the announcement has been made public. If it happens, the arithmetic is immediate: Israeli commentators put the hole at about 12 billion shekels a year in a defence budget already strained by three years of war, with weapons on order and stocks to replenish. The announcement also landed on the same day Israel was briefing Washington on its claim that Iran is developing chemical-weapons delivery methods — a coincidence that captures the new uncertainty: the alliance's military pipeline is being renegotiated in public, on live television, mid-war.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Deterrence and Diplomacy4 November 2026

'Maximum Deterrence' on the Shelf, Talks 'Stalled': Iran Prepares for a War It Says It Doesn't Want

Tehran spent Wednesday speaking in two registers at once. Its military announced that a "maximum deterrence" plan is ready against any fresh American attack, with target lists updated to cover US and Israeli assets across a wider map and a warning of an immediate response. Its diplomats, meanwhile, described the indirect talks with Washington as effectively stalled: the American answer to Iran's Hormuz conditions was dismissed as unconvincing, and Tehran repeated that the naval blockade must lift before any agreement. The backdrop is a strike that already happened — US forces hit Iran's Qeshm Island near the strait, an attack President Trump confirmed in a Fox interview while insisting the war will end on his terms; Iran's deputy foreign minister said Tehran reserves the right of self-defence, and Israel's military denied any involvement in the Qeshm operation. Qatar continues to shuttle between the two sides — a Qatari delegation held three hours of talks in Tehran — and US officials are reported to have strike plans ready from as early as Wednesday, even as flight trackers showed an airlift of advanced air-defence systems into Israel. Around the edges, the war's other fronts stayed warm: Israeli artillery fire in southern Lebanon, dozens of Palestinians detained in West Bank raids, and Israeli forces searching homes in a Syrian border town. Neither side has closed the diplomatic door; both are visibly pricing the alternative.

Kyiv and Zhytomyr Power Stations Hit as Russia Keeps Up the Daily Rhythm on Ukraine's Grid

The bombardment did not pause after Tuesday's mass strike. Latest · Europe · Spain — Minister Falls3 November 2026

Two Crashes, 115 Dead, One Resignation: Spain's Transport Minister Quits Ahead of the Snap Election

Spain's transport minister, Óscar Puente, resigned on Tuesday, six days after a high-speed collision near the village of Urda, south of Madrid, killed 69 people — and less than a year after another crash on the same Madrid–Seville line killed 46. Together the two disasters have taken 115 lives on what is the world's second-largest high-speed rail network. The Urda crash happened after a signalling failure automatically halted trains along a 63-kilometre stretch; a control centre then authorised a state-run Renfe service to proceed, and it ran into the back of a stationary private Iryo train. Puente told lawmakers he had decided to go within minutes of hearing of the crash but stayed until the emergency response, the first investigations and a compensation package were under way — a €30 million aid fund for victims and new rules requiring drivers to slow to 40 km/h after any signalling failure, with services on the line reduced until the safety agency completes its review and the long-delayed European signalling system is rolled out. He also withdrew from the snap general election Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has called for 29 November, saying a transport minister cannot remain in office after two accidents of this severity in under a year, whether or not the responsibility is personally his. Investigators have yet to establish the collision's direct cause.

'No Need to Sit Face to Face': Pyongyang Rules Out Talks, Then Fires Missiles Into the Sea

North Korea answered Washington twice on Wednesday — once in words, once in launches. Hours after Kim Yo Jong, the leader's sister and a senior official, dismissed any return to dialogue, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff reported several missiles fired from the Sondok area toward the sea off the east coast — the fourth launch event in a week, in a test cadence that has recently included cluster-warhead cruise and ballistic missiles and a salvo of around ten short-range ballistic missiles from Wonsan. Kim's statement was aimed at a specific charge: the FBI's attribution of the theft of more than $6 billion from the Binance cryptocurrency exchange to the North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor. She called the accusation a trite provocation and an extension of Washington's hostile policy, said Pyongyang had "no need to sit face to face with such unreliable and hostile persons", and made any future dialogue conditional on the US abandoning that policy — while conceding that choosing peace could, in principle, include talks. President Trump has spent two months hinting he wants to meet Kim Jong Un again, possibly around this month's APEC summit. Wednesday's sequence — rejection in the morning, missiles by midday — suggests Pyongyang intends to set the price of that meeting itself, and in public.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Voting Day4 November 2026

Dry Taps and Long Queues: South Africa Votes in Local Elections That Could Push the ANC Below 40%

South Africans queued from before sunrise on Wednesday for municipal elections being read, above all, as a verdict on the African National Congress. A record 29.2 million voters are registered across 23,696 stations to choose councils for eight metros, 44 districts and 205 local municipalities — the level of government responsible for the water, power and rubbish services whose failures dominate the campaign. The ANC, in power since 1994, fell below 50% in local elections for the first time in 2021 and lost its national majority in 2024; polling now suggests it could drop below 40%, with one Ipsos survey putting it at 30%. In Johannesburg, the biggest prize, some neighbourhoods have had dry taps for at least ten days after a water-system collapse, and a human-rights commission inquiry found nearly 45% of the city's piped water is lost to leaks and decay; the Democratic Alliance's Helen Zille is projected to lead there but short of a majority. Former president Jacob Zuma's MK party is contesting its first local elections, mounting a strong challenge in Durban. Polls close at 9 p.m. local time, results are due Sunday, and the outcome will frame both the road to the 2029 national election and the succession race to replace President Cyril Ramaphosa as ANC leader in 2027.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi's Air4 November 2026

AQI 484 in Rohini, 369 Across the Capital: Delhi Chokes Into Diwali With Curbs Already On

Delhi's annual smog season has arrived ahead of schedule and ahead of the festival that usually makes it worse. The capital's overall air quality index touched 451 on Tuesday morning, deep in the "severe" band, with the Rohini Sector 30 station recording 484; by Wednesday morning the citywide reading stood at 369 — a third straight day of "very poor" air, with neighbouring Gurugram and Noida in the same category and forecasts keeping the region between very poor and severe for days. PM2.5 particles are the main pollutant, fed by traffic, construction dust and stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana, where satellite data counted 1,394 farm fires between 1 October and 1 November. Authorities have already moved: Stage-II of the Graded Response Action Plan is in force across the National Capital Region, and the city's winter framework — including a 50% work-from-home arrangement running from November into February — has begun. The trajectory is what worries officials: readings climbed from 117 on 31 October to severe within four days, and Diwali fireworks have not yet started. For a city of more than 30 million, the question is no longer whether the air turns hazardous this month, but how long it stays there.

Latest · Americas · Florida — The Learjet Inquiry3 November 2026

The Tail Was Found Miles Away: Florida's Learjet Was Breaking Up Before It Hit the Ground

Federal investigators now have physical evidence that the Learjet 60 which crashed near Greenacres, Florida, on Sunday — killing all nine people aboard — was coming apart in the air. The National Transportation Safety Board said the majority of the jet's left tail elevator, the surface that controls pitch, was recovered in three pieces scattered between 1.3 and 3.8 miles from the crash site, a finding its board member Thomas Chapman called "very significant". The aircraft, flying from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas with seven passengers and two crew, plunged from about 35,000 feet through stormy South Florida weather; the crew had reported ice forming on the jet, and icing is a major focus of the inquiry, alongside whether the in-flight breakup caused the dive or resulted from it. The crash displaced close to 600 residents and left a water-filled crater that crews are now draining; investigators are still hoping to recover the cockpit voice recorder, which records suggest was installed at some point but may or may not have been aboard. The victims included a family of four travelling with their nanny and the 20-year-old co-pilot, a West Boca Raton High School graduate. It is the second-deadliest US aviation accident this year; a preliminary cause is not expected soon.

Latest · Europe · Aviation — The Hoax Count Rises4 November 2026

Closer to Ten Flights Than Seven: Europe Counts a Day of Bomb Threats That Found Nothing

The tally from Tuesday's wave of bomb threats across European aviation has grown as authorities compared notes: around ten flights were targeted within hours, alongside a cruise ship evacuated on the Greek island of Syros — and after searches on every aircraft, not a single device was found. Two flights were bound for Warsaw, including a LOT service from London Heathrow diverted to Modlin airport, which briefly closed, and an SAS flight from Copenhagen; two Ryanair services were searched at Rome's Fiumicino, the Dublin flight escorted in by Italian Eurofighters; two flights were checked at Sofia, one arriving from Israel after an emailed threat; and a Ryanair flight from Manchester triggered an emergency protocol on landing at Murcia in Spain. London's Metropolitan Police said it had been alerted to threats against four aircraft linked to Heathrow. Ryanair took the largest share, with four flights affected. No one has claimed responsibility and officials have pointed at no country, but aviation-risk analysts call seven-plus confirmed threats in a single day exceptional, and list the working hypotheses plainly: a single hoax sparking copycats, one group making many threats, or a state-backed hybrid-warfare operation designed to impose cost and rehearsal on Europe's security system. The wave followed a Monday diversion of a Luton–Sofia flight to Budapest under Hungarian fighter escort. Every flight landed safely; the disruption was the payload.
