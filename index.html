Latest · Global Affairs · APEC — First Trump-Xi-Putin Trilateral14 November 2026

Trump, Xi and Putin Plan a First-Ever Three-Way Meeting on Nuclear Arms at APEC in Shenzhen

The leaders of the world’s three biggest nuclear powers are set to sit down together for the first time. The White House confirmed on Friday that President Donald Trump will meet China’s Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin on Thursday 19 November on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, and will raise cuts to nuclear stockpiles, whose spread he calls very dangerous. The talks come nine months after the last US-Russia arms treaty expired in February, leaving the two largest arsenals uncapped for the first time in more than 50 years. Officials played down any deal: one senior US official called it the start of a conversation and said no agreements are expected, while Trump also plans to discuss the war with Iran but steer clear of Ukraine. Trump leaves Monday for a four-day Asia trip that also stops in the Philippines; Beijing and Moscow have yet to formally confirm the trilateral.

Latest · Africa · Niger — Market Convoy Ambushed13 November 2026

Gunmen Ambush Villagers Heading to Market in Western Niger, Killing at Least 38

A routine trip to market ended in one of the Sahel’s deadliest recent attacks. Local sources said residents of Tillim village were travelling early Thursday to the weekly market in Tera, in Niger’s Tillaberi region near the borders with Burkina Faso and Mali, under escort from a local self-defence group when unidentified gunmen ambushed them. Thirty-seven people were killed at the scene and another died of wounds on Friday; the dead include 11 self-defence fighters, 20 men and seven women, and residents said children were among the casualties. No group has been confirmed as responsible, though Russian reporting attributed the attack to Islamic State in the Greater Sahara. Tillaberi sits in the tri-border zone where jihadists linked to al-Qaeda and Islamic State operate, and conflict monitor ACLED ranked it the deadliest region in the central Sahel for civilians last year; close to half of Niger’s 450,000 displaced people live there.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Myanmar — Genocide Verdict Ahead14 November 2026

Myanmar Faces a Week of Reckoning: A Genocide Ruling in The Hague and an ASEAN Freeze Under Review

Myanmar’s past and future collide in the same week. On Thursday 19 November the International Court of Justice is due to rule whether Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority in the case brought by The Gambia over the 2017 crackdown that drove more than 700,000 people into Bangladesh — a judgment on state responsibility, not individual guilt, that will be final with no appeal. The verdict looms over a three-day ASEAN meeting starting Sunday in the Philippines, where the 11-nation bloc will debate the policy that has frozen Myanmar out of summits since the 2021 coup that ousted Aung San Suu Kyi and sparked civil war. Both threads converge on Min Aung Hlaing, installed as president in April after elections rejected by democracy watchdogs and now seeking diplomatic acceptance; analysts say the ruling could deepen divisions among ASEAN members over whether to normalise ties.

Latest · Global Affairs · COP31 — Fight Over Climate Science14 November 2026

EU Vows to ‘Fight’ for Climate Science at COP31 as a Row Over the Next IPCC Report Splits Antalya

A dispute about timing has become a fight about trust in science. At the COP31 summit in Antalya, Türkiye, the European Union said on Friday it would fight to defend the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change after accusing some countries of trying to undermine the world’s leading climate-science authority. Dozens of countries across Europe, Africa and the Pacific want the IPCC’s seventh assessment — its roughly seven-yearly definitive review — published in time to inform the 2028 global stocktake of carbon-cutting efforts, while Saudi Arabia, China and India are among those pushing to delay it until 2029. The IPCC’s October meeting ended without agreement and was suspended until 1 December; its chair Jim Skea warned the impasse creates operational problems and reputational risk. UN Secretary-General António Guterres backed timely publication, and Exeter scientist Pierre Friedlingstein argued updated figures matter more than seven-year-old ones, as pressure grows to settle the question in COP31’s final text.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — IEA Sees Oil Surplus13 November 2026

IEA: Oil Swings Back Into Surplus This Quarter as Gulf Exports Recover, With a Bigger Build in 2027

The oil market is rebalancing for the first time since the Iran war began. In its November report the International Energy Agency said global supply rose 2.3 million barrels a day in October to 105.4 million, after a 3 million rise in September, as Gulf exports recovered to about three-quarters of pre-war levels — prompting it to lift its fourth-quarter supply estimate by 3.3 million barrels a day and forecast a surplus this quarter that widens in 2027. Demand is projected to fall 2.7 million barrels a day in 2026 to 102.3 million before rebounding by 2 million in 2027, when supply could average 108.1 million. Observed stocks rose 52 million barrels in October, the first increase since June. The agency cautioned the surplus is not the whole story: diesel remains scarce and expensive in Europe, tanker attacks continue, and it will speed up releases from emergency stockpiles, with a further 71 million barrels due out through February.

The report landed a day after Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia chief amid the affair and spy allegations Beijing denies.

Latest · Americas · United States — Nationwide Flight Halt Lifted14 November 2026

US Lifts Nationwide Flight Halt After Air-Traffic Communications Failure Strands Thousands of Flights

American air travel ground to a halt on Friday afternoon and then slowly restarted. The Federal Aviation Administration ordered every departing flight in the country to stay on the ground after a telecommunications problem hit all 22 regional centres that guide aircraft between airports — only the second nationwide stop of departures since the September 11 attacks. Aircraft already airborne were allowed to land, and about 4,042 flights were in the air bound for US airports when the order came at 3:54 p.m. Eastern Time. FlightAware counted thousands of delays and more than a thousand cancellations, with later tallies putting cancellations above 4,700 and delays near 10,000. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy later said overloaded switches and routers had begun sending inconsistent data and shut down. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack or other threat, and the stop was lifted after more than four hours, though disruption rolled into Saturday.

Latest · Europe · Norway — Supreme Court Climate Ruling13 November 2026

Norway’s Supreme Court Rules Three Oil Approvals Invalid for Ignoring the Emissions From Burning the Fuel

Climate campaigners won a landmark procedural victory in Oslo. Sitting with 11 judges, Norway’s Supreme Court ruled on Friday that government approvals for three offshore developments — Equinor’s Breidablikk, Aker BP’s Tyrving and Aker BP’s larger Yggdrasil, due on stream next year — were invalid because their environmental assessments counted only production emissions and never assessed the combustion, or Scope 3, emissions produced when the oil and gas are burned. All 11 judges agreed such emissions must be assessed under the European impact-assessment rules that apply in Norway, and a 6–5 majority said cumulative emissions from other existing and approved shelf projects must also be considered. The court stressed the case was decided on procedure: output is not halted and licences do not automatically lapse, but the energy ministry must redo each assessment and decide whether permits can be reissued.

Iran Says an LNG Tanker Hit a Mine and Exploded in the Strait of Hormuz After Using an Unauthorised Route

Tehran says the world’s most important energy chokepoint has claimed another ship. The Revolutionary Guards navy said on Friday that a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas, travelling east of the strait on a southern passage it called unauthorised and dangerous and ignoring repeated warnings, struck a naval mine and exploded violently. No vessel name, flag or casualty details were given, and the claim could not be independently confirmed. It follows Iranian reports on Thursday that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the waterway after entering areas Tehran considers restricted. Since the war began in late February, Iran has insisted ships need its permission to transit and has attacked those that do not comply, even though US Central Command says it cleared mines from the main international lanes. Data cited from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy counts at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly tally since the conflict began.
