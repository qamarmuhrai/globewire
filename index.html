Russia and Ukraine Agree a Ceasefire for Starving Oleshky — and Peace Talks in Abu Dhabi Next Month

Russia and Ukraine confirmed on Friday that they have agreed a local ceasefire in Oleshky, the Russian-occupied town in southern Ukraine where roughly 1,700 to 2,000 residents — most of them elderly, alongside about 50 children — have been living with mined roads, scarce electricity and food and medicine supplies that ran out in February. The White House said the halt to fighting was brokered by First Lady Melania Trump, whose representative dealt directly with both leaderships; the Kremlin praised her role and said Russian and Ukrainian officers were still fixing the exact start date, with Washington citing 27 October in one statement and 29 October in another. The pause is meant to let aid convoys in and allow residents who wish to leave to evacuate to a destination of their choice, an operation Moscow said would take several days. President Volodymyr Zelensky added that negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States have agreed to hold technical-level meetings in the first half of November — in Abu Dhabi or possibly Switzerland — and said he hopes a leaders' summit will follow.

Attacks on Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz Hit a Record High in October

October has become the most dangerous month yet for commercial shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz, according to incident data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, which has tracked maritime attacks worldwide since 2017. The institute has logged 28 confirmed or reported attacks in October alone and 113 since the US-Israeli war with Iran began on February 28 — figures broadly in line with the International Maritime Organization's count of 120 incidents across the region. More than 30 very large crude carriers have been damaged, 11 of them this month; together they represent about 3.5 percent of the world's supertanker fleet, each able to carry some 2 million barrels of oil. Analysts say shipowners have grown more willing to load cargoes inside the Gulf despite the risk, drawn by daily freight rates above $1 million and steep discounts from regional producers. The worst single week came between October 5 and 11, when a dozen vessels were attacked.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen24 October 2026

UN Warns Time Is Running Out for Besieged Taiz, Where 800,000 People Are Cut Off From Aid

The United Nations appealed on Friday for immediate, sustained access to Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city, which Houthi forces have encircled since early October, severing the road that links it to the interim capital, Aden. UN aid coordination spokesman Jens Laerke said the city had been cut off for 20 days, with fighting making it impossible for aid workers to get in or residents to get out. Water production has fallen by about half as diesel for tanker trucks runs short, and the price of trucked water has roughly tripled. The World Health Organization said 42 health facilities in the city are reporting acute shortages of fuel, oxygen and medicine, while UNICEF screening this year has found more than 52,000 acutely malnourished children in Taiz, over 11,500 of them severely so. The World Food Programme says more than 800,000 people in the city have been cut off from assistance. The European Commission responded on Friday with an extra €22.5 million in aid for Yemen. Fighting since the collapse of the 2022 truce in July has displaced nearly 260,000 people, mostly in Taiz and Lahij provinces.

Latest · Americas · Brazil24 October 2026

Brazil's Presidential Runoff Is a Dead Heat a Day Before Voting

Brazil goes into Sunday's presidential runoff with the race statistically tied. A Datafolha poll released on Thursday put Senator Flávio Bolsonaro on 47 percent and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva on 46 percent, with 5 percent of voters planning a blank ballot and 2 percent undecided — well within the survey's two-point margin of error. Other pollsters tell the same story: Ipespe's latest tracking, released Friday, measured Bolsonaro at 50.5 percent of valid votes to Lula's 49.5, a gap that has shrunk from 5.4 points in the same series just over two weeks ago, while a Vox Brasil survey put Lula fractionally ahead. Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, surprised forecasters by leading the October 4 first round with 47.03 percent to Lula's 45.16. His Liberal Party also emerged from the congressional elections as the largest bloc in both chambers, meaning either winner will face a legislature tilted to the right. Turnout, as much as preference, may decide the result.

South Korea Renews Its Offer of Military Talks With the North After a DMZ Mine Blast

South Korea's defence ministry on Friday again proposed military talks with North Korea to pin down the exact course of their land border and reduce the risk of accidental clashes. The offer, announced by Deputy Minister Kim Hong-cheol, follows a landmine explosion in the Demilitarised Zone last month that seriously injured two South Korean soldiers; Seoul concluded the mines had been buried south of the Military Demarcation Line, a finding Pyongyang rejects. North Korean troops have been building tactical roads and fences and laying mines near the line in recent years, at times crossing it in the process, the ministry said. A similar proposal last November went unanswered. The renewed offer comes amid sharpening rhetoric: Kim Yo Jong, sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said this week the North would put loudspeakers on standby along the border and had placed unspecified new countermeasures on alert, while Seoul says it plans to raise the mine incident with the president of the UN Security Council as a violation of the armistice.

Austria Expels Four Russian Diplomats Over Activities 'Incompatible' With Their Status

Austria ordered four Vienna-based Russian diplomats to leave the country on Friday, saying security authorities had found they engaged in activities incompatible with their diplomatic status — the formula governments typically use for espionage. Three of the four served at Russia's embassy in Vienna; the fourth belonged to Russia's delegation to the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe, where he worked on military security and arms control. All four were declared personae non gratae. Russia's deputy foreign minister said a response would inevitably follow. The expulsions bring to 18 the number of Russian diplomats Austria has removed since 2020. Neutral Austria has politically sided with Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, and Vienna — home to the OSCE, the International Atomic Energy Agency and other UN bodies — has long carried a reputation as a hub for intelligence work conducted under diplomatic cover. Hungary expelled ten members of Russia's mission in September, and Romania removed a Russian diplomat in July after drone incursions into its airspace.

Ukraine Says Starlink Speed Caps Still Hinder Its Drones, Despite Trump's Assurance

Speed restrictions on the Starlink satellite service that Ukraine's military depends on remain in force, Ukrainian officials said on Friday, contradicting President Donald Trump's statement that access had been fully restored. Five officials familiar with the matter said connections are still throttled for terminals moving faster than 160 kph — a limit that disrupts drones in flight and has been in place since October 13, as relations between Kyiv and Washington deteriorated. Serhiy Beskrestnov, an adviser to President Zelensky, said officials were not observing any lifting of the limits, aside from isolated cases likely explained by SpaceX's algorithm. Trump said on Thursday that Elon Musk had restored the service, and on Friday said he would straighten the matter out with the SpaceX chief if problems persisted. Kyiv says the caps complicate its defence against Russian jet-drone attacks on cities and its operations along the front line, where Starlink terminals guide drones into occupied territory.

Latest · Americas · Courts24 October 2026

Judge Orders Trump to Sit for a Deposition in His $10 Billion BBC Lawsuit by December 11

A federal judge in Florida has ordered President Donald Trump to answer questions under oath by December 11 in his $10 billion defamation lawsuit against the BBC, warning that failure to comply could bring sanctions up to dismissal of the case. US Magistrate Judge Enjoliqué Lett issued the order from the bench on Thursday after the broadcaster complained that Trump had stalled for months, including cancelling an agreed October 16 deposition on grounds of matters of national importance. BBC lawyer Charles Tobin told the court Trump spent that day addressing a campaign rally in Dalton, Georgia, and attending a Republican fundraiser at Mar-a-Lago, arguing that no plaintiff is above the law. Trump, who filed the suit in December, accuses the BBC of splicing together parts of his January 6, 2021 speech in the documentary 'Trump: A Second Chance' to make it appear he directed supporters to storm the Capitol. The BBC has apologised for the misleading edit — a scandal that cost senior executives their jobs — but denies defamation, and is seeking Trump's financial records to contest his damages claim. His lawyers say he offered dates in January and that presidential duties and security must take precedence.

Latest · Africa · Migration24 October 2026

Amnesty Warns US Deportees Sent to Sierra Leone Risk Being Forced On to Countries Where They Face Abuse

Amnesty International on Friday urged Sierra Leone to halt the expected onward removal of three people deported there from the United States, saying each could face serious human rights violations if sent back to their home countries. The three — Abalo Agba of Togo, Alfred Kamara of Liberia and Fatou Traore of Ivory Coast — had each lived in the United States for more than 20 years and have children under 18 still there. All three had previously won a protection known as withholding of removal, under which a US immigration judge found they were more likely than not to face torture or other abuse if returned home. They were flown to Sierra Leone on October 12 under an agreement in which Freetown accepts up to 300 West African third-country nationals a year — one of several such deals the Trump administration has struck with African governments. Amnesty said earlier deportees under the arrangement were quickly repatriated, calling the practice a revolving door that defeats the protection US courts granted.

Saudi Arabia Postpones the UN World Data Forum to 2027 as Attacks Disrupt the Gulf's Events Calendar

Saudi Arabia's statistics authority announced on Friday that the United Nations World Data Forum and the second Saudi Statistical Forum, scheduled for November 9 to 12 in Riyadh, will be postponed to 2027 to ensure broad international participation. The decision follows Monday's delay of the kingdom's flagship Future Investment Initiative conference to 2027 and a string of other cancellations, including the Six Kings Slam tennis tournament and the Saudi round of the World Rally Championship, amid escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and infrastructure. The disruption extends beyond the kingdom: the US-Israeli war with Iran this year has forced conferences across the Gulf — in the UAE and Qatar as well as Saudi Arabia — to be cancelled, postponed or restructured. The postponements underline how far the conflict's economic fallout now reaches, reaching even gatherings devoted to statistics, far from the front lines.
