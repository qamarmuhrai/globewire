Latest · Asia-Pacific · Markets — KOSPI Closes Down 6.02%6 November 2026

Seoul Stabilises off the Lows, but the Week’s Damage Is Done: KOSPI Ends at 5,059.69

South Korea’s benchmark index closed on Friday at 5,059.69, down 324.11 points or 6.02 per cent, recovering part of a morning collapse that had taken it as low as 4,911.28 — 8.8 per cent below Thursday’s close and through the 5,000 line for the first time intraday since 6 February. The Korea Exchange fired a first-stage circuit breaker at 9:03 a.m., halting all stock and derivatives trading for 20 minutes, the 11th such trigger this year and the 17th on record; a sell-sidecar curbing program trades followed at 9:34 a.m., and the smaller KOSDAQ triggered its own sidecar at 9:06 a.m. before closing down 5.03 per cent at 644.27. The rout was imported: Wall Street’s Thursday AI sell-off — the Nasdaq down 5.52 per cent into correction territory — fed fears that the new Democratic majority in Washington will tighten rules on AI data centres, and Seoul’s market is unusually exposed, with Samsung Electronics and SK hynix together now making up roughly 49 per cent of main-board capitalisation, up from 34 per cent at the end of last year. Foreign investors led the early exit, and brokerage branches in Seoul reported a morning surge of calls from panicked retail holders. Analysts at Daishin Securities called the data-centre regulation fear the main cause of the crash; the index is now some 44 per cent below its June peak.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — Resignation Rejected, Trial Opens6 November 2026

He Tried to Quit. Congress Said No: Peña Faces an Impeachment Trial Instead

Paraguay’s crisis took a constitutional twist overnight. President Santiago Peña announced his resignation on Thursday over the collapse of Ueno Bank, the digital lender seized by the central bank on Tuesday after restated accounts cut its solvency ratio to 5.56 per cent, less than half the 12 per cent minimum — but Congress refused to let him simply walk away. A joint session called to consider the resignation lost its quorum when lawmakers from Peña’s own Colorado Party walked out, and the lower house instead voted 78–0 to impeach him; the Senate has opened proceedings and summoned him to appear on Friday. Peña, a 47-year-old former IMF economist who was a business partner in Ueno’s parent Grupo Vázquez until March 2025, says he will attend the trial to exercise his right to a defence, and he remains in office provisionally while it runs. The charges centre on conflicts of interest, misuse of pension funds and institutional cover-ups in the bank’s rise — in under five years it gathered some 3 million customers, close to half the population, and administered about $850 million in public funds. Eight senior executives including founder Miguel Vázquez have been detained, 27 officials and associates face charges including breach of trust, and the attorney general has opened three criminal investigations. The central bank says deposit-guarantee payments of up to 10 million guaraníes (about $1,630) will start for eligible customers; the guaraní closed near 6,490 to the dollar after falling almost 5 per cent, and sovereign bond prices dropped. If Peña is removed, Vice President Pedro Alliana takes over for the rest of the term to 2028.

Latest · Europe · France — 575,000 March, 448 Arrested6 November 2026

France’s Biggest Protest Day in Years: “A Dignified Wage” Meets Tear Gas

France’s autumn of unrest peaked — for now — on Thursday, when a one-day general strike called by unions for teachers, rail, hospital and postal workers fused with the student movement that has protested school conditions since September. The Interior Ministry counted 575,000 participants nationwide; the CGT union, a co-organiser, put the figure at 1.6 million across some 244 announced marches. The demands are concrete: the CGT wants an immediate 10 per cent rise in the minimum wage, the CFDT a €100 monthly increase, alongside across-the-board pay rises, fairer taxes on large fortunes and protection for public services. About 16 per cent of teachers struck, roughly a third of high schools were shut or blockaded, the Eiffel Tower closed, and nuclear output dipped early in the day — though transport disruption stayed limited and only about 5 per cent of hospital staff walked out. The day was largely peaceful, but police fired tear gas in Paris and Lyon, 448 people were arrested and 53 officers were injured, with 70,000 police deployed. With a presidential election five to six months away, the far right leading in polls and protesters vowing to keep mobilising until Christmas, the government of President Emmanuel Macron faces a movement that now spans students, unions and revived Yellow Vest activists alike.

Latest · Europe · Aviation — Third Day of Hoax Threats6 November 2026

27 Flights, Fighter Escorts, Zero Bombs: Europe’s Hoax-Threat Week Peaks

A wave of emailed bomb threats disrupted at least 27 passenger flights across Europe on Thursday, the third day of such alerts this week, and every search came back empty. The day began with Aer Lingus flight EI402 from Dublin to Rome: after the crew declared an emergency and, French officials said, did not cooperate as expected, French fighter jets intercepted the Airbus A320 and escorted it to the Évreux airbase west of Paris, where all 151 passengers and six crew were taken off safely and bomb-disposal teams inspected the aircraft. Italy’s air force escorted a Finnair flight from Helsinki into Rome Fiumicino; Bulgaria scrambled jets for three Ryanair services bound for Sofia, diverting the London Stansted flight to Plovdiv; a Wizz Air Milan–Warsaw flight diverted to Vienna while another Wizz Air service landed in Bucharest under threat; four aircraft searched in Prague were cleared; Spanish police logged three emailed threats within 20 minutes touching Air Europa and Iberia services; and a LOT flight from Warsaw landed in Brussels to a clean inspection. Rome’s airport was briefly closed and more than 20 threats have now been counted since Monday, at least seven involving Ryanair. The EU says it is not yet clear whether the hoaxes are connected; Britain has called them malicious. No explosives have been found on any aircraft.

Latest · Americas · Vatican — Wheels Up from Rome6 November 2026

Three Weeks after Lung Surgery, Pope Leo Is Airborne: 12 Days, 10 Cities, 31,000 km

Pope Leo XIV left Rome’s Fiumicino airport at 7:05 a.m. on Friday aboard an ITA Airways flight for Montevideo, opening the first Latin American journey of his pontificate — and doing so barely three weeks after an operation on 13 October removed a cancerous tumour from his right lung. The Vatican says the 71-year-old has passed the required convalescence, needs no special medical measures beyond standard papal-trip care, and will keep a programme of 12 flights and 10 cities across Uruguay, Argentina and Peru through 17 November. Landing in Uruguay is expected around 4:30 p.m. local time, followed by a 5:30 p.m. welcome ceremony at Plaza Independencia, meetings with President Yamandú Orsi and an address to authorities and diplomats, then Vespers at the Metropolitan Cathedral. Uruguay — where Christians are 44 per cent of the population, Latin America’s lowest share, and no pope has visited since 1988 — has mobilised some 5,000 police and 1,000 soldiers; Leo will ride a locally built popemobile along the Montevideo waterfront. Ahead lie Saturday’s Mass in Paysandú and a youth vigil at the Estadio Centenario, Sunday’s Mass in Florida at the shrine of Our Lady of the Thirty-Three, then three days in Argentina — its first papal visit in 39 years — and nearly a week in Peru, where Leo served more than two decades as a missionary and bishop and holds citizenship.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 72% Counted, 76 Councils Hung6 November 2026

The ANC’s Worst Result since 1994 Hardens: 31.9%, and Almost Nobody Governs Alone

South Africa’s municipal count passed 72 per cent of voting districts on Friday with the shape of the result no longer in doubt. The Electoral Commission’s live tally showed 19.65 million valid votes counted and 3,568 of 8,825 council seats declared: the ANC on 31.9 per cent and 1,587 seats, the Democratic Alliance on 24.4 per cent, the MK Party on about 9.2 per cent and the IFP on 4.6 per cent — the ANC down from 46 per cent at the 2021 municipal elections, its weakest national performance since 1994. Of the councils decided so far, 76 are hung against only 31 won outright (21 by the ANC, six by the DA, three by MK), so coalition bargaining will decide most of the country’s 213 municipalities; in Gauteng all nine councils, including Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni, are trending hung. The day’s emblematic upset came in Tsantsabane in the Northern Cape, where Rise Tsantsabane Communities — a community movement registered only in February after protests over infrastructure and mine jobs — took seven of 13 seats and 43.8 per cent of the vote, cutting the ANC from a majority to three seats. In KwaZulu-Natal, Jacob Zuma’s MK Party leads the province on 34.6 per cent and is ahead in eThekwini metro on 43.7 per cent, with the ANC third provincially. Turnout is estimated at 54.4 per cent of 29.2 million registered voters, final results are expected over the weekend, and the rand slipped 0.3 per cent to 16.53 per dollar as investors weighed whether hung councils can deliver stable city governments.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi in Four Days6 November 2026

Trump Predicts a Settlement “Soon” as the Abu Dhabi Table Is Set for 10–11 November

President Trump said overnight that the Ukraine conflict will be resolved “soon enough” and announced preparation of a broader agreement on European security extending beyond Ukraine — the latest signal ahead of the first direct Russia–Ukraine working-level talks since February, set for Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November. The Kremlin has confirmed its delegation will be led by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky; US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner will mediate, with talks expected to cover post-war security guarantees, de-escalation, humanitarian arrangements and, on Kyiv’s list, an energy-infrastructure ceasefire, Black Sea shipping for grain exports and more frequent prisoner exchanges. Russia has ruled out European participation, insisting on the trilateral format, while Germany’s Chancellor Friedrich Merz — hosting President Zelenskyy in Berlin this week — demanded Moscow show it is serious about a ceasefire; Zelenskyy says he hopes the talks open a path to a leaders’ meeting with Vladimir Putin. This week’s Brussels meetings on reconstruction and guarantees, and a White House session hosted by First Lady Melania Trump, have fed into the same push. The obstacles are unchanged and large: Moscow still demands Ukrainian withdrawal from Donetsk, which Kyiv rejects, and the air war continues — Russian strikes hit Ukrainian power plants and substations in the Vinnytsia, Dnipropetrovsk and Kharkiv regions as the talks were announced, and Ukraine reported fresh attacks on its energy system on Tuesday.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Decision on Sunday6 November 2026

Herzog to Name His Pick on Sunday — Eisenkot Holds 67, Netanyahu 46

Israel’s week of presidential consultations closed on Thursday with the arithmetic unchanged and the decision deferred to the weekend. President Isaac Herzog finished his round of meetings with the incoming Knesset factions and announced he will task a lawmaker with forming the next government on Sunday, saying he intends to leave no stone unturned in the search for a stable government and will hold private talks with party leaders in the meantime. Gadi Eisenkot of Yashar holds the backing of 67 of the 120 lawmakers — his own party plus Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Democrats, Ra’am and the Joint List, after both Arab-majority parties endorsed him on Thursday — against 46 for caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from Likud, Shas, Religious Zionism, Zehut and Otzma Yehudit. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism recommended no one, leaving seven seats uncommitted. A recommendation is not a coalition promise, and Eisenkot’s hardest work — turning endorsements into 61 voting partners — starts only once the mandate lands; he would then have 28 days, extendable by 14. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November, and Eisenkot’s bloc is also preparing an early vote to replace Speaker Amir Ohana, who said he would schedule one within a week if 61 lawmakers formally ask.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — UNHCR Report6 November 2026

90 Million Displaced People Live Where the Climate Bites Hardest, UN Warns

Three in four of the world’s forcibly displaced people — nearly 90 million refugees and others driven from their homes — now live in countries highly exposed to drought, floods and extreme heat, the UN refugee agency UNHCR said in a report released on Friday, days before the COP31 climate summit opens in Antalya, Türkiye. Afghanistan and Bangladesh are among the most severely exposed hosts named. The overlap runs both ways: more than two-thirds of refugees who returned home in the first half of 2026 went back to countries highly vulnerable to climate hazards, including nearly 4 million recent returnees in Syria facing water scarcity and erratic rainfall, and the agency counts more than 255 million internal displacements from weather events over the past decade. By 2050, it warns, one in three refugee settlements in sub-Saharan Africa could face 100 or more days of dangerous heat a year, and a forecast powerful El Niño could displace up to three million more people in East Africa by the end of 2026. Yet only about 10 per cent of global climate finance reaches the fragile and conflict-affected states hosting these populations — a gap UNHCR wants the Antalya talks, running 9–20 November under a shared Türkiye–Australia presidency, to close. A separate UN report on Wednesday found rich countries well short of the $305 billion a year needed to help developing nations adapt.

Latest · Sport · Football — The No. 7 Shirt Is Handed Back6 November 2026

234 Caps, 146 Goals, Three Trophies: Cristiano Ronaldo Ends His Portugal Career

An era closed on Thursday not with a stadium ovation but with a social-media video. Cristiano Ronaldo, 41, announced his retirement from international football, telling the Portuguese people that “the day has come for me to hand back the No. 7 shirt”. The numbers behind the farewell are unmatched in the men’s game: 234 appearances and 146 goals across 23 years, six World Cups, the Euro 2016 title that made Portugal champions of Europe, and Nations League trophies in 2019 and 2025. His final appearance was the 1–0 win over Wales on 24 September. The ending, though, carries a rift: Ronaldo had fallen out with coach Jorge Jesus — his former club manager at Al-Nassr — after being left unused against Norway, walked out of the squad in late September and accepted a €255 fine and a 30-day suspension before choosing to retire rather than return. The Portuguese Football Federation thanked him and promised “the tribute he deserves”, leaving a farewell appearance possible. Ronaldo continues at Al-Nassr, where he is contracted until June 2027 and has hinted this is probably his last year of football. His exit follows Lionel Messi’s farewell to Argentina last month — the two defining careers of a generation have now both left the international stage.

One Number between the Rout and the Weekend: October Jobs Land at 8:30 a.m. in Washington

The week’s last scheduled shock arrives at 8:30 a.m. Eastern — 6:30 p.m. in Pakistan — when the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October’s employment report. Consensus sits near 65,000 jobs added, more than double September’s weak 29,000, with unemployment expected at 4.2 per cent (some surveys say 4.1) and wages up about 0.3 per cent on the month; estimates range from 40,000 to 100,000, and one forecaster, Pantheon, sees 100,000. The run-in data point both ways: ADP’s private payrolls on Wednesday printed 86,000 against a 67,000 forecast, and weekly jobless claims held contained at 194,000, but September’s report also carried heavy downward revisions — July cut from +21,000 to −10,000 and August from +162,000 to +133,000. The policy stakes are unusually sharp. The Federal Reserve held rates at 3.75–4.00 per cent on 28 October, markets went into Thursday’s crash pricing a strong chance of a December hike, and the Bank of England has just raised the other way — so a hot number hardens the tightening case while AI stocks wobble, and a soft one revives cut hopes at the price of recession talk. Gold, near $3,950 an ounce, has been the week’s pressure gauge: in the last report’s release hour it swung more than $30. After Wall Street’s worst day since April 2025, this print decides whether the week ends in stabilisation or a second leg down.
