Latest · Americas · US Midterms — “I Would Do It Again”5 November 2026

“Largely True”: Trump Blames Gasoline and Iran for the Rout — and Defends the War Anyway

President Donald Trump spent Wednesday absorbing a defeat he partly owned and partly deflected. Asked whether the war with Iran had cost Republicans control of Congress, he said the claim was “largely true” because the conflict had pushed gasoline prices higher, yet insisted the campaign had been necessary to stop Tehran acquiring a nuclear weapon and that he would make the same decision again. He told interviewers he had not been on the ballot himself, blamed some defeated candidates, and said he planned no cabinet changes, while leaving open vetoes of Democratic legislation and eventual cooperation where interests overlapped. Vice President JD Vance was blunter, writing that the reason the party lost was the war and that any honest discussion of what comes next had to start there — a formulation that also raised the stakes for his own standing as a possible 2028 contender. Analysts quoted in coverage said the president now faces an awkward choice: escalation offers no clear route to a better outcome and risks further political damage, while a negotiated exit would require concessions he has so far resisted. He also repeated that Washington has no immediate plans for fresh strikes on Iran.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — Oil Crashes 9%4 November 2026

Brent Falls to $94: Oil Traders Price an Iran Exit After the Midterm Rebuke

Oil delivered the sharpest market signal of the day. Brent crude settled down about $8.98, or 8.7 per cent, at $94.62 a barrel — its lowest close since late August — after touching an intraday low near $94.29, while US West Texas Intermediate fell about 8.3 per cent to $88.12. Both benchmarks are now down more than 15 per cent for the week, a reversal from levels near $118 for Brent only days earlier. Traders linked the move directly to politics: the Democratic capture of both chambers, combined with public acknowledgements from the White House that the war had driven fuel costs and the defeat, was read as increasing pressure for a settlement that could ease the chokehold on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. The sell-off came despite supportive inventory data — US crude stocks fell by about 865,000 barrels to 409.8 million in the week to 30 October, against expectations of a build — and despite a US Energy Department loan of 300,000 barrels from the Northeast heating-oil reserve aimed at easing diesel pressure. Whether pump prices follow crude lower, and how quickly, remains the question voters will judge first.

Tehran Claims the Verdict: Ghalibaf Hails a “No to War” Vote, Khamenei Warns Nothing Will Change

Iran’s leaders read the American result in two registers at once — celebration and caution. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who also leads Tehran’s negotiating team, wrote that whether from conscience or the cost of living, Americans had said no to war, a message Iran endorsed, and warned that if Washington betrayed that public will it would learn that Iran’s own “no” came with consequences. Latest · Middle East · Israel — Herzog Consultations Begin4 November 2026

56 to 46: Eisenkot Leads the Recommendations as Herzog Starts Asking Who Should Govern

Israel’s formal government-formation process began on Wednesday evening at the President’s Residence in Jerusalem, hours after the certified results were handed over. In a break with custom, President Isaac Herzog invited factions from smallest to largest, starting with Religious Zionism and Shas — both of which recommended Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — followed by Yisrael Beytenu, which backed opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, and Zehut, which also backed Netanyahu. By Wednesday night the count favoured Eisenkot: with Yashar, the Democrats, B’Yachad, Yisrael Beytenu and Ra’am behind him, he was expected to reach about 56 endorsements, against about 46 for Netanyahu. The pivotal abstention came from Degel HaTorah, the Lithuanian faction holding four of United Torah Judaism’s seven seats, which confirmed it would recommend no one; the Joint List had not yet declared. Neither bloc has the 61 seats needed to govern, so Herzog’s discretion matters — by law he must pick the lawmaker he judges most likely to succeed, not necessarily the one with the most seats or endorsements. Consultations conclude Thursday with the largest parties, Likud last. The new Knesset is sworn in on 10 November.

Latest · Europe · Spain — A Minister Falls4 November 2026

“Two Accidents of This Severity”: Spain’s Transport Minister Resigns Over Crashes That Killed 115

Spain’s transport minister, Óscar Puente, resigned on Tuesday night after a four-hour grilling by lawmakers, six days after a high-speed collision near Urda, about 130 kilometres south of Madrid, killed 69 people. A Renfe train ran into the back of a stationary Iryo train at an estimated 160 to 200 kilometres per hour after a signalling failure had halted services on the Madrid–Seville line and controllers authorised a resumption. It was Spain’s second catastrophic rail crash this year: a January derailment on the same corridor killed 46, taking the combined toll to 115, among the worst sequences in modern European rail history. Puente said a transport minister in a country like Spain could not stay in post after two such accidents in less than a year, whether or not he bore direct responsibility, and said he had decided within minutes of hearing of the Urda crash but stayed to launch the emergency response, the preliminary investigation and a compensation and safety package. Before stepping down he announced reduced services on the line pending a full review by the rail-safety agency and completion of the delayed European Rail Traffic Management System rollout. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez put his deputy and economy minister, Carlos Cuerpo, in interim charge — and loses one of his most combative campaigners less than four weeks before the snap election on 29 November, in which the Socialists trail in the polls. Puente has also withdrawn his own candidacy.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Counting Under Way5 November 2026

Polls Closed, Boxes Opened: South Africa Starts a Count That Will Take Days, Not Hours

South Africa’s local elections formally ended at 9 p.m. on Wednesday, when voting stations closed and counting began on the spot, in front of party agents and accredited observers. The Electoral Commission’s process is deliberately slow and visible: boxes are opened, ballots checked for the security stamp, then sorted and counted separately for the proportional-representation, ward and, where applicable, district ballots; slips are reconciled against papers issued and spoilt, signed by the presiding officer and countersigned by party agents, displayed at the station, then scanned, captured and independently audited before publication. That is why, in the first hours after midnight, only a tiny fraction of the country’s 23,696 voting districts had reported — early dashboard snapshots covering a few dozen districts are not a national trend and should not be read as one. The scale behind the count is large: a record register of 29.2 million, about 1.43 million special votes cast on 2 and 3 November, more than 136,000 candidates and 10,526 seats across 257 municipalities. The commission has seven days to declare final results and allocate council seats, and has indicated results should be known by Sunday; councils must then be constituted within 14 days, with coalition talks expected to dominate in the major metros where no party is projected to win outright.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Decision Day at Noon5 November 2026

First Rise Since 2023? The Bank of England Decides at Noon With a Hike Almost Fully Priced

Britain’s central bank is expected to raise interest rates on Thursday for the first time in more than three years. Markets have priced a roughly 96 to 97 per cent chance that the Monetary Policy Committee lifts Bank Rate by a quarter point, from 3.75 per cent — where it has sat since a cut last December — to 4.00 per cent, alongside a new Monetary Policy Report with updated forecasts. The case rests on inflation: consumer prices rose 3.5 per cent in the year to September, up from 3.1 per cent in August and the highest in almost a year, against a 2 per cent target, with energy costs linked to the Middle East conflict a central driver. The committee was already split 6–3 in September, with three members voting then for an immediate move to 4 per cent; economist polls point to a 7–2 or 6–3 vote for a rise this time. Because the decision itself is so heavily priced, traders say the vote split and the Bank’s guidance will move sterling more than the quarter point. Markets are also pricing several further rises during 2027, which would take borrowing costs materially higher for mortgage holders — about £62 billion of fixed-rate buy-to-let loans alone mature in the year to next August, according to Paragon Bank research cited in coverage.

The Moving Yellow Line: Gaza’s War Narrows as the Hebron Siege Lifts

More than a year after the US-brokered ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025, Gaza’s health ministry puts the number killed since then at 1,593, with 5,748 wounded — a toll that rose again this week, with at least four people killed on Monday in strikes and gunfire in Khan Younis, al-Musaddar and Gaza City’s Tal al-Hawa neighbourhood and a fifth person dying of earlier wounds. The UN human-rights office warned on 30 October that Palestinians are being pushed into an ever-smaller area, describing the Yellow Line — the boundary behind which Israeli forces were to remain temporarily, initially leaving them in control of about 53 per cent of the territory — as a moving line now associated with effective control of roughly two-thirds of Gaza. Nearly two million people need shelter assistance as winter approaches, and the Red Crescent warned the health system risks collapse, with 26 hospitals out of service and fuel shortages threatening the rest. Israel says Hamas is rearming in breach of the ceasefire; Israeli military assessments cited in domestic reporting put Hamas at about 30,000 armed operatives with hundreds of mostly short- and medium-range rockets and some restored weapons production. In the West Bank, troops withdrew on 2 November from the Hebron area after a ten-day operation that sealed ten entrances and displaced about 5,000 people, according to Al Jazeera’s data unit, while UN monitors counted 397 settler attacks causing casualties or damage in the first 26 days of October.
