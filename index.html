Latest · Asia-Pacific · Jiuquan — First Foreign Astronaut Aboard Tiangong25 November 2026

China’s Shenzhou-24 Lifts Off With a Pakistani Astronaut, the First Foreigner Bound for Tiangong

China’s Shenzhou-24 spacecraft blasted off on Wednesday morning carrying a three-person crew that includes Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistani pilot who becomes the first foreign astronaut to board the Tiangong space station. The craft rode a Long March-2F rocket from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwest China at 8:18 a.m. local time. Ali, flying as a payload specialist, is expected to spend five to ten days at the station conducting experiments. Beijing is promoting Tiangong as a hub for international cooperation in orbit, a role that could grow if the International Space Station is retired around 2030 as planned.

Latest · Europe · Odesa & Kyiv — Child Among the Injured25 November 2026

Russian Strike Kills Two in Ukraine’s Odesa Region as Drones Hit Kyiv

A Russian strike on Ukraine’s Black Sea region of Odesa killed two people and wounded three others, including a child, late on Tuesday, regional governor Oleh Kiper said on Telegram. The attack hit cars as well as private and industrial buildings. Russian forces also sent drones against the capital Kyiv late on Tuesday, setting fire to a multi-storey apartment building and injuring two people, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said. Reuters said it could not independently verify the reports, and noted that both Russia and Ukraine deny deliberately targeting civilians in the war, now well into its fourth year.

Latest · Middle East · Red Sea — Coalition Answers With Strikes25 November 2026

Houthis Threaten Jeddah and Saudi Red Sea Ports as Coalition Strikes Hodeidah

Yemen’s Houthi movement said on Wednesday that the Saudi port of Jeddah and every other Saudi port on the Red Sea coast would be targeted if Riyadh carried out threats to attack the Yemeni port of Hodeidah. The warning sharply raised the stakes along one of the world’s busiest shipping corridors. After the Houthi statement, the Saudi-led coalition said it had struck operational capabilities at Hodeidah and destroyed a number of targets in the Yemeni governorates of Saada and Amran. The exchange marks a serious escalation in the long-running confrontation around Yemen’s western coastline and the Red Sea trade route.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shanghai — Fourth Politburo Member Probed Since 202525 November 2026

Shanghai Party Pledges Loyalty to Beijing as Its Chief Chen Jining Faces Investigation

Shanghai’s senior Communist Party leadership has pledged loyalty to Beijing after the city’s top official, Politburo member Chen Jining, was unexpectedly placed under investigation, the financial hub’s official newspaper reported on Wednesday. China’s anti-graft watchdog announced the probe in a terse statement on Tuesday, making Chen the fourth member of the elite Politburo to be investigated since 2025. A front-page story in the party’s Jiefang Daily said officials held a late-night meeting on the case and voiced understanding of the need to “see things through to the end.” Chen, who last appeared in public last week, had been tipped by political watchers as a candidate for elevation to the Politburo Standing Committee in next year’s leadership reshuffle.

Latest · Europe · Uzhhorod — First Magyar Visit to Ukraine25 November 2026

Zelensky and Hungary’s Magyar Hail a New Era in Post-Orban Ties

Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky and Hungary’s Prime Minister Peter Magyar held talks in western Ukraine on Tuesday and hailed a new era in relations after years of tension under Budapest’s previous, Kremlin-friendly leader Viktor Orban. Magyar, a pro-EU conservative who took office in May, crossed into Ukraine’s Transcarpathia region, home to an ethnic Hungarian minority, laying wreaths at a cemetery in Uzhgorod before talks and a briefing in Berehove — and even a symbolic volleyball game with schoolchildren. Ties have warmed since Kyiv passed legislation on minority rights, after which Hungary allowed the EU to open new sections of Ukraine’s membership negotiations. The two leaders also discussed energy, including Hungarian diesel supplies to Ukraine.

Latest · Africa · Ituri — Ebola Team Relocated From Mongbwalu25 November 2026

WHO Staff Evacuated From DR Congo Hospital After Three Days Sheltering From Fighting

World Health Organization staff, Congolese health ministry officials and other responders have been safely evacuated from a hospital in Akwe, in DR Congo’s Ituri province, after sheltering there for more than three days amid fierce fighting, WHO chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said on Tuesday. Ituri is the epicentre of the country’s deadly Ebola outbreak, and the team had taken refuge in the hospital — at one point in its maternity ward — as fighting raged nearby. Tedros said the evacuation was carried out “under very challenging circumstances” and that, because of the insecurity, WHO has also temporarily relocated its Ebola response team from Mongbwalu until the situation calms.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Asia-Pacific Index Down 2.3%25 November 2026

Stocks Retreat as AI Rebound Stumbles and Fresh Tariff Threats Loom

Asian stocks tumbled early on Wednesday as fresh US tariff threats hit sentiment and a tech-led rally fizzled, with the region’s chip-heavy markets taking the heaviest losses. MSCI’s broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan fell 2.3 percent, South Korean shares dropped as much as 5.3 percent and Taiwan’s market slid 5.1 percent at its worst point, while Japan’s Nikkei fell as much as 2.9 percent. The selloff followed reports that the US Trade Representative’s office is preparing findings from a probe into alleged excess industrial capacity among 16 major trading partners, including China and the EU — a step that could pave the way for new tariffs. Nvidia gave back part of its recent gains, falling 5.9 percent, and investors now await US core PCE inflation data due later on Wednesday, ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Australia and Solomon Islands Sign Security Pact in a Shift for the Pacific

Australia and Solomon Islands signed a security pact on Wednesday that makes Canberra the island nation’s defence partner of choice. The agreement marks a notable shift for the archipelago, which previously signed a security deal with China that had alarmed Australia, the United States and their partners across the Pacific. The new pact deepens Canberra’s role in the islands’ security at a time of intensifying competition for influence in the region, and follows years of Australian efforts to remain the first call for Pacific governments facing security challenges.

Trump Administration Pauses Joint Military Operations With Ecuador, Report Says

President Donald Trump’s administration has paused joint military operations with Ecuador while Washington investigates a lethal operation carried out by Ecuadorean forces, the New York Times reported on Tuesday. The suspension interrupts security cooperation between the two countries at a time when Ecuador has leaned heavily on US support in its fight against drug trafficking gangs, whose violence has surged in recent years. Reuters, which reported the newspaper’s account, gave no timetable for the investigation or for any resumption of the joint work.

Australia Apologises to LGBTQ Veterans for Past Discrimination in the Military

The Australian government issued a formal apology on Wednesday to current and former LGBTQ service members for the harm caused by decades of discriminatory policies in the armed forces. Defence Minister Richard Marles delivered the apology in a speech to parliament, addressing service members whose careers and lives were damaged by rules that once excluded or punished personnel because of their sexuality. The apology follows years of advocacy by affected veterans and forms part of a broader reckoning with the military’s past treatment of LGBTQ Australians.
