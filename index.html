Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — The Final Draft22 November 2026

COP31 Final Draft Recalls the Dubai Fossil-Fuel Pact Instead of Naming It, as the Closing Plenary Slips

The endgame in Antalya is now about wording. Delegates received a third and final draft of the summit’s cover decision in the early hours of Sunday — 94 paragraphs long — and the plenary meant to adopt it slipped from 9 a.m. to 11 a.m. local time as objections mounted. Rather than repeating the phrase “transition away from fossil fuels,” the text recalls the COP28 agreement reached in Dubai three years ago “in its entirety,” a compromise that members of the coalition pressing for explicit fossil-fuel language were reported to be unhappy with. The draft is blunt elsewhere: it records deep concern that peak warming is expected to exceed 1.5°C within a few years, the first time a COP text has acknowledged the Paris threshold will be missed, and it calls for faster carbon removal to eventually reverse the overshoot. A hard numerical target for carbon capture was removed in favour of a collective aspiration to reach gigatonne scale by 2035, while a goal for electricity to make up 35 per cent of global final energy use by 2035 was restored. The $120 billion annual adaptation-finance target pushed by developing countries is not in the text; in its place is a non-binding call to at least triple adaptation funding by 2035. COP31 President Murat Kurum told the late-night plenary the presidency believed it had put forward a text all parties could accept.

Latest · Middle East · Manama — Fire on Sitra22 November 2026

Bahrain Says an Iranian Strike Set a Civilian Industrial Site Ablaze, as Tehran Readies Tankers at Kharg Island

The Gulf confrontation widened on Saturday. Bahrain’s interior ministry said an Iranian attack hit a civilian industrial facility, starting a fire that Civil Defence teams were working to contain, with material damage but no injuries reported. Residents told AFP they saw smoke over Sitra Island, on the east coast, where state oil company Bapco runs a refinery among other industrial sites. Bahrain’s Defence Force said its air defences intercepted a number of further Iranian aerial attacks, with shrapnel causing damage but no casualties. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they had launched a combined missile-and-drone attack on US positions in the kingdom, which hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, naming bases at Al-Riffa and Awali and, in other reporting, Sheikh Isa and Juffair; those claims have not been independently verified. Two tankers were also reported hit in the Strait of Hormuz. At the same time, satellite tracking and Iranian media reports say more than 14 oil tankers, crewed and flying Iranian flags, are gathered near Kharg Island — the terminal for most of Iran’s crude exports — ahead of a possible attempt to run the US naval blockade, now in its 130th consecutive day. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran would take any action necessary to break what it calls an act of war, and confirmed a Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on Friday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Serang, Indonesia — Search Continues22 November 2026

Three Dead and Dozens Missing After Fire Tears Through a Sweets Factory West of Jakarta

Rescue teams were still searching a burned-out confectionery plant on Sunday for workers who have not been accounted for since a fire broke out on Saturday afternoon. The blaze at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in Cikande, in Serang district of Banten province about 50 kilometres from Jakarta, killed at least three people. The National Search and Rescue Agency said 15 people were rescued alive, four of them injured, while the number still being searched for was given as 36 in some official statements and 39 in others as lists were reconciled. The factory makes Milkita milk-based candies and snacks and is described by its parent as part of UNIFAM, one of Indonesia’s major consumer-food groups. Firefighters were alerted at about 4:30 p.m. on Saturday and found the building already engulfed; parts of the roof later collapsed and fires were still burning in sections of the plant on Sunday, slowing the search. Banten’s governor said an estimated 60 people were inside when the fire started. Survivors said it began in a cooling unit on the third floor, but the disaster agency said the cause remains under investigation.

Latest · Europe · Warsaw — “Every Inch”21 November 2026

NATO Chief Tells Warsaw the Alliance Will Defend Every Inch of Poland After Russian Missiles Crossed the Border

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte used a visit to Warsaw on Saturday to give Poland an unqualified guarantee, a day after Warsaw said Russian weapons had violated its airspace. “I can guarantee that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory,” Rutte said, according to the Polish Press Agency, adding that NATO’s European commander would adjust deterrence if needed. Poland’s defence minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, said three objects — probably two missiles and a jet-powered drone — came down during Russia’s overnight attack on Ukraine on 20 November, at Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz in the southeast, one of them a Kh-101 cruise missile about 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it an intentional provocation, noting the timing coincided with exercises in southeastern Poland involving troops from 11 allied countries preparing for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine. Rutte had earlier called the incident clearly dangerous and said NATO jets were scrambled. A meeting of NATO’s North Atlantic Council is scheduled in Brussels on Monday to discuss the violation.

Latest · Europe · Gulf of Finland — Sparta II Ablaze21 November 2026

A Russian Cargo Ship Linked to the Military Burns in the Baltic After a Drone Strike Finland Attributes to Ukraine

Finland spent Saturday fighting a fire at sea and warning that the war is spilling into its neighbourhood. The Russian roll-on/roll-off cargo ship Sparta II was hit by drones on Saturday morning in international waters west of Gogland Island, caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters, where it anchored off Loviisa. Its 16 crew members left in lifeboats, transferred to a nearby Russian vessel and are being taken back to Russia. Finnish Border Guard patrol ships, a patrol aircraft and helicopters worked to extinguish the fire and cool the hull, and a Russian rescue ship, Murman, joined the operation at Finland’s request. The Defence Forces said Ukraine was likely behind the attack. The 121-metre Sparta II, owned by Oboronlogistika, left St Petersburg on Friday bound for Baltiysk in the Kaliningrad exclave, is under US and Ukrainian sanctions, and Finnish officials said military materiel on board could not be ruled out. Leningrad region’s governor said the ship was attacked by two drones about three nautical miles from Russia’s border and that 80 drones were shot down over the region overnight. Hours later Finland suspected a Russian helicopter of violating its airspace, a possible link its defence minister said authorities were examining. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote that the war was spilling over into Finland’s immediate vicinity.

Latest · Europe · Kyiv — 186 Drones Overnight22 November 2026

Ukraine Says It Downed 161 of 186 Russian Drones Overnight, While the Toll From the Kryvyi Rih Strike Climbs to 73 Injured

The air war ran through another night. Ukraine’s Air Force said Russia launched 186 drones between the evening of 21 November and the morning of 22 November — including 72 jet-powered Shahed-type drones — and that air defences shot down or electronically suppressed 161 of them by 8 a.m. Kyiv time. Strikes were still recorded at 18 locations, with debris falling at six more, and the attack was continuing as the figures were published. Shelling also damaged infrastructure in the Dnipropetrovsk region, and a five-storey apartment building was damaged in the Vyshhorod district near Kyiv, forcing residents out. Separately, the count from Friday night’s attack on Kryvyi Rih, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s hometown, kept rising: local officials put the injured at 73, including nine children. The city’s mayor described a triple-tap pattern — ballistic missiles hitting a restaurant car park in the city centre, then jet-powered drones striking rescuers who had rushed in, then a third strike a short time later. A seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic were killed. Zelenskyy said there were no military facilities nearby. Ukraine’s General Staff put Russia’s total personnel losses since February 2022 at about 1,637,170, including 2,310 in the past day.

Latest · Americas · Washington — An Unlikely Alliance21 November 2026

Trump Says He Will Work With the Incoming Democratic Congress to Cap Credit Card Interest at 10 Per Cent

One of the stranger alignments in American politics is back on the table. President Donald Trump said on Saturday that he would work with the incoming Democratic Congress to cap credit card interest rates at 10 per cent, reviving a 2024 campaign promise that stalled under industry pressure and Republican indifference. “I would do it. These credit card companies have made a lot,” he told Fox News’s Saturday in America. The numbers explain the appeal: Federal Reserve data put the average rate on interest-bearing card accounts at 22.36 per cent in the third quarter of 2026, and a New York Fed survey put the median near 22.2 per cent, with many lower-score borrowers paying well above 25 per cent. Democrats won both the House and the Senate in the 3 November midterms and take control in January. Senator Elizabeth Warren, expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee, responded: “Let’s cap credit card interest rates and save Americans money.” A cap would need an act of Congress — the president cannot impose one alone — and banks have argued it would force them to cancel cards for poorer customers or cut rewards. Researchers who modelled the pledge estimated it could save Americans roughly $100 billion a year in interest.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Seoul — A Minister Falls22 November 2026

South Korea’s Education Minister Resigns Over the Handling of an AI Cheating Scare Before the College Entrance Exam

South Korea’s education minister has stepped down over a cheating panic that overtook the country’s most important exam. Choi Kyo-jin offered his resignation on Sunday, taking responsibility for confusion over anti-cheating measures in the run-up to the College Scholastic Ability Test, known as the Suneung, which was held on Thursday. President Lee Jae Myung accepted it within the hour. The scare began on 15 November, when the top scorer in a national tax accountant exam confessed to using a miniature camera and a smartphone with AI to reach a near-perfect score — four days before the Suneung. The ministry, which had initially focused on smart glasses, then revised its rules repeatedly in the final days, adding measures such as blocking public Wi-Fi in test areas and placing stickers over clothing buttons, leaving students and administrators scrambling. Choi’s remarks to the National Assembly three days before the exam added to the criticism. “The exam concluded without any major incidents,” he wrote on X. “However, as the minister in charge, I do not believe I am immune from public criticism that we failed to prepare adequately in advance.” Officials said 111 cases of misconduct involving electronic devices were identified during the exam, nearly half detected after three emergency alerts flushed out phones that candidates had failed to hand in.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Beijing — “Malicious Suppression”22 November 2026

China Condemns a US Ban on Its Telecom Giants Getting American Phone Numbers Directly, and Warns of Countermeasures

Beijing has denounced Washington’s latest telecom restriction as malicious and without factual basis. China’s Commerce Ministry said on Sunday that it firmly opposed a Federal Communications Commission vote on 19 November barring Chinese operators — including China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom — from directly accessing US phone numbers, and urged the US to revoke it, warning that China would take necessary measures to safeguard its interests. The FCC said the order was a first line of defence against illegal robocalling and that voice-over-internet providers would face stricter national-security checks; the rules also reach providers using equipment from firms on its Covered List, including Huawei and ZTE. The ministry accused the regulator of abusing its authority, generalising national-security risks and violating principles of non-discrimination and fair competition, and said recent FCC moves amounted to decoupling in communications and testing that ran counter to the consensus reached by the two heads of state. The timing sharpened the dispute: the vote came hours after President Xi Jinping hosted President Donald Trump at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, where Xi hailed stable and improved ties. The FCC has previously blacklisted Huawei equipment and voted in October to bar Chinese laboratories from testing electronics bound for the US from December 2028.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — Airports to Close Monday23 November 2026

Belgium Braces for a Near-Total Flight Shutdown on Monday as Air Traffic Controllers Join a National Strike

Belgium’s airports are preparing to close for most of Monday. Skeyes, the country’s air traffic control provider, said it cannot guarantee safe handling of regular traffic between 7 a.m. and 9 p.m. on 23 November because of staffing levels during a national strike, and will suspend the services concerned, keeping only a minimum service for emergencies and safety-critical flights. Brussels Airport said the majority of departing and arriving flights will have to be cancelled and that airlines will contact passengers directly. Charleroi Airport, a major Ryanair hub, went further, saying the controllers’ action would mean the complete shutdown of all the country’s airports for the day, affecting thousands of passengers. The strike is the latest in repeated rounds of action against Prime Minister Bart De Wever’s plan to save 10 billion euros over the next few years to cut a high deficit, and three major unions have called a national demonstration in Brussels the same day. Disruption is expected well beyond aviation: Brussels’ STIB operator has warned of major problems on metro, tram and bus lines, Flanders’ De Lijn expects severe disruption, and police expect road closures around a march from Brussels-North to Brussels-South. The national rail operator SNCB said no strike notice had been filed for trains.
