Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Talks Open10 November 2026

“Private Flexibility” on Land: Ukraine and Russia Open US-Mediated Talks in Abu Dhabi

Russian, Ukrainian and American delegations meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday for the first trilateral talks since February, with US officials saying both sides have shown some private flexibility on a possible exchange of territory — even though neither has shifted its public position. Publicly, Moscow still demands the whole of Donetsk region while Kyiv wants the current front line to become the boundary. Washington says no agreement is expected this week; the aim is to clarify positions, narrow the gaps and set a more regular rhythm of negotiations. The agenda splits into two tracks: how a ceasefire would be monitored and prevented from becoming a pause to rearm, and the harder questions of territory and post-war security, including a possible NATO-style Article 5 guarantee for Ukraine, which officials say must be settled together. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner lead the mediation; Vladimir Medinsky heads the Russian team and Rustem Umerov the Ukrainian one, while Germany, France, the UK and Italy join a separate session with NATO and the EU.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Trump Hosts Qatar’s Mediator9 November 2026

No Imminent Deal: Trump Meets Qatar’s Prime Minister as Iran Diplomacy Grinds On

President Donald Trump was due to meet Qatar’s prime minister and foreign minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, at the White House on Monday, with Vice President JD Vance holding parallel talks, as efforts to end the Iran war intensified after the US midterms — though senior American and Gulf officials played down any chance of an imminent agreement. Al-Thani, whose country has acted as the main go-between since early in the war, visited Tehran last week and has brought a Qatari “bridge” proposal built around a short, 48-hour framework of trust-building steps. Iran’s terms remain an end to the US naval blockade of its ports, some sanctions relief and a halt to bombing, in return for Tehran lifting its own closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with nuclear talks to follow; Washington has repeatedly rejected that sequencing, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Tehran’s conditions outrageous on Sunday. Trump, more than eight months into a conflict launched alongside Israel in February with no victory in sight, has said renewed major fighting is “highly unlikely” if talks keep moving.

Latest · Americas · Argentina — Pope Leo’s Record Mass9 November 2026

2.5 Million in the Streets: Buenos Aires Gives Pope Leo the Largest Crowd of His Papacy

An estimated 2.5 million people packed a more than two-kilometre stretch of Avenida del Libertador in Buenos Aires on Monday for an outdoor Mass by Pope Leo XIV, the largest gathering of his 18-month papacy and the centrepiece of the first papal visit to Argentina since 1987. Worshippers camped overnight and streamed in from across the country from the early hours, waving white-and-yellow Vatican flags, while normally busy districts stood near-empty inside a wide police cordon around the Monumento a los Españoles in Palermo. President Javier Milei attended with members of his cabinet. Leo, 71, defended the enduring importance of the Christian faith in his homily and also met victims of abuse by clergy during the day. The Mass came less than a month after lung-cancer surgery, in the middle of a demanding 12-day, three-country South American tour with some 50 events: he arrived from Uruguay on Sunday and travels on to Peru on Wednesday before returning to Rome on 17 November. His previous largest crowd was about 1.2 million in Madrid in June.

Latest · Europe · Türkiye — Steel Plant Explosion9 November 2026

Crane Falls Into Furnace: Steel Plant Blast Near Izmir Kills 16, Erdoğan Orders Probe

An explosion at the HABAŞ iron and steel plant in Aliağa, about 60 kilometres north of Izmir, killed 16 people and injured 13 on Monday, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan said, announcing investigations into one of Türkiye’s deadliest industrial accidents in years. The blast struck at about 11:46 local time during liquid-steel casting; the Izmir governor’s office said initial findings point to an overhead crane snapping and falling into an electric arc furnace. Eight workers were initially reported trapped, and rescue teams worked through the day as a column of thick smoke rose over the plant, with the mayor warning the full toll would only be clear once the rubble was cleared. Two of the injured were in serious condition. HABAŞ, founded in 1987 and one of the country’s largest steel producers with capacity of about 4.5 million tonnes a year, declined to comment. A local labour party branch said it was the third fatal incident at the factory in three weeks — a claim authorities have not confirmed.

Latest · Africa · Migration — Senegal Boat Toll Rises9 November 2026

One Survivor, 39 Dead: Senegal Counts the Cost of the Atlantic Crossing to the Canaries

The death toll from a migrant boat that sank off Senegal has risen to 39, authorities said on Monday, making it one of the country’s deadliest migration tragedies in recent years. The wooden pirogue had left Saint-Louis in the north, bound for Spain’s Canary Islands, when it overturned on Friday off the Lompoul dunes near Kébémer, about 180 kilometres north of Dakar; the wreck was found broken in two near the village of Thieppe. Only one passenger was rescued alive, and an unknown number remain missing as search teams work a 25-kilometre stretch of coast. The toll climbed from 21 on Sunday to 34, then to 39 after four more bodies were recovered on Monday morning; 24 victims have been identified and returned to their families, most of them from the surrounding villages. Interior Minister Mouhamadou Makhtar Cissé visited the site, President Bassirou Diomaye Faye offered condolences and urged people not to attempt the crossing, and the rights group ADHA demanded a parliamentary inquiry. The Atlantic route is among the world’s deadliest: Walking Borders recorded 635 deaths on it between January and May 2026 alone.

“Might Be Too Tough”: Trump Softens on Deportations as Miller Becomes Chief of Staff

Congress returned to Washington on Monday for the final weeks of its term, facing a 11 December deadline to keep the federal government funded — and a White House in reset mode after Democrats swept both chambers in last week’s midterms. Stephen Miller, the architect of the administration’s hardline immigration crackdown, took over as chief of staff on Monday, replacing Susie Wiles, who moves to Trump’s political operation after 21 months in the job. Yet in a 60 Minutes interview President Donald Trump conceded his deportation policy “might be too tough,” acknowledging it had hit undocumented immigrants with jobs and settled lives — even as enforcement continues at a reported pace of about 19,000 arrests a week. Analysts link the crackdown to Republican losses, including in parts of Texas. Democrats, who take control in January, say they will not work with Miller and plan investigations into the immigration drive; Trump has hinted further cabinet changes may follow.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Technology — Nvidia Probe, Beijing Hits Back9 November 2026

“Nothing But Smears”: China Rejects Spy Claims as Nvidia’s Board Investigates Its CEO

China on Monday dismissed as “unsubstantiated” and a smear campaign the allegation that its intelligence services targeted Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, a day after the chipmaker’s board opened an independent investigation into reports he had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. According to The New York Times, Huang acknowledged to the board a relationship with a business executive whom US intelligence agencies assessed was gathering information for Beijing — Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen linked to Singapore-based Megaspeed International, which faces claims over large purchases of advanced Nvidia chips despite US export restrictions. Huang, 63, apologised to employees and his family in a letter and stays on as CEO. Nvidia said an independent committee with outside counsel would examine the reports, but stressed it has no reason to believe US national security or company secrets were compromised; the underlying intelligence has not been made public. Nvidia shares fell about 5% in pre-market trading on Monday. Foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun said the so-called Chinese spy allegation was groundless.

Latest · Europe · Economy — IMF Review Could Unlock $1.67 Billion9 November 2026

A $70 Billion Hole: IMF Opens Review That Could Release $1.67 Billion for Ukraine

The International Monetary Fund began a combined second and third review of its lending programme for Ukraine on Monday, a process that could unlock about $1.67 billion in fresh financing for a war-hit economy facing a funding gap officials put at roughly $70 billion next year. The four-year, $8.1 billion programme was approved in February; Kyiv has so far received an initial $1.5 billion and a further $690 million after its first review, and IMF staff aim to take this combined review to the executive board by December. Talks, held in Lviv and Warsaw with some experts joining online, cover macroeconomic and fiscal policy, financial-sector stability and structural reforms, along with the 2027 budget and external aid. The need is pressing: the economy has slipped into contraction under intensified Russian bombardment of businesses and energy infrastructure, with GDP down 0.7% year on year in the third quarter and the central bank expecting a 0.4% fall in 2026. Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said Ukraine needs $52.6 billion in external financing in 2027, about $32.6 billion of it still uncovered.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Hasina Bids to Come Home9 November 2026

“At Any Cost”: Ousted Leader Sheikh Hasina Asks for a New Passport to Return From Exile

Ousted Bangladeshi prime minister Sheikh Hasina formally requested a new passport and national identity card on Monday, her clearest step yet towards acting on a pledge to return from exile in India in December and surrender before a court. In a letter seen by Reuters, lawyers at London-based Matrix Chambers wrote to Bangladesh’s acting high commissioner in Delhi that her existing passport and identity card had reportedly been revoked, and asked for replacements to be issued expeditiously on the basis of her nationality and international human-rights obligations. Hasina fled to India in August 2024 as a student-led uprising ended her long rule; last November a war-crimes court sentenced her to death in absentia for ordering a deadly crackdown on the protests, charges she denies, and her Awami League party is now banned. Dhaka responded coldly: junior foreign minister Humaiun Kabir said she would not be issued documents and that any return must come through extradition under the treaty with India, while the home ministry said it was still verifying whether the request was genuine.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — The Tunnel Where Goldin Was Held9 November 2026

Eleven Years Underground: Israel Reveals How It Found Hadar Goldin and Sealed a 16km Tunnel

Israel’s military on Monday published new details of the years-long effort to recover Lieutenant Hadar Goldin, a year after his remains were returned from Gaza — more than 11 years after he was killed in Rafah during the 2014 war and his body taken by Hamas. Investigators say the breakthrough came in July 2025, when Southern Command forces and the Shin Bet arrested a Hamas operative suspected of knowing where Goldin was buried. Troops then uncovered the “Dror Lavan” route in southern Rafah: a network stretching more than 10 kilometres, with three levels reaching 30 metres underground and around 80 rooms used as quarters, storage and command facilities. A Hamas team eventually retrieved the remains from the complex under the ceasefire, and they were handed over on 9 November 2025; Goldin was laid to rest two days later. In June 2026, combat engineers finished sealing a network of more than 16 kilometres with over 30,000 cubic metres of concrete, and the shaft used in his 2014 abduction was destroyed on the day his body came home.

Latest · Americas · Diplomacy — Bolivia and the US Reset Ties9 November 2026

After 17 Years Without Ambassadors, Bolivia and the United States Restore Full Ties

Bolivia and the United States will restore full diplomatic relations at ambassador level after a 17-year break, President Rodrigo Paz Pereira and US Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau announced in La Paz following Paz’s inauguration, in a bilateral meeting attended by delegations from more than 70 countries. “It has been very unusual not to have ambassadors in our capitals,” Landau said. “This is an important step, and we hope to announce ambassadors very soon.” No date has been set for the nominations, but both governments said technical talks are under way to expand cooperation in education, public security, economic development, trade and investment, and called the restoration a significant political reset between La Paz and Washington. Relations were downgraded in 2008, when then-president Evo Morales expelled the US ambassador and Washington reciprocated by expelling Bolivia’s envoy; the two capitals have been represented by chargés d’affaires ever since.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31, Day Two in Antalya10 November 2026

An “Age of Climate Extremes”: COP31 Enters Day Two With the US Absent and Electrification the Big Push

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya moves into its second day on Tuesday with the leaders’ segment still to come on 11–12 November, and with an unusual hole in the room: the United States, in the ninth month of its war with Iran and the energy crisis triggered by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, is not sending a delegation. UN Secretary-General António Guterres has framed the moment as an “age of climate extremes,” and the Turkish presidency is pushing a flagship target of raising electricity’s share of global final energy demand from about 23% today to 35% by 2035, presenting this as an implementation conference that delivers earlier pledges rather than adding new ones. Analysts say this energy shock differs from those of the 1970s and 2022 because the alternatives — solar, batteries and electrified transport — are now cheap and scalable. Ukraine, meanwhile, opens its national pavilion on 11 November with more than 70 events centred on water, documenting what the war has done to the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov, the Dnipro and Danube rivers and the destroyed Kakhovka reservoir.
