Latest · Americas · Brooklyn — Building Collapse Toll Finalised12 November 2026

Search Ends in Brooklyn: Eight Dead, Including a Girl of 9 and a Boy of 10, After a 1930 Apartment Block Fell Into a Construction Pit

New York’s fire department ended rescue operations on Thursday afternoon, confirming that eight people — six adults, among them two construction workers, and two children aged nine and ten — were killed when a three-storey apartment building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed into an adjoining excavation site at about 4 p.m. on Wednesday. Six of the victims died at the scene and two in hospital; 18 people were injured, 14 civilians and four firefighters, with most of those still in hospital described as stable and one in a critical condition. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the final toll as the site was handed to the buildings department and police. City records show the 96-year-old, four-unit building had a long history of hazardous violations stretching back to 1972, and a report filed in March described cracks and a facade in poor condition — details investigators are now examining alongside the excavation work next door, where a nine-storey development was planned.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Wall Street Closes Lower12 November 2026

Wall Street Absorbs a Wild Day for Nvidia and Oil, Closing About 0.4 Percent Lower Across All Three Main Indexes

US stocks finished Thursday with modest losses after a session dominated by two sharp swings. Nvidia, whose co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on Thursday, plunged about 14 percent at the open before recovering much of the ground to close down 4.3 percent, while rivals AMD and Broadcom each rose more than 5 percent. The S&P 500 ended down 0.4 percent at 7,592.40, the Dow Jones fell 189 points to 52,297.09 and the Nasdaq lost 0.4 percent to 25,366.09, trimming an early drop of 1.6 percent. The second jolt came from oil: after President Donald Trump posted that the United States does not need a deal with Iran, Brent crude climbed 3.3 percent to settle at $95.82 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate rose 2.55 percent to $88.74, while hawkish comments from Federal Reserve officials lifted the dollar and pushed the euro to its weakest level since April 2025. Investors now await Friday’s US producer price data for October.

Latest · Americas · Washington — Logan Circle Shooting12 November 2026

Gunman Kills One Man in a Washington Gym and Wounds Four Others a Mile From the White House, Then Takes His Own Life

A shooting in Washington’s Logan Circle neighbourhood on Thursday morning left one man dead and four people wounded, one of them critically, before the gunman shot himself during a standoff with police. Interim police chief Jeffery Carroll said the attacker opened fire at about 8 a.m. inside a VIDA fitness centre at 15th and P Streets Northwest, roughly a mile north of the White House, then moved into a nearby building and barricaded himself in a seventh-floor apartment, where officers negotiated with him by phone for nearly an hour before hearing a single shot. US media and a city council member identified the suspect as a physician, Dr Matt Lambert, and police said the attack appeared to stem from a conflict with the homeowners’ association of his condominium building and a related court case involving the man killed in the gym. Mayor Muriel Bowser called it a senseless act of gun violence; federal agents from the FBI, Secret Service and ATF assisted at the scene, and the White House was kept informed.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Outage12 November 2026

A Microsoft 365 Outage Knocks Outlook, Teams and Copilot Offline Across 74 Countries as Servers Fail Their Health Checks

One of the year’s largest workplace outages disrupted Microsoft services for hours on Thursday, cutting off Outlook email, Microsoft 365, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot for users in 74 countries and territories. Reports on the tracking site Downdetector peaked at about 138,000 for Outlook alone around 1 p.m. Eastern Time, with more than 47,000 reports for Microsoft 365, before easing through the evening as Microsoft restarted infrastructure region by region. The company said a large pool of its servers had unexpectedly failed automated health checks and dropped offline, leaving the remaining capacity overloaded, and stressed there was no indication of a cyberattack; it tracked the mailbox failure as incident EX1550543 and the wider disruption as MO1550821. The failure also rippled into tools built on Microsoft’s platform, with OpenAI and Anthropic reporting problems with their Microsoft connectors. By Friday morning in Asia the incident remained formally open, with restoration described as gradual.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — Ebola Outbreak12 November 2026

Attacks on Health Workers Hamper Congo’s Ebola Fight as Cases Pass 11,100 and Deaths Exceed 5,400, MSF Warning the Outbreak Is Still Out of Control

Six months after it was declared, the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo has reached 11,114 confirmed infections and 5,433 deaths, Congolese government figures show, making it the second-worst Ebola epidemic on record — and, Doctors Without Borders (MSF) warned on Thursday, it is still spreading out of control, with epidemiologists estimating that only about half of cases are being detected and 400 to 500 new infections emerging each week. Africa CDC told a briefing that insecurity is now the response’s greatest obstacle: incidents since late September include four armed attacks, 16 assaults on responders and 11 health facilities burned or otherwise targeted, among them last week’s attack on Cantine-Aloya in North Kivu, attributed to the Islamic State-linked Allied Democratic Forces, which killed 66 people including three health workers. More than 1,000 contacts of patients have been lost to follow-up and three confirmed cases remain unaccounted for. No approved vaccine or treatment exists for the Bundibugyo strain, and MSF says many front-line workers are unpaid and underequipped.

Latest · Africa · South Sudan — Amnesty Report12 November 2026

Amnesty Says UN Peacekeepers and South Sudan Had Two Weeks’ Warning Before the Abiemnom Raid That Killed 213 People

Amnesty International has accused South Sudanese authorities and the UN peacekeeping mission, UNMISS, of failing to act on warnings received at least two weeks before one of the country’s largest documented massacres since 2020. In a report published on Thursday, based on interviews with 82 people and satellite imagery, the group said between 1,000 and 1,500 armed men from neighbouring Mayom County overran the town of Abiemnom, near the Sudan border, in a four-hour assault in the early hours of 1 March, shooting residents, burning homes and looting part of the hospital. Authorities put the toll at 213 dead, including 130 residents, 76 security personnel and seven foreign nationals. Amnesty noted that UNMISS, which had roughly three dozen personnel in the town and had suffered a similar raid in April 2025, sent 40 additional police only after the attack. The mission said it had done its best with limited resources, sheltering and treating about 2,500 people at a protected site, after UN budget cuts forced it to scale back; its authorised troop ceiling was later cut from 17,000 to 12,000. Amnesty called for an independent investigation and a separate review of the peacekeepers’ response, weeks before South Sudan’s elections on 22 December.

French Frigate Surcouf Docks at Latakia in the First Visit by a French Warship to Syria, as Paris and Damascus Deepen Naval Ties

The French Navy frigate Surcouf has berthed at the Mediterranean port of Latakia in what Syrian and French officials describe as the first visit by a French military vessel to Syria. Syria’s defence ministry said Brigadier General Mohammad al-Saud, commander of the country’s naval and coastal defence forces, received a French delegation led by Admiral Mackara Ouk aboard the ship on Thursday, and that the two sides discussed expanding maritime cooperation, including training and capacity building. France’s chargé d’affaires in Damascus, Jean-Baptiste Faivre, said the port call demonstrated French support for Syria’s stability and sovereignty. The visit follows a framework military cooperation agreement signed during President Emmanuel Macron’s trip to Damascus in July. The Surcouf (F711) is a La Fayette-class stealth frigate that has served with the French Navy since 1997.

Trump Says Washington No Longer Needs a Deal With Iran, Sending Oil Higher and Clouding the Hormuz Standoff

President Donald Trump has declared that the United States no longer needs an agreement with Iran, a statement that jolted energy markets and further clouded the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz. Writing on his social-media platform, Trump said those days had long since passed and claimed the United States held Iran’s money and its strait — remarks that briefly pushed Brent crude from about $92 to above $94 a barrel before it settled 3.3 percent higher at $95.82. The comments land against Tehran’s position, restated this week, that Hormuz will stay effectively closed until Washington meets its seven conditions, including lifting the naval blockade and sanctions and formally ending the war. Traders also weighed reports of an explosion near Sirik in southern Iran, which some sources attributed to warning shots at vessels breaching navigation rules. Oil has swung between roughly $70 and $120 a barrel since the US–Israeli war with Iran began in late February.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — Winter Energy Support13 November 2026

Japan’s Cabinet Agrees to Spend About 650 Billion Yen From Reserves on Winter Energy Bills as the Yen Intervention Bill Comes Into Focus

Japan’s cabinet, led by Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, decided on Friday to draw about 650 billion yen — roughly $4.1 billion — from emergency reserves to reduce household utility bills over the winter, when energy prices remain elevated amid Middle East tensions. The winter package restarts electricity and gas support for December through February, which the government estimates will cut a typical household’s combined bills by about 7,000 yen, even as Tokyo begins scaling back its much costlier gasoline subsidy from December. Separately, a quarterly report from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York confirmed the scale of July’s coordinated currency intervention: the US government sold euros and bought 79.4 billion yen, about $497 million, alongside Japanese authorities on 31 July to prop up the sagging yen. Tokyo is also managing friction with Washington on other fronts, including a reported US demand that Japan triple its host-nation support for American troops to about 3 trillion yen over five years, and the continued detention in Russia of a Japanese journalist accused of spying, a month after his arrest.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Aviation — Fuel Costs13 November 2026

Asia-Pacific Airlines Face a Prolonged Fuel Squeeze as Cheap Hedges Expire and Jet Fuel Holds Near $148 a Barrel

Airlines across Asia-Pacific are heading into a costlier era for fuel as the cheap hedging contracts that shielded them through the Iran war’s price spike begin to expire, industry executives told a gathering of the Association of Asia Pacific Airlines in Kuala Lumpur this week. Spot jet fuel trades near $148 a barrel — down from almost $180 at the end of October but far above the roughly $85 level before the war began in late February — and fuel now accounts for about 33 percent of operating costs, up from the 25 percent the global industry group IATA recorded last year. The squeeze is visible even at the region’s strongest carriers: Singapore Airlines’ fuel bill jumped 56 percent in the six months to September, to S$3.97 billion, cutting operating profit by 29 percent despite record revenue. Executives are split on how long prices stay high: All Nippon Airways expects its fuel costs to run about 100 billion yen above plan this fiscal year before markets stabilise, while Air Astana’s chief described Brent crude at $90 to $100 as the industry’s new reality, with ticket prices not always rising fast enough to cover the difference.

Houthis Say They Targeted Saudi Arabia’s East-West Pipeline and an Oil Facility at Yanbu on the Red Sea

Yemen’s Houthi movement said on Thursday that its forces had targeted Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline and an oil facility in the Red Sea port city of Yanbu, the latest in a series of claimed attacks on the kingdom’s energy infrastructure this week. The claim, reported in regional coverage of the escalating exchange, follows Houthi statements about strikes on an Aramco facility in Riyadh and the King Khalid airport area, and Saudi announcements that its air defences have intercepted successive waves of missiles and drones. Saudi authorities had not commented on the Yanbu claim in the reports available at the time of publication. The East-West pipeline is strategically significant because it can carry crude from the kingdom’s eastern fields to Yanbu for export without transiting the Strait of Hormuz, whose effective closure by Iran has made alternative export routes central to global oil supply planning.
