Latest · Middle East · Iran — The Blockade Moves Onto Land8 November 2026

Iron Gate: Washington Takes Its Iran Blockade From the Sea Onto the Roads

The United States is widening its pressure on Iran from the water to the land. Central Command announced on Saturday a new unit, Task Force Iron Gate, based in Tampa, Florida, with about 20 military and civilian staff whose job is to stop advanced weapons, dual-use technology, sanctioned goods and illicit revenue moving across Iran’s land borders and through the Caspian Sea. Commander Admiral Brad Cooper said the group would reinforce what he called the ironclad blockade at sea. The announcement landed in a violent weekend around the Strait of Hormuz: Washington said an American aircraft fired on and disabled the Saint Kitts and Nevis-flagged commercial ship M/V Huda in the Gulf of Oman as it tried to run the blockade, while Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit sea mines on Saturday after entering an area Tehran calls unsafe with their navigation systems off, and later reported up to three more tankers catching fire on Sunday — taking the claimed total to six in two days. None of the tanker claims has been independently verified, and Iranian state media also reported explosions on Qeshm Island and in Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 850,000 Without Power8 November 2026

Darkness Before Diplomacy: Russian Strikes Cut Power to Some 850,000 Ukrainians

Russia’s latest overnight strikes on Ukraine’s energy network left roughly 850,000 people without electricity on Sunday, two days before US-brokered talks open in Abu Dhabi. The power company DTEK said more than 600,000 residents of the Dnipropetrovsk region lost supply after key facilities were damaged, with outages across Dnipro city and the Pavlohrad, Samarivskyi and Kamianske districts. In the northeast, the mayor of Zlatopil, Mykola Baksheyev, said a drone hit critical infrastructure and left nearly 250,000 customers in the Kharkiv region in the dark, with no date yet for full restoration. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 164 drones, including 112 jet-powered types, and three guided missiles overnight, and that defences intercepted or suppressed 137 of them; one civilian was killed and infrastructure damaged in Odesa. The blackouts followed a heavier wave a day earlier that cut power in five regions and disrupted transmission in Kyiv. President Zelenskyy has accused Moscow of stepping up the attacks to pressure Kyiv ahead of the talks.

Latest · Europe · Diplomacy — The Road to Abu Dhabi8 November 2026

Umerov Leads Kyiv’s Team, Medinsky Returns for Moscow — and Europe Insists on a Seat

The delegations for Tuesday and Wednesday’s talks in Abu Dhabi are now largely named. Kyiv’s team will be led by chief negotiator Rustem Umerov and include presidential office head Kirill Budanov, parliamentary faction leader David Arakhamia, diplomat Serhiy Kyslytsia and deputy intelligence chief Vadym Skibitsky; Arakhamia confirmed Umerov’s role after days of confusion in Ukrainian media over who would lead. Russia is again sending Vladimir Medinsky, the hardline Putin aide who has fronted most rounds since March 2022, alongside senior diplomats and military officials. The format itself is contested: the meetings were billed as trilateral — Washington, Moscow and Kyiv, convened by US envoy Steve Witkoff to discuss ending the war, post-war security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian steps — but French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said on Sunday that Europeans, including France, will also take part, days after Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov ruled out any European role. It is the fourth Trump-administration round and the first since Geneva in February. Kyiv’s stated priorities include halting strikes on energy infrastructure, reopening Black Sea grain shipments and prisoner exchanges.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens in Antalya9 November 2026

“A Moment of Truth”: Guterres Gives His Final Climate Warning as COP31 Begins

The COP31 climate summit opens on Monday in Antalya, Türkiye, running to 20 November at the Antalya Expo Center, with more than 90 heads of state and government expected and a leaders’ summit on 11–12 November to be opened by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and UN Secretary-General António Guterres. For Guterres it is a farewell: his second and final term ends in December, and in an interview with the Associated Press ahead of the talks he returned to one phrase — a moment of truth — arguing that governments can no longer hold endless discussions in which each tries to push responsibility onto someone else, and that everybody must do more now. He acknowledged that scientists have persuaded him the Paris goal of holding warming to 1.5 degrees is no longer within reach. The conference, presided over by Türkiye’s environment minister Murat Kurum, is expected to centre on emissions cuts, adaptation, climate finance and the move away from fossil fuels, against a backdrop of worsening heatwaves, floods and storms amplified by El Niño. The United States is set to be the only major economy absent.

Latest · Africa · Senegal — The Atlantic Route Claims 21 More8 November 2026

One Survivor, 21 Bodies: A Migrant Boat Goes Down off Kébémer

At least 21 people have died after a small migrant boat sank off Senegal’s northwestern coast, the interior ministry said on Sunday, in the latest disaster on the Atlantic route from West Africa towards Spain’s Canary Islands. The ministry said 21 bodies had been recovered and just one person rescued by Saturday evening, calling the toll provisional while the search continued and families were being assisted. The pirogue-type vessel capsized off Loumpoul, near Kébémer, about 180 kilometres north of Dakar, between the capital and Saint-Louis; the daily Le Soleil reported that most of those who died came from the nearby communes of Léona and Thiéppe. Migrant-support organiser Saliou Diouf of Boza Fii told AFP the boat went down on Friday. The ministry did not say how many people were on board or what caused the sinking. Senegalese authorities said they had separately intercepted 142 migrants on Friday aboard a similar boat also aiming for Spain. The interior minister is expected to visit the area on Monday to offer condolences.

Latest · Africa · Sport — Nigeria’s Flamingos Make History7 November 2026

First for a Continent: Nigeria Beat Japan 2–1 to Lift the U-17 Women’s World Cup

Nigeria’s Flamingos became the first African nation to win the FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup on Saturday, beating Japan 2–1 in the final in Rabat, Morocco. Awawu Bashiru put Nigeria ahead in the 39th minute and Oluebube Umejiaku doubled the lead two minutes after half-time with her fourth goal of the tournament; Japan’s Fuki Ito pulled one back in the 80th minute, but Nigeria held on through the late pressure. Umejiaku was named player of the match and took the Golden Ball as the tournament’s best player. The title is also a first for any African women’s team at a FIFA World Cup at any level, and it capped a remarkable run: the Flamingos lost their opening match to Poland, then won six straight games — the first champions in the competition’s history to lift the trophy after losing their opener. FIFA president Gianni Infantino called the triumph well deserved, and President Bola Tinubu congratulated the squad, singling out the two scorers. In the third-place match, North Korea beat Brazil 6–0.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Dhaka Renews Its Demand8 November 2026

“A Convicted Criminal”: Bangladesh Presses India Again to Hand Over Sheikh Hasina

Bangladesh’s government has renewed its public push for India to extradite ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina. Law Minister Md Asaduzzaman told reporters in Dhaka on Sunday that Hasina is a convicted criminal, that a request for her repatriation has been sent under the two countries’ 2013 extradition treaty, and that his government is taking every legal step to bring her back through due process. He stressed that the Home Ministry, not his own, handles the file because it falls under the treaty, and that what happens next is a matter for New Delhi; if she is handed over, he said, she will face the law in Bangladesh, with no room for emotion. Hasina fled to India on 5 August 2024 as a mass uprising ended her 15 years in power. In November 2025, Bangladesh’s International Crimes Tribunal convicted her of crimes against humanity over the July–August 2024 crackdown. India has acknowledged receiving Dhaka’s requests and says they are being examined under its established processes, but has announced no decision. Days earlier, State Minister for Foreign Affairs Humaiun Kobir ruled out issuing Hasina a passport or travel document, saying the treaty alone is the route for her return — even as Hasina herself has spoken of coming back in December.

Seventy Hours in the Rubble: Seventh Body Found After Old Delhi Building Falls

The death toll from the collapse of a three-storey building under construction in central Delhi’s Hauz Qazi area rose to seven on Sunday, after rescuers recovered another body more than 70 hours into the operation. The building came down at about 9.45 a.m. on 5 November while construction work was under way near Chawri Bazar. The latest victim was identified as Luchhuman Mukhiya, 66, from Darbhanga in Bihar; the other six named dead are Sanjeet Kumar, Kishori Sah, Pankaj Sharma, Vipin Ram, Shantu and Dinesh Sah. Reports say the structure was being built without the required approval on a plot of about 50 square metres.

Latest · Americas · Environment — The Desert That Flowered8 November 2026

Pink, Yellow, Lilac, Purple: The Atacama Bursts Into Its Biggest Bloom in 30 Years

A vast stretch of Chile’s Atacama — the driest nonpolar desert on Earth — has turned into a patchwork of pink, yellow, lilac and purple, in what is being described as its largest and most colourful flowering in 30 years, and perhaps ever. The bloom, known as the Desierto Florido, follows winter rains from the El Niño super cycle that were the heaviest in some places since records began in the 1950s. The same rains brought flash floods in July and August that damaged or destroyed dozens of homes and forced hundreds of people to evacuate. More than 200 plant species are now flowering from seeds, bulbs and tubers that lay dormant in the sand for years. Moisés Grimberg, who runs Llanos de Challe National Park — 113,000 acres of arid coastline — calls the desert a natural seed bank; biologists from INIA, Chile’s national seed bank, are collecting specimens before the brief season ends around mid-November, since each plant must complete its cycle in two to three months before drying out and reseeding the soil. Chile’s tourism board expects about 300,000 overnight trips during the bloom, and Atacama governor Miguel Vargas has begun seeking UNESCO World Heritage recognition for the phenomenon.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Eisenkot’s 28 Days Begin8 November 2026

67 Recommendations, 28 Days: Eisenkot Must Turn Backing Into a Government

Former army chief Gadi Eisenkot formally received the mandate to form Israel’s next government from President Isaac Herzog on Sunday, after 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu in consultations following the deadlocked 27 October election. The Yashar party leader now has 28 days, extendable by 14, to assemble the 61 of 120 Knesset seats a government needs. The arithmetic is treacherous: 19 of his recommendations came from Arab-led parties he has ruled out governing with, his Jewish-led change bloc holds only 48 seats, and a majority runs through the haredi parties — whose demands on yeshiva-student status clash with Avigdor Liberman’s refusal to depend on Arab parties and his insistence on equal sharing of military service. Eisenkot has set four entry principles, including universal service and recognising Hamas as a terrorist organisation to be dismantled. If he fails, Herzog can hand the task to another candidate; if that fails too, a new election follows within 90 days with Netanyahu as caretaker.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — Election Deadlock8 November 2026

3,650 Votes Apart: New Zealand Will Not Know Its Government Until 27 November

New Zealanders woke on Sunday to the tightest election in years and no winner. With 2.8 million ballots counted, Labour led the party vote by just 3,650 votes — 29.36 per cent to National’s 29.21 per cent — yet National’s strength in local races gave Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s conservative bloc 67 seats in an expanded 131-seat parliament, exactly one above the 66 needed. Neither Luxon nor Labour’s Chris Hipkins claimed victory. The result was a rebuke to both major parties, whose combined vote fell below 59 per cent as support scattered to minor parties, including a first-time entrant, the centrist Opportunity party, which took seven seats and could hold the balance if the numbers shift. They may well shift: nearly 400,000 special votes — from overseas and from voters casting ballots outside their electorate, a group that historically leans left — began to be counted on Sunday and will take about three weeks. Parties will not even start coalition negotiations until the official result lands on 27 November, leaving Luxon running a caretaker government in the meantime.

Latest · Americas · Argentina — The Pope and Milei8 November 2026

A Month After Lung Surgery, Leo Flies Into Milei’s Argentina — and a Carefully Watched Speech

Pope Leo travelled on Sunday from Uruguay to Argentina, the second leg of a 12-day, three-country South American tour and the first visit by a Catholic leader to the country since 1987. The 71-year-old, US-born pontiff — making one of the most intense foreign trips by a pope in decades, less than a month after lung cancer surgery — will tour four cities until Wednesday, including a prison and a disability care centre, and deliver an address that will be parsed for what it says to President Javier Milei. The libertarian president, a close Trump ally, has championed deep austerity that the Church says falls hardest on the poorest; annual inflation has fallen from over 200 per cent to about 33 per cent under his programme. Milei has also strongly backed the Iran war, which Leo has criticised to Trump’s irritation. In Uruguay, the pope urged South American countries to tackle regional problems together and to prize patient diplomacy over conflict.
