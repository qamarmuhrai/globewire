Latest · Global Affairs · Shenzhen — First Trilateral Summit Confirmed16 November 2026

Three Presidents, One Table, First Time Ever: China Confirms the Xi–Trump–Putin Summit for Thursday

Beijing closed the last open question on Monday: President Xi Jinping will host Presidents Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, 19 November, in the first trilateral meeting of the three leaders, held on the sidelines of the 33rd APEC leaders’ meeting. The Chinese Foreign Ministry’s brief statement gave no agenda, but the pieces around it are clear. The White House had announced the plan on 13 November and the Kremlin confirmed a day later; the meeting is listed for 2:30 p.m. local time at Kylin Villa, with a separate Trump–Xi bilateral and working lunch the same day and no separate Trump–Putin meeting planned. Washington says Trump wants nuclear arms reductions at the centre of the talks — the New START treaty, the last binding limit on US and Russian strategic arsenals, expired on 5 February — and intends to raise the Iran war, while US officials say Ukraine is not expected on the trilateral agenda and is being handled on a separate track. Beijing has previously resisted joining trilateral arms-control talks, arguing its arsenal remains far smaller than Washington’s or Moscow’s.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Europe Joins the Talks16 November 2026

Europe Takes a Seat at the Table for the First Time — and Kyiv Warns of Major Russian Strikes Tonight

Ukraine’s negotiating track widened on Monday. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said European representatives joined talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in full for the first time — France, Germany, Britain, Italy and Poland, plus EU and NATO officials — the same group Kyiv says will represent Europe in any direct engagement with Russian negotiators. Kushner described the call as productive and said progress was being made, though important challenges remain; he and Witkoff also spoke with Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov about next steps. The optimism came with two qualifications. Zelenskyy called the discussion “not easy” and said all sides had agreed to meet next in Berlin with the Coalition of the Willing countries. And Ushakov said Moscow has not yet agreed to European participation in the next round at all, contradicting what US negotiators reported after meeting Putin in Moscow. The talks, which follow the first US–Ukraine–Russia three-way meeting in nine months in Abu Dhabi last week, unfolded as Zelenskyy warned in his nightly address that Ukrainian intelligence expects major Russian strikes on Monday evening, overnight and into Tuesday, and that North Korea is supplying Russia with new ballistic missiles.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Third Carrier Arrives16 November 2026

Three US Carriers in One Theatre: The Roosevelt Arrives as Iran Ends a Two-Month Lull Over Bahrain

The United States now has three aircraft carrier strike groups in the Middle East. A US official confirmed on Monday that the USS Theodore Roosevelt, which left Singapore on 8 November and transited the Strait of Malacca with Carrier Air Wing 11 and its escorts, entered the Central Command area on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington. Together with more than 20 other Navy vessels, the force supports the blockade of Iranian ports in place since July. Neither the Pentagon nor CENTCOM has formally confirmed the schedule, and reporting suggests the three-carrier overlap may be temporary, with the Roosevelt possibly relieving one of the carriers already on station. The arrival coincided with a sharp uptick in tension: Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they fired six drones at a US early-warning radar installation in Bahrain on Sunday — the second such attack in three days after roughly two months of quiet — while Bahrain said it intercepted several aerial attacks and Washington said the drones were stopped and the radar undamaged. Tehran has also escalated its threats against US forces, shipping and Gulf states, even as President Trump said last week that further strikes on Iran were “quite unlikely.”

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Inspectors Barred16 November 2026

“Until the War Has Permanently Ended”: Iran Locks UN Inspectors Out of Its Bombed Nuclear Sites

Iran will not allow UN nuclear inspectors into its damaged facilities until the Middle East war has permanently ended and the United States meets Tehran’s conditions, Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said in a televised interview. The statement, reported on Monday, hardens a suspension in place since last year’s 12-day war, when US strikes hit Iranian nuclear sites — facilities struck again after the current war broke out on 28 February. IAEA inspectors reached the Bushehr power plant in September but remain barred from Natanz, Fordow and Isfahan, leaving the agency unable to verify the status of key nuclear material. Gharibabadi questioned the purpose of the requested visits, suggesting they were likely aimed at assessing the effectiveness of American weapons, and said Tehran is preparing for three scenarios in dealing with Washington: diplomacy, economic blockade and military aggression. The IAEA had urged Iran days earlier to take steps toward resuming inspections. With inspectors locked out, outside analysts note there is no verified evidence about how Iran’s programme has advanced during the gap — and no way to confirm that it has not.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Day 26116 November 2026

Another Tanker Burns, a Drone Claim Surfaces, and the UN Counts 25 Seafarers Dead Around Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz remained at a near-standstill on Monday, the 261st day since Iran declared it closed. Tracking data again counted just six AIS-visible commercial crossings for the day — one inbound, five outbound, two of them tankers — holding the seven-day average at about seven a day, roughly 8 percent of the pre-crisis norm of nearly 85. The danger behind the numbers kept growing. Britain’s UKMTO reported an outbound tanker struck by an unknown projectile on Monday, with a fire in its engine room and all crew accounted for; Iran’s army said a separate tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine east of the strait late Sunday, a report Reuters could not independently verify. Tehran’s Guards navy also claimed on Monday to have seized control of a US military drone south of Qeshm Island and brought it down intact. The human toll is now formally on record at the UN: at least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the strait since February, the IMO tracked 136 security incidents between March and 11 November, and October was the deadliest month yet for incidents. The threat level stays at severe.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31, Draft on the Table16 November 2026

A First Draft, a “Divan,” and 271 AI Pledges: COP31’s Final Week Opens With the Hard Questions Intact

COP31 moved from technical talks to political bargaining on Monday. Late in the day Australia, which leads the negotiations under the shared Turkey–Australia arrangement, circulated a first draft of an overarching decision — though delegations remain divided not only over its contents but over whether such a text should exist at all. Ministers arriving for Tuesday’s high-level segment will take up the questions technicians could not settle: who pays for climate action, how vulnerable countries are helped to adapt, how workers are protected through the energy transition, and what the final deal says about moving away from fossil fuels. A further dispute concerns the timing of the next IPCC assessment, with vulnerable nations pressing for 2028. Ministers will meet in what the presidency calls a Divan to hunt for compromises. Alongside the negotiations, the presidency launched the Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge, which by Monday morning had drawn 80 endorsements and 271 commitments on sustainable AI, and a Green Industrialisation Compact. Campaign groups say the first week delivered too little, a charge underlined by Saturday’s rain-soaked march through Antalya, the largest protest since the talks opened on 9 November. The summit closes Friday, 20 November.

Nvidia’s board is not considering reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive, people familiar with its deliberations said on Monday, even as a public campaign for his return gathered more than 180,000 signatures and endorsements from Elon Musk and President Trump, who said he would not mind seeing Huang back. Latest · Americas · United States — Flights Recover, Fight Begins16 November 2026

18,000 Cancellations, One Suspect Router: US Flights Recover as Washington Fights Over a $50 Billion Fix

US air travel largely returned to normal on Monday after the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years snarled the country’s skies for three days. More than 18,000 flights were cancelled and nearly 28,000 delayed from Friday, when departures were halted for over four hours in a communications failure now suspected to trace back to a single router installed during routine maintenance. By Monday afternoon cancellations had fallen to about 330, down from more than 2,300 on Sunday. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy put the economic damage in the billions of dollars; brokerage Bernstein estimates a $345 million earnings hit for the four largest US airlines alone. The argument has already moved to money and blame. Duffy has raised his funding request for air traffic control and airports to $50 billion, noting the FAA still runs on ageing equipment including floppy disks and paper strips, while Democrats counter that only $1 billion of the $12.5 billion approved last year has been spent and are demanding hearings. Republicans want the funding passed before the current Congress leaves and Democrats take over in January.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — The Winter Tents Dispute16 November 2026

150,000 Tents Waiting or 93,000 Delivered? Gaza’s Winter Aid Row Turns on Two Sets of Numbers

Gaza’s winter shelter crisis now has two competing accounts. Australia, Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom say 150,000 tents and other essential supplies are being held at the border while flooding from the season’s first storm has inundated camps where about 1.4 million people — roughly 65 percent of the population — live in makeshift shelters. The US-led Board of Peace, the body overseeing President Trump’s Gaza plan, has called Israel’s flood response “insufficient” and declared an immediate humanitarian emergency, a judgement the UN backed. Israel’s account differs sharply: COGAT, the military body coordinating aid, says more than 93,000 tents and over 8,600 trucks of winter supplies have already been delivered under a winter plan drawn up with the Board of Peace and aid groups, with about 600 trucks entering daily through Kerem Shalom. The UN humanitarian office says the flooding collapsed damaged buildings, damaged thousands of shelters and suspended critical services; a sand bank that gave way onto tents in central Gaza on 7 November killed five people, three of them children. Medical sources said an Israeli drone strike near al-Mawasi killed two Palestinians on Sunday, among 1,643 killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, according to Gaza’s health ministry.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola, Six Months On15 November 2026

“Still Not Under Control”: Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Passes 11,200 Cases With No Vaccine for This Strain

Six months after it was declared, the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is still out of control, the head of the Africa CDC said on Sunday. Director-General Jean Kaseya cited deadly attacks on health workers, unpaid staff and a lack of resources, and said more than 80 percent of new cases are emerging outside known contact lists while over 65 percent of deaths occur in the community, outside health centres. Government data put the toll since 15 May at more than 11,200 infections and 5,500 deaths, making this the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record after West Africa’s 2014–16 epidemic. The outbreak is driven by the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is still no licensed vaccine or treatment. Genomic work by Congolese, Kenyan, Ugandan and Nigerian teams on more than 1,100 virus samples traces every 2026 case to a single jump from animals to humans, followed by sustained person-to-person spread. The virus has expanded from one province and three health zones to ten provinces and 66 zones; Kenya’s nine cases all trace to a single introduction from Congo, Uganda’s smaller outbreak has ended, and single imported cases were recorded in Nigeria and France.

Nearly two weeks after the US midterm elections, late counting is still reshaping Congress.
