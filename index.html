Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shenzhen — The Trilateral Day19 November 2026

Three Nuclear Powers, One Lakeside Villa: The Kylin Villa Talks Begin Today

Thursday is the day itself. Xi Jinping hosts Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin at Kylin Villa, a 38-hectare lakeside resort in the foothills of Kylin Mountain in northwestern Shenzhen, for the first trilateral meeting ever held between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia — the world’s three largest nuclear powers. Security around the venue, where North Korea’s Kim Jong-il once stayed in secret two decades ago, has been sharply tightened. A Trump–Xi bilateral comes first, with Putin then joining for the three-way session; the agenda, as briefed by officials, runs from nuclear arms control after the expiry of New START in February, to Trump’s war with Iran, trade, artificial intelligence and — despite US officials saying Trump is not expected to raise it — Russia’s war in Ukraine. Kyiv’s fear of a deal struck over its head, and the inevitable Yalta comparisons, hang over the room; analysts and a senior US official alike caution that no agreement is expected, and that the meeting’s weight is likely to be symbolic — a photograph that lifts China’s standing at the top table as much as any text it produces.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — The Minutes Land19 November 2026

“Almost All” Saw More Hikes Coming: Gold Tumbles to a One-Year Low

The document landed, and it was hawkish. Minutes of the Federal Reserve’s 27–28 October meeting, published at 19:00 GMT on Wednesday, show almost all officials judged further rate increases likely appropriate under their baseline outlook — and a couple floated a move larger than a quarter point at the next meeting if inflation data disappoint. The October hold at 3.75%–4.00%, passed 10–2 with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack preferring an immediate hike, now reads less like a pause than a staging post; staff put September PCE inflation at 3.6%, with core at 3.1%. Markets took the hint they had already priced: spot gold slid about 3% to roughly $3,728–3,732 an ounce, its lowest in more than a year, the dollar index held near 103.78, a 19-month high, bitcoin slipped, and on Wall Street the S&P 500 fell 0.3% while the Nasdaq 100 rose 0.4% as Nvidia’s earnings lifted AI sentiment. Governor Christopher Waller added fuel, urging either a half-point December hike or a faster 2027 pace.

Latest · Europe · Berlin & Brussels — Guarantees and Money19 November 2026

Thirty Flags in Berlin, €45 Billion in Brussels: Europe Builds Ukraine’s Safety Net

Europe moved on two tracks at once. In Berlin on Thursday, leaders of more than 30 countries in the Coalition of the Willing — hosted by Chancellor Friedrich Merz alongside President Emmanuel Macron and Prime Minister Andy Burnham, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy invited and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner expected — discuss deploying a Multinational Force Ukraine once fighting ends. French officials describe the guarantee being built in three parts: Ukraine’s own armed forces as the first line of defence, the multinational deployment, and robust legal and political commitments. The leaders will also review live deployment exercises running in Poland from 9 to 22 November, involving some 2,800 troops from 11 countries. In Brussels, meanwhile, the European Commission proposed a fresh €45 billion loan for 2027 — up to €25 billion for defence and €20 billion for budget support, against a €70 billion funding gap — which Kyiv would repay only after Russia pays war reparations, on top of the €90 billion already agreed; member states must still approve it.

Latest · Europe · Russia — Refining Down 45%19 November 2026

Twenty Days of Drones: Ukraine’s Campaign Nearly Halves Russian Refining

The first aggregate measure of Kyiv’s refinery campaign is stark. Twenty days of near-daily Ukrainian drone strikes have cut Russia’s daily crude processing by about 45% from its late-October level — from more than 700,000 tonnes a day to roughly 400,000 tonnes, about 3 million barrels — according to two industry sources cited by Reuters. Operations have been disrupted or halted at 15 of Russia’s 35 major refineries, up from nine in the middle of last week, including plants at Ryazan, Yaroslavl and Kirishi and across the Volga region; the affected plants’ combined nominal capacity is around 500,000 tonnes a day, some 54% of the national total. The nuance matters: because the decline was gradual, November’s average still sits above 500,000 tonnes a day, only 5–10% below September and October. Even so, fuel rationing has been reported in at least thirteen Russian regions, and Russia’s energy ministry offered Reuters no comment.

One Day Out: Akvan Bears Down on the Andhra–Odisha Coast

Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan enters its final approach on Thursday, with landfall now expected around noon on Friday 20 November somewhere between Machilipatnam and Puri, at sustained winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to 110. The India Meteorological Department forecasts isolated extremely heavy rain across coastal Andhra Pradesh, Yanam and Odisha through Thursday and Friday, with thundersqualls and winds of 80–90 km/h near the coast. Preparations have moved from advisories to closures: schools, colleges and Anganwadi centres in Odisha’s Malkangiri district are shut for 19 and 20 November, officials’ leave has been cancelled in the alert districts, and rescue and fire-service teams stand deployed along the threatened stretch. Akvan is the third Bay of Bengal storm in about three weeks, after Arnab and Murasu — a clustering that has left coastal administrations running the same evacuation and shelter drills for the third time in a month, and residents of low-lying districts watching a familiar, unwelcome track.

Latest · Global Affairs · Deep Space — One Light-Day19 November 2026

A Day of Light Away: Voyager 1 Crosses a Distance No Object Has Reached

At 10:16 UTC on Wednesday, a spacecraft built with 1977 technology passed a threshold no human-made object has reached before. Voyager 1 is now one light-day from Earth — about 25.9 billion kilometres, or 173 astronomical units — meaning its radio signals, travelling at light speed, take a full 24 hours to arrive, and any command from mission control takes another day to get there: a two-day round trip for every exchange. Launched on 5 September 1977 to study Jupiter and Saturn, the probe crossed into interstellar space in 2012 and still sends back measurements at nearly 38,000 mph, though only two of its instruments — the magnetometer and plasma wave subsystem — remain switched on as its plutonium power fades by roughly four watts a year. “Reaching one light-day is an astounding feat, one that I never expected to see,” said project manager Suzanne Dodd. NASA discusses the milestone publicly on Thursday; Voyager 2 is not expected to reach the same distance until 2035.

Latest · Americas · Fort Meade — Code Orange19 November 2026

A Patch Broke the Spy Network: Inside the NSA’s NSANet Outage

The network America’s spies use to talk to each other went down — not to an enemy, but to a fix. NSANet, the National Security Agency’s highly classified internal communications network, failed on Tuesday 17 November, and by Tuesday night the agency had closed its Fort Meade, Maryland campus to most employees under a “code orange” footing, after a morning spent on staggered “code yellow” arrivals. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton attributed the outage to a software patch for serious vulnerabilities in PHP — the language behind roughly 70% of the world’s websites, whose flaws, dubbed PHP2Shell, were found last month with help from AI tools — which left some systems unable to restart. The NSA calls it temporary degradation during scheduled maintenance and insists mission-critical capabilities stayed fully operational; six sources told CNN restoration was still incomplete, and both Clayton and the White House stress there is no evidence of a cyberattack, nor any link to the week’s string of US technology failures from the FAA to Microsoft.

Latest · Americas · Brooklyn — 330 Months19 November 2026

27 Years for a Plot Drawn on a Napkin: The Merchant Sentencing

A Brooklyn courtroom closed one of the strangest chapters of the 2024 campaign. Asif Merchant, 48, a Pakistani businessman, was sentenced on Tuesday to 330 months — 27 years and six months — for attempting an act of terrorism transcending national boundaries and murder for hire, after a jury convicted him in March of travelling to the United States to arrange the killing of American politicians on behalf of Iran’s Revolutionary Guard. Trial testimony put Donald Trump, Joe Biden and Nikki Haley among the possible targets, in a plot prosecutors tied to revenge for the 2020 killing of Qassem Soleimani; the “hitmen” Merchant recruited were undercover FBI agents, alerted by an acquaintance who had watched him sketch the plan on a napkin. Merchant testified he acted unwillingly to protect family in Tehran; Iran denies the plot, and Judge Eric Komitee called the contemplated killing of a president exceptionally serious. Merchant, arrested in Texas on 12 July 2024 — a day before the unrelated Butler attempt on Trump’s life — declined to speak at sentencing.

Latest · Africa · Mogadishu — Five Charges19 November 2026

“A Bitter Truth”: Somalia’s President Lays Five Charges Against the UAE

Somalia’s president has taken his government’s rupture with the United Arab Emirates public and personal. In a televised address on Tuesday, Hassan Sheikh Mohamud accused Abu Dhabi of years of interference: signing port agreements for Berbera, Bosaso and Kismayo directly with regional administrations without federal approval; building military bases and raising security forces outside Somalia’s national command; funding and backing regions against Mogadishu; helping bring about Israel’s recognition of Somaliland; and secretly using Berbera port in January to move a Yemeni separatist leader out of Yemen. The charges formalise a break already made in January, when Somalia’s cabinet annulled all port, security, defence and economic agreements with the UAE and barred Emirati military and cargo aircraft from Somali airspace. The UAE, once among the Horn of Africa’s biggest investors and security partners, had not publicly responded to the address by publication.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Shanghai — The Undisclosed Death19 November 2026

Sacked and Blacklisted: The Gene-Editing Trial That Hid a Child’s Death

China has punished the scientist at the centre of a gene-editing trial that killed a six-year-old girl — and then said nothing about it in print. Shanghai authorities have dismissed neuroscientist Zilong Qiu as deputy director of the Songjiang Research Institute, barred him and a Xinhua Hospital colleague from scientific projects for five years, and entered both in a national database of serious research-integrity breaches, after a municipal investigation found the team failed to follow regulations and overlooked warning signs, including liver damage seen in primate tests. The girl, who had the rare neurodevelopmental disorder Snijders Blok–Campeau syndrome, died in March 2025 from complications after a viral-vector therapy was injected into her spine at Xinhua Hospital; her parents had contributed more than $800,000 to fund the experiment. Qiu’s February paper in Nature made no mention of the trial or the death — an omission exposed in July by a joint Science and Retraction Watch investigation. The hospital and Shanghai Jiao Tong University’s medical school have been reprimanded and ordered to review their own practices.
