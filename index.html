Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz30 October 2026

Iran Says Four Tankers Caught Fire After Striking Mines in Hormuz, as UK Agency Reports Explosives Placed on a Hull

Iran's armed forces said on Thursday that four oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines while trying to navigate the Strait of Hormuz, a day after the US military said it had cleared the main transit routes. The Revolutionary Guards said three vessels exploded on what Tehran calls an unauthorised southern route after switching off radar and navigation systems and ignoring warnings, while the army said a fourth caught fire east of the strait in a restricted area. The claims have not been independently verified, and the flags and exact timing remain unclear. Britain's UKMTO agency gave a different account for one vessel about 25 nautical miles north-east of Khasab, Oman: a small boat approached and attached two explosive devices to the hull above the waterline, sparking an engine-room fire that temporarily disabled the tanker before it was extinguished, with the crew safe. UKMTO also reported a dhow that caught fire and sank, its crew rescued, and another tanker struck by an unknown projectile. Tehran has since declared the strait closed to what it calls violating ships, while US Central Command insists primary routes are mine-free.

Latest · Global Affairs · Crypto Hack30 October 2026

Binance Pledges to Cover All Losses After a Hack Estimated at $6.3 Billion, the Largest in Crypto History

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has suspended deposits and withdrawals while it investigates a major security breach it puts at about $6.3 billion, with blockchain firm Chainalysis estimating about $5.9 billion stolen at the time of the theft — well above the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit in February 2025. The exchange said an initial investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that let an attacker reach a service container and submit unauthorised withdrawals, and stressed it has found no evidence of a wallet private-key leak. Co-chief executive Yi He said user funds are safe, Binance will cover all losses, and withdrawals should resume within 48 hours. Partners have frozen roughly $3 billion of the moved assets, including more than $1.57 billion in USDT frozen by Tether and over $800 million in USDC by Circle; the Arbitrum Security Council also moved 74.56 million ARB tokens from a wallet linked to the attacker into a frozen wallet. Security firms have pointed to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, though Binance has not attributed the attack.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election30 October 2026

Israel's Final Results Leave Netanyahu's Bloc on 53 Seats, Eight Short of a Majority, With Arab Parties as Kingmakers

Israel's Central Elections Committee published final results early on Friday confirming Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing and far-right bloc on 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, with Likud the largest party on 27. The opposition bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot took 48 seats — Yashar 21, Bennett's B'Yachad 12, the Democrats eight and Yisrael Beytenu seven — while the Arab-led Ra'am and Joint List won 11 and eight seats respectively, 19 in total, emerging as potential kingmakers. Turnout was 77.9%, with 5,716,665 votes cast. The count closed amid unsubstantiated claims from Netanyahu's camp that the vote was stolen; committee chair Justice Noam Sohlberg has asked Shin Bet chief David Zini to examine alleged foreign interference in the Arab sector, stressing the inquiry will not change the results. The tally goes to President Isaac Herzog on 4 November, the new Knesset is due to be sworn in on 10 November, and neither bloc can reach 61 without difficult coalition talks — or the prospect of another election.

Latest · Europe · Bosnia — EUFOR Crash30 October 2026

Four Italian EUFOR Personnel Killed as a Chinook Crashes on a Training Flight Near Brčko in Bosnia

An Italian CH-47 Chinook helicopter with the European Union Force in Bosnia crashed during routine training near the village of Goriče in the Brčko district on Thursday, about 180km north of Sarajevo, killing four people and injuring 11. Fifteen people were on board, all Italian nationals; three died at the scene and a fourth in hospital, Brčko hospital spokesman Salih Ibrahimbegović said, with the injured in stable condition. Italy's defence ministry said the dead were two army soldiers and two volunteers from the Italian Red Cross military corps. EUFOR said no civilians were hurt and no civilian property was damaged, and that a full investigation is under way, with no cause yet identified. The force, currently about 1,200 strong, has overseen the military provisions of the Dayton peace agreement since taking over from NATO in 2004; its last fatal helicopter crash in Bosnia was in 2008, when two German officers and two Spanish pilots died.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea30 October 2026

Kim Jong Un Demands 'Absolute Superiority' in AI Drones as North Korea Shows Off Its 600mm 'Sniper' Launcher

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un attended the Unmanned Aeronautical Technology Development-2026 exhibition on Thursday and ordered scientists to put unmanned weapons on a high-level AI basis as a top priority, state media KCNA reported on Friday. The accompanying demonstration featured tactical suicide drones, multipurpose drones firing air-to-surface missiles, cruise missiles and a battalion of 600mm multiple rocket launchers — the system dubbed the Sniper, shown in combat firing publicly for the first time. KCNA claimed the drones and missiles hit an island target with 100% accuracy, a claim that could not be independently verified. Kim, accompanied by his wife Ri Sol Ju and daughter Ju Ae, said strength alone guarantees peace. South Korea's military detected about 10 short-range ballistic missiles fired from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea, travelling roughly 250km, in what Seoul counts as Pyongyang's 17th ballistic launch of the year; officials believe they were KN-25 super-large launchers, which the North says can carry the Hwasan-31 tactical nuclear warhead. Photographs also showed for the first time a tailless, flying-wing stealth-style drone.

Latest · Europe · European Central Bank29 October 2026

Lagarde to Leave the ECB on 7 February, Nine Months Early, as France Slides Toward Recession

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde told the Governing Council and European Council President António Costa on Thursday that she will step down on 7 February 2027, nearly nine months before her term ends in October 2027. The early exit gives current EU leaders time to choose a successor before a French presidential election that polls suggest far-right candidate Marine Le Pen is favoured to win; a source told Reuters Lagarde wants freedom to speak on political matters, with the World Economic Forum mentioned as a possible next role. Former Dutch governor Klaas Knot and former Spanish governor Pablo Hernández de Cos are seen as frontrunners. She leaves mid-way through a rate-hiking cycle aimed at stopping an Iran-war oil shock feeding into wages and prices, with the deposit rate expected to be held at 2.5% and a possible rise in December. The announcement landed as France's statistics agency put third-quarter GDP down 0.1% after a flat second quarter and a 0.2% fall in the first — three quarters without growth, which Economy Minister Roland Lescure described as an economy at a standstill.

Latest · Europe · Spain Train Crash30 October 2026

Spain's King and Queen Visit Toledo Survivors as the Crash Toll Is Reported Rising and Ouigo Cancels All Friday Trains

King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia visited Toledo University Hospital on Thursday afternoon to meet survivors and families of Wednesday's high-speed collision near Urda, cancelling their official engagements as three days of national mourning began; the couple were visibly emotional after thanking emergency and health workers. One report citing the transport ministry put the death toll at at least 74, above the 69 given in most official updates, with the hospital treating 15 patients in intensive care and eight on general wards as identification work continued. Investigators remain focused on how a Renfe train was cleared to proceed without speed limits despite a stop signal after a systems failure halted traffic across more than 60km of the Madrid–Seville line, hitting a stationary Iryo train. Disruption widened on Friday: low-cost operator Ouigo cancelled every service after a court suspended the minimum-service rules for the drivers' strike, and it has also cancelled trains on Monday 2 November. It is Spain's second fatal crash on the same line this year, after January's collision near Adamuz killed 46.

Latest · Americas · US Economy & Midterms29 October 2026

US Economy Grew 3.4% in the Third Quarter Days Before the Midterms, While Household Incomes Squeezed

The US economy expanded at a 3.4% annualised rate in July–September, up from 2.2% in the second quarter and above the roughly 3.1–3.2% economists expected, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said in its advance estimate on Thursday. Investment led the acceleration — private domestic investment rose 19.4%, equipment spending 20.9% on computers and related kit, and inventories added 1.61 percentage points — while consumer spending grew 3.8% and real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.9%. The inflation picture split: the PCE price index rose at a 2.1% annual rate in the quarter, down from 5.0%, but was up 3.6% year-on-year in September, and real disposable income fell 0.3% that month as inflation-adjusted spending barely moved. The figures land in the final days before Tuesday's midterm elections, with more than 43 million early ballots already cast, Republicans holding the Senate 53–47, and President Trump replacing weekend rallies in Iowa and Kansas with telephone rallies across nearly 20 states. The second GDP estimate is due on 25 November.

Latest · Africa · Somalia Mission30 October 2026

Uganda Begins Withdrawing Its Troops From Somalia After Nearly 20 Years as US Funding Is Pulled Back

Uganda announced on Friday that it has begun withdrawing its troops from Somalia, aiming for a full pullout by August 2027, ending a deployment that began in 2007 when it became the first country to serve under the African Union mission there. Defence chief General Muhoozi Kainerugaba confirmed the start of the drawdown. The move follows mounting financial pressure on the successor mission, AUSSOM: in a diplomatic note on 1 July the United States told the African Union it would oppose renewing the mission at the UN Security Council if it kept UN logistical backing, citing frustration at slow Somali security-sector reform. A 25% cut linked to the US contribution has already hit frontline operations that depend on the UN support office — food, water, fuel, medical evacuation and transport under a budget of roughly $500 million a year. Washington's original deadline of 31 December 2026 has been softened into a 12-month extension, with UN logistical services to phase down from January 2027.

Latest · Africa · Rail Corridors30 October 2026

Tanzania and Angola Agree to Link the Atlantic and Indian Ocean Coasts by Rail Through Zambia and DR Congo

Tanzania and Angola have agreed to connect their ocean transport corridors with a railway running through Zambia and the Democratic Republic of Congo, President Samia Suluhu Hassan said on Thursday. The link would join Angola's Benguela railway and the Lobito corridor on the Atlantic coast to the Tanzania–Zambia Railway (TAZARA) and the port of Dar es Salaam on the Indian Ocean, creating a cross-continental route for copper and other freight. No completion date was given. The plan builds on China's $1.4 billion agreement in 2025 with Zambia and Tanzania to modernise TAZARA, which Beijing first financed in the 1970s, and on Italian builder Webuild's tender win this week for a section of the Zambia–Lobito line. TAZARA is widely seen as a counterweight to the US- and EU-backed Lobito Corridor. Angola has also invited Tanzania to invest in the Lobito oil refinery, with Tanzania's state petroleum company assessing a partnership.

Latest · Africa · Ebola — DR Congo29 October 2026

An Ituri Ebola Clinic Empties for the First Time Since May, Even as the Outbreak Passes 10,000 Cases

Staff and residents sang and danced at the Médecins Sans Frontières treatment centre in Mongbwalu, in DR Congo's Ituri province, as its last confirmed Ebola patients were discharged — the first time the clinic has held no confirmed cases since the outbreak was declared in May. The milestone is local rather than national: health authorities said on 27 October that the outbreak, Africa's latest, has passed 10,000 confirmed cases, the vast majority in DR Congo, making it the deadliest in the country's history with nearly half of those infected dying. Experts continue to warn the wider epidemic may be reaching a plateau rather than ending, and response teams remain deployed across the east, where the virus has also been reported in North and South Kivu. The emptying of one front-line centre is being treated by medics as a sign that early detection, isolation and community engagement can turn individual hotspots around even while the overall outbreak continues.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy & Diplomacy30 October 2026

Eleven EU Countries to Release 42.6 Million Barrels From Strategic Reserves as Leaders Deepen Ties With Canada

Eleven European Union countries will release 42.6 million barrels of oil from strategic reserves to stabilise diesel prices before winter, as the Hormuz tanker incidents and the Iran war keep energy markets under strain and Brent heads for a fourth straight monthly gain. The coordinated release is the bloc's latest attempt to cushion households and industry against fuel costs that have also complicated central-bank decisions across Europe this week. In parallel, EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney used a summit in Montreal to deepen EU–Canada ties, with von der Leyen continuing to describe Canada as an associated member of the EU despite reservations from several member states, including Germany; Carney said a formal pact would take several more months of negotiation. Separately at the United Nations, the General Assembly voted on 28 October by 130 votes to 17, with 25 abstentions, for a resolution calling for an end to the US embargo on Cuba, with India among those voting in favour.
