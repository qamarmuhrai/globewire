Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia & Yemen27 October 2026

Fire at Ras Tanura: Houthis Claim a Ballistic-Missile Hit on Saudi Arabia's Biggest Refinery, and Oil Climbs

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Tuesday they had struck Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery — the kingdom's largest, at 550,000 barrels a day on the Gulf coast in the Eastern Province — with a ballistic missile, with military spokesman Yahya Saree claiming the strike caused massive fires. Saudi authorities had not confirmed the claim by Tuesday evening, but Bloomberg reported, citing a person familiar with the matter, that a fire near the Ras Tanura oil terminal in the early hours had been contained. A resident of nearby Al Khobar told AFP he heard explosions around dawn and received a mobile-phone alert. The Saudi-led coalition said separately that it had intercepted three ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province and four drones aimed at the south, and civil defence said the threat had passed in the Eastern Region and Kharj — without directly linking those interceptions to the terminal fire. Brent crude rose about 0.6% to $116.50 a barrel and US WTI about 0.7% to $102.50 as traders weighed the risk: nearly all Saudi oil exports leave through Ras Tanura in the Gulf or Yanbu on the Red Sea, both previously targeted, and the Houthis have escalated attacks on the kingdom since September, opening another front in the wider US-Israeli war on Iran. Ras Tanura was also hit by an Iranian drone attack in early March that forced a partial shutdown.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election27 October 2026

Election Day Turns Tense in Israel: Netanyahu Declares an Emergency, Alleges Vote-Buying, as Turnout Runs High

Voting continued across Israel on Tuesday in the first Knesset election since the 7 October 2023 attacks, with neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot projected to reach the 61 seats needed to govern — final television polls put Netanyahu's allies near 50 seats and the opposition on 52 to 56. Turnout reached 16.68% by 10 a.m., the highest early figure since 1981, and stood at 31.5% by mid-afternoon. Netanyahu declared election day an emergency, urged right-wing supporters to turn out, warned of possible fraud and claimed the left was "stealing" the election, pointing to higher turnout in the Arab sector; he also alleged a vote-buying attempt after three suspects were arrested in the Negev with envelopes of cash and ballot papers bearing the name of Mansour Abbas of the Ra'am party. Eisenkot likewise urged patience and turnout. In Tel Aviv, police arrested a man armed with a knife who was suspected of threatening opposition politician Yair Golan during a campaign tour at HaMesila Park. Polls close at 10 p.m., when exit polls will be published; counting runs overnight, the final tally is expected by 30 October and official results on 4 November, after which the president will give one leader a mandate to try to form a coalition while Netanyahu's government continues in a caretaker role.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations26 October 2026

UN Inquiry Accuses Israel of 'Extermination' in the West Bank and Continuing Genocide in Gaza — Israel Calls It Propaganda

A United Nations-mandated commission of inquiry said on Monday that Israel has committed the crime against humanity of extermination in the occupied West Bank, alongside torture, rape and other sexual violence and persecution on discriminatory grounds, while continuing to commit genocide in the Gaza Strip despite last year's ceasefire. The Independent International Commission of Inquiry, set up by the UN Human Rights Council in 2021 and which does not speak for the UN itself, found that Israeli military operations reinforced by sustained settler violence amounted to a "widespread and systematic attack" on civilians, intended to collectively punish West Bank residents for the Hamas-led 7 October 2023 attack. Its report documented a more than 40-fold rise in airstrike deaths in the West Bank — 254 people killed between October 2023 and September this year, against six in the previous 15 years — and commission chair Srinivasan Muralidhar said the killing and injuring of civilians and destruction of homes formed part of a strategy to displace Palestinians and make life unlivable; he is due to present the report to the UN General Assembly's Third Committee this week. The commission first concluded in September 2025 that Israel was committing genocide in Gaza. Israel rejected the new findings outright: its foreign ministry called the report "a propaganda document detached from reality, aimed at undermining Israel's right to self-defence" and "legitimising jihadist terrorism," and its Geneva mission demanded the commission's immediate dissolution. The report was released the day before Israel's general election.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen27 October 2026

73 Young Recruits Dead in Shabwa: The Toll From the Houthi Missile Strike on a Training Camp Keeps Rising

Provincial authorities in Yemen's Shabwa province have raised the death toll from Monday's Houthi missile strike on a government training camp to 73, with more than 200 people wounded and 14 still missing — the deadliest single attack since fighting reignited in September. Three ballistic missiles hit the Al-Alam camp, the Martyr Ahmed al-Sulaimani training centre in the Jardan district north of the provincial capital Ataq, during a morning training exercise; Reuters-verified video showed injured people and bloodied bodies scattered across the ground, and hospitals in Ataq appealed urgently for blood donations. Governor Awad al-Wazir said the dead were new recruits aged roughly 18 to 20, drawn from across Shabwa and being trained for internal security and police duties — "never before participated in any mobilisation or on any front," he said, urging the deployment of more air defences. One wounded recruit described hearing a loud explosion as shrapnel flew everywhere, and said reconnaissance aircraft had overflown the camp on the previous three nights. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the camp was run by Saudi forces and that Saudi officers were among the dead, offering no evidence; Saudi authorities did not immediately comment, and Yemeni officials rejected the claim. Shabwa holds major oil and gas fields and the Balhaf liquefied natural gas terminal, Yemen's largest energy facility, and government forces fear the strike could precede a ground offensive similar to the Houthi seizure of the Red Sea port of Mocha last month.

Anthropic Files for Its IPO: $16.3 Billion of Revenue in Six Months, an $80.7 Billion Paper Loss, and a Possible $2 Trillion Debut

Anthropic, the San Francisco AI company behind the Claude models, publicly filed its S-1 registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, setting up what could be the largest stock-market debut on record. The filing, which follows a confidential submission in June, plans a listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker "ANT," with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a syndicate of more than 20 underwriters; no share price, share count or valuation is set yet, though investors and press reports put expectations between about $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, with a debut possible next month — potentially surpassing SpaceX's June listing, which raised about $86 billion at a roughly $2 trillion valuation. The numbers are extreme in both directions. Revenue was $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, up from $1.16 billion a year earlier — a year-on-year growth rate the company puts at about 1,300% — after $4.59 billion in all of 2025 and just $41 million in 2023, and the second quarter brought Anthropic's first operating profit, $974 million. Against that sits a first-half net loss of $80.7 billion, of which about $72.4 billion is a non-cash accounting charge: as the company's valuation rose, so did the paper value of convertible notes and similar instruments its holders can turn into stock, leaving an underlying operating loss of $693 million for the half. The five-year-old company, founded by former OpenAI staff, last raised privately in May at a $965 billion valuation; its filing argues that the spread of AI through the global economy is "one of the largest opportunities in history" and warns, in an unusually long risk section, of existential risks from the technology itself.

Microsoft Offers Buyouts to 60,000 Staff — a Quarter of Its Workforce — as AI Spending Sucks Up the Budget

Microsoft confirmed on Monday that it is offering voluntary exit packages to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its global workforce of 223,000, as it tries to hold down costs while pouring money into artificial intelligence infrastructure — capital spending reported at about $175 billion. The offer covers staff at vice-president level and below on select teams in the United States, Canada, India and Britain, including parts of Xbox, Windows and Copilot, while some groups — including data-centre, sales and marketing teams — are excluded. Chief people officer Amy Coleman told employees the "Voluntary Exit Offer reflects an important part of how we're evolving our approach to change," giving eligible staff financial support and career-planning help to "shape their next chapter"; severance terms vary by level and service, and the company has set no target for how many should leave. It is Microsoft's second such programme this year: an April early-retirement offer covering about 7% of US staff was reportedly taken up by around 30% of those eligible. Headcount had already slipped from 228,000 at the end of 2025 to 223,000 by June. The announcement lands days before Microsoft reports quarterly earnings later this week, and alongside similar moves across the sector — Netflix is reported to be cutting about 1,000 jobs and eBay shed 1,000 earlier this month — as the industry's AI build-out makes chips, processors and power ever more expensive, and insiders including Anthropic's chief executive warn of mass job losses to the technology.

Eight Days Before the Midterms, Trump Offers $1 Million for Voter-Fraud Tips That End in a Conviction

President Donald Trump said on Monday that anyone who reports voter fraud in past elections or in the 3 November midterm elections will be paid $1 million if the tip leads to an arrest and conviction — an announcement made on Truth Social and repeated to reporters before he boarded Air Force One for a campaign rally in Corinth, Mississippi. "I'm also going to be issuing a $1 million bonus or reward, or whatever you want to call it, for anybody that reports voter fraud," he said. He did not say whether the money would come from government funds or his own resources, named no reporting mechanism, and Congress has authorised no such fund; the Justice Department last week separately rolled out a $30,000 bounty for tips uncovering fraud in government programmes. The offer is the latest in a series of extraordinary pre-election payments and promises: $500 rebate cheques to about a million people he said were overcharged for health insurance, $90 payments to 21 million seniors drawn from a Medicare fund, 1% "commissions" for fraud whistleblowers, and a pledged $5,000 dividend to every adult American if Republicans keep control of Congress — a proposal costing over $1 trillion that even some allies have criticised as vote-buying. Election-law experts were sceptical: UCLA professor Richard Hasen said he doubted the reward would ever be paid and called the announcement part of a project to generate distrust in the election system. Studies consistently find voter fraud exceptionally rare — one conservative database counts 557 substantiated cases nationwide from 2016 to 2025 — and Trump's claims to have won the 2020 election were rejected in court. Polls show Democrats pulling ahead as Republicans fight to hold both chambers, a loss that would stall Trump's agenda and expose his administration to congressional investigations in his final two years.

Car Driven Deliberately Into a Group Near Hanover: Four to Five Hurt, Police Fire Shots, Occupants Still at Large

Several people were injured shortly before midnight on Monday when a BMW was driven deliberately into a group of people in Neustadt am Rübenberge, a town north-west of Hanover in northern Germany, police said on Tuesday. Four to five people suffered injuries ranging from minor to severe, though none are believed to be life-threatening, and the wounded were treated at the scene before some were taken to regional hospitals. A police patrol conducting a routine traffic check nearby intervened and officers fired shots at the vehicle, preventing further harm; the occupants then abandoned the damaged car and fled on foot, and a manhunt with cordons and search teams continued on Tuesday. Witnesses described several damaged cars at the scene, with bullet holes, flat tyres and a smashed rear window. Initial information suggests those involved were known to each other, and investigators believe the group was deliberately targeted after a prior dispute rather than struck at random, with no indication so far of a terrorist motive. Police are reviewing surveillance footage and interviewing witnesses to establish the exact sequence of events and identify the suspects, whose number has not been confirmed.

Arnab at Its Strongest Over the Bay of Bengal: 170 km From Visakhapatnam, Landfall Near Yanam on Wednesday — and Nepal Goes on High Alert

Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab was about 170 km from Visakhapatnam on Tuesday afternoon and at its peak strength over the Bay of Bengal, with the India Meteorological Department forecasting winds of 150–160 km/h gusting to 180 km/h through Tuesday before a slight easing to 125–135 km/h, gusting to 150 km/h, when it crosses the Andhra Pradesh coast near Yanam early on Wednesday — then weakening fast, below 100 km/h by Wednesday evening and to a deep depression by Thursday. Coastal Andhra districts including East and West Godavari, Krishna and Kakinada remain under red alert for extremely heavy rain, adjoining south Odisha faces similar threats, schools in Odisha's Khordha district were closed from Tuesday afternoon through Wednesday, Chilika boat services are suspended until 29 October, and train cancellations and flight disruption have continued to mount around Visakhapatnam and Vijayawada. The storm's reach extends well inland after landfall: Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority on Tuesday placed the country on high alert from 29 to 31 October, warning that Arnab's remnants will bring moderate to heavy rain, thunderstorms and strong winds from Thursday night, with Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki and Lumbini provinces hit hardest on Friday and Saturday, localised flooding possible, and a particular risk of heavy snowfall, snowstorms and avalanches in high-hill and Himalayan districts. Trekkers and tourists have been told not to travel to high-altitude areas during the alert and those already in the mountains to stay in safe places, while people along riverbanks have been warned of flooding and emergency agencies remain on standby.

Latest · Sport · Ballon d'Or26 October 2026

'Harry, Harry' in London: Kane Wins His First Ballon d'Or — the First English Winner in 25 Years — as Putellas Takes a Third Women's Prize

Harry Kane was crowned the best footballer in the world on Monday night, winning the 70th Ballon d'Or at the London Palladium — six miles from where he scored his first professional goal and eight miles from the Arsenal academy that released him at eight for being "chubby." The 33-year-old Bayern Munich striker beat Kylian Mbappé and Lamine Yamal to the France Football prize, voted on by journalists from the top 100 FIFA-ranked countries, becoming only the fifth English winner and the first since Michael Owen in 2001, in a ceremony moved to London to mark 70 years since Stanley Matthews won the inaugural award and paying tribute to the late two-time winner Kevin Keegan, who died in July. The case was goals, in volume: 73 in 65 matches for club and country in the eligibility window — second in the modern era only to Lionel Messi's 82 in 2011-12 — including 36 in 31 Bundesliga matches for a third straight scoring title, 14 in 13 Champions League games, a club-record 61 in a season for Bayern on the way to a German treble, and six at the 2026 World Cup as England finished third, passing Pelé on the all-time World Cup scorers list. He also collected the Gerd Müller Trophy for best striker. "Wow, I'm almost speechless," Kane said, to chants of "Harry, Harry." "It being here in London as well has made it extra special… I hope my journey — it wasn't straightforward — can give you inspiration." The night had its chaos: the Ballon d'Or website briefly published three separate articles naming Kane, Yamal and Mbappé as winner hours beforehand, and the start was delayed 45 minutes by London traffic. In the women's award, Alexia Putellas won for a third time after captaining Barcelona Femení to a quadruple — league, two domestic cups and the Champions League — before signing for London City Lionesses this summer.
