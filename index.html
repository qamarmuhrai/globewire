Latest · Middle East · Gulf Security21 October 2026

Attack on Dammam Airport Kills One as US Orders Diplomats and Families Out of Saudi Arabia

An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, in eastern Saudi Arabia, killed one resident and injured eight others on Wednesday evening, the kingdom's General Authority of Civil Aviation said, with material damage still being assessed and flights diverted away from the airport overnight. Earlier the same day, debris from an intercepted drone killed two workers — a Nepali and a Filipino national — and injured two others at an Aramco-operated oil field in the country's southeast, the energy ministry said. The United States responded by ordering non-emergency government employees and their family members to depart its missions in Saudi Arabia, with State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott saying the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen had "increasingly engaged in hostilities against Saudi Arabia, including attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure resulting in civilian casualties." The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted and destroyed five drones launched toward the kingdom, and Britain announced it would deploy Royal Air Force fighter jets to help Saudi Arabia protect its territory. Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, closed since a severe attack on 10 October, saw a limited number of Saudi flights resume on Wednesday, though major foreign carriers have cancelled services to the capital through mid-November.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War21 October 2026

Russia Frees Detained American Teacher as Moscow Agrees 'in Principle' to Resume Trilateral Peace Talks

Russia freed Stephen Hubbard, a retired American schoolteacher in his seventies who had been held since April 2022, on Wednesday as President Vladimir Putin met US envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg for three hours of talks on ending the war in Ukraine. Hubbard, detained in Izyum in 2022 and sentenced in a closed-door trial to six years and ten months on mercenary charges he denied, was the only American still designated by Washington as wrongfully detained in Russia; the Kremlin said Putin authorised the release on humanitarian grounds, citing his health, and he was handed over to Witkoff at Pulkovo airport. President Donald Trump said there had been no prisoner swap and called the moment "the closest we've been" to ending the war. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Russia was "in principle, ready to resume" trilateral talks with Ukraine and the United States in mid-November, after the US midterm elections, focused on post-war security — but he warned that the Ukrainian and European proposals Witkoff carried from Tuesday's meeting with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Oslo contained little "substance." Jared Kushner joined the Putin talks remotely, and Ushakov said a summit bringing together Putin, Trump and China's Xi Jinping was also being prepared in Shenzhen next month.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War20 October 2026

Russian Missile and Drone Blitz Kills at Least Nine as US Early-Warning Intelligence for Kyiv Resumes

Russia unleashed another missile and drone blitz against civilian areas across Ukraine, killing at least nine people and cutting power across a dozen regions, Ukrainian officials said, even as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy announced that US early warnings of incoming ballistic missiles — disrupted for at least a week — had resumed. Speaking in Oslo, where he met US envoy Steve Witkoff and European leaders, Zelenskyy said the restoration followed a phone call with Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and stressed that the warnings save lives by giving the public time to take shelter and air defences a chance to intercept. Kyiv says it has nearly run out of US-made Patriot interceptors and accuses the Pentagon of delaying deliveries that other countries have purchased for Ukraine; Rubio, speaking to reporters in India, acknowledged frustration on both sides but denied any change in US policy on intelligence sharing or weapons supplies. Ukrainian officials also say speed restrictions remain in place on Starshield, the military-grade version of SpaceX's Starlink satellite service, hampering some battlefield drone operations.

Trump Blocked Netanyahu's Plan for Major New Strikes on Iran This Month, Report Says

Israel's military was ready to launch a multi-day strike campaign against Iran over the weekend of 10 October — targeting nuclear facilities, regime sites and civilian infrastructure including power plants — but the operation was shelved after President Donald Trump opposed restarting the war before the US midterm elections on 3 November, the Financial Times reported on Wednesday, citing three people familiar with the matter. The planned operation would reportedly have been larger in scale than the US–Israeli campaign in February, and the sources said a joint resumption of strikes in the coming weeks remained "not an unreasonable scenario," with one summarising the choice starkly: "There will either be a deal or an attack." Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who faces a Knesset election next Tuesday, has been campaigning to resume large-scale strikes and recently instructed the military to plan a new offensive. US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Israel on Monday, meeting Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz, and Israeli fighter jets this week flew a practice run toward Iran through Syria and Iraq.

Latest · Americas · Technology21 October 2026

Meta Ordered to Pay $25 Billion After Jury Finds Facebook Misled Users Over Privacy

A judge in New Mexico ordered Meta Platforms to pay $25 billion on Wednesday after a jury found the company had misled consumers about data privacy, in one of the largest corporate penalties in US history. The case grew out of the Cambridge Analytica scandal, in which a political consulting firm involved in Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign obtained data from as many as 87 million Facebook users through a third-party app. A jury in September found that Meta had violated the state's Unfair Practices Act more than 43 million times; Judge Francis Mathew wrote that a penalty of more than $219 billion was warranted under state law, at $5,000 per violation, but reduced the figure to $25 billion, calling Facebook's deception a systematic pattern that "goes to the heart of Facebook's core business practices." New Mexico sued in 2021, accusing the company of lying to users about who had access to their data and about its handling of hate speech and misinformation. Meta said it would appeal, with chief legal officer C.J. Mahoney calling the ruling effectively "a tax on speech." Penalties under New Mexico's consumer protection law fund state education, and Meta previously paid a $5 billion federal penalty in 2019 over the same scandal.

Latest · Africa · Health21 October 2026

Nigeria Reports Its First Ebola Case in Over a Decade as Outbreak Spreads Across Africa

Nigeria has confirmed its first Ebola case in more than a decade, becoming the fourth African country — and the most populous yet — to be affected by an outbreak that health officials describe as the fastest-spreading on record. The patient is a Ugandan woman who arrived in the capital, Abuja, via Addis Ababa on 5 October after business travel to eastern Congo; she fell ill two days later, was admitted to a private hospital on 11 October with malaria-like symptoms, tested positive on 19 October and is now in isolation receiving treatment. Authorities have identified 44 contacts, most of them healthcare workers, and are tracing fellow passengers, though officials do not believe she was symptomatic during the flight. The Bundibugyo strain behind the outbreak kills about half of those it infects and has no approved treatment or vaccine. Since the first cases were confirmed in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May, the disease has killed 4,646 people, with more than 9,500 cases recorded across eight Congolese provinces, and cases have since appeared in Uganda and Kenya. "Ebola is now affecting three regions of Africa. The situation demands urgent action and political leadership at the highest level," Africa CDC director general Jean Kaseya said on Wednesday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia20 October 2026

Student Protests Sweep Indonesian Cities on Prabowo's Second Anniversary in Office

Students and civil society groups protested across Indonesia on Tuesday as President Prabowo Subianto marked two years in office, turning the anniversary into a show of discontent over rising living costs, corruption and a prolonged forest-fire crisis. Demonstrations were reported in Jakarta, Yogyakarta, Bandung, Pekanbaru and Makassar; in the capital, hundreds of students gathered near the Tugu Tani monument while Greenpeace activists climbed the Welcome Monument to unfurl a banner demanding action on the climate crisis and a lower cost of living, with at least six activists detained. More than 9,000 security personnel were deployed in Jakarta, and clashes near Yogyakarta's regional legislature ended with several protesters detained. The fires burning across Sumatra, Kalimantan and Papua — worsened by an unusually severe El Niño-driven dry season — have generated toxic haze, disrupted flights, forced school closures and drifted across borders into Singapore and Malaysia. Protesters demanded that the fires be declared a national disaster, that companies responsible for environmental damage be prosecuted, and that education and healthcare be made more affordable. Polling cited by Reuters puts Prabowo's approval rating at 44.5 percent in October, down from 81.2 percent last November.

Kim Yo Jong Says North Korea's Border Loudspeakers Will Be on Standby Within Days

North Korea will place loudspeakers for psychological warfare against the South on standby along the entire border within days and has put unspecified new countermeasures on alert, Kim Yo Jong, the powerful sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said on Thursday. Speaking after visiting fortification sites with senior military officials on Wednesday evening, she said the loudspeakers would be activated across the border by the end of October, that North Korean tactical units had taken up new positions, and she accused South Korean troops of conducting warning broadcasts and firing warning shots at multiple locations near the frontier. She warned Seoul of "the most horrible and distressing situation" if it continued what Pyongyang calls interference in the North's internal affairs, and said her troops would "mercilessly eliminate" South Korean forces if warning fire harmed North Korean territory or personnel. South Korea maintains its warning shots were legitimate and defensive measures. The threats raise tensions along the heavily fortified frontier to their highest level since President Lee Jae Myung took office and undercut his efforts to ease inter-Korean hostility; Pyongyang has been reinforcing its side of the demilitarised zone with mines, barriers and fencing since 2024 as it moves to define a permanent border.

Oil Climbs as Tanker Attacks and Threats to Saudi Infrastructure Deepen Supply Fears

Oil prices edged higher on Wednesday on Middle East supply concerns, with traders wary of Red Sea shipping risks and threats to oil infrastructure in Saudi Arabia. Brent crude rose about 0.8 percent to $109.64 a barrel in early trade, after settling lower on Tuesday from a five-month high of $111.99, while US West Texas Intermediate crude rose to $98.58. Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said a supertanker caught fire after striking a sea mine south of the Strait of Hormuz, and ship-tracker Kpler has counted at least 18 vessel attacks in two weeks; analysts at LSEG said the strikes have expanded into the Gulf of Oman, threatening loadings outside the Gulf and the ship-to-ship transfers that have kept Middle East exports moving. The Houthis have claimed attacks on Saudi energy sites and airports, and the death toll from a Houthi attack on Yemeni government forces on Monday has risen to 73 as fighting intensifies. Prices had eased a day earlier after Iran's president discussed mediation efforts with Pakistan's army chief in Tehran, but Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Iran had received no response from Saudi Arabia or the Houthis to its offer to help broker peace talks.

Latest · Global Affairs · Cost of Living21 October 2026

From Fuel Pumps to Home Loans, $110 Oil Is Squeezing Households Around the World

With oil at a five-month high of about $110 a barrel and gas prices in Asia and Europe at their highest since December 2022, the energy shock from the Iran war is working its way into everyday budgets far from the battlefield. The war has already pushed global diesel prices to record highs: average British diesel pump prices hit £2.00 a litre for the first time this month, and US prices reached a record $6.53 a gallon in September, prompting President Donald Trump to open tax-exempt red-dyed diesel — normally reserved for off-road equipment and vehicles — to all buyers until the end of the year. Costlier fuel also feeds into food, clothing and packaging prices, and by fuelling inflation it pushes up borrowing costs: Britain's inflation and government borrowing both rose more than expected last month on higher petrol and diesel costs, while the Reserve Bank of Australia has raised its cash rate to a 15-year high, adding roughly A$3,900 a year to repayments on an average A$731,000 home loan compared with 2025 rates.
