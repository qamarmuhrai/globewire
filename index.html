Latest · Americas · New York — Guilty on 10 of 12 Counts25 November 2026

US Jury Convicts Huawei of Bank Fraud and Stealing Trade Secrets

A federal jury in Brooklyn on Tuesday found Chinese telecoms giant Huawei guilty on 10 of the 12 criminal counts it faced, concluding an 11-week trial in a case first brought in 2018. Jurors convicted the company of bank fraud, wire fraud, money laundering, sanctions violations and conspiring to steal trade secrets, but cleared it of racketeering conspiracy — the most serious charge — and of conspiring to obstruct justice. Prosecutors said Huawei misled HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas about its business in Iran, and over two decades took technology from US rivals, including Cisco router source code, Quintel antenna designs and a CNEX circuit board. More than 60 witnesses testified for the prosecution. Huawei, which was placed on a US trade blacklist in 2019, denied wrongdoing throughout.

Latest · Americas · Cartagena — Five Elderly Women Released25 November 2026

Gunmen Hold 25 People Hostage in a Cartagena Supermarket After a Failed Robbery

Armed men were holding 25 people hostage inside an Éxito supermarket in the Colombian tourist city of Cartagena on Tuesday after a botched attempt to rob an armoured cash vehicle at a shopping centre, authorities said. President Abelardo de la Espriella said the attackers initially seized about 30 people and later freed five elderly women, while police snipers took positions on nearby rooftops and streets around the La Castellana store were sealed off. Local media reported the gunmen were demanding nearly $2 million, two armoured cars and safe passage to Barranquilla, about 130 kilometres away — demands AFP said it could not independently verify. De la Espriella blamed a breakaway faction of the former FARC guerrilla movement. Landings at the city’s Rafael Núñez airport were briefly suspended as security forces used a runway during the operation.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Ship Stopped North of Khasab25 November 2026

Container Ship Hits a Mine on an ‘Unauthorised’ Route Near Hormuz, Iran’s Guards Say

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said a container ship struck a sea mine and came to a halt on Tuesday while travelling on what they called an unauthorised route near the Strait of Hormuz, state agency IRNA reported. The Guards’ navy said the vessel was north of Oman’s Khasab port, south of the strait, and state media said it had switched off its navigation and positioning systems and ignored maritime traffic instructions. Tehran did not name the ship, its flag or destination, and gave no word on casualties or damage. The report followed a string of similar incidents in the waterway, including a fourth tanker said to have exploded after hitting a mine east of the strait, forcing two accompanying tankers to turn back toward the Gulf. The Guards urged captains to use authorised routes and heed their traffic control centre’s warnings.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hanoi — Blast at Opening Time in Dong Da25 November 2026

Death Toll From Hanoi Hot Pot Restaurant Blast Rises to Seven

The death toll from Tuesday morning’s explosion at a hot pot eatery in Vietnam’s capital has risen to seven, with nine other people injured, VnExpress reported. Hanoi police said a suspected gas blast partially collapsed a single-storey building behind the E4 apartment block on Nguyen Van Tuyet Street in Dong Da Ward at around 8 a.m., just as the restaurant at number 168 was opening for business near a gas stove. The explosion shattered windows in nearby houses, cracked walls, tore metal shutters from their frames and brought down corrugated roofs and awnings. More than a dozen people were taken to hospital in earlier counts, among them one patient with a traumatic brain injury, and investigators said preliminary findings pointed to a gas explosion while inquiries continued.

Latest · Global Affairs · Vienna / New York — 47,000 Killed in 202525 November 2026

Five Women Killed Every Hour by a Partner or Relative in 2025, UN Finds

About 47,000 women and girls were intentionally killed by an intimate partner or a family member during 2025 — an average of five every hour, or 129 a day — according to the 2026 Femicide Brief published on Tuesday by the UN Office on Drugs and Crime and UN Women. The victims account for 60 percent of the roughly 78,000 intentional killings of women and girls worldwide last year, and the agencies said the annual figure has barely moved for years, fluctuating between 45,000 and 51,000. Released for the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women on 25 November, the brief says home remains the most dangerous place for women and girls, warns that recorded cases likely understate the true toll, and notes that a separate UN Women study found only about 7 percent of women who experience violence report it to police.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Seoul — Third Such Entry in a Week25 November 2026

Russian Warplanes Edge Toward South Korean Airspace, Triggering a Rare Island Alert

About five Russian warplanes entered South Korea’s air defence identification zone over the East Sea early on Tuesday and flew close to Ulleung Island, prompting Seoul to issue a rare public alert and scramble F-15K fighters, the Joint Chiefs of Staff said. The aircraft stayed in the zone for roughly 20 minutes, approaching the very edge of territorial airspace before turning toward Japan and heading north; no actual violation of South Korean airspace occurred, and an earlier interior ministry message that wrongly suggested an intrusion — and at first pointed to suspected North Korean planes — was corrected. The defence ministry summoned a Russian military attaché to complain, and the foreign ministry conveyed its regret. It was the fifth such entry this year and the third in a week, and closer than any before it. Russia’s embassy said its aircraft broke no international law and does not recognise the unilaterally declared zone.

Latest · Americas · Washington State — Sentencing Set for March 202725 November 2026

US Army Sergeant Admits Selling Military Secrets to Buyers Linked to China

An active-duty US Army sergeant, Jian Zhao, 37, has pleaded guilty to conspiring to gather and pass national defence information to people connected to China, the Justice Department said on Tuesday. Zhao, a battery supply sergeant at Joint Base Lewis-McChord in Washington with a security clearance, photographed restricted presentations on Army operations, communications networks and rocket and missile systems with his phone, shared excerpts from HIMARS technical manuals, and offered military computers and government hard drives for sale. Prosecutors say that from late 2021 until March 2025 he worked with former soldier Ruoyu Duan, who recruited serving troops and handled contacts and payments with buyers in China. Agents who arrested Zhao at his home in Lacey found about $30,000 in cash and multiple Army hard drives. He faces up to 10 years in prison and a $250,000 fine, with sentencing set for 1 March 2027.

Latest · Americas · Washington — $100 Million in Consumer Redress25 November 2026

Shein to Pay $100 Million to Settle US Claims Over Shipping, Reviews and Cotton Claims

Fast-fashion retailer Shein will pay $100 million in consumer redress to settle Federal Trade Commission allegations that it broke its shipping promises, suppressed negative product reviews and made deceptive claims about its supply chain and the origin of its cotton, the agency said on Tuesday. The FTC said Shein ships nearly a million packages a day to the United States yet, as of September 2025, employed only five customer service agents for US phone inquiries and did not publish their number, making it unreasonably hard for shoppers to resolve problems. Under the proposed order, the money will go to harmed consumers and Shein will be barred from misrepresenting delivery times, hiding unfavourable reviews or making misleading audit and traceability claims. A company spokesperson said the settlement is not an admission of liability and that Shein disagrees with parts of the FTC’s account.

Latest · Americas · Iowa — Blast Set for 9 A.M. Wednesday25 November 2026

Army Readies Safeguards for Wednesday Detonation of Degraded Explosives in Iowa

The US Army said on Tuesday it has put extensive measures in place to limit toxic lead dust when it detonates nearly 10,000 pounds (about 4,500 kg) of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown on Wednesday, a step officials say is needed to beat the risk of a spontaneous explosion. The chemical, used to trigger munitions, was gathered from installations nationwide and stored at the plant from 2009; inspectors found it deteriorating during a review in March. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said the controlled blast is planned for 9 a.m. local time to suit forecast winds, after a federal appeals court cleared the Environmental Protection Agency’s emergency permit despite a challenge from environmental groups. Mitigation includes earthworks around the two concrete storage magazines, misting systems to dampen the ground and foam intended to capture particles from the blast.

Latest · Europe · Madrid — Snap Vote on Sunday 29 November25 November 2026

Spain’s Sánchez Faces Conservative and Far-Right Challengers in Sunday’s Election

Spaniards vote in a general election on Sunday, with Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez — in power since 2018 and one of Europe’s few remaining left-wing leaders — trying to hold off a conservative and far-right challenge. His main rival, Popular Party leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo, campaigns as a steady, scandal-free administrator, while Vox leader Santiago Abascal hopes to act as kingmaker and carry the far right into a governing coalition for the first time since the Franco era. Sánchez, 54, has built a reputation as a political escape artist but now faces corruption cases touching a former minister, senior party officials and his wife, who denies wrongdoing. Polling suggests his fate rests on smaller left-wing parties beating expectations; most surveys instead point to a combined majority for the Popular Party and Vox, whose rivalry with an even harder-right newcomer, Se Acabó la Fiesta, is splitting the right-wing vote.
