Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Ships Hit, Blasts Disputed8 November 2026

Not Necessarily an Attack, Says Tehran: Both Sides Report Vessels Struck Near Hormuz

Iranian state television suggested on Sunday that explosions heard across the south on Saturday evening — including on Qeshm Island — may have come from Iranian military activity rather than a fresh American strike, even as both capitals reported shipping hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran said two vessels struck mines and a third caught fire; Washington said its air force fired on the stern of a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman, disabling it as it tried to run the blockade of Iranian ports, and UK monitors reported a bulk carrier involved in an incident 18 nautical miles east of Khor Fakkan. Reuters could not confirm any of the reported strikes. Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry added that falling debris damaged an Aramco administrative building in Riyadh after an attempted attack, with no injuries. Qatar, mediating between the sides, denied reports that talks had collapsed, and Iran has put a nuclear proposal to Washington — but President Trump’s hints of renewed major attacks have so far produced no large-scale operation.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Eisenkot Gets the Mandate8 November 2026

67 Recommendations, 28 Days: Eisenkot Must Turn Backing Into a Government

Former army chief Gadi Eisenkot formally received the mandate to form Israel’s next government from President Isaac Herzog on Sunday, after 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu in consultations following the deadlocked 27 October election. The Yashar party leader now has 28 days, extendable by 14, to assemble the 61 of 120 Knesset seats a government needs. The arithmetic is treacherous: 19 of his recommendations came from Arab-led parties he has ruled out governing with, his Jewish-led change bloc holds only 48 seats, and a majority runs through the haredi parties — whose demands on yeshiva-student status clash with Avigdor Liberman’s refusal to depend on Arab parties and his insistence on equal sharing of military service. Eisenkot has set four entry principles, including universal service and recognising Hamas as a terrorist organisation to be dismantled. If he fails, Herzog can hand the task to another candidate; if that fails too, a new election follows within 90 days with Netanyahu as caretaker.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Barrage Before Abu Dhabi8 November 2026

Only Four of 36 Brought Down: Kyiv Counts the Cost as Europe Joins the Talks Table

Russia’s overnight barrage of 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones — among the heaviest of the war — has become the backdrop to this week’s diplomacy. President Zelenskyy said air defences intercepted only four of the missiles, blaming a shortage of interceptors for Ukraine’s US-made Patriot systems, and that preliminary examination of impact sites pointed to North Korean-made KN-23 missiles striking energy facilities, including substations linking the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants to the grid; other reporting put at least six dead, among them a seven-year-old child. Zelenskyy accused Moscow of escalating deliberately to pressure Kyiv days before US-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the fourth round of the Trump administration’s mediation and the first since February. On Sunday, French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot confirmed Europeans will also take part in the Abu Dhabi discussions, as Kyiv lists halting strikes on energy infrastructure, Black Sea grain shipments and prisoner exchanges among its priorities.

Latest · Europe · Poland — A City in Mourning8 November 2026

The Trumpet Call Falls Silent for the First Time Since 1939 as Kraków Buries Its Shock

Poland’s two days of national mourning began on Sunday with Kraków’s Main Market Square still sealed off and mourners leaving candles at its edges. In a gesture without precedent in living memory, the traditional trumpet call from St Mary’s church on the square was suspended for 12 hours — the first such silence since 1939, the state agency PAP reported. Authorities now put the wounded at 17, including two police officers and the gunman himself, a 24-year-old who remains in hospital in critical condition after police shot him; his parents, aged 53 and 52, and his 21-year-old sister were found dead at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. Officials say the attack was likely inspired by far-right ideology, and it has landed on a country already shaken by a string of school stabbings in October that were linked to extremist content online. Warsaw police have reinforced security in busy tourist areas, and a leading daily wrote that the shooting shattered Poland’s sense of being safer than its western neighbours.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · New Zealand — Election Deadlock8 November 2026

3,650 Votes Apart: New Zealand Will Not Know Its Government Until 27 November

New Zealanders woke on Sunday to the tightest election in years and no winner. With 2.8 million ballots counted, Labour led the party vote by just 3,650 votes — 29.36 per cent to National’s 29.21 per cent — yet National’s strength in local races gave Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s conservative bloc 67 seats in an expanded 131-seat parliament, exactly one above the 66 needed. Neither Luxon nor Labour’s Chris Hipkins claimed victory. The result was a rebuke to both major parties, whose combined vote fell below 59 per cent as support scattered to minor parties, including a first-time entrant, the centrist Opportunity party, which took seven seats and could hold the balance if the numbers shift. They may well shift: nearly 400,000 special votes — from overseas and from voters casting ballots outside their electorate, a group that historically leans left — began to be counted on Sunday and will take about three weeks. Parties will not even start coalition negotiations until the official result lands on 27 November, leaving Luxon running a caretaker government in the meantime.

Latest · Americas · Argentina — The Pope and Milei8 November 2026

A Month After Lung Surgery, Leo Flies Into Milei’s Argentina — and a Carefully Watched Speech

Pope Leo travelled on Sunday from Uruguay to Argentina, the second leg of a 12-day, three-country South American tour and the first visit by a Catholic leader to the country since 1987. The 71-year-old, US-born pontiff — making one of the most intense foreign trips by a pope in decades, less than a month after lung cancer surgery — will tour four cities until Wednesday, including a prison and a disability care centre, and deliver an address that will be parsed for what it says to President Javier Milei. The libertarian president, a close Trump ally, has championed deep austerity that the Church says falls hardest on the poorest; annual inflation has fallen from over 200 per cent to about 33 per cent under his programme. Milei has also strongly backed the Iran war, which Leo has criticised to Trump’s irritation. In Uruguay, the pope urged South American countries to tackle regional problems together and to prize patient diplomacy over conflict.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 and the Coal Villages8 November 2026

“Come and See the Mine”: Villagers Want COP31 Leaders to Leave the Conference Hall

As delegates gather in Antalya for COP31, which opens Monday and runs to 20 November, villagers living beside a lignite mine in southwestern Türkiye are asking the visitors to make a detour of a few hundred kilometres — to Ikizkoy, near the Akbelen forest, and see what decades of coal extraction have done to their land and lives. Local official Nejla Isik is among those pressing the case. The summit’s host has proposed a voluntary goal of expanding electrification, but critics note that electricity only cuts emissions if it is low-carbon: coal was Türkiye’s largest power source last year at 34 per cent of generation, making it Europe’s biggest coal-fired electricity producer, according to the think tank Ember. Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar told foreign media Ankara is pursuing a just transition but that the mines employ thousands, and pointed to a target of 10 gigawatts of nuclear capacity by 2040 — roughly today’s coal output. Human Rights Watch, meanwhile, reported in October that Turkish authorities have arbitrarily arrested and prosecuted environmental protesters in Akbelen and elsewhere.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — The Taiz Market Strike6 November 2026

A Crowded Market, Three Strikes, Two Stories: Taiz Argues Over Who Fired

Dozens of people were killed or wounded on Friday at al-Hadhara market in the al-Waziyah district of Yemen’s Taiz province, and almost everything else about the attack is disputed. The Houthi-run health ministry put the toll at 30 dead and eight wounded — other Houthi-linked reporting said 25 killed and 40 injured — and Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said three strikes were flown by a Saudi jet from the Khamis Mushait base against the busy bazaar. Yemen’s Saudi-backed government forces flatly denied any air operation in the area and accused the Houthis of hitting the market with a ballistic missile themselves. The Associated Press could not independently verify either claim, and Saudi Arabia has not publicly responded to the accusation. The strike came four days after at least eight people died in an attack on another Taiz market, al-Makh’af, and amid an intensifying Houthi offensive in the province that began in September and a wider escalation between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia across the region.

Latest · Europe · Technology — The Data Centre Backlash7 November 2026

Cranes, Flares and 200 Arrests: Europe’s Anger at Data Centres Moves Inside the Fence

Protesters broke into the construction site of a Microsoft data centre in Amsterdam’s Sloterdijk district on Friday, some climbing a crane and setting off flares, in the most dramatic action of a coordinated weekend against the buildings that power artificial intelligence. Dutch police detained between 150 and 200 people suspected of trespassing; all were later released. Nathan Timmer of the group Geef Tegengas — “Push Back” — said the occupation aimed to stop construction outright. The action was part of “Stop Dirty Data Centres” events planned across 15 European countries, from a march in Dublin — where data centres consumed 23 per cent of Ireland’s metered electricity in 2025, up from 5 per cent a decade earlier — to about a hundred demonstrators outside Poland’s parliament and a small gathering near Berlin. Campaigners want a moratorium on large projects, stricter environmental permits and a share of benefits for host communities. The fight is over scale: Europe is projected to draw some $5.6 trillion in data-centre investment between 2026 and 2050.

Latest · Americas · Business — Berkshire’s New Era7 November 2026

Profit Down, Buybacks Up: Greg Abel Spends $9.6 Billion on Berkshire’s Own Shares

Berkshire Hathaway’s third quarter under new chief executive Greg Abel told two stories at once. Operating earnings fell about 6 per cent to $12.71 billion and net earnings dropped 28 per cent to $22.11 billion, dragged down by the insurance businesses — underwriting profit fell 43 per cent, and car insurer Geico’s pretax profit dropped about 45 per cent as claims grew costlier. Revenue, though, rose 11 per cent to $105.65 billion. The bigger signal was what Abel did with the cash: Berkshire repurchased $9.6 billion of its own stock in the quarter and about $2 billion more in October, a pace suggesting the 64-year-old successor to Warren Buffett is readier than his predecessor to deploy the conglomerate’s enormous reserves. Even after the buying, Berkshire ended September holding $356.9 billion in cash and equivalents. Berkshire itself cautions that quarterly net earnings swing with unrealised gains and losses on shares it has no intention of selling, and are a poor guide to the health of its dozens of operating businesses.

Latest · Africa · Ethiopia — An Economy Recounted4 November 2026

59 Per Cent Bigger on Paper: Ethiopia Counts Itself Into Africa’s Top Five

Ethiopia has become Africa’s fifth-largest economy — not by building anything overnight, but by measuring itself better. A rebasing of the national accounts, announced by Finance Minister Fitsum Assefa on 4 November, moved the reference year from 2015-16 to 2024-25 and captured activity the old framework missed, including digital finance and services. The 2024-25 economy came out 59 per cent larger than previously estimated, at $201.4 billion, and output for the year ended in July was put at $210.7 billion — behind South Africa, Egypt, Nigeria and Algeria, and ahead of Morocco and Kenya on available estimates. Analysts at Oxford Economics note the revision mechanically improves Ethiopia’s debt-to-GDP ratios while it restructures loans under the G20 Common Framework, without changing what it actually owes or the foreign currency it needs. The government projects 10.2 per cent growth for the coming year; whether the new ranking endures will depend on growth, exports and those debt talks, and the figures have yet to be folded into the IMF’s comparable international tables.

Latest · Americas · United States — Crypto Cut Off6 November 2026

$16.7 Billion in Suspect Flows: Washington Moves to Sever MEXC From US Banks

The US Treasury’s financial-crimes unit, FinCEN, has issued an order cutting the cryptocurrency exchange MEXC off from the American banking system, after finding at least $16.7 billion in likely illicit flows through the platform between May 2021 and May 2026. The 6 November order is among the strongest tools US regulators hold against a foreign financial platform, since access to dollar banking underpins most global crypto trading. It lands in the same week that a separate Treasury-adjacent change — a new immigration-services memo adding factors officers may weigh when judging the “good moral character” of citizenship applicants — signalled how broadly the administration is rewriting rules by memorandum in the wake of the midterm elections. For MEXC’s users, the practical question is whether the exchange can find banking routes outside the United States quickly enough to keep withdrawals and settlements moving normally.
