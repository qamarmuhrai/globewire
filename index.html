Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz30 October 2026

First Limpet-Mine Strike of the War Disables a Tanker in Hormuz, as the US Denies Losing a Sea Drone

The Strait of Hormuz has seen its first confirmed limpet-mine attack since the US–Iran war began in February: a small boat fixed two explosive devices to the hull of the Panama-flagged tanker MT Louise 1, about 25 nautical miles northeast of Oman's Khasab, sparking an engine-room fire that briefly disabled the ship, maritime security sources told Reuters. All 27 Indian seafarers aboard were unhurt, the fire was put out and the vessel continued to its next port; nobody has claimed the attack. A second ship, the Liberia-flagged MT Arzanah, carrying two Indian nationals, was hit by a projectile a day earlier, and late on Friday the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported yet another vessel struck and set alight 40 nautical miles south of Iran's Lavan Island, its crew safe. The incidents sit alongside competing claims: Iran's navy said it had destroyed a US unmanned surface vessel east of the strait, which US Central Command flatly denied, saying every American sea drone in the region is accounted for, while Tehran's Revolutionary Guards repeated their claim that three tankers had hit naval mines on an unauthorised route — an account news agencies say they cannot verify. CENTCOM chief Admiral Brad Cooper insists the strait's main international routes have been cleared of Iranian mines and that more than 3,000 commercial vessels have transited with US support in six months.

Latest · Global Affairs · G20 Atlanta30 October 2026

G20 Ministers Scrap Their Family Photo in Atlanta as a Third Refuse to Be Pictured With Lavrov

The G20 foreign ministers' meeting opened in Atlanta on Friday without the traditional group photograph, after more than a third of the attending officials threatened to walk out rather than be pictured alongside Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who is hosting the two-day gathering, considered staging two separate photos — one with Lavrov, one without — before cancelling the shoot altogether, officials told CNN. Lavrov brushed the episode aside, saying that if colleagues care most about how they look in a photo, "that says it all," and said he had held a brief, fairly good conversation with Rubio on the sidelines. In his opening remarks Rubio told ministers the world faces a critical moment, with crises threatening energy and food supplies, trade and freedom of navigation. The meeting, held days before the US midterm elections, is meant to prepare December's leaders' summit in Miami, but diplomats expect no joint declaration — only a US-drafted chair's summary — given the deep splits over the wars in Ukraine and Iran and the surge in energy prices. China sent a deputy rather than Foreign Minister Wang Yi, Canada's minister missed the opening session, and South Africa was not invited by the US presidency.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War31 October 2026

Zelenskyy Flies to Istanbul for Erdoğan Talks, Boasting That Ukrainian Strikes Now Reach Siberia

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is in Istanbul on Saturday for a working visit at the invitation of Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, the Turkish presidency announced, with the agenda centred on the war with Russia, Black Sea security and closer bilateral cooperation. Türkiye has repeatedly offered to host a leaders-level meeting to end the war, and Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan reaffirmed that readiness during a visit to Kyiv in July. Ahead of the trip, Zelenskyy used a military briefing on Ukraine's deep-strike campaign in September and October to claim that Russian strategic facilities along a line from Yamal to Western Siberia are now within range of Ukrainian missiles and drones, and that Kyiv is working to extend that reach further. The claim was underscored on the ground: Ukraine struck the Novokuybyshevsk oil refinery in Russia's Samara region early on Friday, ending a two-week pause in attacks on Russian oil plants that reports attributed to pressure from the Trump administration — a pause that had been expected to last until after next week's US midterm elections. The Rosneft-owned plant processes about 8.8 million tonnes of crude a year and had already been hit in August.

Putin Asks Whether Anyone Wants 'the Baltic to Become Like Hormuz' and Says Russia Is Ready for War

Russian President Vladimir Putin has warned Europe that Moscow is prepared for a war it says it does not want, and that any confrontation pushed too far would leave "no winners at all." Speaking to reporters in Moscow after the Russia–Africa summit, and referring to recent calls with the leaders of Finland and Norway, Putin singled out the Baltic Sea: Russia opposes any restrictions on navigation there, he said, asking, "Does someone want the same situation in the Baltic as in the Gulf or the Strait of Hormuz? His remarks followed a warning from Estonia's foreign minister that NATO could close the Baltic within hours and mobilise 50,000 troops if Russia kept threatening its neighbours. Washington, meanwhile, has privately urged Baltic allies including Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania to soften their public rhetoric about the Russian threat, according to Reuters reporting carried by Indian media, fearing it could further strain relations with Moscow. Putin insisted Russia remains open to restoring full relations with European countries, even as Britain's defence minister warned the UK must prepare for a war scenario within the next 1,000 days.

Latest · Europe · Spain Rail Disaster30 October 2026

Spain Struggles to Identify Its 69 Train-Crash Dead as Investigators Examine a Failed Safety System

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said on Friday that Spain is working "tirelessly" to identify the 69 people killed in Wednesday's high-speed train collision near Urda, in Toledo province, as relatives provide DNA samples and regional officials warn the toll may not be final — 70 missing-person reports have been filed and all but five of the bodies remain unidentified, with some 50 injured people still in hospital. Investigators are focusing on the LZB automatic train-protection system, which failed at about 8:31 a.m. over roughly 63 kilometres of the Madrid–Seville line after a computer communications fault, halting trains along the stretch. According to the transport ministry's reconstruction, a controller then cleared a Renfe AVE service to proceed at normal speed while a stationary Iryo train stood ahead of it; the Renfe train struck the Iryo's rear at an estimated 160 to 200 kph. The line has used LZB since 1992 and was due to have been upgraded to the newer European ERTMS system by early 2026 — work still unfinished at the time of the crash. Spain is in the second of three days of national mourning, and the opposition is demanding Transport Minister Óscar Puente's resignation ahead of the 29 November general election. It is the country's second fatal high-speed crash this year, after January's Adamuz accident killed 46 on the same line.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Oleshky Truce30 October 2026

Ukrainian Officials Reach Starving Oleshky Under the Local Truce, With Evacuation Buses Waiting

Ukraine's human rights commissioner, Dmytro Lubinets, and his delegation have safely entered Russian-occupied Oleshky, in the Kherson region, under a US-brokered local ceasefire that has now held for more than 80 hours. The town, on the left bank of the Dnipro, had been cut off for months, and Lubinets said he was speaking with residents to learn what help they need most; 25 buses with 750 seats, plus ambulances, have been prepared for those who wish to leave. The truce, announced by the White House on 23 October and credited to First Lady Melania Trump, took effect on 27 October and is due to run until late on 1 November. The open question is the evacuees' destination: Washington promised residents transport to their "preferred destination," and Lubinets has said everyone who wants to go to Ukraine will be able to — but under the published agreement, those choosing Ukraine would travel with his delegation via Anapa in Russia and then on a Russian military transport flight to Homiel in Belarus, expected on 31 October. The Institute for the Study of War warns Russia may exploit the pause to rebuild its forces and may not give residents a genuinely free choice, after President Putin described Oleshky as a "Russian settlement."

Latest · Global Affairs · Ports Strike30 October 2026

Dockworkers Strike Across 113 Ports in 22 Countries Against Arms Shipments and Seafarer Deaths

Dockworkers walked out across Europe and beyond on Friday in a coordinated strike spanning 113 ports in 22 countries, protesting the use of their ports for arms shipments and the rising toll of seafarers killed in the wars around the Gulf and the Black Sea. More than 25 unions backed the action, first called in Italy, France, Greece, Spain's Basque Country, Türkiye and Morocco; on Friday it disrupted major hubs including Piraeus, Valencia, Le Havre, Algeciras and Genoa, Morocco's Tangier Med and India's Nhava Sheva. In Piraeus, cargo operations were disrupted and ferry services to the Greek islands suspended as about 3,000 dockworkers and seafarers joined the walkout. Organisers cited stark figures: 28 attacks on vessels in the Gulf in the first 21 days of October alone — a record since the Iran war began — and 570 confirmed attacks in the Black Sea this year, leaving 40 seafarers dead and hundreds wounded. India's WTWFI union said about 15 Indian seafarers had died in the Iran war, with many more stranded at sea. Beyond the anti-war demands, strikers also sought pay rises, better conditions and the right to carry doctors aboard vessels crossing war zones. An activist coalition said the protest could halt some 52,000 containers and snarl cargo worth $2.8 billion, a figure Reuters could not independently verify.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bhutan — India Floods30 October 2026

A Glacial Lake Bursts in Bhutan and More Than 10,000 People Flee the Sankosh River in India

A glacial lake outburst flood at Shintaphu Lake, in Bhutan's Gasa district, has put northern West Bengal and Assam on high alert, with more than 10,000 people moved to safety along the Sankosh river and its tributaries. An estimated 7.4 million cubic metres of water surged out of the lake — one of 17 Bhutan lists as potentially dangerous — on Thursday evening, racing down the Mo Chhu river system, damaging the road to Laya, washing away the Gasa Tshachu hot-spring complex and cutting part of the Punakha–Gasa highway. No casualties have been reported in Bhutan. In India's Alipurduar district, police said 4,000 to 5,000 people were sheltering in school buildings, with over 6,000 more relocated to safer ground, after loudspeaker warnings went out to riverbank villages late on Thursday night. Disaster-response and civil-defence teams are deployed, a National Disaster Response Force team is on its way, and Assam officials said the Sankosh's level was steady early on Friday even as a red alert stayed in force and heavy rain was forecast for Alipurduar. The flood was expected to reach the border plains within six to eight hours of the breach, and Indian and Bhutanese authorities are coordinating monitoring of river levels.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South China Sea30 October 2026

China Steps Up Combat-Readiness Patrols Around Scarborough Shoal, Calling Them a Countermeasure

China's military said on Friday that its naval and air forces had conducted combat-readiness patrols around Scarborough Shoal, the long-running flashpoint in its dispute with the Philippines in the South China Sea. The People's Liberation Army's Southern Theater Command described the patrols as a "necessary countermeasure" against what it called Philippine rights violations and provocations, and said such patrols have been strengthened through this month; the China Coast Guard separately ran law-enforcement patrols in the same waters with identical language. Neither statement specified which Philippine actions it was answering, and neither the Philippine embassy in Beijing nor the Philippine military immediately commented. The announcement came a day after tensions between the two countries resurfaced at the close of a two-day ASEAN defence gathering. Beijing claims almost the entire South China Sea under its nine-dash line, a claim that cuts into the exclusive economic zones of the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei and Indonesia and was rejected by an international tribunal in 2016 — a ruling China does not recognise.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Fire Smoke30 October 2026

Wildfire Smoke Killed an Estimated 3,560 People in Indonesia in September Alone, a Study Finds

Smoke from months of wildfires across Indonesia caused an estimated 3,560 deaths in September — double the August figure — according to a study released on Thursday by the Helsinki-based Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA). About 57.8 million Indonesians were breathing air classified as unhealthy to hazardous during the month, the researchers said, with the number exposed to hazardous-level air in Kalimantan, on Borneo, nearly tripling to 6.4 million on the worst days. The estimates count deaths from short-term exposure to fine PM2.5 particles, which can penetrate the lungs and bloodstream; most occurred in Indonesian Borneo, Sumatra, Java and the country's east, and fire smoke accounted for 94–99% of pollution deaths in Kalimantan and over 80% in Sumatra. Pollution from other sources — industry, power generation, transport and waste burning — added an estimated 1,750 deaths, bringing September's total to 5,310 in a country of about 290 million. Average PM2.5 exposure in September ran more than seven times the World Health Organization's annual guideline nationally, ten times in Sumatra and 24 times in Kalimantan. Deforestation monitor Nusantara Atlas estimates the fires have burned more than 1.9 million hectares in the nine months to September, and over 330,000 cases of acute respiratory infection have been recorded nationwide.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Thailand — Scam Crackdown28 October 2026

Thailand Moves to Seize Over $1 Billion From an Alleged Laundering Ring Behind Regional Scam Syndicates

Thai authorities say they will seize more than US$1 billion in assets from a group accused of laundering the proceeds of Southeast Asia's online scam syndicates through foreign banks and funds and back into shares on the Thai stock exchange. A criminal court has issued arrest warrants for seven people, including the alleged ringleader, South African Ben Smith — also known as Benjamin Mauerberger — his Thai wife Cattaliya Beevor, and Cambodian businessman Yim Leak, on charges of conspiracy to launder money and acting as a criminal organisation. The Anti-Money Laundering Office says the seizures cover shares worth more than 14 billion baht — including holdings in Bangchak, one of Thailand's biggest oil refiners — plus real estate, cash and vehicles valued at about 21 billion baht. Investigators allege the network routed criminal proceeds through overseas financial firms before trading them into Thai stocks, echoing a pattern seen across the region's multibillion-dollar scam industry, much of it run from compounds in Cambodia. Officials say confiscated assets will be used to compensate victims of online scams.

Latest · Africa · Ebola — US Travel Rules30 October 2026

Airlines Urge Washington to Rethink the Ebola Declaration Every US-Bound Passenger Must Sign From 3 November

The world's airline industry is asking the United States to reconsider a new Ebola rule that, from 3 November, will require every passenger on an international flight to the US — citizens, residents and transit travellers alike — to sign a declaration that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo during the previous 21 days, the maximum incubation period for the disease. Airlines must check each attestation before boarding, refuse passengers who have not completed one, and keep copies for at least 42 days; even an airport connection in the DRC counts as presence. IATA, which represents more than 370 airlines, and Airlines for America have written separately to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention warning that the short notice leaves carriers too little time to rebuild check-in systems, and questioning why the rule covers air travel but not sea or land arrivals. The order, signed on 22 October and due to run until 18 December, responds to the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak declared in the DRC in May — now, by CDC figures, at 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths across five countries as of 26 October, with the DRC accounting for 10,141 cases and Kenya reporting nine, including two deaths. The CDC says no case linked to the outbreak has been confirmed in the United States and rates the risk to the American public as low.
