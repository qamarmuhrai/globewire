Latest · Africa · South Africa — Down to 10,627 Votes5 November 2026

Nearly Level: ANC 31.36%, DA 31.04% as the Count Reaches 18%

South Africa’s local election count has become a genuine cliffhanger. By 08:01 on Thursday, with about 18 per cent of votes counted, the Electoral Commission’s dashboard put the ANC on 31.36 per cent nationally — 1,039,116 votes — against the DA’s 31.04 per cent and 1,028,489 votes, a gap of just 10,627 ballots. The MK Party held third place on about 5.4 per cent, with the EFF, Freedom Front Plus and Patriotic Alliance all clustered between roughly 4.4 and 5.2 per cent. On seats, the ANC led in ten councils and had secured 153 seats to the DA’s 113, while the PA was leading in one council with 33 seats. The provincial split remains stark: the DA is far ahead in the Western Cape, where Cape Town’s count already shows it above 60 per cent, and leads comfortably in Gauteng, while the ANC holds the Eastern Cape and the MK Party leads in KwaZulu-Natal. With more than four-fifths of ballots still to be captured when those figures were published — and some 23,696 voting districts and 257 councils in play — officials stress the standings can still move substantially. The commission has seven days to declare final results, expected by Sunday, after which councils must be formed within 14 days.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — The Sentence5 November 2026

Ten More Years: Joshua Wong Sentenced in His Second Security Case

The sentence is in. Hong Kong’s High Court on Thursday jailed Joshua Wong for ten years for conspiring to collude with foreign forces under the Beijing-imposed national security law — his second conviction under that law. Judge William Tam ruled the offence was of a “grave nature”, described Wong and exiled activist Nathan Law as ring-leaders of the conspiracy, and set a starting point of 13 years before cutting it to ten in recognition of Wong’s guilty plea in September. The new term runs concurrently with the roughly two months left on his existing four-year, eight-month sentence for subversion in the “Hong Kong 47” case, which means the 30-year-old — in custody almost continuously since November 2020 and previously expecting release in January — is now not due out until late 2036, when he will be 40. Prosecutors said that between July and November 2020 Wong and others asked foreign governments and bodies to impose sanctions or blockades on Hong Kong and China. Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International both condemned the sentence; Wong’s mitigation letter said the fresh charge, brought in June 2025, made him feel he was back at the beginning. He first rose to prominence as a teenage founder of the Scholarism student movement and a leader of the 2014 Umbrella Movement protests.

Latest · Americas · Markets — The Dow’s Best Day of 20265 November 2026

Up 1,569 Points: Wall Street Roars as the Midterms End and Oil Falls

Markets gave their verdict on the midterms on Wednesday, and it was emphatic. The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 1,569.40 points, or about 3 per cent, to close at 53,356.12 — its strongest session since April 2025 and, according to CFRA Research, its biggest post-midterm gain since 1982. The S&P 500 rose 1.6 per cent to 7,688.62, its best day in three months, and the Nasdaq Composite added 1.4 per cent to 25,736.51. Almost every sector climbed: consumer discretionary jumped about 4.3 per cent while energy fell about 4.8 per cent, tracking crude’s plunge below $95. Traders read the Democratic sweep of both chambers two ways at once — divided government limits how much of any agenda can pass, which markets historically like, and a Congress hostile to the Iran war raises the odds of a negotiated exit that would normalise oil supply. The rally had casualties: data-centre and crypto-mining names such as IREN and Hut 8 tumbled on fears of tighter AI rules, Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX both fell more than 3 per cent despite his heavy political spending, and Qualcomm dropped in extended trade on disappointing results. Ten-year Treasury yields eased to about 4.88 per cent.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — A Fourth Day Down5 November 2026

Brent at $93.82: Oil Falls Again, but Analysts See $110–$125 if War Resumes

Oil extended its slide on Thursday, falling for a fourth straight session as traders continued to price a lower chance of fresh escalation in the US-Israeli war on Iran after the Republican midterm defeat. Brent futures were down 80 cents, or 0.85 per cent, at $93.82 a barrel by 0426 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate fell $1.20, or 1.36 per cent, to $86.92 — a day after both benchmarks plunged more than 8 per cent to their lowest settlements since 31 August. The political logic is straightforward: President Trump has acknowledged the war and the fuel prices it caused hurt his party, Vice President JD Vance has said the same publicly, and Washington says it has no immediate plans for new strikes. But the pricing is fragile. Eurasia Group cut its odds of renewed US strikes only modestly, from 65 to 60 per cent, and actually lowered the chance of a deal from 20 to 10 per cent, warning that if escalation does come, Brent could snap back into a $110–$125 band. JPMorgan estimates the market was already in surplus by about 1.1 million barrels a day in September and 1.4 million in October, while Vortexa tracking shows Gulf exports averaged 17.1 million barrels a day in October — still only about three-quarters of pre-war levels.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Consultations, Day Two5 November 2026

Last in the Door Is Likud: Herzog Wraps Up His Rounds Tonight

Israel’s president is hearing the final voices before he decides who tries to build a government. The second and last day of Isaac Herzog’s consultations at the President’s Residence in Jerusalem runs through Thursday, ending with the two biggest factions — Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar and, last of all, Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud. Wednesday’s rounds set the pattern: the Religious Zionist Party, Shas and Zehut recommended Netanyahu, while Avigdor Liberman’s Yisrael Beiteinu backed Eisenkot. Thursday’s expected recommendations — from Otzma Yehudit for Netanyahu, and from the Democrats, Ra’am, the Joint List, Bennett’s B’Yachad and Yashar itself for Eisenkot — would leave the former army chief clearly ahead in endorsements, at roughly 56 to 67 depending on how the Arab-led parties’ support is counted, against about 46 for the prime minister. The seat maths explains the tension: Netanyahu’s bloc won 53 of 120 Knesset seats, Eisenkot’s 48, and the Arab-led parties a record 19 — nobody has the 61 needed to govern, so Herzog must pick whoever he judges most likely to succeed. He has until 11 November; whoever receives the mandate gets 28 days, plus a possible 14-day extension. The new Knesset is sworn in on 10 November either way.

Enemies at One Table: Melania Trump Hosts Kyiv and Moscow Envoys Today

At 11 a.m. Washington time on Thursday — 9 p.m. in Pakistan — First Lady Melania Trump is due to stand in the White House with officials from two countries actively at war with each other. The gathering caps the Oleshky operation: a six-day localised ceasefire around the Russian-occupied Kherson-region town, which she helped broker, during which aid convoys got in and roughly 750 to 800 people were evacuated. Peskov, for his part, praised her humanitarian work and said Moscow hoped it would continue. The White House meeting is explicitly a prelude: full US-mediated trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and Washington follow in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the first such round since February.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — The Search Goes On4 November 2026

40 Dead at an Aid Point: Komyshuvakha Counts Its Losses

The death toll from Monday’s Russian bombing of a humanitarian aid distribution point in the village of Komyshuvakha, in Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia region, has risen to 40, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed on Wednesday, with 59 people still missing according to regional defence council co-head Ivan Fedorov and more than 30 feared buried under rubble. Ukrainian officials say the strikes — including, Kyiv says, a 3-tonne FAB-3000 bomb — first hit an underground school building being used to hand out aid as residents arrived, then hit the shelter itself in what Ukraine describes as a double-tap attack; two rescue workers and four police officers are among the dead, and only 12 of the bodies recovered had been identified by Wednesday. Russia’s defence ministry says its forces targeted Ukrainian military units around Komyshuvakha and two other settlements and killed up to 25 servicemen. The same night brought a mass drone assault — Ukraine’s air force counted 310 drones, 81 of them jet-powered — that knocked out power and water across parts of Kyiv for hours. The village, about 17–20 kilometres from Russian positions, was hit a day after a brief US-brokered local ceasefire expired, and less than a week before the Abu Dhabi talks.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · South Korea — Toward Hormuz, Carefully5 November 2026

Seoul Sends Its Navy Toward Hormuz — but Not Into the War

South Korea is making its first contribution to the effort to keep the Strait of Hormuz open — on strictly non-combat terms. The defence ministry said Thursday it will expand the operating area of the Cheonghae anti-piracy unit, currently escorting shipping in the Gulf of Aden off Somalia, north-eastward to waters south of Oman in the Arabian Sea — roughly doubling its radius to about 1,930 kilometres. The reinforced unit gains 75 personnel, for a total of 395, plus a P-8A maritime patrol aircraft, an explosive-ordnance disposal team, unmanned surface and underwater vehicles and mine-sweeping systems, the last aimed at a possible post-war operation to clear the strait. Seoul is emphatic about the limits: operations stay in open waters south of Oman, the patrol aircraft is barred from targeting or monitoring Iranian territory, waters or the strait’s interior, the unit acts independently of allied forces, and President Lee Jae Myung’s principle of non-intervention in the Iran war stands. Because the change expands an overseas deployment, it needs National Assembly approval; the government plans Cabinet endorsement next week and a parliamentary motion after that, with the unit’s current mandate expiring on 31 December. Tehran has previously warned Seoul against such a deployment.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — “Tending Toward Treason”4 November 2026

The Chief of Staff Who Allegedly Bugged His Own President

Indonesia’s president has sacked his chief of staff over what his office calls conduct that may amount to treason, in a wiretapping scandal that has put troops on the streets of Jakarta. Listyo Sigit Prabowo — no relation to President Prabowo Subianto — was arrested on Tuesday with two other senior police officers, accused of illegally tapping the phones of the president, cabinet ministers, journalists, politicians, business figures and civil-society leaders from early 2025 onward, while he was still national police chief, a post he held for five years before becoming chief of staff only last month. The other two detained officers were named in local media as Ahrie Sonta Nasution, head of the police legal division, and Eko Hadi Santoso, a senior officer at the police education institution. Presidential spokesman Prasetyo Hadi described the taps as the work of “rogue individuals”; senior legal-affairs minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra said the motive has not yet been established. As the scandal broke on Wednesday, the military said thousands of troops were being mobilised in the capital to, in a navy spokesman’s words, safeguard the stability of Indonesia’s security.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — Decision at 5 p.m. PKT5 November 2026

The Bank of England Decides at Noon in London, With a First Rise Since 2023 Almost Fully Priced

At noon in London on Thursday — 5 p.m. Pakistan time — the Bank of England announces whether Bank Rate rises for the first time in more than three years. Going into the decision, markets had priced a roughly 96 to 97 per cent chance that the Monetary Policy Committee lifts the rate by a quarter point, from 3.75 per cent — its level since a cut last December — to 4.00 per cent, alongside a fresh Monetary Policy Report with updated growth and inflation forecasts. The argument for moving is prices: consumer inflation rose 3.5 per cent in the year to September, up from 3.1 per cent in August and the highest in almost a year, against a 2 per cent target, with Middle East-driven energy costs a central factor. The committee was already split 6–3 in September, when three members wanted an immediate rise to 4 per cent; economist surveys point to a 7–2 or 6–3 vote this time. Precisely because the outcome is so heavily priced, traders expect the vote split and the Bank’s forward guidance to matter more for sterling than the quarter point itself. Households will feel it in the refinancing queue: research cited in coverage puts about £62 billion of fixed-rate buy-to-let loans alone maturing in the year to next August, and markets are already pricing several further rises during 2027.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Japan — A Network Down, a Breach Suspected5 November 2026

Docomo Goes Dark: Japan’s Biggest Carrier Hit by a Nationwide Outage

Japan’s largest mobile carrier spent Thursday dealing with two crises at once. NTT Docomo said voice calls, mobile data and text messages became unstable across the country from about 11:18 a.m., with knock-on effects for smaller providers that run on its network, and that it was investigating the cause — including whether emergency calls to 110 and 119 were affected. Hours earlier, from about 1:05 a.m., the company had already suspended its online shop and the My docomo customer portal after concluding an outside party had likely gained unauthorised access to its servers, leaving customers unable to sign up for plans, check bills or change contracts. Docomo said it did not yet know whether the suspected intrusion and the network failure are connected, and gave no restoration estimate for either problem. The outage is the second embarrassment in weeks for the carrier: in mid-October a system malfunction stopped its Area Mail service delivering an emergency earthquake alert across eastern Japan — an alert the meteorological agency later retracted after overestimating the quake — and the company said then that it had found the cause and expected a fix within days.
