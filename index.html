Latest · Americas · Guasave, Sinaloa — The $5 Million Fugitive21 November 2026

Mexico Captures “El Chapo Isidro,” One of the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted, in a Night Raid That Sparked a Gun Battle

Mexican special forces have captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, the alleged cartel leader known as “El Chapo Isidro,” ending a hunt that had placed him on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list with a $5 million reward on his head. Mexico’s security chief said the 44-year-old was seized on Friday night in Guasave, in the northern state of Sinaloa, in an operation the Defence Ministry described as built on stealth and surprise and carried out with United States intelligence support. Two other senior figures were taken with him: his second-in-command, identified as Germán “N,” alias “Serafín,” and the group’s alleged finance operator, José “N.” All three were flown to Mexico City. Washington accuses Meza Flores and his organisation of moving vast quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States over more than two decades; he is linked to the Beltrán Leyva organisation and to a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel. The arrest did not stay quiet for long: armed clashes erupted around Guasave, cartel gunmen set up flaming roadblocks, and gunfire forced the evacuation of a professional baseball game at Kuroda Park. The capture is the latest in a string of high-profile arrests and killings of suspected traffickers as Mexico acts under sustained pressure from Washington.

Latest · Middle East · Washington / Tehran — “A Permanent Deal”21 November 2026

Trump Says Iran Wants a Deal but Not on His Terms — and Vows Any Agreement Will Be “Permanent”

The diplomatic channel between Washington and Tehran is open, but the two sides remain far apart on what it should produce. President Donald Trump said on Friday that Iran wanted an agreement, just not one he could accept — “They want to make a deal, but they don’t want to make a deal that’s acceptable to me” — and on Saturday he told Fox News he intended to make any settlement “permanent,” structured so that no future president could let Tehran obtain a nuclear weapon. Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi answered that Tehran still hopes diplomacy can end the war, that messages continue to pass through mediators, but that Washington must first take confidence-building steps and offer a “good, honourable and dignified” agreement that guarantees no renewed conflict. The war of claims ran alongside the diplomacy. Iran’s army said its air defences fired on a US F-16 over the Persian Gulf on Friday evening and forced an emergency landing; US Central Command flatly denied it, posting that Iran “did not attack a US F-16 fighter aircraft yesterday — at all” and that every American fighter jet in the region was safe and accounted for. Iranian state television meanwhile aired footage of a new dual-missile launcher at an underground facility, and the US military said its blockade of Iran had reached 130 consecutive days, choking off billions of dollars in Revolutionary Guard oil exports.

Latest · Middle East · Riyadh — Third Night of Alerts21 November 2026

Saudi Air Defences Down Three Houthi Missiles Aimed at Riyadh as the Yemen War Spills North Again

Riyadh spent another night under emergency alerts. The Saudi-led coalition said early on Saturday that its air defences intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles fired by Yemen’s Houthi movement toward the Saudi capital, after civil defence issued an aerial-threat warning at 1:53 a.m. and residents heard two explosions. It was the second attack on the capital in days: on Thursday a Houthi strike damaged the Public Investment Fund tower, headquarters of the kingdom’s sovereign wealth fund and Riyadh’s tallest skyscraper. The coalition later said it had also destroyed a drone headed for the kingdom’s south, and two further ballistic missiles were intercepted over the Jizan region near the Yemeni border, with debris falling at Al-Shuqaiq. The exchange followed the coalition’s “special operation” on Friday against the Houthi-held Red Sea port of Hodeidah and military targets in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz, which spokesman Turki al-Malki said hit capabilities used to smuggle weapons and ballistic missiles. The Houthis said they were striking targets across Saudi Arabia, threatened Jeddah and other Red Sea ports and a power station, warned airlines that Saudi airspace was unsafe, and said coalition strikes had killed 41 people in the previous 24 hours — a toll that could not be independently verified. Saudi civil defence issued eight public emergency alerts in the 24 hours from Friday morning alone.

Latest · Global Affairs · Geneva — The Word They Would Not Use21 November 2026

“Killer Robot” Talks End With a Promise to Keep Talking: No Binding Rules, and a Deadline Pushed to 2028

After a week of negotiation in Geneva, the world’s governments have agreed to keep discussing how to govern weapons that can select and kill targets with little or no human involvement — and little else. The 128 states party to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons closed their review conference early on Saturday by agreeing to “elaborate” a possible instrument on lethal autonomous weapons by 2028. The wording matters: nearly 100 countries had pushed to open formal negotiations on legally binding rules, but major arms producers including the United States, Russia and Israel resisted, and because the forum decides by consensus, any single state can block a stronger outcome. The chair, Swiss diplomat Julien Thoeni, noted that some delegations had rejected even the word “negotiate,” yet hailed the result as a significant achievement after more than a decade of talks. UN Secretary-General António Guterres, who leaves office at the end of the year, had set 2026 as the deadline to launch treaty negotiations. Campaigners were blunt: the Stop Killer Robots coalition, representing some 300 organisations, called the mandate watered down and warned that delaying binding rules puts civilians at risk while the technology races ahead in conflicts from Ukraine to the Middle East. The UN’s disarmament chief, Izumi Nakamitsu, conceded the outcome could be read as either a success or a disappointment.

Latest · Global Affairs · New York — Correction Territory20 November 2026

An AI Sell-Off Drags the S&P 500 Into Correction and Erases the Nasdaq’s Gains for the Year

Wall Street’s artificial-intelligence boom met its sharpest reversal yet on Friday. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39 — its worst weekly loss in more than 18 months — leaving the benchmark more than 10% below its 6 October record high, the technical definition of a correction. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.3% to 22,617.73, sliding into negative territory for the year, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 949.73 points to close at 48,147.33. Chipmakers and AI-linked firms took the heaviest blows: Nvidia fell about 14%, its ninth down session in ten, with Micron, Broadcom and Advanced Micro Devices also sharply lower, alongside companies tied to the data-centre build-out such as Corning and Bloom Energy. The selling crossed oceans overnight, with Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s Kospi each falling more than 5% and European indexes down steeply. Analysts point to a pile-up of doubts that have been building for weeks: whether AI valuations and the enormous spending on data centres can be justified by future earnings, whether the Federal Reserve may yet raise interest rates, and a disappointing market debut for AI lab Anthropic, whose shares fell a day after a record-breaking listing. By one estimate the rout wiped some $2.7 trillion from global market value.

Latest · Europe · Brussels — A Decade After Brexit20 November 2026

Britain and the EU Seal a Reset Deal on Food Trade, Carbon and Youth Mobility — but Student Fees Still Divide Them

Ten years after the Brexit referendum, Britain and the European Union have agreed the first substantial rebuilding of their relationship. At a Brussels summit on Friday, Prime Minister Andy Burnham, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa confirmed three agreements: checks and paperwork on trade in plant and animal products will be scrapped under a shared sanitary and phytosanitary area; the two sides’ emissions-trading schemes will be linked, exempting goods such as steel from each other’s border carbon levies; and a youth mobility scheme will let up to 100,000 people under 30 from each side live and work in the other’s territory for up to three years. Burnham said the package could add 0.5% to Britain’s economy, about £17 billion, calling it “a good day for Britain” and “a good day for Europe,” with an implementing bill due in Parliament next week. Practical changes run from British sausages and live shellfish returning to EU menus to lifetime pet passports for travel. One issue was left open: Brussels wants EU students at British universities to pay domestic rather than international fees, and both sides gave themselves six months to settle it, with the EU signalling the other agreements will not be signed until the fees question is resolved. The new trade and carbon regimes are slated to begin in 2028, and youth mobility in 2030.

Denmark Expels Russia’s Defence Attaché Over an Alleged Plot to Kill a Ukrainian Rapper

Denmark has ordered Russia’s defence attaché to leave the country within seven days after its intelligence service concluded that Moscow planned to assassinate a Ukrainian-American rapper at a concert near Copenhagen. The Security and Intelligence Service, PET, said Russian intelligence recruited members of Loyal to Familia, a Danish street gang banned since 2021, to kill the performer known as Kyivstoner — real name Albert Vasiliev — before a show north of the capital on 25 September. Police stopped three men in a car near the venue shortly before the concert and found firearms and knives inside; two men were placed under formal investigation on Friday for attempted murder and for assisting a foreign intelligence service. “It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services,” PET chief Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen said, calling it an attempted execution on Danish soil. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen summoned the Russian ambassador to deliver the expulsion, said Denmark did not want to sever diplomatic relations but intended to send “a very, very strong signal,” and added that suspending Schengen visas for Russians was under consideration. Russia’s embassy in Copenhagen called the allegations baseless and the expulsion vengeful, saying Denmark had presented no evidence; Moscow’s foreign ministry promised an appropriate response.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Deadlock Over Two Words: Fossil Fuels21 November 2026

COP31 Deadlocks as France and Panama Lead a Revolt Against a Draft That Never Mentions Fossil Fuels

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya was supposed to end on Friday. Instead it has hardened into open deadlock. A revised draft of the final deal, circulated by the Australian-led presidency, contains no mention of fossil fuels at all — language on phasing them down that appeared earlier in the week was removed, and in its place the text leans on carbon capture and storage, with bracketed targets for storing hundreds of millions of tonnes a year by 2035. On Friday evening a bloc of countries led by France and Panama refused to accept a final decision that stays silent on oil, gas and coal, joined by ministers from island states and EU members including Spain, Belgium, Sweden and Finland. “It is unacceptable for a COP conference or the final decision not even to mention fossil fuels; we simply will not accept it,” said Panama’s environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro. Money is the second fault line: a proposed $120 billion adaptation-finance target has vanished, replaced by an unquantified discussion of tripling adaptation funds by 2035, and a proposal to lift electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035 no longer specifies that the power be renewable. In a first for the COP process, the draft — styled the “Antalya Accords” — acknowledges that the goal of holding warming to 1.5°C has not been achieved. Climate Action Network International said it was rejecting the text outright, and a new, likely final draft was expected on Saturday.

Latest · Americas · Junín, Peru — Magnitude 6.820 November 2026

A Magnitude 6.8 Earthquake in the Peruvian Andes Sends Lima Residents Into the Streets

A strong earthquake shook central Peru on Friday evening, rattling the capital Lima and sending residents out of their homes as a precaution. Peru’s Geophysical Institute put the magnitude at 6.8, centred in the Andean region of Junín, roughly 320 kilometres east of Lima, at a depth of 129 kilometres; the US Geological Survey measured the depth at 108 kilometres and timed the quake at 7:09 p.m. local time. Television footage showed lawmakers leaving Congress in Lima as the tremor was felt across the city, and video from the coast captured rock and dirt cascading off the Costa Verde cliffs onto a highway below in a cloud of dust. Junín’s regional government released photographs of damaged houses near the epicentre, but officials said there were no immediate reports of casualties or significant destruction. A provincial council member in Junín told local radio that no damage reports had reached authorities and that it was the first quake of this size felt in the area. Peru sits on the seismically active Pacific “Ring of Fire,” where the Nazca Plate slides beneath South America, and deep-focus quakes like this one are often felt over a wide area while causing less surface damage than shallow tremors.

Latest · Africa · Johannesburg — 1954–202620 November 2026

South Africa Mourns Phumzile Mlambo-Ngcuka, Its First Woman Deputy President and Former Head of UN Women

Phumzile Mlambo-Ngcuka, who broke the highest glass ceiling in South African politics and went on to lead the United Nations’ gender-equality agency, has died at the age of 71. The Presidency said she passed away on Friday evening, 20 November, following complications from a medical procedure she had undergone about two weeks earlier. A former teacher and anti-apartheid activist who helped found the Natal Organisation of Women in 1983, she entered Parliament in 1994 in the country’s first democratic government and helped draft its democratic constitution. She served as deputy minister of trade and industry and then as minister of minerals and energy before President Thabo Mbeki appointed her deputy president in 2005 — the first woman to hold the office, and at the time the highest-ranking woman in South African government history. In that role she drove the Accelerated and Shared Growth Initiative, insisting that economic growth be shared with the poor. From 2013 to 2021 she served as UN Under-Secretary-General and executive director of UN Women, leading global campaigns for gender equality. President Cyril Ramaphosa, speaking from a working visit to California, called her death “a sad day for the country” and “a real leading light for the cause of women”; UN Secretary-General António Guterres was among those paying tribute. A prayer service is planned for Sunday at the family home in Morningside, Sandton, with a funeral expected at her family home in Middledrift next Saturday and a state burial anticipated.
