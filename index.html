Latest · Americas · United States — Nationwide Flight Halt Lifted14 November 2026

US Lifts Nationwide Flight Halt After Air-Traffic Communications Failure Strands Thousands of Flights

American air travel ground to a halt on Friday afternoon and then slowly restarted. The Federal Aviation Administration ordered every departing flight in the country to stay on the ground after a telecommunications problem hit all 22 regional centres that guide aircraft between airports — only the second nationwide stop of departures since the September 11 attacks. Aircraft already airborne were allowed to land, and about 4,042 flights were in the air bound for US airports when the order came at 3:54 p.m. Eastern Time. FlightAware counted thousands of delays and more than a thousand cancellations, with later tallies putting cancellations above 4,700 and delays near 10,000. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy later said overloaded switches and routers had begun sending inconsistent data and shut down. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack or other threat, and the stop was lifted after more than four hours, though disruption rolled into Saturday.

Latest · Americas · United States — Mississippi River Collision14 November 2026

Tanker Loses Steering and Rams Docked Riverboat in New Orleans; 32 Taken to Hospital, No One Missing

A routine docking turned into a mass rescue on the Mississippi. At about 12:30 p.m. local time on Friday the tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the American Heritage, a five-storey paddle-wheeler operated by American Cruise Lines that was tied up at the Port of New Orleans with 129 passengers aboard and roughly 180 to 190 people in total including crew. The riverboat took on water, listed and partially submerged, sending some passengers into the river — at least 14 were pulled from the water. Mayor Helena Moreno said 32 people, including two harbour police officers hurt during the evacuation, were taken to hospital and none had critical injuries, with everyone accounted for in what officials called a “miracle on the Mississippi.” Coast Guard Captain Melanie Burnham said the tanker appeared to suffer a loss of steering, possibly after losing power. The Coast Guard closed nearby river traffic and the National Transportation Safety Board is sending a team to investigate, due on scene Saturday.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Record Drone Wave and Wider Talks14 November 2026

Ukraine Fires Almost 1,200 Drones at Russia as Peace Talks Widen to Europe’s Post-Cold-War Security

Diplomacy widened as the fighting intensified. Russia’s defence ministry said its air defences downed 1,198 Ukrainian long-range drones between late Thursday and early Friday, in one of Kyiv’s largest aerial attacks in nearly five years of war, and Ukraine’s General Staff said it hit an oil refinery in Volgograd, setting it ablaze. The barrage followed two days of talks in Abu Dhabi, after which US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner briefed Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin and then Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin, where he was meeting European leaders. Kushner said Russia had agreed to include Europeans in the next round — a shift after Moscow long demanded their exclusion — so post-war security arrangements can be discussed alongside territory. European leaders are wary: Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Europe’s existing security framework does not need renegotiating, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas argued for a ceasefire first, and the Institute for the Study of War warned Moscow aims to dismantle NATO and the post-Cold-War order. A major prisoner exchange was agreed in Abu Dhabi, but territory and guarantees remain unresolved, and Moscow later said it had not yet agreed to European mediators.

Rubio Assails the International Criminal Court and Argues International Law Should Not Restrain US Power

Secretary of State Marco Rubio used a speech to the conservative Federalist Society in Washington on Friday to attack the International Criminal Court and argue for a muscular foreign policy unconstrained by international law. It was his first prepared address since Republicans were routed in the 3 November midterms, and it will fuel speculation about whether he leaves his dual roles as top diplomat and national security adviser to run for president in 2028. Rubio dismissed criticism that the administration broke international law by seizing Venezuela’s then-president Nicolás Maduro and striking boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific that Washington says carried drugs, and said the goal of the US-Israeli war on Iran was to stop Tehran ever obtaining a nuclear weapon. The administration has long opposed the Hague court, founded in 2002 to prosecute war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide, and has sanctioned its prosecutors and judges over arrest warrants for Israeli officials, including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, and an inquiry into US personnel in Afghanistan. Those sanctions are due to take effect in April unless the court’s supporters reach a deal with Washington.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Outage Recovers14 November 2026

Microsoft Says Sorry as Two-Day Global Outage Eases; Fault Traced to a Network Upgrade, Not an Attack

One of the longest Microsoft disruptions in years is finally receding. Services including Outlook, Teams and the wider Microsoft 365 suite began failing on Thursday 12 November, hit users in more than 70 countries across the Americas, Europe and Asia, and disrupted businesses, banks and government bodies including Britain’s House of Commons and New Zealand’s foreign ministry, with about 140,000 Outlook reports recorded at the peak on Downdetector. On Friday the company said it was “deeply sorry,” reported broad recovery and stability, and said the cause was a technical fault introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced after a recent network change — explicitly not a cyberattack and not related to AI agents. Microsoft said the underlying problem has been fixed, restoration is being validated in stages, some customers and regions were still waiting for full service with no early estimate for complete recovery, and a full post-incident report will follow its review. The company also extended its email timeout window to 48 hours to protect delayed messages.

Latest · Europe · Norway — Supreme Court Climate Ruling13 November 2026

Norway’s Supreme Court Rules Three Oil Approvals Invalid for Ignoring the Emissions From Burning the Fuel

Climate campaigners won a landmark procedural victory in Oslo. Sitting with 11 judges, Norway’s Supreme Court ruled on Friday that government approvals for three offshore developments — Equinor’s Breidablikk, Aker BP’s Tyrving and Aker BP’s larger Yggdrasil, due on stream next year — were invalid because their environmental assessments counted only production emissions and never assessed the combustion, or Scope 3, emissions produced when the oil and gas are burned. All 11 judges agreed such emissions must be assessed under the European impact-assessment rules that apply in Norway, and a 6–5 majority said cumulative emissions from other existing and approved shelf projects must also be considered. The court stressed the case was decided on procedure: the oil companies were not parties, output is not halted, and licences do not automatically lapse. The energy ministry must now redo each assessment properly and decide whether permits can be reissued, and said it will comply. Greenpeace Nordic and Nature and Youth, which first sued in 2023 and won in two lower courts, called the result historic; lawyers and campaign groups challenging licences elsewhere are expected to study it closely.

Latest · Europe · Hungary — Buried Cash Seized14 November 2026

Hungary Seizes $19.5 Million in Cash Buried in Forests in Probe of an Orbán-Era Minister

Investigators in Hungary say they have dug up part of the proceeds of alleged corruption. The National Asset Recovery and Protection Office, a new body with investigative and prosecutorial powers created under Prime Minister Péter Magyar’s accountability drive after he ended Viktor Orbán’s 16 years in power in April, said a two-day search in Budapest and three counties recovered 5,071,632,000 forints, €2,658,350 and $1,257,100 — about $19.5 million in total. Cash was found buried in forests and hidden in farm buildings, warehouses, a home and a high-value safe in a secluded location, and has been deposited with the State Treasury. The searches form part of proceedings against Miklós Seszták, national development minister from 2014 to 2018, who is suspected of taking more than $37.2 million in bribes and kickbacks linked to the state bus company buying secondhand buses at inflated prices and to state construction projects.

Latest · Europe · Netherlands — Coalition Crisis Talks14 November 2026

Dutch Prime Minister Holds Hours of Crisis Talks to Save His Coalition After a Tax Deal Splits It Open

The Netherlands’ youngest prime minister is fighting to keep his government alive less than nine months into a four-year term. Rob Jetten held an hours-long meeting on Friday with the leaders of his two coalition partners after his centrist D66 and the Christian Democrats cut a deal with centre-left opposition parties PRO and Volt to pass next year’s tax package — including a measure PRO leader Jesse Klaver calls a “millionaire’s tax” — in a tight vote on Thursday. The third partner, the pro-business VVD, called the move a breach of the coalition agreement, voted against its own government’s plan, and warned through parliamentary leader Ruben Brekelmans that the way its partners acted was “disconcerting.” A finance-ministry review also warned one amendment could open a funding gap above €400 million. Jetten, who skipped a European leaders’ summit in Ireland for the debate, conceded the coalition needs a “very serious fresh start.” Late Friday the partners agreed only to keep talking in the coming days. A collapse would point toward a fifth general election in a decade.

Iran Says an LNG Tanker Hit a Mine and Exploded in the Strait of Hormuz After Using an Unauthorised Route

Tehran says the world’s most important energy chokepoint has claimed another ship. The Revolutionary Guards navy said on Friday that a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas, travelling east of the strait on a southern passage it called unauthorised and dangerous and ignoring repeated warnings, struck a naval mine and exploded violently. No vessel name, flag or casualty details were given, and the claim could not be independently confirmed. It follows Iranian reports on Thursday that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the waterway after entering areas Tehran considers restricted. Since the war began in late February, Iran has insisted ships need its permission to transit and has attacked those that do not comply, even though US Central Command says it cleared mines from the main international lanes. Data cited from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy counts at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly tally since the conflict began — even as Kpler data showed LNG exports through the strait reached their highest level since February last month.

Latest · Americas · Space — Starship Flight 1514 November 2026

Starship Reaches Orbit for Only the Second Time, but the Booster Catch Is Aborted to the Gulf

SpaceX’s boldest test yet delivered a split result. Starship Flight 15 — Ship 42 riding Super Heavy Booster 22, marked with the words “Don’t Panic” — lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 5:47 p.m. Eastern Time on Friday on a planned 22-hour mission to attempt the first-ever double catch, recovering both stages with the launch tower’s mechanical arms. The booster instead failed its catch criteria after separation, was diverted to a controlled splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico and burst into flames, which SpaceX has not yet explained. The upper stage flew on, reached orbit at 6:13 p.m. — only the second time Starship has done so — and began deploying its payload of 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites. Whether Ship 42 attempts its own tower catch, now scheduled for about 4:17 p.m. Eastern Time on Saturday after roughly 14 orbits, depends on the health of every system needed to guide it through re-entry; if they are not all healthy, it will be directed to a controlled splashdown in the Pacific instead.
