Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Record Drone Barrage14 November 2026

Ukraine Fires Nearly 1,200 Drones at Russia in One of the War’s Biggest Aerial Attacks

Kyiv has answered talk of a negotiated end to the war with a show of reach. Russia’s defence ministry said its air defences intercepted 1,198 Ukrainian long-range drones between late Thursday and early Friday, spread across 16 regions, Crimea and the Black Sea, with several hundred said to be heading for the Moscow area. Ukraine’s general staff said its drones struck the Lukoil refinery at Volgograd, one of Russia’s ten largest, starting a fire; the regional governor said four refinery workers were killed and two injured, and wider reporting put the Russian death toll at seven, including a 12-year-old. Russia replied with strikes on Ukrainian energy sites that knocked out power across roughly half the country’s regions. The barrage landed as US-led talks turned to wider European security arrangements, and Kyiv — which says its refinery campaign has already neutralised more than half of Russia’s refining capacity — appeared intent on proving it is far from beaten.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Chaos Rolls On14 November 2026

More Than 2,000 US Flights Cancelled on Saturday as Airlines Dig Out From the Longest Ground Stop in 25 Years

The flying stopped for four hours; the disruption will take days. More than 2,000 flights were cancelled across the United States on Saturday as carriers repositioned aircraft and crews after Friday’s nationwide ground stop, the longest since the September 11 attacks and only the second full halt to departures since then. FlightAware’s tallies for Friday passed 6,600 cancellations and 10,000 delays, and data firm Cirium estimated about 1.5 million travellers were caught up in it. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed ageing hardware: new traffic-planning technology loaded onto decades-old routers and switches produced inconsistent radar and radio data across all 22 en-route control centres, forcing the halt at 3:54 p.m. Eastern until airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack, and officials promised normal operations well before the Thanksgiving rush — but warned the weekend would stay messy.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · APEC — Beijing Confirms Trilateral Talks14 November 2026

Beijing Says It Is in Contact With Washington and Moscow as the Trump-Xi-Putin Trilateral Takes Shape

China has given its first public signal on next week’s headline meeting. Foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Friday that Beijing is in communication with the United States and Russia about the planned three-way summit between Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. The White House says Trump wants the session — the first time the leaders of the three largest nuclear powers have met together — to open a conversation on cutting nuclear stockpiles, nine months after the New START treaty lapsed and left Washington and Moscow uncapped for the first time in over half a century. Trump and Xi are also due to hold a bilateral meeting and working lunch, their fourth encounter since Trump returned to office, while no separate Trump-Putin one-on-one is planned. US officials stress no agreements are expected; the symbolism, analysts say, is the point.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — ‘No Easing’ on Myanmar14 November 2026

ASEAN Envoy Insists the Bloc Is Not Softening on Myanmar as Foreign Ministers Open Talks in Manila

Southeast Asia’s bloc is holding its line — at least in public. ASEAN foreign ministers began meeting in Manila on Saturday ahead of the 49th ASEAN Summit from 15 to 17 November, and the bloc’s special envoy on Myanmar, Philippine Foreign Secretary Maria Theresa Lazaro, told reporters bluntly there is no easing of the approach: the Five-Point Consensus agreed after the 2021 coup remains the framework, and a draft review of it is still under negotiation. Myanmar’s military-backed leaders remain barred from high-level meetings and will be represented only by a foreign-ministry director-general, despite pressure from some quarters to relax the five-year freeze. The leaders’ agenda is crowded besides: South China Sea code-of-conduct talks that Manila puts at roughly 70 percent complete, the economic fallout of the Middle East war on fuel costs, transboundary haze and an approaching El Niño. The Manila talks also sit in the shadow of Thursday’s expected International Court of Justice ruling on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya.

Latest · Americas · New Orleans — ‘Miracle on the Mississippi’13 November 2026

Everyone Survives as a Tanker That Lost Steering Rams a Docked River Cruise Ship in New Orleans

Officials are calling it a miracle, and the numbers explain why. At about 12:30 p.m. on Friday the 524-foot asphalt tanker Atlantic Asphalt lost steering on the Mississippi River and slammed into the American Heritage, a docked American Cruise Lines paddlewheeler carrying 129 passengers plus crew at the Port of New Orleans. The cruise boat took on water, listed and overturned, throwing people into the river; rescuers pulled passengers from the water, 32 were taken to hospital, and none had critical injuries. Mayor Helena Moreno said every person was accounted for. Coast Guard Captain Melanie Burnham confirmed the tanker suffered some form of steering failure, river traffic was closed nearby, and the National Transportation Safety Board is sending an investigative team — whose arrival was itself delayed by Friday’s nationwide flight halt. The 2015-built American Heritage, capacity 150, was finishing an eight-day Mississippi cruise.

Latest · Americas · Space — Starship’s Boldest Test14 November 2026

Starship Reaches Orbit and Deploys 26 Starlink Satellites as Saturday’s First-Ever Ship Catch Approaches

Half of SpaceX’s most ambitious plan worked; the harder half is still to come. Starship Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 5:47 p.m. Eastern on Friday, but engine trouble forced the upgraded Super Heavy booster to abandon its return to the launch tower and ditch in the Gulf of Mexico, where it exploded — ending hopes of an unprecedented double catch. The upper stage, Ship 42, flew on into orbit, deployed 26 next-generation Starlink satellites and settled into a roughly 22-hour, 14-orbit journey at about 275 kilometres up. On Saturday afternoon Eastern time it is due to re-enter and attempt something never tried: a mid-air catch of an orbital spacecraft by the tower’s mechanical arms. SpaceX says the ship and its heat shield remain healthy. A successful catch would mark a major step toward a fully reusable rocket system aimed at the Moon and Mars.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Flood Warning14 November 2026

UN Warns of More Flooding in Gaza After Rains Hit Over 200,000 People and Damage 40,000 Families’ Shelters

Winter has arrived in Gaza before the shelters did. The UN humanitarian office, OCHA, warned on Saturday that further rain could flood displacement sites again and contaminate drinking water, after the season’s first heavy storms swept all five governorates from 6 November. Spokesperson Jens Laerke said more than 200,000 people were affected and shelters housing about 40,000 families were damaged; earlier OCHA reporting counted over 6,000 family shelters destroyed and 17,400 damaged, more than 3,900 households left with no shelter at all, and at least five people killed when a sand dune collapsed onto tents near Deir al-Balah, among them three children. About 1.4 million people — some 65 percent of Gaza’s population — live in tents or makeshift cover, and aid stocks inside the enclave are described as virtually exhausted. Laerke said the UN had warned Israeli authorities months ahead and asked for more tents and supplies to be let in; over 150,000 tents remain staged outside.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola Out of Control13 November 2026

UN Says Armed Attacks Are Hampering the Ebola Response as Congo’s Deadliest Outbreak Passes 11,000 Cases

In eastern Congo, responders are fighting a virus and a war at once. UN officials said on Friday that repeated deadly attacks by Allied Democratic Forces fighters on civilians and health workers are obstructing efforts to contain the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak, declared in May and now the country’s largest ever: more than 11,000 confirmed cases and over 5,000 deaths, with health ministry figures putting the toll at 11,042 infections and 5,387 deaths across eight provinces. The World Health Organization estimates 400 to 500 new infections are still being found each week, and Médecins Sans Frontières warned this week that the outbreak remains out of control, with hospital transmission in some areas and perhaps only half of cases detected. The Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine or treatment, though an experimental vaccine trial began enrolling in Ituri province in late October. UN coordinator Julien Harneis noted grimly that in recent days the ADF has killed more people than Ebola has.

Latest · Global Affairs · COP31 — Halfway Mark14 November 2026

COP31 Reaches Its Halfway Point Split Over Money, With an Empty American Chair and a 1.47°C Warning

The Antalya climate summit has reached halftime with its hardest arguments unresolved. Nearly 200 countries and some 90,000 participants are gathered in Türkiye until 20 November under an unusual split presidency — Türkiye’s Murat Kurum runs the summit while Australia’s Chris Bowen leads the negotiations — and for the second straight year the United States has sent no official negotiating delegation, its Paris Agreement withdrawal having taken effect in January. The science framing the talks is stark: the World Meteorological Organization says January–September averaged about 1.47°C above pre-industrial levels, 2026 is tracking among the four warmest years on record, and sea levels sit at a satellite-era high. Secretary-General António Guterres has conceded a temporary overshoot of the 1.5°C limit now looks unavoidable while insisting its length can still be limited. Developing countries are pressing for faster, simpler access to climate finance, and a parallel argument over the timing of the next IPCC assessment has hardened into a test of trust in climate science ahead of the final text.

Microsoft Says Services Are Restored After a Global Outage — and Rejects Any Link to the US Flight Halt

Two failures, one news cycle, no connection — that is Microsoft’s position. The company said its Microsoft 365 services, including Outlook, Teams and Copilot, had fully recovered after an outage that began Thursday, spread across 74 countries and generated more than 130,000 problem reports on Downdetector at its US peak, disrupting businesses, banks and government agencies. Latest · Europe · Norway — Landmark Climate Ruling13 November 2026

Norway’s Supreme Court Voids Three Oil Approvals for Ignoring the Emissions From Burning the Fuel

Norway’s top court has redrawn what an environmental assessment must count. In an 11-judge ruling on Friday, the Supreme Court held that approvals for three offshore fields — Equinor’s Breidablikk and Aker BP’s Tyrving and Yggdrasil — are invalid because officials assessed only the emissions from producing the oil and gas, never the far larger combustion emissions when the fuel is burned. All 11 judges agreed those Scope 3 emissions must be assessed under the European rules Norway follows, and a 6–5 majority added that cumulative emissions from other shelf projects must also be weighed. The judgment is procedural, not a shutdown order: production continues and licences do not lapse automatically, but the energy ministry must redo each assessment and decide whether the permits can lawfully be reissued — a precedent other European licensing rounds will now have to reckon with.

Iran Says an LNG Tanker Struck a Mine in the Strait of Hormuz as Attacks on Shipping Reach a War-Time High

The world’s busiest energy chokepoint remains dangerous water. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said on Friday that a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas, travelling east of the strait on a southern route Tehran calls unauthorised and ignoring repeated warnings, hit a naval mine and exploded; no ship name, flag or casualty figures were released, and the claim has not been independently verified. It followed Iranian reports a day earlier of explosions aboard four oil tankers in the waterway. Since the war began in late February, Iran has demanded that ships seek its permission to transit and has struck vessels that did not, while US Central Command says it has cleared mines from the main international shipping lanes. Figures cited by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy count at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October — the highest monthly total since the conflict started.
