Latest · Middle East · Kuwait — Second Attack This Month26 November 2026

Iranian Drone Strike in Kuwait Kills Two Italian Defence Technicians

Two Italian technicians were killed on Thursday when Iranian drones struck Kuwaiti military sites, including an air-defence detection station where they were working, Italy’s foreign minister Antonio Tajani told the Senate in Rome. The men were employed by Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian arm of the German defence group Rheinmetall. Kuwait’s defence ministry said the pair died of their injuries after debris fell on what it called a vital facility, and confirmed that a Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were also wounded. Kuwait said its forces had detected and engaged several drones in its airspace and that the strike caused material damage. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni offered condolences to the families, while EU chief Ursula von der Leyen called for greater pressure on Tehran. Iran’s foreign ministry did not immediately comment. It was the second reported Iranian attack on the US-allied Gulf state this month, amid a continuing stand-off between Washington and Tehran over the Strait of Hormuz.

Latest · Middle East · West Bank — Village of Jaloud26 November 2026

Settlers Smash Foundations of Palestinian Family’s Homes Despite Court Order

Israeli settlers used a bulldozer to damage the foundations of two houses belonging to a Palestinian family in the West Bank village of Jaloud on Thursday, in an attack caught on security cameras at around 1 a.m. The Tubasi family say they were forced out months ago after settlers set fire to the homes, cut water and electricity and threatened them, and that a court order requiring the army to remove the settlers has still not been enforced. In September the military escorted the family back, but they say some 200 settlers attacked within minutes and drove them out again. The Israeli military condemned Thursday’s damage as a serious criminal act and said it was investigating. UN figures put settler attacks at an average of 53 a week since the start of 2026, and Israeli rights group Yesh Din says 93.6 percent of investigations into settler violence close without indictments. The family now lives in a small house nearby, within sight of the homes they cannot re-enter.

Latest · Global Affairs · Energy — Brent Near $9526 November 2026

Oil Edges Higher as Mine Strikes in Hormuz and Houthi Attack Fuel Supply Fears

Crude prices rose again in early Asian trade on Thursday after Yemen’s Houthis said they had struck Saudi Arabia’s largest oil refinery at Ras Tanura and Iranian reports said at least two tankers had hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent futures traded around $94.71 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate near $84.03, extending Wednesday’s sharp gains of 3.5 percent for Brent and 2 percent for WTI. One tanker caught fire after striking a mine in a restricted zone of the strait, according to Iran’s semi-official Fars agency, disrupting a recovery in Gulf export flows that had only recently returned toward pre-war levels. Analysts said the widening conflict is rebuilding a geopolitical risk premium, with OPEC+ ministers due to meet on 29 November to set 2027 output policy. US crude stocks also lent support, rising by 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million in the week to 20 November, less than markets had expected.

Latest · Africa · Bissau — Vote Moved to November 202826 November 2026

Guinea-Bissau Pushes Elections Back Two Years, Extending Military Rule

Guinea-Bissau’s military rulers on Thursday postponed presidential and legislative elections by about two years, abandoning the one-year transition they promised after seizing power in a coup last November. A decree from transitional president Major-General Horta Inta-a moved the vote, which had been due next month, to November 2028, saying the country lacks the technical, material and financial means to hold a credible ballot. The army took power as a presidential election was under way, ousting incumbent Umaro Sissoco Embaló, who had been seeking another term. The regional bloc ECOWAS had pressed for a short and transparent return to civilian rule, and voters approved a constitutional change in August giving the presidency greater powers. Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té had already warned last month that conditions were not ready for a December vote. The small Portuguese-speaking state has a long history of coups and serves as a transit route for cocaine bound for Europe.

Latest · Africa · Mombasa — Manslaughter Ruling Due Friday26 November 2026

Kenyan Cult Leader Already Jailed for 200 Years Is Convicted on Terrorism Charges

A court in Mombasa on Thursday convicted Kenyan cult leader Paul Mackenzie of terrorism-related offences, adding to a 200-year sentence he is already serving for murdering nearly 200 children. A magistrate found him guilty of organised criminal activity, radicalisation, facilitating a terrorist act and possessing an article connected with an offence under the Prevention of Terrorism Act. Mackenzie, who had pleaded not guilty, will appeal after sentencing, one of his lawyers said. He led an apocalyptic church in a forest near Kenya’s Indian Ocean coast, urging followers to starve themselves and their children to reach heaven before the end of the world; police began uncovering bodies there in April 2023, in one of the deadliest cult mass killings of modern times. He was also convicted on Wednesday of cruelty to children and assault, and still faces a manslaughter charge, with a ruling expected on Friday. President William Ruto said in 2023 that he took responsibility for the state’s failure to prevent the deaths.

Latest · Europe · London — Lowest Since 1997 Outside Covid26 November 2026

UK Net Migration Drops to 77,000, the Lowest Annual Level in Almost 30 Years

Net migration to Britain fell to 77,000 in the year to the end of June, official figures showed on Thursday — the lowest annual total since 1997, apart from the pandemic slump. The Office for National Statistics said about 738,000 people arrived and 661,000 left, leaving a balance down from 204,000 a year earlier and roughly 90 percent below the peak of nearly a million recorded in 2023. The ONS said fewer arrivals from outside the European Union, to work or study, drove the latest fall, following tighter rules on worker, student and asylum routes. The milestone eases political pressure on the government, which pointed to a drop of more than 90 percent from the peak, but economists and analysts warned that sharply lower work migration could weigh on growth, public services and university finances if the squeeze goes too far. Channel crossings also fell, with about 23,500 arrivals by sea from January to mid-November, down nearly 40 percent on 2025.

Latest · Europe · Bucharest — AUR Still Leads Polls26 November 2026

Romania Charges Hard-Right Leader George Simion With Inciting Violence

Romanian prosecutors on Thursday formally charged George Simion, leader of the opposition Alliance for Uniting Romanians (AUR), with inciting violence against election officials ahead of last year’s contested presidential contest. The case stems from March, when election authorities barred far-right frontrunner Călin Georgescu from a re-run of the cancelled December 2024 vote and violent protests erupted in Bucharest. Simion told supporters online that officials had staged a coup and should be “skinned alive in the public square,” later saying he had not meant the words literally. He dismissed the prosecution as a politically motivated judicial farce, and his lawyer argued the remark was hyperbolic political speech protected under European law. Simion went on to lose the May 2025 re-run to centrist Nicușor Dan, but AUR — critical of the EU, military aid to Ukraine and defence spending — remains Romania’s most popular party at around 35 percent in November polling, ahead of a general election due in 2028.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pyongyang — Trump Overture Ignored26 November 2026

Kim Jong Un Tours Nuclear Plants and Orders a Bigger Arsenal

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has inspected factories that produce nuclear material and called for more nuclear weapons, days after ignoring an offer from President Donald Trump to meet during his recent Asia trip. State news agency KCNA said on Thursday that Kim visited two nuclear-materials plants — one newly opened and one being expanded — as well as a nuclear-powered equipment base and a research institute, with photographs showing rows of centrifuges and what appeared to be warheads. Kim described expanding the arsenal in quality and quantity as an irreversible reality, cited what he called shifting nuclear threats around the Korean Peninsula, and said deploying low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons across different services was crucial and urgent. Analysts say the tour was meant to display the programme that underpins his rule and to make clear that any future talks would not be about giving it up.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Technology — Flagships Slipping to 202726 November 2026

US Chip Curbs and Anti-Distillation Push Slow China’s AI Model Race

New US restrictions on overseas computing power, combined with tougher defences by OpenAI and Anthropic against so-called distillation, are delaying China’s next generation of AI models, people familiar with the matter say. Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, has missed its planned release for its flagship GLM-5.5 model — designed at roughly 3 trillion parameters — and a launch once hoped for by year-end could now slip into early 2027; on Monday it instead released the smaller GLM-5.4. Beijing-based Moonshot AI, which rents much of its training capacity abroad, expects its usual six-month gap between major releases could stretch to a year or more if overseas access is cut, pushing its Kimi K4 model toward mid-2027. A Commerce Department rule that took effect on 19 November targets Chinese firms’ use of advanced US chips in data centres outside the US, covering setups above roughly 750 Nvidia H100 equivalents. The squeeze threatens the low-cost development model that let Chinese labs rival US firms despite years of export controls.

Latest · Americas · Thanksgiving — Storm and Runway Chaos26 November 2026

Thanksgiving Disrupted: FedEx Jet Overruns Miami Runway as Storm Delays Macy’s Parade

America’s Thanksgiving was hit twice over. In Miami, a FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 arriving from Memphis overran runway 26R at about 8:40 p.m. on Wednesday, forcing all but one runway to close on one of the busiest travel nights of the year; neither crew member was hurt, the Federal Aviation Administration is investigating, and by Thursday morning three of four runways had reopened, though the airport still logged 35 cancellations and 77 delays by 9:30 a.m. ET. In New York, the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed to Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET after forecasts of damaging winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding — the first weather postponement in the event’s history, after cancellations only during the Second World War. The National Weather Service issued high-wind and coastal flood warnings for Thursday into Friday, with gusts expected to make the parade’s giant balloons unsafe; balloons already inflated on Wednesday were deflated and will be re-inflated on Friday. Macy’s executive producer Will Coss said safety had to come first, and Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the decision tough but necessary.
