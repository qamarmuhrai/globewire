Latest · Middle East · Iran–UK Escalation1 November 2026

Iran Brands Britain's Royal Navy and RAF 'Terrorist Organisations' and Labels MI6 a 'Hostile Group'

Tehran sharply escalated its confrontation with London on Sunday, designating the Royal Navy and the Royal Air Force as terrorist organisations and classifying the Secret Intelligence Service, MI6, as a hostile group. The Foreign Ministry said the steps, authorised by the Supreme National Security Council and effective from 1 November, were retaliation for what it called Britain's hostile actions over the past year — above all London's decision on 13 July to place the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps under the State Threats Act 2026, and its alleged provision of military bases, facilities and logistical support for the US-Israeli war against Iran. The navy and air force designations were framed as a reciprocal measure under Iran's existing law on countermeasures to the American designation of the IRGC, while the MI6 label was applied under separate legislation on espionage and cooperation with hostile states. The move follows a security incident at RAF Fairford, used by US bombers during strikes on Iran, after which two men appeared in a London court on national-security charges. A senior IRGC political official, Brigadier-General Yadollah Javani, said Iranian forces were fully prepared for any scenario and were operating on the assumption that the US or Israel could strike at any moment. No immediate British response to the designations had been reported.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War1 November 2026

Russian Strikes Cut Power to Hundreds of Thousands in Ukraine as Kyiv's Drones Heavily Damage a Russian Industrial Site

The air war ran in both directions on Sunday even as a rare local ceasefire around the occupied town of Oleshky reached its final day. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said overnight Russian strikes on power infrastructure in four regions had left hundreds of thousands of households without electricity, while Ukraine's air force put the scale of the assault at 433 long-range drones launched since Saturday evening, 371 of them shot down or jammed, with dozens still airborne on Sunday morning and six separate alerts sounded in Kyiv since midnight. In return, Zelenskiy said Ukrainian forces had struck the Kamensky plant in Russia's Rostov region, which he described as involved in manufacturing Russian missiles. In Nizhny Novgorod region, Governor Gleb Nikitin reported significant damage to an industrial facility after a large-scale Ukrainian drone attack, without naming the site; the region hosts the NORSI refinery, one of Russia's largest, and officials urged motorists to buy fuel only as needed and avoid panic buying. Moscow said its defences downed 235 Ukrainian drones overnight. Around Oleshky in the south, meanwhile, the six-day humanitarian truce was due to expire on Sunday evening after an evacuation that moved hundreds of residents — via Crimea, the Russian city of Anapa and flights to Gomel in Belarus — back into Ukrainian-held territory, while aid was delivered to those who chose to stay.

Latest · Africa · Ebola Emergency1 November 2026

Ebola Deaths in DR Congo Pass 5,000 in the Country's Deadliest Outbreak, With Cases Now Confirmed in Three Neighbouring States

The Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo has become the deadliest in the country's history, with government figures putting deaths at 5,026 and confirmed infections at 10,351 since the outbreak was declared in mid-May. The case fatality rate stands at 48.6%, and eight of Congo's 26 provinces are affected, with the epicentre still in Ituri in the north-east — a conflict zone where insecurity complicates contact tracing and treatment. The outbreak is driven by the Bundibugyo species of the virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment, and experts believe it may have been circulating undetected since February, months before the official declaration. It is now the second-largest Ebola epidemic ever recorded, behind only the 2014–2016 West African outbreak that killed more than 11,000 people; Congo's previous worst, in 2018–2020, claimed 2,299 lives. The virus has crossed borders: Kenya has recorded nine confirmed cases and two deaths, Uganda two deaths among 20 early cases with none reported since August, and Nigeria has isolated suspected cases for testing. About 1,100 patients are currently in treatment or quarantine and 2,788 people have recovered. The World Health Organization's Africa office says ending the outbreak will require a continued scale-up of control measures, better access to affected areas and stronger community outreach, while an adaptive trial that began in Bunia on 23 October is testing whether the existing Ervebo vaccine offers any protection against this strain.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Two Days Out1 November 2026

Two Days to the US Midterms: Democrats Hold an Eight-Point National Lead as Trump Campaigns in Iowa and Kansas

With voting day on Tuesday, the final polling picture still favours the Democrats, but the Senate remains the chamber most likely to spring a surprise. The RealClearPolling generic-ballot average has edged to 51.5% for Democrats against 43.1% for Republicans, a THP/MM survey puts the gap at 54% to 41%, and an Emerson College poll gives Democrats a ten-point lead. One simulation-based forecast updated early on Sunday projects a median of 233 Democratic seats in the House, with a 93% chance of a Democratic majority, and 52 Democratic seats in the Senate, a 71% chance — though its ranges are wide enough to include a Republican hold in either chamber. Republicans currently control the Senate 53–47, and because Vice President JD Vance breaks a 50–50 tie, Democrats need 51 seats for outright control. State races stay tight: in Kansas, a Washington Post–Schar School poll has Democrat Adam Hamilton leading Republican Senator Roger Marshall 52% to 43%, while Emerson finds a much closer 50% to 47%. President Trump, whose approval stands at about 37% approve against 60% disapprove in aggregated polling, was campaigning in Iowa and Kansas on Sunday, while Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed held closing events amid what his campaign describes as rising Islamophobic attacks. More than 43 million Americans have already voted early, well above 2022 levels.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — UN Death in Custody1 November 2026

UN Demands an Independent Investigation After a Human Rights Staffer Dies in Houthi Detention in Yemen

The United Nations has reacted with outrage to the death of Mohammad el-Shami, a Yemeni staff member of its Human Rights Office who had been held by the Houthis since 6 June 2024. High Commissioner Volker Türk called the death utterly devastating, said the family had been given no explanation of how or why he died and had only been informed more than a month after the fact, and demanded full transparency and an urgent, independent and thorough investigation. Secretary-General António Guterres said he was appalled and deeply saddened, noted that the Houthi authorities had provided neither information about el-Shami's well-being nor proof of life despite repeated requests, and renewed the call for the immediate and unconditional release of everyone still held. El-Shami is the second UN employee known to have died in Houthi custody, after a World Food Programme colleague detained in January 2025 died the following month, prompting a suspension of UN work in the stronghold province of Saada. Seven of Türk's own staff remain detained, and the UN puts the total number of its personnel held at around 70, alongside former staff and humanitarian and civil-society workers. The Houthis have accused detained aid and UN workers of spying for the United States and Israel — charges the UN rejects and which can carry the death penalty in Yemen.

Latest · Africa · Dakar 202631 October 2026

Africa Hosts Its First Olympic Event as the Dakar Youth Games Open With 2,655 Athletes From 204 Nations

Senegal has made Olympic history: the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games opened on Saturday night at the 55,000-capacity Abdoulaye Wade Stadium in Diamniadio, the first Olympic sporting event ever staged on African soil. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye declared the Games open after a two-hour ceremony built around the theme The Rise of Sitoé, which traced Africa's past, present and future through three symbolic characters and celebrated Senegal's tradition of teranga, or hospitality. Senegalese music icons Kiné Lam, Baaba Maal and Kya Loum performed alongside contemporary hip-hop artists, social-media star Khaby Lame made a cameo, and Olympic medallist Amadou Dia Ba was among the final torch bearers. IOC President Kirsty Coventry — the first woman and first African to lead the organisation — told the crowd that Africa was no longer on the margins of the Games but at their heart. Around 2,655 athletes aged 14 to 17 from 204 national committees, plus a Youth Refugee Olympic Team appearing at a Youth Games for the first time, will compete in 25 sports across Dakar, Diamniadio and the coastal town of Saly until 13 November, with ten further engagement sports. Dakar was awarded the Games in 2018 for 2022 before they were postponed to 2026, and this edition is the first Youth Olympics to reach full gender equality across quotas, sports and events.

Latest · Europe · London Protest Arrests1 November 2026

139 Arrested Outside the US Embassy in London for Backing Banned Group Palestine Action, Days Before a Supreme Court Appeal

London's Metropolitan Police arrested 139 people on Saturday at a mass protest outside the US Embassy in Nine Elms in support of Palestine Action, the direct-action group the British government proscribed as a terrorist organisation in July 2025. Officers began detaining demonstrators shortly after the lunchtime action began, most of them for expressing support for a proscribed organisation — an offence that carries a maximum sentence of 14 years — and said further arrests were expected. Many of those present were older demonstrators carrying placards reading “I oppose genocide, I support Palestine Action” and “We are protesters, not terrorists.” The protest, organised by the campaign Defend Our Juries, was one of a series held in London and Edinburgh; the group says more than 4,000 people have now been arrested for defying the ban since it took effect. Palestine Action was outlawed after a series of actions against defence facilities, particularly those of the Israeli arms company Elbit Systems, and sits on a blacklist alongside Hamas and Hezbollah. The timing is pointed: the Supreme Court begins a two-day hearing on Wednesday on an appeal against the designation, after the High Court ruled the ban unlawful in February and the Court of Appeal found it justified and proportionate in June. In August the United States also sanctioned the group as a terrorist organisation.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Deadlock30 October 2026

Israel's Final Count Leaves Netanyahu Eight Seats Short and Arab Parties Holding the Balance of Power

Israel's Central Elections Committee has published the final seat allocation from Tuesday's vote, confirming a deadlock that leaves every route to power politically fraught. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud is the largest party with 27 seats, but his bloc of right-wing, ultra-Orthodox and far-right allies totals only 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats — down from 68 in the outgoing parliament and eight short of the 61 needed to govern. The opposition bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot holds 48, with his Yashar party on 21, Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad on 12 and Yisrael Beytenu on seven. The remaining 19 seats belong to the Arab-led Joint List, with 11, and Ra'am, with eight — a historic high that makes them the only bloc able to carry either side past 61, even though leaders on both sides have publicly ruled out relying on them, and Ra'am's Mansour Abbas has himself voiced scepticism about propping up an Eisenkot minority government from outside. The alternatives under discussion are a broad unity government combining Likud with opposition parties, which could command around 67 seats, or a repeat election in the spring. Turnout was 77.9%, with more than 5.7 million votes cast — among the highest in decades. The certified results go to President Isaac Herzog on 4 November; he will consult the parties before handing one lawmaker 28 days, extendable by 14, to form a government. The new Knesset is sworn in on 10 November, with Netanyahu remaining caretaker prime minister meanwhile.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz Diplomacy1 November 2026

Iran Says Hormuz Could Reopen in Two Days if Washington Meets Its Terms — as Its Supreme Leader Warns 'Nowhere Is Off Limits'

The diplomatic track and the military threats ran side by side this weekend. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a proposal worked on with Qatari mediators could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days — shortened from an initial seven days, then five at Qatar's suggestion — once the United States implements Tehran's conditions: lifting sanctions imposed after the July collapse of the Islamabad memorandum, restoring the June oil waivers, releasing frozen funds including some $6 billion held in Qatar, drawing down US forces in the region, and recognising a shipping route agreed with Oman while closing others. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei insisted all seven Iranian conditions must be met before talks resume, and Tehran has described a letter from Vice President JD Vance, delivered via Qatar, as a marked change in tone; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington would be open to a diplomatic solution. Against that, Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei warned the US and Israel of fresh attacks if they strike again, after President Trump suggested he might order new action once the midterms are over, and Iran has previously threatened undersea cables in the strait. Shipping remains hazardous: Qatar's Nakilat says its LNG carrier Al Mayeda was struck, UK maritime authorities count 81 projectile strikes in the strait since 6 July with October the worst month of the war, and US Central Command says its counter-blockade of Iranian ports has redirected 144 vessels. The strait normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · North Korea1 November 2026

North Korea Tests a Cluster Warhead It Says Can Scour 13 Hectares, and Vows to Field Four Types by Year's End

North Korea has tested a cluster-bomb warhead designed for its tactical ballistic missiles and says its units will be able to choose from four categories of the weapon by the end of this year, state media reported on Sunday. The Missile Administration carried out the test on Saturday to judge the warhead's combat adaptability and power, according to the Korean Central News Agency, and its research institutes concluded that the Standard Type 2 warhead can devastate a target area of 12 to 13 hectares — roughly 18 football pitches — at its highest density. South Korea's military said it tracked a launch towards the Yellow Sea from around Chongju county in the northwest, and a Seoul defence analyst identified the missile as apparently belonging to the Hwasong-11 Ra family. Photos released by KCNA showed a projectile leaving a mobile launcher and cluster warheads striking an island target. Leader Kim Jong Un did not attend this test, which was overseen by General Kim Jong-sik; Kim had personally watched an earlier cluster-warhead launch in April alongside his daughter Ju-ae. The test is the latest in a run of recent trials that have included attack drones, a precision-guided torpedo system and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile. Cluster munitions, which scatter submunitions over a wide area, are banned by more than 100 countries under a 2008 convention that neither Korea has signed.

Latest · Europe · Youth Protests Spread1 November 2026

Europe's Youth Protests Reach a Third Weekend: 320,000 March in France, Looting in Frankfurt, 34 Detained in The Hague and Barricades in Zurich

The youth-led protest wave that began over living costs, housing and education has now rolled through a third consecutive weekend and across most of Western Europe. France's interior ministry counted about 320,000 demonstrators nationwide on Saturday, including some 70,000 in Paris, under a deployment of roughly 70,000 police and gendarmes; a preliminary tally put arrests at 422. In Germany, the movement calling itself “Gen Z regelt!” rallied in more than 30 cities, and in Frankfurt masked groups looted shops and set fires, with police using water cannon and pepper spray in several cities and several officers injured. In the Netherlands, police dispersed crowds near The Hague's central station and detained 34 people, most of them minors, suspected of violence, illegal fireworks and wearing face coverings; one officer was hospitalised with suspected hearing damage after lit fireworks were thrown, and shops had boarded up their windows in advance. In Zurich, an unauthorised student demonstration saw burning barricades in the Langstrasse district, while in Lisbon thousands marched peacefully over the housing crisis, and Spanish police stepped up security in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia. Organisers stress that most marchers are peaceful and that the grievances — unaffordable housing, food and education costs, cuts to public services — are shared across borders, which is why the protests keep jumping from country to country.

Latest · Europe · Serbia1 November 2026

Serbia Marks Two Years Since the Novi Sad Station Collapse With a Memorial Walk and 16 Minutes of Silence at 11:52

Serbia is marking the second anniversary of the Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse on Sunday with a memorial walk organised by the student movement that the disaster gave birth to. Sixteen people died when the concrete canopy fell at 11:52 on 1 November 2024, and one young woman was seriously injured. Columns were set to gather at 09:30 at three points — outside the Faculty of Medicine, in Liman Park and at the Varadin Bridge — and depart at 10:00, merging near Futog Market before walking together to the station, where the commemoration begins at exactly 11:52 with 16 minutes of silence, one for each victim. An indictment has been filed against 13 people over the collapse, which investigators and protesters link to corruption and failures in a renovation carried out as part of the Belgrade–Budapest high-speed railway project; the station had recently been rebuilt by Chinese contractors and, reporting on the case notes, was used for months without an operating permit. The movement that grew from the tragedy spent nearly two years demanding accountability and institutional reform, and last Sunday won Serbia's parliamentary election, ending Aleksandar Vučić's long rule — a backdrop that gives this year's commemoration a weight beyond mourning. Thousands were expected to take part in Novi Sad, many of them students who had walked for days from across the country for the first anniversary a year ago.
