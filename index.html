Latest · Europe / Global Affairs · Moscow Talks — Ukraine Peace Push12 November 2026

US Envoys Arrive in Moscow for Talks With Putin After Abu Dhabi Agrees a Major Prisoner Exchange but No Ceasefire

American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff have arrived in Moscow for talks with President Vladimir Putin, the latest step in a renewed push to end the war in Ukraine. The Kremlin meeting, expected in the first half of Thursday, is Witkoff’s tenth in-person encounter with Putin and follows two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi that closed on Wednesday with agreement on another large prisoner exchange but no public breakthrough on wider terms. Putin’s envoy Kirill Dmitriev met the Americans off their plane in wet snow and travelled with them into central Moscow. Kyiv continues to reject demands to surrender the whole of the eastern Donbas in return for peace, and US officials say territory remains among the biggest unresolved issues despite public optimism from President Donald Trump. The envoys are then expected to travel to Ireland to brief President Volodymyr Zelenskyy alongside European leaders.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Tankers Disabled12 November 2026

Two Supertankers Carrying More Than Four Million Barrels Are Disabled by Explosions in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran’s Guards Say

Two oil supertankers were left disabled and on fire after massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday night, according to Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy, which said the vessels had ignored repeated warnings against using an unauthorised southern route. Iranian state media later said the ships exploded after hitting naval mines, while the Guards’ own statement described an “accident” on a prohibited path without specifying the cause. Between them the tankers were carrying more than four million barrels of oil through a waterway that normally handles about a fifth of global oil shipments. In the earlier months of the US-Israeli war on Iran, which began in February, the Guards announced direct strikes on tankers, and the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency has in several cases reported vessels in the strait being hit by unknown projectiles. The incident deepens uncertainty for shipping and energy markets already strained by the conflict.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — Vice President Resigns12 November 2026

Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka Resigns as Courts Question Whether His Education Met Election Rules

Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka, the 39-year-old son of former President Joko Widodo, resigned on Thursday after months of scrutiny over his schooling, submitting his letter to President Prabowo Subianto and to parliament’s upper house. Gibran, elected alongside Prabowo in 2024 and two years into a five-year term, had offered to step down in a meeting with the president on Tuesday, saying he wanted to avoid a prolonged controversy. Last month the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court ruled that documents he submitted about his overseas education did not meet election requirements, and this week the Supreme Court ordered the education ministry to withdraw his equivalence certificate. Under the constitution there is no new election for the vacancy: the People’s Consultative Assembly must choose a replacement from two candidates nominated by the president within 60 days. The government said the economy had not been disrupted, though the departure threatens a key political alliance.

Latest · Americas · New York — Building Collapse Toll Rises12 November 2026

Brooklyn Collapse Toll Rises to Five Dead and 19 Hospitalised as Hundreds of Rescuers Keep Searching the Rubble

The death toll from Wednesday’s collapse of a three-storey building in Brooklyn’s Flatbush neighbourhood has risen to five, with 19 people hospitalised, New York’s fire department confirmed on Thursday as the search entered a second day. The building fell into an adjoining excavation site at about 4 p.m. on Wednesday, trapping residents and construction workers. More than 250 firefighters and emergency personnel reached the scene within a minute and have been working with dogs and drones, pulling debris away piece by piece; among those rescued alive on Wednesday night was a pregnant woman carried out on a stretcher. Fire Chief John Esposito said officials still do not know how many people may be missing and warned the operation could take several days, with heavy rain also forecast. Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the Department of Buildings and law enforcement would investigate the cause, which remains unknown, and city records show the neighbouring demolition site was cited in April for work carried out despite a stop-work order.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Nasser Hospital Strike12 November 2026

Israeli Strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis Kills Six People Including a Surgeon, Gaza Officials Say

An Israeli airstrike on the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis early on Thursday killed at least six people, including surgeon Dr Rezik Abushira, and wounded about 20 others, according to Gaza’s Civil Defence and health authorities. The strike hit the surgery department on the second floor of the Al Yasin building, collapsing ceilings and leaving rubble across damaged wards at one of the few large hospitals still functioning in southern Gaza. A British surgeon volunteering at the hospital said the blast just after midnight shook the whole building. Israel’s military said it struck the site jointly with the Shin Bet, saying it targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas’s Khan Younis Brigade, and other militants it said were using the complex, and that it was still reviewing the results. Gaza’s Health Ministry condemned the strike and called for international protection for the health system.

Latest · Europe · Spain — General Strike Over Housing11 November 2026

Four Million Join Spain’s First General Strike in 14 Years as Anger Over Rents and Evictions Dominates the Election Run-Up

Spain is counting the cost of its first general strike in 14 years, a 24-hour walkout on Wednesday that unions say drew four million workers in protest at a housing crisis less than three weeks before a snap election on 29 November. Backed by the UGT and CCOO unions and by tenants’ associations, the strike disrupted transport and slowed factories, with unions reporting stoppages of about 90 percent in the car and port sectors. The Bank of Spain estimates a shortfall of about 750,000 homes, rising above one million by 2028, while the Youth Council says average rent of 1,176 euros a month now absorbs about 98.7 percent of a young person’s average salary. The movement was galvanised by the September eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal in Madrid, who died days afterwards. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called the election after parliament rejected his housing decrees, hoping to put housing at the centre of the campaign.

Russian Missiles and Jet Drones Hit 17 Sites Across Ukraine, a Second Strike Kills Two Firefighters, and Kyiv Reports 167 Clashes

While diplomats travelled to Moscow, the war on the ground intensified. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched ballistic missiles, Zircon missiles and 123 attack drones overnight, 73 of them jet-powered, with hits recorded at 17 locations and debris at 18 more; grid operator Ukrenergo reported partial outages in nine regions. In Kyiv a drone struck a nine-storey block in the Solomianskyi district at about 8 a.m., killing a 44-year-old resident and injuring seven people, and in Yahotyn a second Russian strike on a fire already being fought killed two firefighters and wounded two more. Ukraine’s General Staff separately reported 167 combat clashes in the past day, 84 Russian airstrikes and 2,150 Russian troops killed or wounded, with the heaviest fighting around Kostiantynivka. Separately, a car bomb killed a Russian serviceman, reported by Fontanka to hold the rank of colonel, near St Petersburg.

Latest · Americas · Peru — Pope Leo Homecoming12 November 2026

Three Million Greet Pope Leo in Lima as He Returns to Peru, the Country He Calls His Homeland, for the Final Leg of His Tour

Pope Leo XIV has been welcomed in Lima by an estimated three million people, the largest crowd of his papacy, on the final and most personal leg of a three-nation, 12-day Latin American tour that has already taken him to Uruguay and Argentina. The 71-year-old pontiff, born in Chicago and a dual US-Peruvian citizen who served in Peru as a missionary for about two decades and as bishop of Chiclayo from 2015 to 2023, toured in an open popemobile weeks after surgery for lung cancer and appeared in good form. President Keiko Fujimori greeted him at Callao air base, and in his first speech he urged her to build confidence among Peruvians after her narrow and contested election victory, alluding to the legacy of her father, former President Alberto Fujimori. The six-day visit includes three days in Chiclayo and 22 scheduled events, among them Masses in Lima, Cusco and the Amazon city of Pucallpa.

Ten Killed and Six Injured as a Chhath-Bound Electric Vehicle Plunges Hundreds of Metres Off a Mountain Road in Nepal

Ten people were killed and six injured early on Thursday when an electric passenger vehicle travelling from Kathmandu to Sarlahi for the Chhath festival left the Bhimphedi-Kulekhani-Kathmandu road at Chisapani Gadhi in Makwanpur district and fell about 500 metres down a steep slope. Police said the crash happened at about 5:45 a.m. in Bhimphedi Rural Municipality-6 and identified the driver as Ravi Shankar Kumar Mandal of Sarlahi; the dead comprise five men, three women and two girls. Rescue teams from the police, Armed Police Force, army and provincial disaster managers reached the remote site, about eight kilometres north of the Bhimphedi area police office, and a three-year-old child was among those pulled out alive according to early reports. The cause remains under investigation. Nepal recorded 2,707 road deaths in the fiscal year that ended in mid-July, underlining the dangers of its mountain highways.

Latest · Global Affairs · COP31 — Finance Test in Antalya12 November 2026

At COP31 in Antalya the Argument Is No Longer About Pledges but About Money, as Iran Seeks a Mechanism for War’s Environmental Damage

The leaders’ summit at COP31 in Antalya, running alongside the UN climate conference from 9 to 20 November, has turned into a test of whether climate diplomacy can still deliver finance and action, with a new assessment finding little global progress in five years. Türkiye is hosting the conference while Australia presides over negotiations under an unusual shared arrangement, and Thursday’s sessions covered the second decade of the Paris Agreement, access to climate finance, urban resilience, clean cooking in Africa and the security of electricity grids. Finance remains the central sticking point, with developing countries pressing for faster access to funds and a monitoring system for the 1.3 trillion dollar roadmap. At the summit Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian called for the environmental damage caused by military activity to be recognised and assessed and for a compensation mechanism to be created, linking the wars overshadowing the talks directly to the climate agenda.

Latest · Africa · Libya — 602 Migrants Repatriated to Chad12 November 2026

Libya’s Eastern Authorities Repatriate 602 Migrants to Chad in a Border Campaign, as the Country Remains a Main Transit Route to Europe

Libya’s eastern security authority has repatriated 602 migrants to Chad, its anti-migration agency said, as part of a campaign it describes as protecting the homeland and the border. Spokesperson Abdulmenam al-Ghomati said those sent back on Wednesday had been detained in recent operations or caught trying to enter Libyan territory illegally, in areas controlled by Khalifa Haftar’s Libyan National Army in the east and much of the south. The transfer by truck to the Chadian border took a day and a half, and photographs released by the agency in Benghazi showed pickup vehicles loaded with people in a remote area. The campaign is continuing on the orders of deputy commander Saddam Haftar, including raids on homes rented by migrants in residential neighbourhoods. Libya, split between rival eastern and western authorities since the fall of Muammar Gaddafi in 2011, remains a major transit country for people fleeing conflict and poverty, with UN data suggesting well over 900,000 migrants across its 100 municipalities.
