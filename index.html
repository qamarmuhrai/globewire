Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Traffic at 8% of Normal16 November 2026

Six Ships Crossed All Day: Hormuz Stays Severely Restricted as Tehran Claims Six Tankers Hit

By Monday evening the Strait of Hormuz was moving at a fraction of its normal pace. Tracking data counted just six AIS-visible commercial crossings by mid-afternoon UTC — one inbound, five outbound, only two of them tankers — against a pre-crisis average of nearly 85 a day. Over the past week the average has been about seven crossings a day. The slowdown sits behind competing claims from the waterway itself. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards say six tankers have struck mines in the southern corridor since Sunday, including a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels that Tehran says sank. Maritime authorities have confirmed a narrower picture: the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre verified an attack on a tanker approaching the strait on 15 November, identified by Vanguard Tech as the Al Dhannah, hit near the stern and turned back toward Dubai with its crew safe, and a second strike at 05:58 UTC on Monday when an outbound vessel, identified as the Panama-flagged On Phoenix, took a projectile about three nautical miles off Oman and suffered an engine-room fire, also without casualties. Both vessels appear on Tehran’s blacklist of ships it calls non-compliant. The claimed sinking remains unverified, with the Guards’ video showing fire on open water and no clear vessel. Tehran continues to insist only its northern corridor is authorised.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Record Barrage Confirmed16 November 2026

1,336 Drones, a Bridge Arch Hit at Dawn: Ukraine’s Largest Attack of the War, by Moscow’s Own Count

Russia’s defence ministry said its air defences intercepted 1,336 Ukrainian drones overnight across 16 regions, Crimea and the Black and Azov seas — surpassing the previous record of 1,254 set on 29 October and marking the biggest single-night total since the full-scale invasion began in 2022. The most consequential hit was on the 19-kilometre Kerch Bridge linking mainland Russia to annexed Crimea. Crimea’s Russian-installed head Sergey Aksyonov confirmed the crossing was struck, with road traffic closed from about 2:22 a.m. and roughly 2,000 vehicles queued on both approaches. Monitoring channels reported an impact on an arch over the navigable span around dawn, and Russian channels claimed the arch had shifted visibly, though the extent of damage has not been independently verified. The Kremlin described the damage as minor, said an assessment was under way and noted rail services continued while road repairs were carried out. Kyiv had not officially commented by Monday evening. The barrage came days before Presidents Putin, Trump and Xi are due to meet in Shenzhen, and on the same day EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said Europe would need a ceasefire and Russian concessions before serious peace talks could begin.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Manila, Day Two16 November 2026

China Pitches Free Trade in Manila, Trump Lands Tuesday: ASEAN’s Summit Week Turns to the Superpowers

Chinese Premier Li Qiang used Monday’s ASEAN meetings in Manila to present Beijing as a defender of open trade, telling Southeast Asian leaders that economic ties between China and the bloc had strengthened even as unilateralism, protectionism and geopolitical conflict had grown. He spoke across a packed second day of the 49th ASEAN Summit, which also held sessions with Australia, South Korea and the United Nations. UN Secretary-General António Guterres renewed calls for a ceasefire in Ukraine and for wider humanitarian access in Myanmar, and said the UN stood ready to support any denuclearisation initiative involving the United States, Russia and China. The summit’s centre of gravity now shifts to Tuesday, when President Donald Trump is due to arrive near its close — facing expected protests after days of demonstrations in Manila — to meet ASEAN leaders and join the first Quad leaders’ gathering in two years alongside Japan, Australia and India. Trump then travels to the APEC forum in Shenzhen, where a first-ever trilateral meeting with Presidents Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin is scheduled for Thursday. ASEAN leaders ended their own summit on Sunday without completing the long-negotiated South China Sea code of conduct the Philippines had hoped to finish this year.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31, Final Week16 November 2026

Ministers Arrive in Antalya With Finance Still Unresolved: COP31 Enters Its Decisive Week

The UN climate conference in Antalya entered its final week on Monday with ministers from nearly 200 countries arriving to try to close gaps that negotiators left open — above all on climate finance, adaptation support and how to put a promised just-transition mechanism into practice. COP31 President Murat Kurum urged delegations to deliver on pledges already made, while Australia’s Chris Bowen, who chairs the formal negotiations under the shared Turkey–Australia arrangement, said some questions now need political decisions. The first week produced incremental progress but no breakthrough is expected before the summit closes on Friday, 20 November. Saturday’s march through downtown Antalya — thousands of protesters in the rain, in the largest demonstration since the talks opened on 9 November — set the tone for the ministerial phase, with campaign groups accusing the process of moving too slowly for countries already facing severe climate impacts. On Monday the presidency also launched a package linking technology to climate action, including an Antalya AI Pledge on responsible artificial intelligence and a Green Industrialisation Compact aimed at cutting emissions from industry, which accounts for roughly a quarter of the global total. This is the first COP since the United States’ withdrawal from the Paris Agreement took effect in January.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — 150,000 Tents Held at the Border16 November 2026

“Stop the Delays Now”: Four Countries Tell Israel to Let Winter Aid Into a Flooded Gaza

Australia, Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom issued a joint appeal urging Israel to allow immediate, rapid and unimpeded humanitarian access across Gaza, saying 150,000 tents and other essential supplies are being held at the border while conditions inside deteriorate. The foreign ministers described the situation as dire and, in the four governments’ wording, entirely reversible — a phrase that places responsibility on decisions that can still be changed. The statement followed flooding from the first winter storm of the season, which the UN humanitarian office said had collapsed damaged buildings, inundated shelters and disrupted essential services. Heavy rain earlier this month swept away tens of thousands of tents in a territory where almost the entire population has been displaced at least once. The appeal builds on a broader call last week by 53 UN member states and organisations for aid to enter through all crossings and for corridors via Egypt and Jordan to operate predictably. It landed on a day when medical sources in Gaza said an Israeli drone strike near al-Mawasi, northwest of Rafah, killed two Palestinians — deaths Gaza’s health ministry counts among 1,643 killed since the October 2025 ceasefire took effect. No Israeli response to the four-country statement had been reported by Monday night.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia — The Haze Apology15 November 2026

“Not Our Fault, But We Apologise”: Prabowo Addresses Singapore and Malaysia After Four Months of Fires

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto apologised to Singapore and Malaysia at the ASEAN Summit in Manila for the transboundary haze that drifted across their borders during nearly four months of wildfires — while also saying the disaster was not Indonesia’s fault and that his government had done its best. Speaking on behalf of his government and people, he said 71,000 personnel had been deployed to fight the blazes, 16 firefighters had died and two helicopters had been lost. Indonesia is enduring its most intense fire season in 11 years, with blazes concentrated on Sumatra, Borneo and Papua. Monitoring by the Nusantara Atlas puts the area burned so far in 2026 at about 2.88 million hectares. Prabowo said Jakarta was enforcing a zero-tolerance approach to companies linked to the fires: 17 concessions revoked, with dozens of firms charged or under investigation — one official account put 104 companies under police inquiry — and a new prevention body in preparation. By early November Indonesia’s health ministry had recorded more than 400,000 acute respiratory infections across seven affected provinces. Singapore and Indonesia agreed in October to set up a joint task force and a foreign-ministry hotline on fire-related information sharing.

Latest · Europe · Sweden — The Kingmaker Chooses15 November 2026

Centre Party Picks the Social Democrats: Andersson Gets a Path Back, With One Condition Still Blocking It

Sweden’s Centre Party said on Sunday it will negotiate further with the Social Democrats on forming the next government, giving opposition leader Magdalena Andersson renewed momentum and dealing a serious blow to caretaker Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson’s effort to build a centre-right alternative. Centre leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist said the party had reviewed rival proposals submitted on Friday by Andersson and by Kristersson, who leads the Moderates, and had chosen the Social Democratic option for detailed talks on policy and financing. Andersson’s plan is a three-party cabinet of Social Democrats, Centre and Greens, supported from outside by the Left Party. Together the four parties hold 176 of the Riksdag’s 349 seats after September’s inconclusive election. The obstacle that has blocked every attempt so far remains: Centre will not sit in government with the Left Party and wants it to drop its demand for cabinet posts, while the Left has said it will vote against Andersson if it is excluded. Parliament has already rejected both Andersson and Thand Ringqvist in prime-ministerial votes. Kristersson is still due to report his own formation efforts to Speaker Andreas Norlén on Tuesday, 17 November, but Swedish media now assess his chances as slim. Sweden has been under a caretaker government since September.

Latest · Europe · Estonia — Border Shuts 1 December15 November 2026

After a Foiled Arson Attack Blamed on the GRU, Estonia Will Close Every Crossing With Russia

Estonia will close all its border crossing points with Russia from 1 December, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna announced on Sunday, after an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, a maker of reconnaissance and combat drones supplied to Ukraine. Attackers poured flammable liquid through a smashed window at the company’s premises in Viimsi, near Tallinn, in the early hours of Saturday; the security service KAPO said the building would have suffered considerable damage had the plan succeeded. Eight people, all resident in Latvia, were detained. Tsahkna said there was clear evidence the operation was ordered by Russia’s GRU military intelligence — the second GRU-attributed attack on an Estonian defence firm since a fire at Milrem Robotics in August — though Tallinn has not published the underlying evidence. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Russian intelligence exploits the border crossings to exert influence and that the risk had to be reduced; no end date for the closure has been given. The three crossings at Narva, Koidula and Luhamaa carry pedestrians and freight, and the shutdown will fall hardest on Narva, whose residents often have family across the river in Ivangorod. Moscow’s foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova dismissed the move as Russophobia.

Latest · Europe · Czechia — Letná Fills Again15 November 2026

350,000 on the Plain Where Communism Fell: Prague Rallies Against the Public-Media Bill

Hundreds of thousands of Czechs filled Prague’s Letná Park on Sunday — the ground where the Velvet Revolution gathered in 1989, two days before its anniversary — to protest Prime Minister Andrej Babiš’s plan to overhaul how public broadcasters are funded. Organisers from the Million Moments for Democracy movement put the crowd at 350,000 to 450,000, potentially the largest demonstration since the fall of communism; police published no estimate. The bill, pushed through its first reading at 4:36 a.m. on 24 October after the coalition limited opposition speaking time — the rally was named “Letná Against 4:36” in its honour — would abolish the household licence fees that fund Czech Television and Czech Radio and pay both broadcasters from the state budget instead, at roughly 15 percent less than they planned to collect. Protesters and the European Commission warn that whoever controls the budget line controls the broadcaster; public-media staff have been on strike since 3 November, backed by unions, universities and artists. Babiš rejects claims the reform is meant to exert political control. The bill still faces two more parliamentary readings, and organisers have invited the government to a public debate on 2 December.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Thailand — Phuket Fire16 November 2026

Eleven Die in Choking Smoke at Patong: A Transformer, a Fuel Pump, and 100 Motorcycles Waiting to Be Rented

Eleven people were killed and 16 injured when fire tore through four shophouses in Patong, on Thailand’s resort island of Phuket, in the early hours of Monday. The blaze began at about 3:15 a.m. on Phang Muang Sai Kor Road when, investigators say, a roadside transformer exploded during a storm and the flames reached a coin-operated fuel dispenser in front of a motorcycle rental business — where about 100 motorcycles were stored alongside petrol, engine oil, plastic and tyres. Firefighters took roughly two hours to bring the fire under control. Eight women and three men died, most from smoke, in rooms above the shops; the injured were Thai nationals and one person from Myanmar, and police said no tourists were among the dead or injured. Phuket’s police commander said CCTV footage and witnesses had been secured and arson ruled out; the governor said an initial investigation pointed to a short circuit on a substation pole. Thailand’s prime minister directed assistance for the injured and a full investigation into the cause — and into how a fire in a shop became so deadly for the people sleeping above it.

Latest · Technology · Space — Vulcan’s First Failure15 November 2026

Ten Minutes Into Flight, Twenty Years of Reliability Ended: A Vulcan Rocket Dies With 40 Amazon Satellites Aboard

United Launch Alliance lost a rocket on Sunday for the first time in the company’s 20-year history. Its Vulcan launcher — only the type’s fifth flight, and its first in nine months — lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 12:40 p.m. Eastern carrying 40 satellites for Amazon Leo, the company’s planned rival to SpaceX’s Starlink, then suffered an anomaly about ten minutes into flight during the Centaur upper stage’s burn and failed to reach orbit. The six solid boosters, the source of nozzle problems on two earlier Vulcan flights, performed normally this time; ULA, the Boeing–Lockheed Martin joint venture, said it did not yet know what went wrong and would investigate with Amazon before flying again. The loss is a schedule blow on both sides: it was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has bought to carry more than 1,500 Leo satellites, and Amazon has about 396 satellites in orbit against Starlink’s more than 11,100, with a regulatory deadline to deploy half its 3,232-satellite constellation by July 2028. Its next launch, an Ariane 6 carrying 36 satellites, is targeted for 23 November at the earliest. A lengthy investigation is expected to ground Vulcan for months.

Latest · Europe · Migration — The Vienna Announcement16 November 2026

Unveiled in Vienna: Five EU States Confirm the Rwanda Return Hub, a Pilot of About 100 Places

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands confirmed in Vienna on Monday what diplomats had briefed for days: a deal to establish a migrant return hub in Rwanda, the first deportation facility run by EU states outside the bloc. Rwanda, which said in August it had begun preliminary discussions with several European governments, sent a delegation to the Vienna migration conference for the announcement, and the European Commission welcomed the initiative while noting financing would in principle fall to the countries involved. The scheme starts as a pilot of roughly 100 places, each country sending around 20 people, with a target opening in 2027. Its legal basis is the EU’s new Returns Regulation, which speeds deportations and expressly permits return hubs in third countries. The design differs in one crucial respect from Britain’s abandoned Rwanda scheme, ruled unlawful by the UK Supreme Court in 2023: the European hub would take only people who have exhausted every legal avenue to remain in the EU, not asylum seekers with claims still pending. Rights groups have raised concerns about the model, which caps an EU push since 2024 to raise deportation numbers as arrivals fall.
