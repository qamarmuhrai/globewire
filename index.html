Latest · Africa · Ethiopia27 October 2026

At Least 60 Christians Killed in Two Attacks in Ethiopia's Oromia, Church Says — and Both Sides Blame Each Other

At least 60 people have been killed in two separate attacks in Ethiopia's conflict-hit Oromia region over the past week, according to the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, whose Holy Synod said on Monday that 14 believers were killed in Merti district on 20 October and 46 more in Sherka district on 24 October. The church said all the dead were Christians, that more than 20 people were hospitalised with severe injuries and that more than 20 homes were burned. It did not identify the attackers. The Oromia regional government accused a newly formed rebel alliance — which includes the Oromo Liberation Army and announced itself last month with the aim of overthrowing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government — of carrying out the killings to ignite religious conflict, but offered no evidence. A source linked to the alliance countered that the regional government itself was responsible, citing accounts from two local residents. The OLA, which officials have blamed for earlier attacks on Orthodox Christians, did not immediately comment. Conflict monitor ACLED has recorded a rising pattern of attacks on Orthodox Christians in Oromia since February 2023, in a region of some 40 million people already strained by insurgency and tensions between its Muslim, Protestant and Orthodox communities; residents told AFP at least five of the dead in Sherka were children, and many families have fled.

Latest · Africa · Sudan27 October 2026

The Lights Go Out Again: Drone Strikes on Power Stations Darken Sudan's East — Then Reach the Capital

Sudan's war came for the electricity grid twice in two days. On Monday, the state electricity company said paramilitary Rapid Support Forces drones struck power stations across the southeast — at Sinja, El Hawata, Al Fao and Mashkour, and at Al-Junaid in Gezira state — cutting power in Sennar, Gedaref, White Nile and Gezira, four states covering roughly half the territory the army holds, and threatening the stability of the whole national grid. Early on Tuesday, witnesses said a drone hit the Al-Markhiyat substation in northwestern Omdurman, knocking out electricity across large parts of Khartoum itself, with smoke seen rising from the facility and no official statement on responsibility by mid-morning. The RSF did not comment on either attack. The United Nations has repeatedly warned that strikes on power infrastructure endanger health and basic services for millions already facing the world's largest hunger crisis — nearly 20 million people in extreme hunger — and past outages after similar strikes have lasted days, spoiling refrigerated medicine and food. By a UN tally, drone strikes alone killed more than 300 people in the first three weeks of October, in a war between the army and its former ally that has run since April 2023, killed by some aid estimates more than 200,000 people and displaced nearly 13 million.

Latest · Africa · Gabon26 October 2026

Five Still Missing After a Drilling Rig Sank Off Gabon — 110 of the 115 Aboard Brought to Safety

Search teams were still looking on Monday for five people missing after the Prospector 5 jack-up rig sank off Gabon's southern coast on Saturday evening while preparing to drill on BW Energy's Dussafu Marine licence. Operator Borr Drilling said the rig suffered a "punch-through" during pre-loading operations at about 20:37 local time on 24 October — a failure in which one or more legs suddenly sink far deeper into the seabed than intended — lost stability, listed and went down with 115 people aboard. One hundred and ten were safely accounted for and brought ashore; the five missing are three Gabonese nationals, an Indian and a Croatian, according to the government in Libreville. Reuters, citing three sources, reported that one Indian worker had been found dead, but Gabonese authorities have not confirmed any death and continue to list five missing. Gabon's navy and three helicopters joined the search, with underwater equipment deployed at the site; Oil Minister Clotaire Kondja said no release of harmful substances had been observed, an inquiry has been opened, and pollution-response resources are guarding the coast near Mayumba National Park. Production at BW Energy's nearby MaBoMo facility was shut as a precaution, and shares in Borr Drilling, BW Energy and partner Panoro Energy all fell sharply in Oslo trading on Monday.

Latest · Americas · Brazil26 October 2026

Lula Wins a Historic Fourth Term by 4.4 Million Votes — and Flávio Bolsonaro, Like His Father, Refuses to Concede

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won an unprecedented fourth term in Sunday's runoff, defeating Senator Flávio Bolsonaro 51.79% to 48.21% — 64,256,232 votes to 59,826,041, a margin of about 4.43 million, according to the Superior Electoral Court. It is the first time since Brazil restored direct presidential elections in 1989 that the candidate who lost the first round has gone on to win the runoff: Bolsonaro had led the opening vote 47% to 45%. The defeated senator, eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro — now serving a 27-year sentence for plotting a coup after refusing to accept his own 2022 loss — said it was "impossible to recognize" the result and that he would seek its annulment, attacking a late-Sunday ruling by Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin that extended voting by two hours over alleged voter-transport irregularities in São Paulo and Bahia. The challenge is weakened by the facts that Bolsonaro's own campaign also requested an extension, citing heavy rain in the conservative south, and that electoral court president Kássio Nunes Marques — himself appointed by Jair Bolsonaro — confirmed the result and said the extension had not changed it. Analysts say annulment is very unlikely, though protests by Bolsonaro supporters are possible. The 80-year-old Lula begins his new term on 5 January 2027 facing a Congress where the Liberal Party holds its largest bench in decades; markets that had bet on a Bolsonaro win sold off sharply, with a Tokyo-listed Brazil fund down nearly 15% in Asian trade, and President Trump, asked about the vote on Monday, called it "rigged."

Latest · Middle East · Israel27 October 2026

Election Day in Israel: 7.3 Million Eligible Voters, 37 Lists, and Still No Bloc Anywhere Near 61 Seats

Voting is under way across Israel in the first Knesset election since the 7 October 2023 attacks, with 7,331,981 eligible voters, 12,018 polling stations and some 100,000 election workers, in a ballot costed at 690 million shekels (about $223 million). Polls opened at 7 a.m. and close at 10 p.m., when exit polls will be published; turnout among overseas voters reached 86.7%. Thirty-seven parties and lists are competing, and under Israel's proportional system a party needs 3.25% of valid votes to enter the 120-seat parliament and 61 seats to govern. All three final television polls put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on 50 seats, the opposition on 52 to 56 counting Arab parties separately — short of a majority either way — with former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar leading Likud in every final survey. Eisenkot opened the day by invoking the moment the 2023 assault began — "06:29. Remember this hour when you go to vote" — and promising to replace "the government of October 7th," while Netanyahu projected a strongman image and claimed President Trump would act against Iran again only if he stayed in power, a claim Eisenkot called fear-mongering. Counting runs overnight, official results are expected on 4 November, and Netanyahu's caretaker government remains in place while the president gives one leader 42 days to assemble a coalition.

Syria Shuts Its Only Crossing With Jordan After Jordanian Jets Strike the South — and the Two Capitals Tell Opposite Stories

Syria closed the Nassib crossing on Tuesday, suspending all passenger and commercial traffic with Jordan, a day after Jordanian warplanes struck what Amman called militant hideouts in southern Syria in response to Friday's church funeral attack in Karak that killed nine people. Syria's Civil Defence said seven people were recovered from four sites in rural Daraa and Sweida — three men, a woman and three children — including a family of five killed in a tent in the village of Al-Tai, while some Syrian reports put the toll at eight. The diplomatic row is over coordination: Jordan's armed forces said the operation was carried out “in coordination with the Syrian government,” but Damascus said it had conveyed its “complete reservation” over strikes carried out without prior notice, announced an investigation, and stressed the need for field coordination in border areas. Jordan said it had linked suspects in the church attack — three of whom were arrested after a shootout in Karak on Monday — to terrorist groups operating across the border. Nassib, known as Jaber on the Jordanian side, is the two countries' only passenger and commercial crossing and a key overland trade route, and no reopening date has been given.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine27 October 2026

A Small Silence in Kherson: The Oleshky Ceasefire Takes Effect as Russia Halts Strikes to Let Aid In

Russia and Ukraine began a localised ceasefire early on Tuesday around the occupied town of Oleshky in southern Kherson, a rare pause agreed on 23 October with the involvement of US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo. Russia's defence ministry said shelling towards Kherson city stopped at 01:00 GMT to prepare for a humanitarian mission later this week, and Ukraine's human-rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets confirmed Kyiv was honouring the arrangement. Up to 2,000 people remain in Oleshky, down from 24,000 before the war, cut off by mines and drones and facing acute shortages of food, medicine and water after years of occupation and the flooding that followed the 2023 destruction of the Kakhovka Dam. The truce is due to run through Saturday, with doctors, supplies and water to go in before evacuation corridors open. It changes little elsewhere: a Russian missile attack on Kyiv on Monday killed at least one person and injured ten, including students in a dormitory, with further deaths reported in Kharkiv and Pavlohrad and fresh explosions heard in the capital on Tuesday morning. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow was open to more local humanitarian truces but would not slow its wider offensive, as technical talks involving Ukraine, Russia and the United States are planned for the first half of November in Abu Dhabi.

Behind Closed Doors in Beijing: Xi Opens a Four-Day Party Plenum on Discipline, With a Fourth Term on the Horizon

China's Communist Party began its fifth plenum on Monday, a closed-door, four-day meeting of the Central Committee in Beijing that state media say is centred on "full and rigorous party self-governance" — tightening discipline, fighting corruption and making sure cadres actually implement Beijing's orders. President Xi Jinping opened the session by presenting the draft decision, with roughly 300 members in attendance and few details expected before the meeting ends on Thursday. The stakes are as much about personnel as policy. Xi's anti-corruption campaign has purged thousands of officials over 14 years and has lately reached his own appointees: two more Politburo members have fallen, leaving the body at 21, and the Central Military Commission has shrunk from seven members at the start of Xi's third term to just two — Xi himself and General Zhang Shengmin, who oversaw the purges of his peers. The Asia Society Policy Institute estimates attendance could be the lowest on record, with dozens of full and alternate members absent. Analysts see the plenum as the last major party gathering before the 2027 congress, where the 73-year-old Xi is widely expected to secure a historic fourth five-year term and oversee a leadership reshuffle; state media trailed the meeting with a January speech in which Xi railed against "two-faced" officials who comply outwardly while resisting inwardly.

Cyclone Arnab Closes In on Andhra: Gusts Now Forecast at 150 km/h, 89,000 People Moved to Relief Camps

Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab was about 220 km from Visakhapatnam on Tuesday morning and closing on India's east coast, with the India Meteorological Department raising its landfall forecast to sustained winds of 125–135 km/h and gusts up to 150 km/h when the storm crosses near Yanam, between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, by Wednesday forenoon. Preparations have escalated sharply: nearly 89,000 people along the Kakinada coast are being moved into 101 relief camps, Odisha plans to evacuate 48,000 more, schools are closed, fishing is banned and at least 61 trains — most in the Godavari–Krishna delta — have been cancelled, while Visakhapatnam airport has seen flights cancelled and diverted and Air India has warned of disruption at Visakhapatnam and Vijayawada. Rain and strong winds are already lashing north coastal Andhra Pradesh and parts of Odisha, with heavy rain also forecast for West Bengal and Jharkhand over the next three days. Arnab, the first cyclonic storm of the 2026 North Indian Ocean season, is also expected to drag moisture northeast after landfall: Nepal's forecasters expect cloud and rain to build from Wednesday, with the heaviest falls on Thursday and Friday and Himalayan snowfall at altitude.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Pakistan Economy27 October 2026

Petrol Up Again in Pakistan — and Markets Ordered Shut by 9 p.m. as Austerity Returns

Pakistan's government raised fuel prices again from Tuesday under its new daily pricing mechanism: petrol rose Rs1.54 to Rs379.25 per litre and high-speed diesel Rs2.16 to Rs395.82, with kerosene up Rs4.21 to Rs330.96 and light diesel oil up Rs7.78 to Rs275.24, according to the Petroleum Division notification. The daily system, introduced on 17 July, sets prices from a rolling seven-day average of international rates — which has kept pump prices hostage to the Middle East conflict: diesel, Rs281 a litre before the regional escalation in late February, peaked at a record Rs520.35 on 3 April, and petrol touched Rs458.41 in March. With levies still running at about Rs114 per litre on petrol and Rs100 on diesel, Islamabad has revived a package of austerity and conservation measures: commercial markets must close by 9 p.m., and fuel allocations for official government vehicles have been halved for an initial three months. A proposed relief scheme would return Rs100 per litre to riders of motorcycles and three-wheelers and owners of cars up to 800cc — roughly 11.8 million people — worth up to Rs2,000 a month for two- and three-wheeler users and Rs3,000 for small-car owners. The fuel rise lands a day after the central bank lifted its policy rate to 12%, citing energy-driven inflation back above 10%.
