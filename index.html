Oil Climbs Above $114 a Barrel as Houthi Attacks Keep Middle East Supply Risks High

Oil extended its rally on Friday, with Brent crude rising $1.30, or 1.2 percent, to $114.47 a barrel by 0639 GMT and US West Texas Intermediate up $1.37, or 1.4 percent, to $100.84. Brent, which closed Thursday at its highest level since May 4, is heading for a third straight weekly gain, while WTI is on course for a second. Traders are pricing in a growing chance that fighting involving Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi movement will disrupt exports, after Saudi Aramco evacuated its headquarters in Dhahran on Thursday as clashes intensified near Bab el-Mandeb, the narrow gateway to the Red Sea. ING analysts said markets are increasingly worried about a renewed escalation in the Persian Gulf that could threaten flows that have so far held up, warning that even the possibility of escalation is likely to keep a sizable risk premium in prices. In Libya, output was also trimmed after a pipeline valve linking the Hamada and Tahara fields to Zawiya port was shut, the head of the National Oil Corporation told Reuters.

Tech Selloff Deepens as AI Spending Doubts Hit Wall Street and Drag Asian Markets Lower

A sharp retreat from technology shares spread from Wall Street into Asia on Friday as investors questioned whether the huge sums pouring into artificial intelligence will deliver returns fast enough. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6 percent to 26,009 on Thursday, its worst day since June, while the S&P 500 dropped 1.2 percent to 7,663 and the Philadelphia semiconductor index plunged 6.7 percent. The Dow Jones Industrial Average was little changed, underscoring how concentrated the selling was in AI-linked names. In Asia, Tokyo's Nikkei 225 was down 1.7 percent at the break, Shanghai fell 1.8 percent and Hong Kong eased 0.2 percent, even as higher oil prices added to inflation worries. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell sharply despite rising crude, a sign that investors were moving into bonds for safety, while gold slipped below $4,000 intraday before recovering to around $4,029. Analysts said two pillars of the recent rally — optimism about AI profits and confidence that consumers can withstand higher energy costs — are now being tested at the same time.

Two Killed Near Hebron and 36 Arrested as Attacks on Palestinian Farmers Surge During Olive Harvest

Violence rose sharply in the occupied West Bank on Friday, with the Israeli military saying it killed two suspected attackers after a shooting near the Judea Junction area near Hebron and imposed a cordon while searches continued. In separate raids, Israeli forces arrested three more Palestinians in Nablus and Jerusalem — in the village of Burin, the town of Awarta and the Qalandiya refugee camp — as part of a wider sweep that Al Jazeera put at 36 arrests. The Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission said it documented 17 attacks by Israeli forces and settlers on Thursday alone against Palestinian farmers and their land during the olive harvest, across Hebron, Qalqilya, Nablus, Ramallah and el-Bireh, Jenin and Salfit. It said the incidents included assaults on farmers, blocking access to groves, theft of crops and equipment, burning and damage to olive trees, threats at gunpoint and live fire. The harvest attacks drew international attention this week after US Democratic congressional candidate Brad Lander was briefly detained on Wednesday while volunteering to help Palestinian farmers pick olives.

Latest · Africa · Health23 October 2026

Ebola Reaches Nigeria as WHO Warns of a 'Complex' Outbreak Spreading Across Africa

The World Health Organization warned on Thursday that Africa faces a complex Ebola outbreak after the virus spread from east Africa to Nigeria, the first confirmed case in the west of the continent. Nigeria announced on Tuesday an imported case of the Bundibugyo strain — the same strain driving a major outbreak in Congo — detected in the wife of a diplomat who had travelled from Uganda to the capital, Abuja. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate said on Thursday that contacts of the woman had been traced since her arrival earlier this month and that an undisclosed number of people judged to be at high risk have been placed in full quarantine in a facility. Nigeria, Africa's most populous nation, is now the fourth country affected by the current outbreak, raising the challenge for health teams that must track travel links across a wide region while screening continues at airports, including Lagos's Murtala Muhammed International Airport.

Student Protests Spread Across Eight European Countries Over Rents, Schools and a Bleak Future

Young people have taken to the streets in at least eight European countries in recent days, in a wave of protests with different local demands but a shared anxiety about the future. In Spain and Portugal, students pitched tents in the capitals to protest high rents. In France and Italy, large marches for better school conditions were marred in places by looting and clashes, including a demonstrator kicking back a tear gas canister in Milan's Piazza Duomo on October 17. Greek students pressed for more university funding, while young Albanians clashed with police after joining broader anti-corruption demonstrations against a government they accuse of blocking their prospects. The unrest has prompted travel warnings beyond Europe: Thailand advised its citizens to postpone non-essential travel to protest-hit parts of Europe, and Malaysia warned its nationals to be cautious in France, as did China earlier in the week after town halls and shops were looted and set on fire.

Indonesia Seizes 1.16 Tonnes of Ketamine Worth $193 Million Hidden on a Tanker

Indonesian authorities have seized 1.16 tonnes of ketamine found in 58 sacks aboard a Mongolian-flagged tanker intercepted earlier this week off Tanjung Balai in North Sumatra, national police chief Suyudi Ario Seto said on Friday. Police and customs officers arrested eight people — seven Indonesians and one Taiwanese national — including the captain, engineers and an oiler. The haul has an estimated street value of 3.48 trillion rupiah, about $192.6 million. Seto said the vessel had sailed from Pasir Gudang in Malaysia, crossed the Malacca Strait and headed toward the Andaman Sea, where investigators believe it received the drugs in a ship-to-ship transfer. Officials said they acted after noticing irregularities in the tanker's Automatic Identification System tracking. Seto said the scale of the shipment showed how destructive international syndicates are to national security. Indonesia enforces some of the world's strictest drug laws.

New Zealand's Top Military Officer Resigns Over an Undisclosed Relationship While in Command

The chief of New Zealand's Defence Force, Tony Davies, resigned on Friday after acknowledging an inappropriate relationship from his time in a position of unit command that he had not disclosed. In a statement released by the Defence Force, Davies said he had informed Defence Minister Paul Goldsmith of his decision, describing the resignation as being for personal reasons and the result of errors of judgement for which he was taking responsibility. He said the matters revolved around a relationship he had while in unit command and that he regretted not disclosing it when he had the opportunity. Goldsmith said he agreed Davies's position was no longer tenable, citing integrity matters that had not previously been disclosed, and said he would recommend that the Governor-General accept the resignation. Vice Chief Mat Williams will serve as acting chief. Davies joined the military in 1984 and was appointed to the top job in 2024.

China Clears DeepSeek and MiniMax AI Models for Global Use as Export Controls Tighten

Two of China's leading artificial intelligence developers, DeepSeek and MiniMax, have received state approval for the global use of their AI models, according to a post on Thursday by an account affiliated with state broadcaster CCTV, Yuyuan Tantian. A follow-up post on Friday said overseas internet users had urged other Chinese open-source large language models to seek the same clearance. Neither company immediately commented. The approvals come a week after China, on October 16, proposed export controls on 23 AI technologies, including models, agents and computing clusters. The commerce ministry said at the time that most AI technologies would remain exportable, with simplified licensing for open-source exports and for Chinese firms running their own computing centres overseas. The proposed controls cover overseas deployment of models containing restricted technology and are Beijing's latest step to limit the transfer of tools it views as vital to national security amid intensifying technological competition with Washington.

Volvo Cars Swings to a Quarterly Loss and Plans 2,500 Job Cuts as China and US Demand Weakens

Volvo Cars reported an operating loss for the third quarter on Friday and said it will cut about 2,500 jobs, as a slow production ramp-up for its new EX60 electric SUV and weak demand in China and the United States weighed on sales. The Swedish automaker, listed as VOLCARb.ST, had been counting on the EX60 to lift its electric lineup, but delays in getting production to full speed coincided with softer orders in two of its most important markets. The job reductions are the latest sign of pressure across Europe's car industry, where makers face high energy and borrowing costs, intense competition from Chinese electric-vehicle brands, and uneven consumer demand for EVs. Volvo did not use Friday's update to change the broader strategic direction, but the swing into loss underlines how quickly a model delay can hit earnings when market conditions are already fragile.

New York Votes to End Its 160-Year Horse-Carriage Era, Naming the Law for a Teen Killed in Central Park

New York City Council voted 40 to 7 on Thursday to ban horse-drawn carriages, phasing out a tourist tradition dating to the 1800s by June 2028. The law, known as Romanch's Law, is named for 18-year-old Indian tourist Romanch Mahajan, who was killed in June after a carriage horse bolted in Central Park. Council Speaker Julie Menin said the vote reflected a commitment to animal welfare, public safety and protecting workers. Mayor Zohran Mamdani supports the measure and is expected to sign it; he said earlier this month that the industry was coming to an end in the city. The plan gives the roughly 150 active drivers time to find new work, includes job referrals and possible compensation for licence holders, and bars owners from selling the nearly 200 horses for slaughter or to other carriage businesses. The Transport Workers Union has vowed to challenge the ban in court, while President Donald Trump called the carriages a great tradition that will be missed. PETA said it would donate $50,000 toward care for retired horses.
