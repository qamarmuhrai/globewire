Latest · Asia-Pacific · Afghanistan — Kunar Blast28 November 2026

Blast Beside School in Afghanistan’s Kunar Kills at Least 10 Students, Injures About 110

At least 10 students were killed and around 110 people injured when an explosion tore through a house next to a middle school in Asadabad, the capital of eastern Kunar province, at about 10am local time on Saturday, Afghanistan’s Interior Ministry said. The house in the Andar Ser area was storing dynamite intended for mining, and ministry spokesman Mufti Abdul Mateen Qani said initial findings point to negligence and carelessness as the cause. Kunar’s director of information and culture, Mawlawi Sami-ul-Haq, confirmed the location and timing to Al Jazeera. Injured children and other victims were taken to hospital in Asadabad for treatment. The blast is among the deadliest incidents involving schoolchildren in Afghanistan this year and has renewed attention on the unsafe storage of explosives in residential areas near schools.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — MSF Clinic Strike28 November 2026

Israeli Strike on Doctors Without Borders Clinic in Gaza City Kills Two Watchmen

Two staff members of Doctors Without Borders, known by its French initials MSF, were killed when an Israeli strike hit one of the organisation’s clinics in Gaza City, the aid group said. Both victims worked as watchmen. In a statement on X, MSF said it was devastated and outraged by what it described as a direct hit on its facility. World Health Organization chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus condemned the killing of health and aid workers as outrageous and unacceptable. The strike came in a week when Israeli attacks across Gaza killed several more Palestinians, including an earlier strike that killed Adel al-Helou, who had worked for 35 years for UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees. MSF has repeatedly called for its medical facilities and staff to be protected under international humanitarian law.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan — Local Elections28 November 2026

Taiwan Votes for Mayors and County Chiefs as China Sends Warplanes and Rebukes Taipei Candidate

Voters across all 22 of Taiwan’s cities and counties chose mayors, county chiefs and thousands of local officials on Saturday in elections widely seen as a gauge of support ahead of the 2028 presidential vote. Polls closed at 4pm local time, with results expected in the evening. Campaigning centred on local concerns such as transport and the environment, but Beijing weighed in repeatedly, denouncing Puma Shen, the governing Democratic Progressive Party’s candidate for mayor of Taipei and an expert on information warfare. Taiwan’s defence ministry said it detected 30 Chinese military aircraft around the island on Friday, 26 of which crossed the Taiwan Strait median line in a joint combat readiness patrol with warships. China, which claims Taiwan as its own, called reunification an unstoppable trend and did not respond to requests for comment on the patrol. The closest race was in Taipei, where incumbent Chiang Wan-an of the Kuomintang faced Shen; the KMT won 14 cities and counties to the DPP’s five at the last local elections four years ago.

Latest · Americas · Haiti — Salvadoran Deployment28 November 2026

A Month Into Bukele’s Haiti Deployment, Salvadoran Troops Win Praise but Few Territorial Gains

One month after El Salvador’s President Nayib Bukele sent more than 2,000 soldiers into Haiti, many Haitians have welcomed the intervention as a rare attempt to confront gang warfare, but security experts say the force has yet to secure and hold territory. Bukele launched the deployment on 18 October under a bilateral arrangement outside the UN mandate, saying it aimed to recover four missing Salvadoran technicians; Salvadoran forces have since carried out airstrikes well beyond where the men disappeared, and neither government has published the terms of their security agreement. The US-based monitoring group ACLED recorded 317 deaths between 18 October and 20 November as clashes between security forces and gangs intensified, including at least 10 civilians killed by gangs in mid-November in Croix-des-Bouquets, near where the technicians vanished. Gangs still control more than 80 percent of Port-au-Prince and key road checkpoints. Haiti has also announced plans for Salvadoran-style prisons after its security minister visited one of El Salvador’s incarceration facilities.

Latest · Europe · United Kingdom — DWP Cyber Incident28 November 2026

UK Benefits and Pension Services Stay Offline Through Weekend After DWP Cyber Incident

Online services for Britain’s Department for Work and Pensions remained unavailable on Saturday after the department took them offline on Thursday evening following suspicious activity, in what Downing Street called a live and ongoing incident. Claimants have been unable to sign in or make new claims for universal credit, personal independence payments and state pensions online, and the disruption is expected to last most of the weekend while systems are checked and restored, with telephone lines set up for urgent new claims. The DWP, which serves around 20 million people and makes about 2.5 million payments a day, stressed that regular benefit and pension payments are unaffected and will be paid as normal, and that people directly affected will not face sanctions. The department is investigating with the National Cyber Security Centre, part of GCHQ, and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre. Downing Street said there is currently no evidence of data loss and no sign other departments were targeted, but it could not rule out a malicious actor. The incident came weeks after the National Audit Office warned of a high risk of a breach disrupting the department.

Latest · Americas · United States — Thanksgiving Storm Cleanup28 November 2026

US East Coast Cleans Up After Thanksgiving Storm as Power Returns and Delayed Parade Is Rescheduled

Rain and flooding eased along the US East Coast on Friday and Saturday as crews restored power to hundreds of thousands of customers after a holiday storm system brought heavy wind, rain and snow from the Midwest to New England. Outages that peaked above 900,000 on Thursday evening had fallen to about 264,000 by Friday evening, with Pennsylvania and Missouri the hardest hit, according to poweroutage.us. The storms downed trees and power lines, forced water rescues from New York to Virginia, and disrupted one of the busiest travel periods of the year, with about 1,600 flight delays and more than 500 cancellations still reported on Friday. In New York City, preparations were under way for Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to take place on Saturday after an unprecedented postponement because of rain and high winds; Central Park recorded its wettest Thanksgiving on record. Coastal flood warnings remained in place from New Jersey to Virginia, record tides flooded parts of the California coast from Humboldt County to Ventura County, and forecasters warned the slow-moving system would take time to weaken, with lingering showers and isolated freezing precipitation into Saturday.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Inflation Outlook28 November 2026

India Warns Weather and Supply Disruptions Could Push Inflation Higher in Coming Months

Inflation in India could accelerate in the months ahead because of weather and supply-related uncertainties, the government said in its monthly economic review released on Saturday, even as moderating oil prices and a stronger rupee may help contain price pressures. The report pointed to festivities extending into November and the start of the wedding season as factors likely to raise demand and lift both food and non-food costs in the near term. It also warned that continuing conflict in the Middle East could keep oil import bills and maritime freight rates volatile, while an uncertain global trade environment could weigh on India’s external sector. The assessment balances near-term price risks against some relief from energy markets and currency strength, and it will be watched closely ahead of the central bank’s next policy decisions.

Latest · Americas · Mexico — Minimum Wage Debate27 November 2026

Mexico’s Popular Wage Strategy Faces Its Strongest Business Pushback as Double-Digit Rise Is Debated

A government proposal to raise Mexico’s minimum wage by double digits again next year has drawn the strongest resistance yet from business leaders, who are seeking a far more moderate increase and warning that another large hike could fuel inflation, erode competitiveness and weaken formal employment. The debate exposes a central tension for President Claudia Sheinbaum and her ruling Morena party, whose popularity rests in part on economic policies that have lifted millions out of poverty, including double-digit wage increases every year since Morena came to power in 2018. The minimum wage has nearly quadrupled over that period to 9,451 pesos a month, about $480 — the largest increase in Latin America, according to official figures. Two sources told Reuters the government-run National Minimum Wage Commission is considering an increase similar to this year’s, while Coparmex, a leading business lobby, has proposed a rise of up to two percentage points above inflation, which ran close to 4 percent in the first half of November. Talks are due to conclude early next week.

Latest · Africa · Sudan — Khartoum Cleanup28 November 2026

Khartoum Residents Join 50-Day Cleanup as Rubbish and Disease Risks Overwhelm War-Battered Capital

Residents returning to Khartoum are joining a government-led 50-day sanitation campaign as accumulated rubbish, blocked drainage and rubble threaten public health across Sudan’s capital. The war has created one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises, with about nine million people displaced nationwide, yet some 2.88 million people had returned to Khartoum State by late October — the highest return figure of any state. Returnees such as Musa Abdelrahman, 54, who came back in March through a government repatriation scheme, say the waste problem now shapes daily life, creating breeding grounds for flies, mosquitoes and rodents that raise the risk of disease. The Khartoum State Cleaning Corporation says 90 percent of its sanitation and waste transport fleet has been lost to theft or damage during the war. Japan and the Japan International Cooperation Agency, working with UN-Habitat, announced a $4.5 million project on 4 November to help clear rubble in Khartoum State.

Latest · Africa · Kenya — Tanzanian Activist Missing28 November 2026

Disappearance of Tanzanian Activist in Kenya Raises Fears of Cross-Border Repression

The whereabouts of Tanzanian opposition activist Twaha Salim Mwaipaya remain unknown more than a month after he was seized from a taxi in Kamulu, in Kenya’s Machakos County, on 25 October, according to the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights. Unidentified individuals in three vehicles, including a Toyota Land Cruiser Hardtop, forcibly removed him, Amnesty International reported. The International Federation for Human Rights and the World Organisation Against Torture have described the case as an enforced disappearance and called for an independent investigation, including into possible cross-border involvement; who ordered the abduction has not been established. Mwaipaya, a former youth organiser for Tanzania’s opposition party Chadema, fled to Kenya in 2025 citing threats to his safety. Kenya’s inspector general of police, Douglas Kanja, appeared before the High Court on 3 November to account for the activist’s whereabouts, and the case returned to court on 23 November. The disappearance echoes the January 2025 abduction in Nairobi of Tanzanian critic Maria Sarungi Tsehai, who escaped, and has renewed questions about Kenya’s duty to protect people fleeing political persecution.
