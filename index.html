Latest · Middle East · Qatar19 October 2026

Qatar to Announce a Defence Production Hub and a New Regional Security Partnership

Qatar will soon unveil a domestic defence production hub and a new partnership with regional allies aimed at strengthening protection against ballistic missiles, drones and cyberattacks, its minister of state for defence affairs, Sheikh Saoud bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, told the Global Security Forum in Doha on Monday. He said Qatar is working with a regional partner alongside the United States, the United Kingdom and France, both to build local protection and to invest in defence companies, but gave no further details. The move marks a shift for a country that has traditionally relied on foreign procurement and external security guarantees. The Iran war hit Qatar hard: it was forced to shut much of its main LNG complex at Ras Laffan early in the fighting and suffered from the disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

Latest · Middle East · Israel18 October 2026

Trump Gives Netanyahu His “Complete and Total Endorsement” Nine Days Before Israel’s Election

US President Donald Trump on Sunday formally endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for re-election, ending months of hesitation in a relationship strained by disagreements over Gaza, Lebanon and the Iran war. In a Truth Social post addressed to “the Great People of Israel,” Trump called Netanyahu “Strong, Brave, and Brilliant” and a “Great Warrior Prime Minister.” Israel votes on October 27 in its first national election since the Hamas attack of October 7, 2023, and polls suggest a tight race with no candidate holding a clear path to a governing coalition. Netanyahu quickly thanked Trump and said re-election would let them “eliminate the Iranian threat” together, while his main rival, Gadi Eisenkot, responded that only Israeli citizens will decide who leads the country.

Latest · Americas · United States19 October 2026

Vance Concedes “Mistakes and Fundamental Failures” in the US Handling of the Iran War

US Vice President JD Vance has acknowledged “a lot of mistakes and a lot of fundamental failures” in Washington’s handling of the Iran war, during a CBS News town hall where he faced pointed questions about the conflict’s human and economic costs. He was responding to Joey Amor, whose wife, Master Sgt. Nicole Amor, was among six US Army Reserve soldiers killed by an Iranian strike on a military site in Kuwait in the war’s opening hours; at least 21 US service members have been killed and hundreds wounded so far. Vance admitted that where troops had been positioned was “a huge mistake” that has since been corrected, said information sometimes “doesn’t filter up” to him, and pointed to cheap drones as a threat that has forced changes in US tactics. Pressed on why victory has not been decisive, he replied that the US had “in some ways … won decisively,” but added: “The enemy gets a vote here.”

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Indonesia19 October 2026

Rescuers Race to Board Capsized Indonesian Ferry as 129 People Remain Missing in the Java Sea

Hundreds of Indonesian rescuers, backed by the navy and coast guard, intensified the search on Monday for 129 people still missing after the passenger ship Virgo Transport 8 capsized in the Java Sea, with six deaths confirmed so far. The vessel, carrying 243 people — 213 passengers and 30 crew — lost contact early on Sunday in bad weather while sailing from Surabaya in East Java to Banjarmasin in South Kalimantan; 108 people have been rescued. The national rescue agency has deployed at least 622 personnel, 17 ships and five helicopters and aircraft, but high waves and strong winds are hampering efforts, and rescuers have struggled to get inside the capsized, partially submerged ship. Its captain had reported the vessel listing in waves of up to three metres before contact was lost, about 148 kilometres from Banjarmasin.

Latest · Europe · Vatican City18 October 2026

Pope Leo Pledges a Gradual Return to Duties in His First Public Event Since Cancer Surgery

Pope Leo XIV greeted some 15,000 faithful in St Peter’s Square on Sunday in his first public appearance since surgery for lung cancer, promising to “gradually resume my activities, always with the help of God and your prayers.” The 71-year-old pontiff, discharged from Rome’s Gemelli hospital on Saturday, underwent an operation on October 13 to remove the lower lobe of his right lung after doctors discovered a small nodule; tests confirmed adenocarcinoma, the most common form of lung cancer, and analysis of the tumour and lymph nodes is continuing to determine whether further treatment is needed. The Vatican has cancelled the pope’s other public appearances until at least Wednesday. His lead surgeon said there is no clinical reason to cancel Leo’s planned November 6–17 trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, though the Vatican’s secretary of state, Cardinal Pietro Parolin, cautioned that the pace of his schedule may have to adjust to his health.

Latest · Europe · Albania19 October 2026

Albania’s Prime Minister Refuses to Quit Amid Violent Student-Led Protests

Albanian police fired water cannon, tear gas and rubber bullets in Tirana on Sunday as anti-government protests entered a sixth day and Prime Minister Edi Rama rejected demands to resign. The unrest, driven by anger over poor university conditions, high tuition fees and corruption, saw protesters set fire to a police station and the entrance of the interior ministry, smash windows at the ruling Socialist Party’s headquarters and hurl rocks, Molotov cocktails and fireworks; 28 people were reported injured on Sunday, most of them police officers. Rama said on Monday that 295 people had been injured over the course of the protests, blamed the violence on “an outside hand,” and said his government is ready to talk but will not negotiate with those who commit violence. The US embassy urged restraint and calm on all sides, while Rama warned the disorder threatens Albania’s hopes of joining the European Union.

Latest · Americas · Brazil19 October 2026

Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro Trade Personal Attacks in a Fiery First Run-Off Debate

Brazil’s President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro clashed in a chaotic first run-off debate on Sunday, a week before the October 25 vote that will decide the presidency. The two men repeatedly called each other liars, traded corruption accusations and sparred over public security and healthcare, with Lula branding his rival a “habitual liar” and a “militiaman,” and Bolsonaro — son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year sentence over efforts to overturn the 2022 election — retorting that Lula has corruption “in your DNA.” Bolsonaro led the first round with 47% to Lula’s 45%, and polls show him favourite but the race narrowing. Both campaigns are courting the 33 million voters — about one in five of the electorate — who stayed home in the first round. A second debate is scheduled for October 23.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Philippines19 October 2026

Philippines Summons China’s Ambassador Over His “We Could Easily Win” Remark

The Philippines’ Department of Foreign Affairs summoned Chinese Ambassador Jing Quan on Monday to convey “serious concern and strong objection” to his remark that China could “easily win” if it chose to use force — comments he paired with an assurance that Beijing “will not use force” because it “loves peace.” The foreign ministry had already publicly rebuked Jing last week, calling his words a “thinly veiled threat,” and President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said the statement “sounds very much like a threat” and was not diplomatic. The row has escalated quickly: one senator advised Jing on Monday to simply go home rather than wait to be declared persona non grata, while the Chinese embassy warned that Philippine officials would not be welcome in China if the ambassador is expelled. The dispute comes amid repeated confrontations between the two countries in the South China Sea.

Rubio Denies Any Pause in US Intelligence Sharing With Ukraine as the Sanctions Waiver Row Deepens

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking to reporters in Uzbekistan on Monday, denied that Washington had paused intelligence sharing with Ukraine, contradicting President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who said in an interview aired Sunday that the US blocked critical intelligence for three days during Russian ballistic missile attacks before restoring it. Despite Trump’s demands that Kyiv stop striking Russian energy infrastructure, Ukraine launched one of its largest drone attacks of the year on Russia over the weekend, hitting an oil terminal in St Petersburg, according to Russian authorities.

Latest · Europe · Greece19 October 2026

Twelve Migrants Die in Two Aegean Incidents, Including a Collision With a Patrol Boat off Lesbos

Twelve migrants have died in the Aegean Sea in two separate incidents as they tried to reach Europe from Turkey, according to Greek authorities and Euronews reporting. Off the island of Lesbos, a speedboat carrying migrants collided with a coast guard patrol vessel and overturned early on Monday, killing six people — an infant and four women among them — while eight others were rescued; the Greek government has vowed “zero tolerance” toward migrant smugglers. Near Samos, a small boat capsized, killing six people — five men and a woman — while 14 were rescued, including the alleged organiser of the crossing, said to be from Iran. According to the International Organization for Migration, at least 1,209 migrants have died or gone missing in the Mediterranean so far in 2026.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India18 October 2026

Twelve Killed as Runaway Truck Ploughs Into Devotees Outside a Temple in Madhya Pradesh

At least 12 people were killed and dozens injured when a truck loaded with paddy lost control on a steep road in Gwalior, in India’s central Madhya Pradesh state, broke through police barricades and crashed into the outer wall and gate of the Sheetla Mata Temple in the early hours of Sunday, police said. Senior police official Dharmveer Singh said 39 people were taken to hospital, of whom 12 died; five or six patients were critically injured with fractures, and two police officers on duty were also hurt. The crash happened at around 1:30 a.m. on a stretch where heavy vehicles are restricted, during the Navratri festival period when large numbers of pilgrims were travelling to the temple. Local officials said an investigation is under way into how the truck entered the restricted road and what caused it to veer off course.
