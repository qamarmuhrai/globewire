Latest · Asia-Pacific · Markets — Circuit Breaker in Seoul6 November 2026

The Rout Crosses the Pacific: KOSPI Crashes 8.5%, Nikkei Falls 6%

Wall Street’s AI sell-off hit Asia with full force on Friday morning. South Korea’s KOSPI plunged 8.55 per cent within minutes of the open, falling to 4,923.47 — below the 5,000 mark for the first time in nine months — and triggering a first-stage circuit breaker that halted all stock and derivatives trading for 20 minutes from 9:03 a.m. local time. The index had already lost 5.28 per cent on Thursday. The memory-chip giants at the heart of the AI trade took the worst of it: Samsung Electronics fell about 10 per cent to 181,400 won and SK hynix dropped 11.7 per cent to 1,116,000 won, extending Thursday’s slides of 7.6 and 9.5 per cent. Foreign investors led the exit. In Tokyo the Nikkei 225 lost about 6.2 per cent, breaching 60,000, with Kioxia down 14 per cent and Advantest sharply lower; Taiwan’s market fell more than 8 per cent and TSMC about 8.5 per cent. Traders blame the same fear that sank New York on Thursday — that the new Democratic majority in Washington will tighten oversight of the AI data-centre build-out just as valuations peak. The Nasdaq 100’s 6.4 per cent Thursday fall erased some $2.3 trillion in value, its worst session since March 2020.

Latest · Americas · Paraguay — President Resigns6 November 2026

A Bank Falls, a President Follows: Peña Quits as Ueno Collapse Engulfs Paraguay

Paraguay’s President Santiago Peña resigned on Thursday, undone in days by the collapse of a digital bank he was once tied to. In a video posted on X, the 47-year-old former IMF economist said he was stepping down to preserve the country’s stability, social peace and democratic institutions. The trigger was Ueno Bank: a fintech that grew in under five years into a household name claiming some 3 million customers — close to half the population of 6.5 million — and administering about $850 million in public funds. The central bank seized it on Tuesday after restated accounts pushed its solvency ratio to 5.56 per cent, less than half the 12 per cent minimum, freezing customers out of their money and sending protesters into the streets. Eight senior executives, including founder Miguel Vázquez and bank president Juan Manuel Gustale, have been detained. Peña, a business partner in the Vázquez group until March 2025, faced impeachment accusations of conflicts of interest, misuse of pension funds and cover-ups; minutes after his announcement, lawmakers voted unanimously to open impeachment proceedings anyway, while sparing Vice President Pedro Alliana, who is set to succeed him for the rest of the term to 2028 if Congress accepts the resignation. The guaraní fell nearly 5 per cent on the day, past 6,500 to the dollar.

Ukraine diplomacy moved on three tracks at once on Thursday. In Komyshuvakha, President Zelenskyy put the confirmed toll from Monday’s bombing of the aid-distribution school at 43, with one person previously missing found alive. “The Reason We Lost Is the War”: Vance Breaks Ranks as Hegseth Fights Impeachment Talk

The Republican reckoning over Tuesday’s midterm rout turned public and personal this week. Latest · Sport · Football — The No. 7 Shirt Is Handed Back6 November 2026

234 Caps, 146 Goals, Three Trophies: Cristiano Ronaldo Ends His Portugal Career

An era closed on Thursday not with a stadium ovation but with a social-media video. Cristiano Ronaldo, 41, announced his retirement from international football, telling the Portuguese people that “the day has come for me to hand back the No. 7 shirt”. The numbers behind the farewell are unmatched in the men’s game: 234 appearances and 146 goals across 23 years, six World Cups, the Euro 2016 title that made Portugal champions of Europe, and Nations League trophies in 2019 and 2025. His final appearance was the 1–0 win over Wales on 24 September. The ending, though, carries a rift: Ronaldo had fallen out with coach Jorge Jesus — his former club manager at Al-Nassr — after being left unused against Norway, walked out of the squad in late September and accepted a €255 fine and a 30-day suspension before choosing to retire rather than return. The Portuguese Football Federation thanked him and promised “the tribute he deserves”, leaving a farewell appearance possible. Ronaldo continues at Al-Nassr, where he is contracted until June 2027 and has hinted this is probably his last year of football. His exit follows Lionel Messi’s farewell to Argentina last month — the two defining careers of a generation have now both left the international stage.

Latest · Europe · Serbia — Final Results Certified6 November 2026

140 Seats, Certified: Serbia’s Students Now Have 30 Days to Convene, 90 to Govern

Serbia’s political earthquake is now official arithmetic. On Thursday the Republic Electoral Commission adopted its overall report on the 25 October parliamentary elections, including repeat voting at 53 polling stations on 1 November, confirming the “Student List — Students Win” in first place with 2,354,417 votes and 140 of the 250 seats in the National Assembly — a clear absolute majority above the 126 needed. Aleksandar Vučić’s “United Serbia” list took 1,731,323 votes and 103 seats, ending 14 years of dominance by his Serbian Progressive Party; the remaining seven seats went to national-minority lists, while the Socialists and two other opposition lists won none. The movement behind the list grew out of the protests that followed the November 2024 collapse of a railway-station canopy in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people, and campaigned on anti-corruption reforms — independent police bodies to investigate graft, and overhauls of the judiciary, media and state institutions — while pledging to continue EU integration without promising a break with Russia. The constitutional clock now runs: the new Assembly must hold its first session by 5 December, and from the day it is constituted it has 90 days to elect a government or face dissolution. The students have not yet named a prime minister; a technocratic cabinet of experts, rather than career politicians, is the stated plan.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — UNHCR Report6 November 2026

90 Million Displaced People Live Where the Climate Bites Hardest, UN Warns

Three in four of the world’s forcibly displaced people — nearly 90 million refugees and others driven from their homes — now live in countries highly exposed to drought, floods and extreme heat, the UN refugee agency UNHCR said in a report released on Friday, days before the COP31 climate summit opens in Antalya, Türkiye. Afghanistan and Bangladesh are among the most severely exposed hosts named. The overlap runs both ways: more than two-thirds of refugees who returned home in the first half of 2026 went back to countries highly vulnerable to climate hazards, including nearly 4 million recent returnees in Syria facing water scarcity and erratic rainfall, and the agency counts more than 255 million internal displacements from weather events over the past decade. By 2050, it warns, one in three refugee settlements in sub-Saharan Africa could face 100 or more days of dangerous heat a year, and a forecast powerful El Niño could displace up to three million more people in East Africa by the end of 2026. Yet only about 10 per cent of global climate finance reaches the fragile and conflict-affected states hosting these populations — a gap UNHCR wants the Antalya talks, running 9–20 November under a shared Türkiye–Australia presidency, to close. A separate UN report on Wednesday found rich countries well short of the $305 billion a year needed to help developing nations adapt.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — 69% of Districts In, 800 Objections6 November 2026

19 Million Ballots Counted, 73 Councils Hung: South Africa’s Bargaining Deepens

South Africa’s count moved past two-thirds complete in the early hours of Friday. By 04:04 local time the Electoral Commission had captured 16,382 of 23,696 voting districts and 18,998,352 ballots — 18.67 million of them valid. The national shares barely moved: the ANC on 31.79 per cent with 5.93 million votes, the DA on 24.78 per cent and the MK Party on 8.90 per cent. Of the 104 municipalities where counting is complete, 73 have no party majority — including eight deadlocked in ties — against just 31 with an outright winner, so coalition talks will decide most of the country’s 213 councils. Some 2,221 of 4,485 wards and 3,277 of 8,825 seats are complete, but no province and no metro outcome is final. The Commission says it has received about 800 objections from political parties, with a 5 p.m. Friday deadline for complaints that could affect outcomes. Turnout is estimated at 54.5 per cent of 29.2 million registered voters. Final results are slated for announcement on Sunday, after which councils have 14 days to constitute and elect mayors — the real contest, in dozens of towns, starts then.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Decision on Sunday6 November 2026

Herzog to Name His Pick on Sunday — Eisenkot Holds 67, Netanyahu 46

Israel’s week of presidential consultations closed on Thursday with the arithmetic unchanged and the decision deferred to the weekend. President Isaac Herzog finished his round of meetings with the incoming Knesset factions and announced he will task a lawmaker with forming the next government on Sunday, saying he intends to leave no stone unturned in the search for a stable government and will hold private talks with party leaders in the meantime. Gadi Eisenkot of Yashar holds the backing of 67 of the 120 lawmakers — his own party plus Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Democrats, Ra’am and the Joint List, after both Arab-majority parties endorsed him on Thursday — against 46 for caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from Likud, Shas, Religious Zionism, Zehut and Otzma Yehudit. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism recommended no one, leaving seven seats uncommitted. A recommendation is not a coalition promise, and Eisenkot’s hardest work — turning endorsements into 61 voting partners — starts only once the mandate lands; he would then have 28 days, extendable by 14. The new Knesset convenes on 10 November, and Eisenkot’s bloc is also preparing an early vote to replace Speaker Amir Ohana, who said he would schedule one within a week if 61 lawmakers formally ask.

Latest · Americas · Vatican — Opening Day in Montevideo6 November 2026

Pope Leo Lands in Montevideo Today: 36 Speeches, 14 Flights, One Very Secular Host

Pope Leo arrives in Montevideo on Friday to open the first Latin American journey of his pontificate — a 31,000-kilometre, 11-day tour begun barely three weeks after lung surgery in which doctors removed a lobe of his right lung before clearing the 71-year-old to travel. Day one is choreographed for a proudly secular host: President Yamandú Orsi at the Estévez Palace, an address to authorities and diplomats at Plaza Independencia, the keys of the city at the Cabildo, and evening prayer with bishops and clergy at the Metropolitan Cathedral. Saturday brings Mass in Paysandú honouring Blessed Jacinto Vera, a visit to the poor neighbourhood of Casavalle and a youth vigil expected to draw 60,000 people to the Estadio Centenario; on Sunday he celebrates Mass in Florida at the shrine of Our Lady of the Thirty-Three, Uruguay’s patroness. The programme — 36 speeches, 14 flights including two by helicopter, nine public Masses — then moves to Argentina for three days, the first papal visit there in 39 years, and on to Peru, where Leo served as a missionary from 1985, later became a bishop and holds citizenship. Even the transport is local: his popemobile was built by Uruguayan manufacturer Nordex and handed to the nunciature this week. Ahead of the tour, activists have called on the Pope to expel abusers from the Church — a pressure point expected to shadow the visit.

Latest · Middle East · Energy — Brent at $98.296 November 2026

Oil Rebounds as Hormuz Claims Fly: Tanker Fires Alleged, None Confirmed

Oil steadied at elevated levels as the week closed, with Brent settling at $98.29 a barrel and US crude at $90.52, against a Strait of Hormuz backdrop that remains more claim than confirmation. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said on Thursday that two tankers and a tug had been set ablaze in the strait; no independent confirmation of the fires has emerged. The claim follows a reported explosion on Iran’s Qeshm Island near the strait on 3 November, which US media described as a “defensive” American strike — also provisional — while Qatari mediation efforts continue in the background. The uncertainty is doing its own work in energy markets: central bankers from London to Washington have cited Middle East energy costs passing into household bills as a reason inflation is proving stubborn, with the Bank of England this week projecting UK inflation to peak near 4.8 per cent next spring. For now, shipping through the world’s most important oil chokepoint continues under a cloud of unverified claims — and every unconfirmed report moves the price.

After the Rout, One Number Left: October Jobs Land at 8:30 a.m. in Washington

US markets go into Friday’s session bruised and waiting on a single release. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the October employment report at 8:30 a.m. Eastern — 6:30 p.m. in Pakistan — with forecasters expecting roughly 55,000 to 65,000 jobs added, up from September’s weak 29,000, and unemployment holding at 4.2 per cent, with wages up about 0.3 per cent on the month. The stakes are higher than the modest headline suggests. The Federal Reserve held rates at 3.75–4.00 per cent on 28 October, markets were pricing a strong chance of a December hike before Thursday’s crash, and the Bank of England has just moved the other way — so a hot jobs number would strengthen the case for tighter US policy just as AI stocks wobble, while a soft one would revive rate-cut hopes at the cost of recession talk. Context from the week so far: September job openings came in stronger than expected at 7.523 million on Tuesday, and Thursday’s claims and productivity data landed mid-rout. Gold, near $3,950 an ounce, has been the week’s pressure gauge — in the release hour of the last report it swung more than $30 before closing the day $40 lower. After the worst day on Wall Street since April 2025, Friday’s number decides whether the week ends in stabilisation or a second leg down.
