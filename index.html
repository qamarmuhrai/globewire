Rescuers Race to Reach Survivors After a Magnitude 7.0 Earthquake Flattens Hundreds of Homes in Tibet

China has mounted an all-out rescue effort in a remote, high-altitude corner of Tibet after a magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck late on Thursday night, killing at least 16 people and injuring 76. The quake hit at a shallow depth of about 10 kilometres, with its epicentre in Maru township, Baqen County, roughly 190 kilometres east-northeast of the city of Nagqu. Local officials said 339 homes had collapsed or been damaged, more than 12,000 residents had been moved to safety, and 413 aftershocks had been recorded by Friday. President Xi Jinping ordered rescuers to go all out to find survivors and treat the injured, and Beijing released 50 million yuan in emergency relief funds, with a further 30 million earmarked for recovery. Satellites and drones were sent up to map the damage, rockfalls forced the closure of part of National Highway 317, and more than 1,600 people from the county alone joined the relief work. It is the strongest earthquake to hit China since January 2024.

Latest · Middle East · West Bank24 October 2026

UN 'Extremely Alarmed' as Israel Seals Hebron and Orders a Wider West Bank Lockdown

The United Nations said on Friday it was extremely alarmed by escalating violence in the occupied West Bank, after Israel moved to seal off Hebron following a gun attack near a military checkpoint outside the city that killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded at least five others; the two Palestinian attackers were also killed. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered Hebron sealed, pledged house-to-house raids and home demolitions, and reportedly threatened a wider operation — the fourth time in a month Israel has imposed restrictions across the territory. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said the closures severely restrict Palestinians' freedom of movement, access to basic services and livelihoods, and voiced concern over reports of demolitions and possible displacement. The UN human rights office called the lockdown another episode in a stream of suffocating measures, while Guterres also condemned what he described as record numbers of settler attacks on Palestinians in 2026 — more than 160 in October alone.

Typhoon Champi, the Strongest Storm on Earth This Year, Brings Taiwan to a Standstill

Taiwan shut schools, offices and financial markets and cancelled close to a thousand flights on Friday as Typhoon Champi — the strongest tropical cyclone recorded anywhere in 2026 — lashed the island's north-east. The storm reached peak sustained winds of about 296 km/h, equivalent to a Category 5 hurricane, before weakening somewhat as it passed just off Taiwan's coast. Authorities said one person had died after being swept away by waves, 171 people were injured and around 1,000 residents evacuated, with forecasters warning that up to 90 centimetres of rain could fall on the north-east before the storm clears on Saturday evening. In Japan's southern Okinawa prefecture, nearly 8,400 households lost power and more than 160 flights were cancelled. Champi is expected to linger off China's eastern coast before curving north-east toward Japan's main islands. Meteorologists link the season's exceptional Pacific storms to a strengthening Super El Niño warming the ocean.

Latest · Europe · Technology24 October 2026

Greece Votes to Ban Social Media for Children Under 15 From 2027

Greece's parliament approved a law on Friday banning children under 15 from using social media platforms, adding the country to a fast-growing list of governments curbing young people's access to the apps. Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis's New Democracy party used its majority to pass the bill after a heated two-day debate; no other party backed it, and the socialist PASOK opposition abstained after proposing a softer alternative of an under-13 ban plus parental controls for 13- and 14-year-olds. The ban takes effect on January 1, 2027, requires platforms to verify users' ages and imposes heavy penalties for non-compliance. Mitsotakis, who first announced the plan in April in a TikTok video, said the platforms have about two and a half months to devise age checks that also protect privacy, and framed the law as standing by parents against the endless scrolling that costs children sleep and concentration. Australia was the first country to legislate such a ban, for under-16s; a similar French law was struck down by its Constitutional Council in August.

Latest · Americas · Weather24 October 2026

Hurricane Vance Rapidly Strengthens Off Mexico's Pacific Coast and Could Reach Category 3

Hurricane Vance rapidly intensified into a Category 2 storm on Friday as it churned off Mexico's Pacific coast, packing maximum sustained winds of 105 mph (170 kph) about 425 miles south-southwest of Acapulco, the US National Hurricane Center said. Forecasters said it could strengthen further into a major Category 3 hurricane, though cooler waters and wind shear may cap it. Vance was moving slowly northwest and is expected to continue that track through the weekend, approaching the Puerto Vallarta area by Monday; its outer bands were already funnelling gale-force winds and rough seas into the Gulf of Tehuantepec, and swells are expected to bring life-threatening surf and rip currents to Mexico's southern and southwestern coasts. Vance is the first V-named storm in the eastern Pacific since 2018 and would be the seventh major hurricane in a row in an exceptional Pacific season that forecasters tie to worsening El Niño conditions.

Latest · Africa · Kenya24 October 2026

Rescuers Make Contact With Survivors Trapped Inside a Collapsed Nairobi Building

Rescue teams in Nairobi worked through the night after a multi-storey building under construction collapsed on Friday afternoon in Eastleigh, one of the capital's busiest commercial districts. Interior Minister Kipchumba Murkomen said all 67 workers on site had been accounted for — 66 escaped unharmed and one was injured — but seven people were reported missing, among them occupants of ground-floor shops, including an electronics store, a butchery and a grocery, that had opened inside the unfinished structure. Murkomen said rescuers had made contact with some survivors still trapped in the rubble, raising hopes they could be pulled out alive, while the Kenya Red Cross said two injured people had been taken to Kenyatta National Hospital. Police warned the collapsed structure was leaning against a neighbouring building, raising the risk of a second collapse. Governor Johnson Sakaja said the project had construction approval but no occupancy certificate, making the shops' presence illegal, and renewed calls for tougher enforcement against developers who flout building rules.

Latest · Africa · Health24 October 2026

Nigeria Traces 44 Contacts After Confirming an Imported Ebola Case in Abuja

Nigeria is tracing and testing 44 contacts of its first confirmed Ebola case in more than a decade, an imported infection identified in the capital, Abuja, on October 20. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate said on Friday that genomic analysis shows the virus is closely related to the strain found in Kenya's index case — the first genetic link established between the outbreaks now spreading from the Democratic Republic of Congo — though there is no evidence the Kenyan case travelled to Nigeria. The Nigerian patient, a diplomat who had recently been in eastern Congo, arrived on an Ethiopian Airlines flight on October 3, fell ill days later and tested positive at a private hospital; laboratory work identified the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment. Some contacts have tested negative and others are still awaiting results. Uganda, through which the patient transited, said it remains Ebola-free but has reinstated arrival locator forms and will no longer allow travellers with a recent history of travel to Congo to transit the country.

UN Nuclear Chief Says the Basis for an Iran Deal Exists — but Inspectors Must Get Back In

The head of the International Atomic Energy Agency, Rafael Grossi, told the UN General Assembly on Friday that the technical groundwork has been done and the bases on which a diplomatic agreement with Iran could be concluded already exist, urging all sides to redouble their efforts. A return of inspectors to Iran's nuclear sites, he said, requires a return to the negotiating table. Tehran has sharply restricted the agency's access since the June 2025 strikes on its nuclear facilities during a 12-day war with Israel. The IAEA estimated in March that just over 200 kilogrammes of uranium enriched to 60 percent remained at Isfahan, with more held at Natanz, and in September its board of governors referred Iran's non-compliance to the UN Security Council — a step Tehran contests. Grossi warned that the global non-proliferation regime depends on dialogue, pointing to the agency's work in Syria and at Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia plant, where it has brokered eight localised ceasefires to repair power lines.

Latest · Africa · Sudan24 October 2026

One Year After El Fasher Fell, Survivors Still Live in Fear and Without Support, UN Women Finds

A year after paramilitary Rapid Support Forces seized El Fasher in Sudan's Darfur region, the trauma of the city's fall still hangs over the thousands who fled it, the UN's gender agency said on Friday. In a survey of 50 women's organisations supporting the displaced, nine in ten reported women and girls showing signs of psychological distress who had received no mental health, psychosocial or legal support since escaping, and nearly all said the distress persists a year on. UN investigators have documented more than 6,000 killings in the first three days of the final assault alone, alongside systematic starvation, torture, rape and ethnic targeting. UN Women's regional director Anna Mutavati told reporters in Geneva that some people have begun returning to the city, but the fear of violence is constant, and that drone attacks are increasing across several Sudanese states, with most of those killed or injured women and children — many of whom had already fled violence once before.

China's Communist Party Prepares a Closed-Door Plenum That Will Set the Tone to 2027

The elite Central Committee of China's ruling Communist Party will meet behind closed doors in Beijing from Monday to Thursday, in a plenum expected to set the political tone ahead of the once-every-five-years party congress in late 2027 and to weigh key personnel moves. It is the fifth time the roughly 300 full and alternate members of the Central Committee — the largest of the party's top decision-making bodies — have gathered since the 2022 congress confirmed Xi Jinping's norm-breaking third term as leader. The official theme is tightening discipline within the party's ranks, but deliberations on disgraced former officials are also likely to feature. True to tradition, attendees are confined to the venue to prevent leaks, foreign media and most Chinese reporters get no access, and little will be made public until the meeting closes, when the state news agency Xinhua is expected to issue a communiqué couched in the party's customary opaque language, with a fuller document to follow days later.
