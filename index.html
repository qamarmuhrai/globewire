Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi Demands14 November 2026

Russia’s Abu Dhabi Price Emerges: All of Donbas, a Rolled-Back NATO and Finland Out of the Alliance

The first direct Ukraine talks in nine months produced one agreement — and a long list of demands. Reporting from the 10–11 November sessions in Abu Dhabi, mediated by US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, says Moscow circulated an informal plan for a new European security order: Russia keeps all territory it holds and takes the whole of Donbas, Ukraine accepts permanent non-alignment and drops its NATO bid, no alliance bases are set up in Estonia, Latvia or Lithuania, and Finland — a member since 2023 — restores its non-aligned status, with NATO pulled back toward its 1997 borders. Finland’s defence and foreign ministers said its membership is not open to outside negotiation, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov denied Moscow had demanded a Finnish exit. The one concrete result was agreement in principle on the war’s largest prisoner exchange, potentially 1,000 to 1,500 people from each side. Separately, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said US negotiators had for the first time asked whether Kyiv would trade a Donbas withdrawal for NATO membership, stressing no concrete offer exists, while fighting continued: a Ukrainian strike killed a couple and their two-year-old in Russia’s Belgorod region, and Russian attacks injured 22 people in Zaporizhzhia on Saturday afternoon.

Latest · Americas · United States — Flight Chaos, Day Two14 November 2026

More Than 2,500 US Flights Cancelled on Saturday as Duffy Tells Stranded Passengers ‘It Is Safe to Fly’

The halt lasted four hours and 21 minutes; the hangover is lasting the weekend. More than 2,500 flights were cancelled across the United States on Saturday, on top of Friday’s tally of over 4,700 cancellations and some 10,000 delays, as airlines worked aircraft and crews back into position after the nationwide ground stop — the longest in 25 years and only the second full departure halt since the September 11 attacks. The FAA ordered the stop at 3:54 p.m. Eastern on Friday after a telecommunications outage hit all 22 en-route control centres while more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne; airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, addressing stranded travellers on Saturday, insisted flying is safe, blamed decades-old analogue technology and overloaded routers, and said capacity should be restored well before Thanksgiving — while warning the return to normal would be slow. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin repeated there was no sign of a cyberattack or any other hostile act, and the company behind the FAA’s new AI traffic platform denied any link to the failure.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · APEC — Trilateral Confirmed for 19 November14 November 2026

Trump, Xi and Putin to Meet in Shenzhen on Thursday in the First-Ever Summit of the Three Nuclear Powers

The date is now set, even if the agenda is not. The White House and the Kremlin have both confirmed that Presidents Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin will meet on Thursday 19 November on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen — the first time the leaders of the three largest nuclear arsenals have sat down together. US officials say Trump wants to open a conversation on reducing stockpiles nine months after the New START treaty lapsed, leaving Washington and Moscow uncapped for the first time in over half a century, and to raise the war with Iran; they also say Ukraine will deliberately not be discussed in this forum and that no agreement is expected from this first encounter. Beijing’s embassy in Washington said China is in communication with the relevant parties, while Chinese academics quoted by Reuters call nuclear limits a nonstarter for Beijing, whose arsenal is far smaller. Russian state media are already comparing the gathering to Yalta in 1945; analysts put the likely yield the other way — heavy symbolism, thin substance — with Trump and Xi also due a bilateral meeting and working lunch earlier that day.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · ASEAN — Leaders Arrive in Manila14 November 2026

ASEAN Holds Its Myanmar Line as Leaders Land in Manila for a Summit Shadowed by Energy Costs

Manila is filling up for the bloc’s biggest week of the year. Singapore’s Lawrence Wong, Malaysia’s Anwar Ibrahim, Indonesia’s Prabowo Subianto, Thailand’s Anutin Charnvirakul and UN Secretary-General António Guterres all arrived on Saturday for the 49th ASEAN Summit from 15 to 17 November, with US President Donald Trump expected on Tuesday after the main sessions. Host President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has put energy and food security at the top of the agenda as Middle East war costs bite, alongside South China Sea code-of-conduct talks Manila puts at about 70 percent complete, transboundary haze and an approaching El Niño. The Philippines hands the rotating chairmanship to Singapore at the closing ceremony.

Latest · Americas · Alaska — First Democrat Since 199814 November 2026

Alaska Elects a Democratic Governor for the First Time Since 1998 in a Midterm Wave for the Party

Alaska has broken a 28-year pattern. The Associated Press and NBC News have projected that Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, a 37-year-old former state representative from the island community of Sitka, has won the governor’s race — the first Democrat elected to the office since Tony Knowles in 1998, and at 37 the youngest governor in state history. Under Alaska’s ranked-choice system he beat three Republicans, including Bernadette Wilson, who carried President Donald Trump’s endorsement, along with Dave Bronson and Treg Taylor, after fellow Democrat Tom Begich withdrew in August and backed him. He was also endorsed by Republican Senator Lisa Murkowski, campaigned on working across party lines and plans a cabinet drawn from all affiliations, with independent Zac Johnson as lieutenant governor. The result, called on Friday after more than a week of counting mail and remote ballots, lifts Democrats to 30 governorships nationwide, up from 24, with flips also in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas. Kreiss-Tomkins succeeds term-limited Republican Mike Dunleavy and takes office on 7 December; certification is due by 25 November.

Latest · Americas · Space — Starship Return Window14 November 2026

Starship Circles Earth 14 Times Toward a 3:20 p.m. Texas Catch Attempt After Its Booster Aborted and Self-Destructed

The double catch is gone; the harder single catch is still on. SpaceX says Ship 42 and its heat shield remain healthy in orbit on Flight 15, with no vehicle or range issues tracked, as it completes 14 circuits at about 275 kilometres before a return planned for 3:20 p.m. Eastern (2120 GMT) on Saturday — a first attempt to pluck an orbital spacecraft from the air with the launch tower’s mechanical arms at Starbase, Texas. The mission’s other half ended minutes after Friday’s liftoff of the 124-metre rocket: the upgraded Super Heavy booster, initially bound for capture at Launch Pad 2, detected a guidance, navigation and control fault near the end of its descent, diverted offshore and automatically self-destructed over the Gulf of Mexico in a fireball. Before that, the ship deployed 26 advanced Starlink satellites. SpaceX has caught boosters three times before, but never a returning ship falling far faster from far higher — the step the company needs toward a fully reusable system for the Moon and Mars. If fresh safety concerns arise, controllers can still send the ship to a controlled ocean splashdown instead.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — EU Aid Demand14 November 2026

EU Demands Israel Reopen All Gaza Crossings as Floods Soak Tents and a Drone Strike Kills Two in Jabaliya

Brussels has joined the pressure over Gaza’s flooded camps. In a statement issued Saturday through foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas’s office, the European Union urged Israel to allow immediate, unimpeded humanitarian access at scale, reopen every border crossing and protect civilian infrastructure and medical facilities, saying severe flooding and winter conditions are aggravating the suffering of an already exhausted population. UNICEF estimates heavy rain has directly affected at least 106,000 people in recent days, with thousands of displaced families’ tents submerged and latrines overflowing; the WHO separately warns respiratory infections are running about 33,000 cases above last year as nearly a million displaced people shelter in flood-exposed areas. Israel’s COGAT agency says more than 93,000 tents and 8,600 truckloads of winter supplies have entered and over 850 further requests approved. The diplomatic push came the same day an Israeli drone strike killed two Palestinians walking in Jabaliya, north of Gaza City, according to Shifa Hospital director Mohamed Abu Selmiya; the Israeli military said it targeted two operatives. Gaza’s health ministry says at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took hold more than a year ago.

Latest · Africa · Congo — Ebola Spreads Rural14 November 2026

Congo’s Ebola Count Reaches 11,197 as the Outbreak Shifts From Its First Epicentres Into Remote Villages

Six months in, the map of Congo’s Ebola emergency is moving. The latest health-ministry figures relayed through UN channels put the Bundibugyo outbreak at 11,197 confirmed cases and 5,471 deaths across eight provinces and 68 health zones, with a case-fatality rate near 49 percent and just over 3,000 recoveries; a single reporting day added 83 cases, concentrated in North Kivu and Ituri, and 38 deaths. UN emergency officials say infections have fallen in early hotspots such as Bunia, where some treatment centres now run at half capacity, but transmission is seeding remote rural communities that are slower to reach, while contact follow-up sits at about 81 percent against a 95 percent target. Violence keeps cutting into the response: an early-November attack on Cantine-Aloya killed 66 civilians including three health workers and saw three facilities looted and burned, leaving more than 1,000 contacts lost to follow-up. The WHO still counts 400 to 500 new infections a week and calls the outbreak a public-health emergency of international concern; there is no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo species, though vaccine trials are under way. One bright spot: Kenya has discharged its final patient and begun the 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free.

UN Climate Chief Tells COP31 to ‘Avoid Tactical Delays’ as a Fight Over Science Joins the Fossil-Fuel Deadlock

Antalya’s second week will decide whether this summit produces a deal or a declaration. UN climate chief Simon Stiell urged negotiators on Saturday to move swiftly to the hardest issues and avoid tactical delays, as COP31 heads into its final stretch under its split leadership — Türkiye’s Murat Kurum as president, Australia’s Chris Bowen steering the talks — with a first draft of the closing text expected early next week. Big fossil-fuel producers and consumers are still resisting any wording that sets firm targets for moving away from oil, gas and coal; host Türkiye is pushing instead to centre the deal on rapid electrification of cars, boilers and furnaces. A second fault line has sharpened: the EU says it will fight for science after accusing some countries of undermining the IPCC, whose next assessment Europe, Africa and Pacific states want ready for the 2028 stocktake while Saudi Arabia, China and India push for 2029. The United States, the world’s largest oil consumer, has again sent no executive delegation, though a congressional group is attending. A UN report last month put the world on course to overshoot 1.5°C, and migration chief Amy Pope warned more than 200 million people live in highly climate-vulnerable communities.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology — Microsoft Outage, Final Word14 November 2026

‘All Products Are Operational’: Microsoft Closes Out One of Its Longest Outages After 7.4 Million Reports

Microsoft’s status page finally reads clear. The company confirmed on Saturday that it had completed work on the underlying fault and restored service to the last affected customers and regions, ending a Microsoft 365 outage that began with Exchange Online failures around midday Eastern on Thursday 12 November and ran for more than 24 hours across at least 74 countries. Outlook, Teams and Copilot all went down for businesses, governments, hospitals, courts and airlines, some of which fell back on phone lines and paper; Downdetector logged over 7.4 million reports, peaking near 20,000 a minute. Microsoft traced the cascade to a flaw introduced during an earlier network-capacity upgrade and exposed by a later network change, compounded by backend servers dropping out and surging retry traffic, and deployed a patch to an authentication and routing component. It apologised, said it found no evidence of malicious activity and ruled out both a cyberattack and any AI cause, and has promised a full post-incident report. Consumer services recovered first, on Friday afternoon GMT, with enterprise accounts following overnight.

Latest · Europe · Norway — Landmark Climate Ruling13 November 2026

Norway’s Supreme Court Voids Three Oil Approvals Unanimously on Burned-Fuel Emissions — but the Oil Keeps Flowing

The full judgment, published Saturday, is sharper than the first reports suggested. Norway’s Supreme Court, sitting as an 11-judge grand chamber in case HR-2026-2428-S, held unanimously that the development approvals for Equinor’s Breidablikk and Aker BP’s Tyrving and Yggdrasil fields are invalid because their impact assessments never covered the emissions from burning the oil and gas produced — emissions the EU assessment directive, which binds Norway through the European Economic Area, requires to be examined before approval. By six votes to five the court went further, requiring each project’s combustion emissions to be weighed together with those of other existing and approved shelf projects. The case, brought by Greenpeace Nordic and Nature and Youth and already won twice in lower courts, ends with the state ordered to pay more than 5 million kroner in costs. The energy ministry says it will comply through fresh assessments. But the court stressed the ruling decides the legality of the state’s decisions, not the companies’ rights: the operators were not parties, their permits do not lapse automatically, the two producing fields keep producing, and Yggdrasil remains due on stream next year — even as every future Norwegian approval must now pass this test.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Three Tankers Ablaze14 November 2026

Iran Says Three Oil Tankers Hit Mines in Hormuz in One Morning as US Central Command Calls the Claims False

Tehran’s grip on the strait tightened again on Saturday — by its own account, at least. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said two oil tankers that had switched off radar and positioning systems and tried to cross the southern Strait of Hormuz by an unauthorised route exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines, one of them a supertanker; the regular navy separately reported a third tanker ablaze and stopped after hitting a mine in a prohibited zone east of the strait, also with its tracking switched off. No vessel names, flags or casualty figures were given, and none of the claims has been independently verified. Since 26 October Tehran has required every ship to obtain permission and coordinate with the Guards’ maritime traffic centre, and it has attacked vessels that did not comply since the war began in February; the United States, which blockades Iranian ports and escorts tankers, dismissed the mine claims through Central Command as false and an attempt to intimidate. Iranian state media also claimed a 17th US drone destroyed over Qeshm Island. Traffic through the strait is running about 70 percent below pre-war levels, down from roughly 130–140 transits a day to around 36.
