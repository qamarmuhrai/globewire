Latest · Europe · Warsaw — Three Minutes Over Poland20 November 2026

Russian Missiles Fall Inside Poland, and Warsaw Calls It a Provocation

The war crossed a NATO border in physical form on Friday. Poland’s defence minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, said two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down on Polish soil during Moscow’s massive overnight assault on Ukraine — a Kh-101 cruise missile near Nowy Lipowiec, a second missile at Rudnik nad Sanem, and a drone at Molodycz, all in the south-east, 40 to 100 kilometres from the Ukrainian frontier. No one in Poland was hurt and no infrastructure was destroyed, but the geography sharpened the alarm: the impact sites lie close to Nowa Deba, where troops from the European “Coalition of the Willing” are exercising for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine. Prime Minister Donald Tusk convened ministers and security chiefs and called the crossings an intentional provocation, a reading Britain’s prime minister echoed in condemnation. Polish commanders said 11 objects threatened their airspace, three crossed, and allied jets stayed airborne with Patriot batteries active — yet no shot was fired, because in roughly three minutes of warning, in poor weather, crews could not positively identify what they were tracking. Warsaw is investigating all three sites and has asked for additional NATO support.

Kyiv Reopens One Bridge as the Toll From the Night Barrage Rises to Nine

By Friday afternoon Kyiv was learning to move again around the holes in its map. Mayor Vitali Klitschko announced that traffic from the western to the eastern bank of the Dnipro had been restored over the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi Bridge, while engineers were still assessing the Darnytskyi Bridge and the Southern Bridge remained under partial restrictions. Two employees of Kyiv’s water utility died in the capital. Moscow says it targeted bridges used to move weapons, in answer to Ukraine’s third strike this week on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea; Kyiv says a city of three million was punished for it, hours after the Shenzhen declaration spoke of peace.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · The Hague — The Genocide Verdict20 November 2026

“Unfair,” Says Myanmar, as the World Absorbs a Genocide Ruling Without Precedent

A day after the International Court of Justice found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the judgment’s aftershocks are spreading in opposite directions. In Cox’s Bazar, refugees who fled the 2017 crackdown that drove more than 730,000 people into Bangladesh watched a court finally use the word they have used for nine years. In Naypyidaw, a spokesperson for the military-backed government dismissed the ruling on Friday as unfair and pointedly noted it was not unanimous. The 9–6 decision, read over nearly three hours by President Yuji Iwasawa, is the first time in the court’s 80-year history that a state has been held responsible for genocide. Judges found the evidence established at least 10,000 Rohingya killed, alongside widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, committed with intent to destroy a substantial part of the group. The court ordered Myanmar to stop ongoing violations, investigate and punish those responsible, and provide reparations — including facilitating safe, voluntary return, restoring property, and accounting for the disappeared — with a report on restitution due within two years. The court cannot enforce its orders, but lawyers say the precedent will echo through every other case now before it.

Anthropic’s debut was supposed to be the celebration; it became the occasion for a rout. Trump Puts the Iran Decision Off Until the New Year. Netanyahu Wants It Now.

The two allies are now openly working to different clocks. Flying home from China, President Trump told reporters in three separate interviews that he is in no rush on Iran: “I am not rushing into any decision. We will see what happens after the beginning of the year.” The remark pushes any choice about another major bombing campaign past 1 January — and past the seating of a Democratic-controlled Congress his party’s midterm losses have made inevitable. In Jerusalem, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing the opposite case, telling a security meeting that military action is inevitable and, according to Israeli reporting, seeking a joint four-to-seven-day campaign against Iran’s rebuilding missile and drone forces; his Mossad chief was in Washington and Miami last week lobbying for it. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has sketched a third option: keep the naval blockade and economic pressure doing the work without restarting the war. Gulf governments, watching tanker attacks near Hormuz and strikes across the region, are warning that a renewed campaign could bring retaliation against the energy infrastructure the world economy runs on. For now, the pause holds — though, as Axios notes, this president has said “no decision” before, on the eve of ordering strikes.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Through the Night20 November 2026

At COP31, “Ambition” Is Quite Literally Missing — and the Talks Run On

Outside the Antalya venue on Thursday, activists pinned up spoof missing-pet posters for a cat named Ambition, last seen loving a transition away from fossil fuels. Inside, negotiators were having the same trouble finding it. The UN’s own account of the day confirms that the latest draft cover decision has dropped explicit references to moving away from coal, oil and gas, while acknowledging for the first time at a climate summit that the world will overshoot the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C limit — language Australia’s Chris Bowen, who leads the negotiations, vowed would be paired with a clear decision that the overshoot must be kept as small and short as possible. Finance, adaptation and just-transition tracks remain stalled: the draft reaffirms tripling adaptation funding by 2035 but leaves how to count the increase for later. What momentum exists sits in the voluntary action agenda, including Türkiye’s 35-by-35 electrification package, aiming to lift electricity to 35% of global final energy use by 2035. Bowen promised consultations through the night rather than leaving Antalya without consensus. The summit’s Friday deadline has effectively become a starting gun for overtime.

Haiti’s Police Fight Their Way Back Into a Corridor Gangs Held for Five Years

For five years, Martissant was a place the Haitian state did not go. On Thursday, it went in with armoured vehicles. The National Police say at least 34 suspected gang members were killed in an operation in the southern Port-au-Prince neighbourhood, carried out with an anti-gang task force that includes personnel from Vectus Global, the private security firm founded by Blackwater’s Erik Prince. Police reported no officers or civilians hurt, and said none of the roughly 2,500 Salvadoran troops in the country took part. The stakes are geographic as much as tactical: Martissant commands the corridor linking the capital of some three million people to Haiti’s west and south, and the area sits under the sway of the Gran Ravine, Ti Bwa and 5 Segond gangs. The raid is the first significant attempt to retake the district since armed groups seized it, in a capital where gangs have steadily taken over neighbourhood after neighbourhood while the state contracted. Whether one operation can hold ground that was lost for half a decade is the question now hanging over the corridor.

Latest · Global Affairs · Deep Space — One Light-Day20 November 2026

A Message That Takes a Day to Arrive: Voyager 1 Crosses a Distance No Object Has Reached

Humanity’s farthest traveller has passed a milestone measured not in miles but in light. NASA confirmed on Thursday that Voyager 1 crossed one light-day from Earth at 5:16 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday — about 16 billion miles, or 26 billion kilometres, away — the first human-made object ever to travel so far. The agency waited a day to announce it for a fitting reason: the confirming signal itself needed roughly 24 hours to make the trip home. Launched in 1977 and still moving at nearly 38,000 miles per hour through interstellar space, the probe now requires a full day for any command to reach it and another day for a reply. It is heading toward the star Gliese 445, which it will pass within 1.7 light-years in about 40,000 years. Its twin, Voyager 2, is not expected to reach the same distance until 2035 — by which time Voyager 1’s power is forecast to be nearly spent, with communications expected to end around 2036 after almost six decades of flight.

Six Dead, a Toddler Pulled Alive, and a Day of Mourning After Tbilisi’s Gas Explosion

Georgia will hold a national day of mourning on 22 November for the six people killed when a suspected natural-gas explosion tore through a five-storey apartment block in Tbilisi’s Ponichala district on Thursday morning. A 31-year-old man remains in critical condition with burns to most of his body. Mayor Kakha Kaladze said 28 families, more than 100 people, would be moved to hotels after inspectors declared two sections of the building unsafe, and state flags will fly at half-mast across the country on Sunday.

Latest · Africa · Nairobi — The Countdown20 November 2026

No Patients, No Contacts in Quarantine: Kenya’s 42 Days to an Ebola-Free Declaration

Kenya is counting down to the end of its first Ebola outbreak. Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale announced on 11 November that the country’s last patient had been discharged healthy and free of the virus, and that the final 11 contacts had completed monitoring and gone home — leaving, for the first time since the outbreak began, no one in treatment and no one in quarantine. The 42-day countdown, twice the virus’s longest incubation period, began on 12 November; if no new case appears, Kenya will be declared Ebola-free on 23 December. The outbreak of Bundibugyo virus disease was confirmed on 6 October, a day after the first imported case was identified in a traveller from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where a far larger regional outbreak declared in May has passed 11,000 cases. Kenya recorded nine confirmed cases in total: the first patient and his mother died, while seven patients recovered — five of them discharged on 3 November as the first Kenyans ever to recover from the disease. Duale said prevention measures will stay in place until the December date is reached, and the Africa CDC praised the response while urging continued vigilance.
