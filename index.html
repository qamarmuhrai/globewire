Latest · Middle East · Israel27 October 2026

Israel Votes at Last: First Election Since October 7 Puts Netanyahu's Survival — and a Reckoning — on the Ballot

Israelis go to the polls on Tuesday in the country's first parliamentary election since the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023, and for many voters the ballot is a verdict on one man. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving leader, has refused to accept blame for the security collapse that let Hamas kill some 1,200 people and seize 251 hostages, and he has resisted calls for an independent inquiry, arguing instead that his wars in Gaza, Lebanon and against Iran will leave a safer Middle East — and that only he can finish the job by bringing down Iran's clerical rulers. In Be'eri, the kibbutz where one in ten residents was killed, survivor Gal Cohen told Reuters the thought of Netanyahu's re-election was painful: “If this government will stay, many hearts and minds will be broken.” Polls have for months pointed to a sharp fall for Netanyahu's Likud, yet the rival alliance ranged against him — led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot and spanning right, left and centre — has no clear route to a majority either. The likeliest outcome, analysts say, is stalemate: months of deadlock with Netanyahu still in office at the head of a caretaker government, and the reckoning his opponents want postponed once again.

Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia & Yemen26 October 2026

Houthis Claim Strike on Saudi Arabia's East–West Oil Pumping Stations as Coalition Downs Three More Drones Over Riyadh

The air war over Saudi Arabia showed no sign of easing on Monday. The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted and destroyed three drones over Riyadh and the kingdom's south, hours after Yemen's Houthi movement claimed it had attacked the pumping stations of the East–West crude pipeline — the artery that lets Saudi oil bypass the Red Sea — as well as Riyadh's airport. Riyadh offered no confirmation of the pipeline claim, though a satellite image circulating online appeared to show significant damage at a pumping station. The coalition also said it had destroyed two ballistic missile launchers in Sanaa, including one used to fire a missile towards the capital. The exchange follows a grim pattern: King Khalid International Airport only partly reopened on Sunday, two terminals at a time, after a 10 October Houthi attack killed 13 people there, and Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree has vowed the airport will “never be safe.” Shrapnel from intercepted missiles injured two people in Khamis Mushait and Ahad Rafidah over the weekend, and flight-tracking data showed barely any traffic using the reopened airport. With the Houthis advancing towards the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the 2022 truce in ruins, the Yemen front has become a pressure point in the wider war involving Iran.

Latest · Africa · Egypt & Saudi Arabia26 October 2026

Egypt's Parliament Authorises Military Support for Saudi Arabia — Defence Only, Cairo Stresses, and Only If Riyadh Asks

Egypt moved a step closer to the region's widening war on Monday, as its parliament voted by the required two-thirds majority to authorise President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi to take measures, in coordination with Riyadh, to defend Saudi Arabia's territory, sovereignty and holy sites against Houthi attack. The decision followed three closed-door sessions — on 18, 19 and 26 October — held at Sisi's request, and it stops short of announcing any deployment: Cairo has been careful to say its role would be limited to defending Saudi territory and borders, not attacking Yemen. The stakes for Egypt are direct. Houthi advances threaten the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the gateway to the Suez Canal on which Egypt's economy depends, and Saudi Arabia is Cairo's indispensable patron, supplying billions of dollars in aid, central-bank deposits and energy. Egypt has not joined the Makkah mutual-defence pact signed in August by Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye, though Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said this month that membership remained “under serious consideration.” Cairo's history counsels caution: its 1960s intervention in Yemen was costly, it played only a limited naval role in the Saudi-led coalition after 2015, and it has mounted no large foreign ground deployment since the 1991 Gulf War.

“The American Side Is Not Serious”: Tehran Dismisses US Talks as Security Chiefs From Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye Head to Iran

Iran used its weekly foreign-ministry briefing on Monday to pour cold water on diplomacy with Washington while preparing to host an unusual gathering of regional security chiefs. Spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said the United States was “not serious about negotiations,” accused it of confusing diplomacy with dictating demands, and repeated Tehran's position that it has never sought nuclear weapons. He also said Iran still holds Qatar responsible for clarifying the fate of three Iranian pilots whose aircraft were hit over the Gulf state. The sharper news was who is coming to Tehran: Pakistan's Chief of Defence Forces, Field Marshal Asim Munir — who proposed the meeting — Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin are to hold joint talks with Iranian officials, the first such encounter since Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye signed the Makkah defence pact in August. Iranian officials frame the meeting as a step towards an “indigenous” regional security order, with the US war on Iran, Israeli policy and Lebanon on the agenda. President Masoud Pezeshkian, meanwhile, travelled to Russia on Monday night for a summit of gas-exporting countries.

Latest · Middle East · Jordan26 October 2026

Three Suspects Seized After Shootout Over Jordan Church Massacre That Killed Nine at a Funeral

Jordanian security forces arrested three armed suspects on Monday after a gun battle in Karak, in connection with Friday's attack on a Greek Orthodox church funeral in the village of Hamoud that killed nine people and wounded 40. The Public Security Directorate said the men were found hiding in an abandoned house in Karak governorate, about 120 km southwest of Amman; they refused to surrender, opened fire, and were captured after being wounded, with firearms and ammunition seized and no security personnel hurt. Authorities have described the attackers as “extremist terrorists,” though no group has claimed responsibility and the suspects were not named. The shooting — several minutes of gunfire during a funeral service — was a rare assault on Jordan's Christian minority and on a country that prides itself on stability; King Abdullah has ordered an “iron fist” response. Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya said officials were examining how the attackers were radicalised and promised that anyone found to have taken part would be held accountable.

Latest · Americas · Brazil26 October 2026

Lula Does It Again: A Historic Fourth Term in Brazil — Won From Behind — as Flavio Bolsonaro Refuses to Concede

Brazil's electoral court confirmed on Sunday that Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, has won a fourth term as president, defeating Senator Flavio Bolsonaro by more than four million votes in a runoff that defied the polls, the anti-incumbent mood sweeping the world, and a string of right-wing victories across Latin America. Lula is the first candidate since Brazil's return to democracy in the 1980s to win after finishing second in the first round, and the margin was more than twice the one by which he beat Flavio's father, Jair Bolsonaro, in 2022. Flavio refused to concede, saying he would seek to annul the result over a Supreme Court ruling that extended voting by two hours — a move analysts consider hopeless, not least because his own campaign had also sought extra time when rain hit the conservative south. Markets that had bet on a Bolsonaro win recoiled: a Tokyo-listed fund tracking Brazil's Bovespa index fell nearly 15% in Asian trading. Lula's prize comes with a trap, however: Brazilians simultaneously elected the most conservative Congress since the 1980s, and Bolsonaro's party and its allies may hold opposition majorities in both chambers, against an economy forecast to grow just 2.3% next year with public debt above 82% of GDP.

Xi Opens the Fifth Plenum: Four Days on Party Discipline That Point to a Fourth Term in 2027

China's Communist Party opened its fifth plenum in Beijing on Monday, a four-day closed-door meeting of the Central Committee formally devoted to “full and rigorous party self-governance” — tightening discipline, fighting corruption and making sure officials actually carry out Beijing's orders rather than mouthing them. President Xi Jinping briefed the gathering on the draft document, and state agency Xinhua warned against complacency and empty rhetoric. The subtext is succession in reverse: analysts expect the plenum to be the last major party gathering before the 2027 leadership reshuffle, at which Xi is expected to keep the top job for a fourth term. “The plenum will present Xi as in control, and able to continue his command,” said Kerry Brown of King's College London, adding that appearances may matter as much as substance this time. Xi's anti-corruption campaign has purged hundreds of senior officials and top generals, including former allies; critics say the military purges risk readiness, while the party portrays them as a regenerative “phoenix-like rebirth.” Xinhua argued the drive also serves outsiders, promising investors a more predictable China where rules prevail over connections.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India26 October 2026

“Declared Dead”: 130 Million Names Struck Off India's Voter Rolls, and Living Voters Queuing to Prove They Exist

Kamta Prasad, a 53-year-old Delhi tailor, has been spending his days — and forfeiting his daily wage of 250 rupees, which feeds a family of four — going from office to office trying to prove he is not dead. He is one of some 130 million names removed from India's electoral rolls in the Special Intensive Revision, a vast clean-up that has deleted voters for reasons ranging from “logical discrepancies” to being recorded as deceased; 4.2 million names were struck off in Delhi alone under draft rolls published on 31 August. The revision has triggered near-daily protests in the capital and beyond, met at times with police force, and become the biggest political challenge yet to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government. Opposition parties accuse Election Commission chief Gyanesh Kumar of manipulating the exercise to benefit Modi's BJP — a charge both the commission and the party deny — while the commission insists it is removing only duplicate, dead, shifted or ineligible entries and that anyone wrongly excluded can claim, document and appeal before the rolls are finalised. For poorer families the fear goes beyond voting: a struck-off name can also mean losing subsidised food rations and other welfare.

Oil Slips From Near Six-Month Highs as Qatari Mediators Reach Tehran — but Traders Warn the Reprieve May Not Last

Oil prices retreated on Monday from the near six-month highs hit last week, as Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran in a US-coordinated effort to cool regional tensions. Brent crude fell $2.21 to $114.51 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate dropped $2.23 to $101.04, unwinding part of a rally that had added 6% to Brent the previous week on the back of Houthi attacks inside Saudi Arabia. Analysts were quick to pour cold water on the optimism. “Concerns over renewed military hostilities in the Middle East… have eased this morning, but the reprieve may prove temporary,” said Vandana Hari of Vanda Insights, noting that President Donald Trump has not dispelled expectations of US strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms — leaving volatility elevated and risks skewed to the upside. The structural picture also remains tight: shipping through both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb is still disrupted, and IEA chief Fatih Birol repeated in Singapore on Monday that he does not expect energy markets in 2027 to return to pre-war levels. In a sign of how producers are adapting, Saudi Aramco will begin storing crude in Singapore from the second half of 2027 to harden its Asian supply chain.

Two Weeks After Lung Cancer Surgery, Pope Leo Is Cleared for a 31,000 km Latin America Tour — 10 Cities, 36 Speeches

The Vatican confirmed on Monday that Pope Leo's trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru will go ahead as planned from 6 to 17 November, after doctors attested that his recovery from lung cancer surgery is on track. The 71-year-old pontiff had the lower lobe of his right lung removed on 13 October, when he was diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma, the most common form of the disease. “The Holy Father's recovery after the surgery continues, and the latest medical examinations attest to his good health and favorable clinical condition,” the Vatican said, citing his personal physician Luigi Carbone, and adding that nothing in his current state barred the journey. The schedule has not been softened: some 31,000 km (19,300 miles) across 10 cities with 36 speeches planned, among the most intense foreign tours by a pope in decades — though Leo did cancel a day visit to Assisi scheduled for Tuesday on medical advice, and is trimming his daily routine in consultation with doctors. For the first US-born pope, who spent decades as a missionary in Peru before Francis brought him to Rome in 2023, the trip is also a homecoming: “I am looking forward to returning to the continent where I've experienced so many moments of my life,” he said.
