Latest · Asia-Pacific · Cyclone Arnab28 October 2026

Cyclone Arnab Stalls Off India's East Coast: Landfall to Last Two More Hours, More Than 180,000 Evacuated and No Deaths Reported

Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab has stalled close to India's east coast, with authorities saying on Wednesday that the landfall process is expected to continue for about two more hours. The India Meteorological Department described the storm as practically stationary — its edge has moved on to land while its centre remains over the Bay of Bengal — after it began crossing earlier than expected near Yanam on the Andhra Pradesh coast. Winds of 115 to 125 km/h, gusting to 140 km/h in the latest bulletin, are forecast, with a storm surge of 1 to 1.5 metres above normal tide likely to inundate low-lying areas; earlier bulletins put gusts as high as 150 km/h and rainfall above 200 mm in parts of Anakapalli and Visakhapatnam districts. More than 180,000 people have been evacuated in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, at least 609 schools in Andhra Pradesh have been converted into rehabilitation centres, and no loss of life has been reported in either state so far, though trees have been uprooted, power supplies disrupted and roads blocked in Kakinada and Visakhapatnam. Operations at three Andhra Pradesh airports were suspended and some train and flight services cancelled. In neighbouring Nepal, authorities have warned tourists against venturing into high-altitude areas from Thursday night through Saturday, fearing the storm's remnants could trigger torrential rain, snowstorms and avalanches in the Himalayas.

Latest · Africa · Sudan War28 October 2026

Drone Strike on a Crowded Market in Nyala Kills More Than 100 People, Rights Group Says — MSF Treats More Than 150 Wounded

More than 100 people were killed and 113 injured when a drone struck a crowded market in Nyala, capital of Sudan's South Darfur province, on Monday evening, according to Emergency Lawyers, a Sudanese rights group tracking violence in the war. The group said the strike hit Tanqong Market in the Dumaya neighbourhood of Nyala, a city controlled by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, blamed the Sudanese military for the attack, and said many of those killed and injured were women and children. Doctors Without Borders (MSF) said Nyala Teaching Hospital received 36 bodies and treated more than 150 wounded patients, with victims suffering shrapnel wounds and severe burns and some brought in on tuk-tuks and donkey carts as medical teams worked through the night. Emergency Lawyers condemned the strike, saying it had turned a civilian marketplace into a scene of mass killing, and called for an independent investigation. The toll reported by the group and by the health minister of the RSF-aligned Tasis government has not been independently verified. The attack comes amid an intensification of drone warfare across Sudan, where the army and the RSF have fought since April 2023 in a conflict that has displaced millions; on Tuesday a separate drone strike damaged an electricity substation in Omdurman, disrupting power across large parts of Khartoum.

Latest · Business & Energy · Oil Markets28 October 2026

Oil Rises After Houthis Say They Hit Saudi Arabia's Biggest Refinery: Brent at $115.36, WTI at $101.12

Oil prices rose on Wednesday after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said they had hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery — the kingdom's biggest, at 550,000 barrels per day — with a missile, renewing concerns about supply risks in the Middle East. Brent crude futures rose $1.48, or 1.3%, to $115.36 a barrel by 0140 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate rose $1.22, or 1.22%, to $101.12. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the strike on the Gulf-coast facility. The rebound followed a drop of nearly $2 on Tuesday, when talks in the Middle East had eased supply worries, and came despite data expected to show a small rise in US crude inventories — stockpiles rose by about 15,000 barrels in the week ended 23 October, according to a Reuters poll of eight analysts. Looking ahead, OPEC+ producers are expected to keep their December output targets steady at a meeting on Sunday, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters, with production in most of the seven core members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman — running below target. Traders also said crude exports from Russia's Baltic and Black Sea ports could fall next month as domestic refiners increase diesel output following a deal with Washington.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War27 October 2026

Zelenskyy Says Ukrainian Forces Are Retaking Territory — Including a Pipeline Operation Near Kupiansk That Turned Russia's Own Tactic Against It

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Tuesday that Ukrainian forces are retaking territory through a series of offensives, describing Kyiv's battlefield position as the strongest in some time in the four-and-a-half-year war. In his nightly video address, citing reports from senior commanders, he disclosed for the first time an operation codenamed "Veles" near Kupiansk in the northeastern Kharkiv region, in which Ukrainian troops used underground gas pipelines to outflank Russian positions. "The Russians tried to use underground gas pipelines to advance past our positions," Zelenskyy said. "Our fighters turned this tactic against the Russians and used the underground infrastructure against the occupiers with extraordinary success." He also referred to an air assault operation in eastern Ukraine and to "Vivaldi," an offensive further north designed to prevent Russian advances toward the fortress belt of Sloviansk and Kramatorsk, where a senior commander reported last month that the Third Army Corps had cleared 75 square kilometres of Russian infiltration groups and recaptured a further 10 square kilometres. Zelenskyy said the position could be made stronger with more deliveries of air defence systems from Western allies — a pointed remark, as he has also warned that prolonged delays in delivering Patriot and NASAMS interceptors are helping Russia, after overnight attacks involving 16 ballistic missiles and more than 280 drones struck Kyiv and 11 regions.

Latest · Europe · Kherson Ceasefire28 October 2026

Kherson Ceasefire Holds With No Violations Reported — and Widens Beyond Oleshky to Clear the Way for Aid Convoys Until 1 November

The localised ceasefire in southern Ukraine that began at 4 a.m. on 27 October is holding, Ukrainian human rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets confirmed, with no violations reported by either side as of Tuesday evening. The truce, brokered with US First Lady Melania Trump acting in what the White House called an "Exclusive Intermediary" role — with Pope Leo also reported to have been involved in the mediation — was originally arranged for the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky, across the Dnipro from Kherson, so food, medicine and clean water could reach civilians and residents who wish to leave could be evacuated. Melania Trump said she had moved the ceasefire forward by two days to protect civilians and had expanded its geographical scope beyond Oleshky to other areas of Kherson Oblast to ensure the safe passage of humanitarian aid convoys. David Arakhamia, parliamentary leader of Ukraine's ruling party and its chief negotiator, said the ceasefire will remain in effect until 10:59 p.m. on 1 November and described it as "the greatest de-escalation since 2022," while Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said it could serve as a confidence-building measure for future negotiations. The truce remains limited: fighting has continued elsewhere, including Russian strikes on Kyiv, and three-way US-mediated talks are set to resume in early November.

Latest · Europe · Nordic Diplomacy28 October 2026

Finland and Norway Leaders Hold a Rare 90-Minute Call With Putin — 'Frank and Substantive,' With Big Differences Exposed

Finland's President Alexander Stubb and Norway's Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre held a rare phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Tuesday to discuss efforts to end the war in Ukraine, the Nordic leaders said — the latest attempt by Kyiv's allies to open a channel to Moscow. "The outcome of this war will have a direct impact on our and the broader European security. Ending the war requires direct discussions," Stubb said in a joint statement. Stubb told reporters in Helsinki that the idea originated at a 20 October meeting in Oslo between Støre, Stubb and President Zelenskyy, together with US envoy Steve Witkoff, with President Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner joining remotely; the Americans and Ukrainians had both thought reaching out was a good idea, and Kyiv had asked the Nordic countries to help open a channel of communication. After the Oslo talks, Witkoff travelled to St Petersburg to meet Putin. Støre, in Helsinki for a meeting of Nordic leaders, said the 90-minute call had highlighted "big differences in views" on Ukraine. The Kremlin has downplayed the significance of the call. The conversation comes as Russian attacks on Ukraine continued — including a strike on transport infrastructure in Kyiv — and as the sides prepare for US-mediated technical talks in Abu Dhabi in early November.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election28 October 2026

Israel's Count Is Under Way: Final Tally Expected by 30 October, Official Results on 4 November — and No Bloc Anywhere Near 61

Israel's Central Elections Committee has begun counting ballots from Tuesday's election — the first since the 7 October 2023 attacks — with the final count expected by 30 October and official results scheduled for publication on 4 November. Exit polls from Channels 11, 12 and 13 all left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc on about 50 seats and the opposition bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot on 52 to 53, with Arab parties projected at a record 17 to 18 — leaving neither side with the 61 seats needed to govern the 120-member Knesset. Netanyahu's Likud remained the largest single party. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar argued on X that celebrations on the left were premature, pointing to Likud's position as the biggest party. Turnout reached 77.7% when polls closed, the highest at that hour since 1999, according to the Associated Press. Under Israel's proportional system, winning the most seats does not automatically confer the right to form a government: after official results are published, President Isaac Herzog will consult the factions and nominate the lawmaker he judges most likely to assemble a coalition, who will then have up to 42 days to secure the support of at least 61 lawmakers. If successive nominees fail, Israel could face another election, with the outgoing government remaining in a caretaker capacity meanwhile.

Trump Says He Will Respect the Midterm Results 'If They're Honest Elections' — Six Days Before the 3 November Vote for Control of Congress

US President Donald Trump said on Tuesday that he would respect the results of the 3 November midterm elections if they were "honest," regardless of which party wins control of Congress. Asked by reporters as he departed for a rally in Texas whether he would accept the outcome even if Republicans lost, Trump replied: "I will, if they're honest elections." Polling continues to point to a possible blue-wave midterm, with Democrats widely favoured to win the House and holding multiple paths to the Senate. In the run-up to the vote, Trump has regularly accused Democrats of cheating without presenting evidence, alleged broad vulnerabilities in voting systems during a primetime address in July, and this week announced a $1 million reward for anyone who reports voter fraud — in past elections or the midterms — leading to an arrest or conviction. In a sign the Justice Department could challenge results, the department last week sued to block Alaska's certification of its US Senate race if Republicans lose, alleging ballot confusion because Republican Senator Dan Sullivan faces an opponent with the same name; Trump told reporters he directed the department to pursue the lawsuit.

Latest · Business & Asia-Pacific · New Zealand Rates28 October 2026

New Zealand's Central Bank Shocks Markets by Holding Rates at 2.75% in a 4–3 Vote — the Kiwi Dollar Slumps 1.7%

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand unexpectedly held its official cash rate at 2.75% on Wednesday, defying a Reuters poll in which 22 of 27 economists had forecast a rise to 3.00%. The monetary policy committee voted 4 to 3 to hold, with the three dissenters preferring an immediate increase, and the bank said committee members agreed tighter policy would probably be needed in future, conditional on the economic outlook. Inflation remains above the target range — headline CPI was 3.7% year-on-year in the September quarter, easing from 4.1% in June, and the bank forecasts it could reach 4.4% in the December quarter — driven largely by high global fuel costs, while wage growth stayed subdued and medium-term inflation expectations stable. The New Zealand dollar sank as much as 1.7% to $0.5585 on the decision. "The market just got that wrong," said Mike Houlahan, director at Electus Financial in Auckland, who also noted the country's election is only about 10 days away. The economy is recovering gradually but unevenly, with unemployment still around its highest level in 11 years. The surprise hold came as the US dollar sat at a two-week high ahead of a Federal Reserve decision, where traders price a roughly 91% probability of no change.

Latest · Global Affairs · Russia-Africa Summit & UN28 October 2026

Third Russia-Africa Summit Opens in Moscow With 48 Countries and 22 Heads of State — as the UN Chief Warns Conflict-Related Sexual Violence Cases Passed 9,700 Last Year

The third Russia-Africa Summit opened in Moscow on Wednesday, bringing together representatives of 48 African countries for two days of talks on political relations, trade, investment, energy, food security, science and technology, digital transformation and artificial intelligence. The summit is co-chaired by President Vladimir Putin and Burundi's President Évariste Ndayishimiye, the African Union chairperson; Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov said 22 countries are represented by heads of state, up from 17 at the 2023 summit in St Petersburg. Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov meets African foreign ministers on the opening day, with the leaders' plenary following on 29 October, and the meeting is expected to adopt a joint declaration, a Russia-Africa Partnership Action Plan for 2027–2029 and a declaration on cooperation in the civilian use of AI, alongside an Economic and Humanitarian Forum drawing officials, businesses and experts. Russia's foreign ministry has accused Western governments of pressuring African capitals to stay away — a charge Ghana's President John Dramani Mahama answered by saying partnerships should deliver investment and fair returns rather than be dictated by competition among outside powers; Mahama has also said he will press Putin for a ceasefire in Ukraine. In New York, UN Secretary-General António Guterres told a Security Council debate on women, peace and security that the world is experiencing its highest number of conflicts since the Second World War, that UN-verified cases of conflict-related sexual violence rose sharply to more than 9,700 last year — with the true number certainly much higher — and urged governments to strengthen women's participation in peace negotiations and restore funding for women-led organisations.
