Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz30 October 2026

Iran Says Tankers Hit Mines and Caught Fire in Hormuz, as Oman Rescues 25 Crew From a Burning Ship

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said early on Friday that three oil tankers had exploded and caught fire after striking mines while crossing the Strait of Hormuz by what Tehran calls an unauthorised southern route, and later said two further tankers had been halted after unspecified incidents. The claim has not been independently confirmed: the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency has verified only that one tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile near Khasab, Oman, on Thursday evening, with no crew injuries, while Oman's Royal Navy evacuated 25 crew from a Panama-flagged commercial vessel that caught fire off Musandam. Tehran says the ships had switched off their navigation systems and ignored Guards warnings, and also claimed to have captured a US drone, a claim the US military has denied. US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper said American forces had cleared the strait's main international routes of Iranian mines and that more than 3,000 commercial vessels had transited with US support over six months. The disruption is rippling into aviation: Air India and Air India Express have suspended flights to Jeddah, Riyadh and Dammam until 14 November, and Singapore Airlines has cancelled its Dubai services until the end of February 2027.

Latest · Middle East · US–Iran Diplomacy30 October 2026

Qatari Mediators Return to Tehran With a Draft Peace Plan as Washington Says a 'Timeout' Is Very Close

Qatari mediators were back in Tehran on Friday carrying a draft plan to end the eight-month US–Iran war, an official briefed on the talks told Reuters. The proposal, first presented to Iran on Sunday, sets out a seven-day timeline of daily, reciprocal confidence-building steps, with several countries acting as guarantors, and could begin early next week. The two sides remain far apart on sequencing: Washington wants nuclear issues on the table from the start, while Tehran insists the United States must first end its blockade of Iranian ports and lift sanctions on its oil exports, and a senior Iranian official has already dismissed the Qatari proposals as unacceptable. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says Iran's own seven conditions must be met first. Even so, US envoy Tom Barrack said late on Thursday that the two countries were "very close" to agreeing a pause in the fighting. President Trump, meanwhile, has said a recent letter from Vice President JD Vance to Tehran concerned medical supplies, following reports that it was an attempt to reopen negotiations, and senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan met Iranian leaders in Tehran on Monday as part of the same de-escalation push.

Latest · Middle East · Israel Election30 October 2026

Final Israeli Results Leave Netanyahu Eight Seats Short, With Arab Parties Holding the Balance of Power

Israel's Central Elections Committee published the final results of this month's election early on Friday, confirming Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc on 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats — eight short of the 61 needed to govern — against 48 for the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot. Netanyahu's Likud remains the largest single party with 27 seats, followed by Eisenkot's Yashar on 21 and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad on 12. The decisive numbers belong to the two Arab-led parties: the Joint List, with 11 seats, and Ra'am, with eight, together hold a record 19 seats and could make or break either bloc, although Netanyahu has called a government dependent on Arab parties unthinkable. Turnout reached 77.9%, with more than 5.7 million ballots cast, the highest in decades. The count closed amid unproven fraud claims from Netanyahu's camp, which election officials rejected; the Shin Bet security service is separately investigating suspected foreign interference in the vote. The results go to President Isaac Herzog on 4 November for coalition consultations, and the new Knesset is due to be sworn in on 10 November.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War30 October 2026

Russian Barrage Kills Around 20 Across Ukraine and Leaves More Than 200,000 Without Power in Dnipropetrovsk

Russia launched another mass overnight attack on Ukraine, firing ballistic missiles, four guided air-launched missiles and 113 drones — 83 of them jet-powered — of which Ukrainian air defences said they downed 96 drones and three missiles. A missile strike on Kyiv's Dniprovskyi district killed one person and partially destroyed a building, while in Dnipro, strikes on energy infrastructure triggered a citywide blackout; regional governor Oleksandr Ganzha said engineers had restored supply to most customers but that more than 200,000 subscribers remained without power, with grid operator Ukrenergo imposing emergency cuts. The assault followed Thursday's attacks, in which a drone hit on Dnipro's main station killed 14 people — the toll rose by one overnight — two teenage girls were killed in Chernihiv, three people died in Kremenchuk and 56 people, including ten children, were wounded across the three cities. Moscow, for its part, said its air defences shot down more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones overnight, and all four of the capital's airports briefly suspended operations. The barrage came even as the US-brokered local truce around Oleshky, in the southern Kherson region, raised tentative hopes of renewed diplomacy.

Latest · Europe · Spain Rail Disaster30 October 2026

Spain's Crashed Train Was Travelling at Least 160 kph, a Preliminary Report Finds, as the Toll Reaches 69

The Spanish train that smashed into a stationary service near Urda on Wednesday was travelling at between 160 and 200 kph at the moment of impact, according to a preliminary report by the independent accident investigation authority AITAT, in a crash that has now killed at least 69 people. The Civil Guard says it has received reports of 64 people missing, and officials said 49 autopsies had been carried out with five victims so far identified, a process they warned would be slow. Investigators are reconstructing a chain of failures: an automatic signalling fault first halted trains along the line, Madrid's central control then took over, and a state-run Renfe service carrying 379 passengers and seven crew was authorised to proceed even though a stationary Iryo train, with 147 passengers and five crew, was standing ahead of it — contrary to the protocol that the front train should have moved first. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has declared three days of national mourning, from 29 to 31 October, and King Felipe VI has visited victims. It is Spain's second fatal high-speed crash this year, after January's accident on the same Madrid–Andalusia line killed 46, and the country's deadliest rail disaster since the 2013 Santiago de Compostela derailment, in which 80 people died.

Latest · Europe · Bosnia — EUFOR Crash30 October 2026

Four Italian Peacekeepers Killed as an EUFOR Chinook Crashes on a Training Flight in Bosnia

Four Italian personnel serving with the European Union's EUFOR mission in Bosnia were killed and eleven others injured when their CH-47 Chinook helicopter came down during routine training near Gorice, in the Brčko district about 180 kilometres northeast of Sarajevo, on Thursday. All fifteen people on board were Italian nationals. Italy's defence ministry named the dead as two army sergeants, Simone Ruggia and Claudio Marchesi, and two members of the Italian Red Cross volunteer military corps, Lieutenant Franco Adriano Zecchillo and Sergeant Laura Procacci; three died at the scene and a fourth later in hospital. The eleven injured are in stable condition, and no civilians were hurt and no property damaged. The Brčko district declared a day of mourning on Friday. EUFOR and the Italian military have opened investigations, and the force has appealed against speculation about the cause. EUFOR Althea, which fields between 1,000 and 1,500 personnel, has operated in Bosnia since 2004 in support of the Dayton peace agreement, and Italy has held command of the mission since January.

Latest · Europe · European Central Bank29 October 2026

Lagarde to Quit the ECB Early, Saying She Cannot Stay Neutral, and Opens the Race for Her Successor

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde announced on Thursday that she will step down on 7 February, nine months before her eight-year term was due to end in October 2027, saying the neutrality her role demands has left her unable to speak out on matters of importance beyond monetary policy. "I don't think that I can remain neutral," she told a press conference after the bank's policy meeting. Her early departure gives sitting EU leaders time to choose a successor before France's presidential election next spring, in which polls favour the far-right candidate Marine Le Pen. European Council President António Costa said the appointment process would begin immediately and proceed without delay, thanking Lagarde, in office since November 2019 after leading the International Monetary Fund, for her service. The former Dutch and Spanish central bank governors, Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernández de Cos — now head of the Bank for International Settlements and seen by many economists as the favourite — lead the field. The succession is part of a wider reshuffle at the top of the ECB: executive board member Isabel Schnabel leaves in early January to join the IMF, and chief economist Philip Lane departs in May.

Latest · Europe · Energy30 October 2026

EU Countries Agree to Release 42.6 Million Barrels From Strategic Reserves to Cool Diesel Prices Before Winter

European Union member states have pledged to speed up the release of 42.6 million barrels of oil from their strategic reserves by the end of January, almost half of it — 20.5 million barrels — diesel, Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen said after a meeting of energy ministers in Luxembourg. Jorgensen warned that the bloc faces a difficult winter, although he saw no immediate risk to security of supply; the burden, he said, is the cost to households. The European Commission's latest bulletin put the EU's average retail diesel price at about €2.15 a litre on 26 October, up roughly 35% from late February, before the Middle East conflict disrupted supplies from the region and Russia. France is going furthest: for the first time, it is releasing reserves specifically to cut pump prices, putting 10 million barrels of diesel on the market at cost, which Economy Minister Roland Lescure said should trim 12 to 18 cents a litre from prices within days, with distributors encouraged to advertise reductions from 1 November. The European move follows the G7's agreement earlier this month to release up to 100 million barrels, completing a 400-million-barrel emergency release coordinated by the International Energy Agency in March.

Kim Jong Un Demands 'Absolute Superiority' in Drones as North Korea Shows Off Its 'Sniper' Rocket Launcher

North Korea has staged a firepower demonstration of suicide attack drones, cruise missiles and its 600mm "Sniper" multiple rocket launcher, with leader Kim Jong Un calling drone supremacy "an indispensable option" in modern warfare and artificial intelligence a top priority for his military, state media KCNA reported on Friday. The display, held on Thursday alongside an exhibition of unmanned aircraft that Kim attended with his wife and daughter, was the first public firing of the Sniper launcher, and KCNA claimed the drones and missiles struck an island target with 100% accuracy; photographs showed mock targets resembling South Korean guard posts and US Abrams tanks. The same day, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff detected around ten projectiles, including short-range ballistic missiles, fired from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea, flying about 250 kilometres — the North's 17th ballistic missile launch of the year. Seoul believes the launches involved the KN-25 super-large rocket system, which Pyongyang says can carry its Hwasan-31 tactical nuclear warheads. Analysts say the emphasis on cheap, mass-produced drones reflects lessons North Korea has drawn from the war in Ukraine.

Latest · Africa · DR Congo — Ebola29 October 2026

Last Patients Leave the Mongbwalu Ebola Centre Where Congo's Outbreak Began, but the Epidemic Is Far From Over

Health workers sang and danced as the last confirmed patients walked out of the Médecins Sans Frontières Ebola treatment centre in Mongbwalu, in DR Congo's Ituri province — the first time the facility has stood empty of cases since the outbreak was declared in May, and a poignant milestone, since Mongbwalu is where this epidemic began. Nobody is calling it the end. National figures released this week show the outbreak has passed 10,000 confirmed cases, with close to 5,000 deaths — a fatality rate near 50% — more than 2,600 recoveries, and spread across 66 health zones in eight provinces, making it the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record. It is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a rarer species for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment. The centre's medical director, Dr Richard Lokudo, cautioned that an empty ward does not mean the epidemic is beaten, and responders elsewhere describe overwhelmed facilities, community mistrust and strikes by staff over bonuses unpaid since late August. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus shared the discharge footage with the message that the outbreak is far from over, but every life saved is worth celebrating.

Latest · Americas · US Economy29 October 2026

US Economy Grows a Stronger-Than-Expected 3.4% as AI Investment Surges but Inflation Stays Sticky

The US economy expanded at an annualised rate of 3.4% in the third quarter, up from 2.2% in the spring and ahead of the roughly 3.2% economists had forecast, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said in its advance estimate on Thursday. The acceleration was powered by business investment — gross private domestic investment jumped 19.4%, with equipment spending up 20.9% as companies poured money into information-processing and AI hardware — alongside consumer spending growth of 3.8% and a 5.3% rise in government spending. Trade dragged on the headline figure: exports fell 5.7% while imports surged 19.7%, much of the increase in capital goods feeding the same investment boom. The companion inflation data was less comfortable: the PCE price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge, rose 3.6% in September from a year earlier, with core inflation at 3.1%, keeping price growth well above the Fed's 2% target a day after the central bank held its benchmark rate at 3.75–4.00%. The BEA publishes its second estimate of third-quarter growth, with corporate profits, on 25 November.

Latest · Global Affairs · United Nations28 October 2026

UN General Assembly Votes 130 to 17 to Demand an End to the US Embargo on Cuba

The UN General Assembly has again called on the United States to lift its decades-old economic, commercial and financial embargo on Cuba, adopting its annual resolution on Wednesday by 130 votes to 17, with 25 abstentions. India was among the countries voting in favour, with its counsellor Soumya Nair delivering New Delhi's statement to the Assembly. The resolution, a fixture of the UN calendar for more than three decades, is non-binding and has never changed US policy, but the widening margin — and the small minority willing to side with Washington — is closely watched as a barometer of international opinion on the sanctions regime, which Havana blames for deepening shortages of fuel, food and medicine on the island. The vote comes as Cuba's government struggles with a severe economic crisis and rolling blackouts, issues its diplomats put at the centre of their case to the Assembly this year.
