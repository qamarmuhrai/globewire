Latest · Americas · Brazil25 October 2026

Brazil Votes in a Cliffhanger Runoff: a Fourth Term for Lula, or the Bolsonaros Back in Power

Nearly 159 million Brazilians were eligible to vote on Sunday in a presidential runoff that pits the incumbent, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, against Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro. Polls that opened at 8 a.m. Brasília time closed at 5 p.m., with electronic counting expected to produce a result the same night. Flávio Bolsonaro won the October 4 first round with 47.03% of valid votes to Lula's 45.16%, and final polls — Datafolha and MDA among them — put the pair within a point or two of each other. Lula, who turns 81 next week and is seeking an unprecedented fourth term, cast himself as a shield against US interference and promised wider social protection; Bolsonaro, 45, campaigned on crime and spending cuts, telling reporters after voting in Rio de Janeiro that “the time has come for real change.” No candidate who finished second in a Brazilian first round since 1989 has gone on to win the runoff. Governors were also being chosen in five states and the Federal District.

Latest · Europe · France25 October 2026

France's Protest Toll Climbs Past 1,300 Arrests as 53 Officers Are Injured

The final count from Saturday's second weekend of cost-of-living protests in France passed 1,300 arrests, officials said on Sunday, with 53 police officers injured in clashes across the country — though the feared repeat of the previous weekend's looting and rioting did not materialise. About 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed, and the Interior Ministry said faster interventions and pre-demonstration checks kept most marches peaceful; turnout was put at 410,000 by the ministry and around 800,000 by the CGT union. In Paris, where 142 people were arrested, a largely peaceful march from Porte d'Italie ended in confrontation near Porte de Vincennes, as masked youths built barricades, smashed a car dealership's windows and hurled projectiles before police charged with batons and tear gas. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez blamed several thousand black-clad “ultra-left” activists, including some 500 identified rioters. Two protesters were seriously injured, in Toulouse and Clermont-Ferrand. The movement, which began over school conditions, has widened into a broader revolt over living costs six months before France chooses a successor to President Emmanuel Macron.

Houthi Strikes on Saudi Oil Sites and Airports Force Airlines to Extend Suspensions Into November

Air India and Air India Express have extended their suspension of flights to Riyadh and Dammam until November 7, and to Jeddah until October 31, as Yemen's Houthi movement presses a drone and missile campaign against Saudi Arabia's airports, energy installations and other critical infrastructure. IndiGo has cancelled Riyadh services until October 31 and flights to Jeddah, Dammam and Madinah until October 27, while Akasa Air has suspended all Saudi routes until October 27; affected passengers are being offered free rebooking or full refunds. The Saudi-led coalition said early on Sunday that it had intercepted and destroyed a drone over the Riyadh region, and the Houthis have warned that Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport is a military target — even as the airport resumed operations with a revised terminal split. The escalation follows Wednesday's attack on Dammam's King Fahd International Airport, which killed an Indian national and injured two others, and a separate strike the same day on Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield that injured another Indian worker.

US States Prepare to Shadow a Record 1,000 Federal Election Monitors as Obama Joins the Trail

With the November 3 midterms nine days away, the Justice Department is preparing to send about 1,000 election monitors to polling places in all 50 states — roughly triple the 2022 figure and the largest observation effort in its history — and several Democratic-led states are preparing to watch the watchers. Minnesota, whose officials deployed about 40 state employees in pairs to track federal lawyers during its August primary, expects to shadow monitors again, and California has launched a similar programme; Oregon's Secretary of State has insisted federal observers follow state rules. The department says its staff will only observe compliance with federal voting-rights law, but critics call the deployment of prosecutors a form of intimidation, and California's attorney general Rob Bonta said the federal government is itself “the source of interference.” On the campaign trail, former president Barack Obama rallied Democrats in Ohio and Michigan on Saturday, endorsing Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed in Detroit, while President Donald Trump also campaigned in Michigan.

Israel to Close All West Bank Crossings on Election Day as Hebron Operation Widens

Israel said on Sunday that every land crossing with the occupied West Bank will close on Tuesday, October 27, when the country votes, and that Palestinian workers will be barred from settlements and industrial zones through election day. The lockdown follows a Friday checkpoint attack near Hebron in which gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three, and a military push into Hebron that has displaced more than 2,600 residents by Palestinian officials' count. Defence Minister Israel Katz put the figure at about 2,000 and said those forced out would not be allowed back until Israel had finished “dealing with all the Hamas issues in Hebron,” while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu promised an operation on a scale not seen since 1967. Netanyahu faces a tight race for a new term, his security-first reputation badly damaged by the October 7, 2023 attack. The Palestinian Authority called for international pressure to protect residents caught up in the operation.

“Ashamed to Work at Google”: DeepMind Researchers Quit Over Pentagon AI Contracts

Several researchers at Google DeepMind have resigned over the company's expanding contracts to supply artificial intelligence to the US and Israeli militaries, saying they no longer trust its safeguards once models pass into classified systems. Sofia Liguori, a London-based research engineer who left this week after three years, told the BBC: “Realising my company is pushing more and more for AI systems in military operations makes me feel ashamed to work at Google.” AI researcher Andreas Kirsch, who quit earlier in October, said Google's governance was “not sufficient or meaningful enough” for military deployment, that current models are “not robust enough,” and that the company seems to have little ability to control or even observe how the Pentagon uses its technology. A Pentagon agreement signed in April allows Google's AI to be used for “any lawful government purpose,” and staff concerns have grown since the December launch of the GenAI.mil platform and Google's move to the IL6 clearance level for classified workloads. Google said it follows a consistent review process under its AI principles and acceptable-use policies, with protected channels for staff to raise concerns.

Typhoon Champi Sweeps Past Taiwan: One Dead, 527 Injured, Hundreds of Flights Grounded

Typhoon Champi, at its peak the strongest storm recorded anywhere in the world this year, brushed Taiwan's north-east coast over the weekend, killing one person and injuring 527, the island's fire department said. The centre of the storm made a rare loop offshore near Yilan around midnight, driving gusts of about 227 km/h that tore down power lines and sent roof tiles flying; a retired teacher in Yilan called it “the most terrifying” night he could remember. The single death was a man swept away by waves on the island of Liuqiu. At one point more than 288,000 homes lost electricity, though most were reconnected by Saturday, and the transport ministry counted 242 cancelled flights on Saturday — down from almost 1,000 on Friday, when hundreds of services across East Asia were grounded and more than 160 flights were cancelled in Japan alone. Taipei and much of northern and central Taiwan declared a second day of school and office closures. Champi is now tracking north along China's coast toward southern Japan; in Zhejiang province, authorities readied 400 rescue workers, put more than 41,000 firefighters on emergency duty and brought over 3,800 fishing boats back to harbour.

Latest · Africa · Uganda25 October 2026

Uganda Orders 21-Day Quarantine for Citizens Returning From Congo as Ebola Fears Spread

Ugandan citizens coming home after travelling to the Democratic Republic of Congo will now be quarantined for 21 days, Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi announced on Sunday, as the country tightens Ebola precautions while insisting it remains free of the virus. Kampala has also barred travellers with a recent Congo history from transiting through Uganda at all, and reinstated passenger locator forms for everyone entering by air, land or water, with screening stepped up at Entebbe airport and land borders. The measures follow the case of a Nigerian woman who spent two weeks in Uganda before testing positive for Bundibugyo Ebola in Abuja, where she was diagnosed on October 20; genomic analysis has linked Nigeria's case to the strain found in Kenya's index case. Baryomunsi said all 60 contacts traced from a Kenyan case had completed their monitoring period and been discharged. Congo, where the current outbreak began, is battling a fast-spreading epidemic, and Uganda has set up three treatment units across the border with 240 beds.

Cyclone Arnab Bears Down on India's East Coast; Odisha Cancels Leave and Braces for Landfall

India's east coast is preparing for Cyclone Arnab, which the India Meteorological Department expects to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Tuesday morning, October 28, as a very severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h and gusts to 140 km/h. Neighbouring Odisha has cancelled leave for district officials, positioned rescue teams and shut schools and colleges in five southern districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri — for October 28 and 29. Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said the state would take no chances despite the forecast landfall in Andhra Pradesh, restating Odisha's zero-casualty goal. Heavy rain and winds of 70–90 km/h are expected across Odisha's southern and coastal districts, and President Droupadi Murmu has postponed a planned visit to the state.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate25 October 2026

El Niño Strengthens Toward a December Peak That Could Rank Among the Strongest Since 1950

The El Niño now under way in the Pacific is intensifying fast and is expected to peak in December, the World Meteorological Organization says in its latest seasonal outlook, with sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific forecast to reach record levels. The agency's Niño 3.4 index is projected to average about 3.8°C above the reference level from November to January, up from 2.8°C in September, and forecasters see a near-100% likelihood that the event persists through February 2027 — potentially one of the strongest since at least 1950. Above-normal temperatures are likely across much of Africa, southern Europe, the Arabian Peninsula, India, East Asia and parts of North America, while shifting rainfall patterns raise the risk of drought in some regions and flooding in others. WMO chief Celeste Saulo noted that El Niño releases heat stored in the ocean rather than creating it: “The ocean it's drawing from is the warmest we've ever measured it. There is simply more heat available to release.”
