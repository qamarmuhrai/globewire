Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Trump Concedes the Cost4 November 2026

“A Part of It Was Iran”: Trump Admits the War Hurt Republicans as the Democratic Wave Settles

The morning's projections have become the evening's arithmetic. Democrats are on course for about 53 Senate seats against 42 for Republicans, with a handful still uncalled, and have passed 228 in the House on the Associated Press tally — control of both chambers, won by flipping seven Republican Senate seats in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas, and by taking House districts that voted for President Trump by 20 points two years ago. In Texas, James Talarico beat Ken Paxton in the most expensive Senate race in US history and Gina Hinojosa unseated three-term Governor Greg Abbott — the party's first statewide wins there since 1994. In Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed became the first Muslim elected to the Senate. The president's own verdict, given to Fox News and the Wall Street Journal, was unusually candid: the Iran war was “a piece of it,” he said, pointing to fuel prices as the sharpest liability, while insisting he did the right thing and would do it again. He promised vetoes for Democratic bills he called insane, praised Democrats for fighting hard, and argued the biggest factor was that he was not on the ballot. What voters told pollsters was blunter — cost of living first, then a demand that Congress check his power.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — The Supreme Leader Answers4 November 2026

“Seriously Mistaken”: Khamenei Tells Iranians the Vote Changes Nothing in Washington

Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, used the anniversary of the 1979 US embassy seizure to close a door his own negotiators had left ajar. In a written statement on Wednesday he said anyone who still hopes American behaviour will soften because power shifted in Congress — or because Tehran offers concessions — is “seriously mistaken,” describing Washington's goal as Iran's unconditional surrender. He warned that if US attacks resume, the war's scope will become “much wider,” echoing his order last week for an immediate, geographically unrestricted response. The message cuts against the more tactical reading offered the same morning by Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's chief negotiator, who called the midterm result a public “no to war” and urged Washington not to ignore it. Both men are describing the same election; they differ on what it buys. Ghalibaf treats it as leverage in the stalled Hormuz talks. Khamenei, who has not appeared in public since succeeding his father in March after US-Israeli strikes killed him, treats it as irrelevant — a reminder that in Tehran's system, the negotiating line and the final word are not the same voice.

Four Tankers Burning, Two Halted, Still No Independent Confirmation in Hormuz

By evening, Iran's claim about the Strait of Hormuz remained exactly that — a claim. The Revolutionary Guards said four oil tankers that tried a prohibited passage south of the strait struck naval mines, were destroyed and were still burning, that two other vessels had been stopped, and that a US MQ-9 drone had been downed. No ship has been named, no flag state, insurer or tracking service has confirmed a mine loss, and neither the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre nor US Central Command has corroborated it. The one incident the UK centre did log was separate and different: a tanker — identified in shipping reports as the Barbados-flagged VLCC Grit — hit by two projectiles in its engine room off Oman's Limah coast on Tuesday evening, with no casualties. Maritime security firms are warning of an elevated risk of renewed US-Iran escalation through 6 November. The unverified tally matters less for what it proves than for what it signals while a Qatari-brokered two-day reopening plan waits on Washington: Tehran wants the price of defying its control of the strait understood, whether or not this particular fire can be photographed.

Latest · Middle East · US–Israel — The Aid Cutoff3 November 2026

$3.8 Billion a Year, Ending at Year's End: Trump Says US Aid to Israel Stops — Jerusalem Says It Wasn't Told

President Trump used a Fox News phone interview on election day to announce the end of one of the most durable arrangements in American foreign policy: the roughly $3.8 billion a year in military assistance Israel receives under a ten-year memorandum signed in 2016 and running to 2028. Trump said he and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had agreed months ago to stop the payments, that Netanyahu himself raised the idea, and that Israel — “a very thriving country” — no longer needs the money, though Washington would “help Israel if they need it.” The Israeli side of the story does not yet match. Reporting in Israel says the Finance Ministry first learned of the plan from the broadcast, a spokesman for Netanyahu told NBC News he knew nothing of the remarks, and the existing agreement still has two years to run — no order, bill or budget request giving effect to the announcement has been made public. If it happens, the arithmetic is immediate: Israeli commentators put the hole at about 12 billion shekels a year in a defence budget already strained by three years of war, with weapons on order and stocks to replenish.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — 310 Drones in One Night4 November 2026

310 Drones, Six Regions, One Target System: Russia's Nightly War on Ukraine's Grid Intensifies

Ukraine's air force counted 310 attack drones launched overnight into Wednesday — 81 of them jet-powered — aimed overwhelmingly at energy infrastructure. Air defences shot down or suppressed 272, but strikes were recorded at 19 locations across Volyn, Zhytomyr, Ternopil, Odesa, Kirovohrad and Rivne regions, with debris falling at 15 more. In Volyn's Kovel district an energy facility was hit and power cut widely before crews reconnected every settlement by morning; in Zhytomyr region, facilities and private homes were struck across three districts. Russia's defence ministry separately claimed hits on power stations in the Kyiv and Zhytomyr regions and on a cargo vessel near Odesa. The state grid operator Ukrenergo says energy sites were attacked more than 20 times in the preceding 24 hours alone, a campaign that began in late September ahead of the heating season. It comes a week before US-brokered talks resume in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, and a day after a mass missile-and-drone strike shut three of DTEK's five thermal plants. Germany's Chancellor Merz has urged Moscow to engage seriously; the drones are Moscow's counter-argument, delivered nightly.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — Brazil's Roadmap3 November 2026

No Deadlines, No Mandates — But a First Map: Brazil Publishes a Global Plan to Leave Fossil Fuels

Brazil has closed its year as president of the UN climate process by publishing what no government has produced before: a global roadmap for phasing out fossil fuels, paired with a second roadmap for halting and reversing deforestation. The documents were the compromise that saved last November's COP30 summit in Belém, where a fight over whether the final text should name fossil fuels at all ended with the presidency promising to write the plan itself. The fossil-fuel roadmap — called the TAFF Mutirão, after an Indigenous term for collective effort — sketches a voluntary three-stage path to 2033: national transition plans by 2028, infrastructure and renewables build-out by 2030, then accelerated replacement of coal, oil and gas. Suggested first steps are practical — cut methane flaring and venting, phase out fossil subsidies, expand clean power. The criticism arrived on cue: the plan is non-binding, sets no deadlines or benchmarks, and leaves each country to diagnose its own dependence. COP30 president André Corrêa do Lago argues that is the point — “We don't need more negotiations. What we need is for governments to start taking action” — five days before COP31 opens in Antalya, Turkey, on 9 November.

Latest · Europe · Spain — Minister Falls3 November 2026

Two Crashes, 115 Dead, One Resignation: Spain's Transport Minister Quits Ahead of the Snap Election

Spain's transport minister, Óscar Puente, resigned on Tuesday, six days after a high-speed collision near the village of Urda, south of Madrid, killed 69 people — and less than a year after another crash on the same Madrid–Seville line killed 46. Together the two disasters have taken 115 lives on what is the world's second-largest high-speed rail network. The Urda crash happened after a signalling failure automatically halted trains along a 63-kilometre stretch; a control centre then authorised a state-run Renfe service to proceed, and it ran into the back of a stationary private Iryo train. Puente told lawmakers he had decided to go within minutes of hearing of the crash but stayed until the emergency response, the first investigations and a compensation package were under way — a €30 million aid fund for victims and new rules requiring drivers to slow to 40 km/h after any signalling failure, with services on the line reduced until the safety agency completes its review. He also withdrew from the snap general election Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has called for 29 November. Investigators have yet to establish the collision's direct cause.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Voting Day4 November 2026

Polls Close Tonight on the ANC's Hardest Local Test: Dry Taps, Power Cuts and a Record Electorate

South Africans have been voting since before sunrise in municipal elections that pollsters expect to deliver the African National Congress its weakest local result since it took power in 1994. A record 29.2 million voters are registered across 23,696 stations to choose councils for eight metros, 44 districts and 205 local municipalities — the tier of government blamed for dry taps, power cuts and uncollected rubbish. The ANC won 45.6% in the last local elections in 2021 and 40.2% nationally in 2024; surveys now place it between roughly 30% and 34%, still the largest party but short of control in most big cities. In Gauteng, the Democratic Alliance leads polling in Johannesburg, Ekurhuleni and Tshwane without clearing 40% in any of them; in Durban's eThekwini metro, former president Jacob Zuma's MK party — contesting its first local elections — polls above 40%. Polls close at 9 p.m. local time. The electoral commission has a week to declare final results, after which parties get two weeks to form councils — meaning the coalition bargaining, not the vote count, will decide who actually runs South Africa's cities.

AQI Near 380 and Climbing: Delhi Enters Diwali in “Very Poor” Air for a Third Straight Day

Delhi's smog season is running ahead of its festival. Neighbouring Gurugram and Noida are in the same category, and forecasts keep the region between very poor and severe for days. Stage-II of the Graded Response Action Plan is already in force across the National Capital Region, and the city's winter framework — including a 50% work-from-home arrangement running from November into February — has begun. Readings climbed from 117 on 31 October to severe within four days, and Diwali fireworks have not yet started. For a city of more than 30 million, the question is no longer whether the air turns hazardous this month, but how long it stays there.

Latest · Americas · Florida — The Learjet Inquiry3 November 2026

The Tail Was Found Miles Away: Florida's Learjet Was Breaking Up Before It Hit the Ground

Federal investigators now have physical evidence that the Learjet 60 which crashed near Greenacres, Florida, on Sunday — killing all nine people aboard — was coming apart in the air. The National Transportation Safety Board said the majority of the jet's left tail elevator, the surface that controls pitch, was recovered in three pieces scattered between 1.3 and 3.8 miles from the crash site, a finding its board member Thomas Chapman called “very significant”. The aircraft, flying from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas with seven passengers and two crew, plunged from about 35,000 feet through stormy South Florida weather; the crew had reported ice forming on the jet, and icing is a major focus of the inquiry, alongside whether the in-flight breakup caused the dive or resulted from it. The crash displaced close to 600 residents and left a water-filled crater that crews are now draining; investigators are still hoping to recover the cockpit voice recorder. The victims included a family of four travelling with their nanny and the 20-year-old co-pilot, a West Boca Raton High School graduate. It is the second-deadliest US aviation accident this year; a preliminary cause is not expected soon.

Latest · Europe · Aviation — The Hoax Count Rises4 November 2026

Closer to Ten Flights Than Seven: Europe Counts a Day of Bomb Threats That Found Nothing

The tally from Tuesday's wave of bomb threats across European aviation has grown as authorities compared notes: around ten flights were targeted within hours, alongside a cruise ship evacuated on the Greek island of Syros — and after searches on every aircraft, not a single device was found. Two flights were bound for Warsaw, including a LOT service from London Heathrow diverted to Modlin airport, which briefly closed, and an SAS flight from Copenhagen; two Ryanair services were searched at Rome's Fiumicino, the Dublin flight escorted in by Italian Eurofighters; two flights were checked at Sofia, one arriving from Israel after an emailed threat; and a Ryanair flight from Manchester triggered an emergency protocol on landing at Murcia in Spain. London's Metropolitan Police said it had been alerted to threats against four aircraft linked to Heathrow. Ryanair took the largest share, with four flights affected. No one has claimed responsibility and officials have pointed at no country, but aviation-risk analysts call seven-plus confirmed threats in a single day exceptional, and list the working hypotheses plainly: a single hoax sparking copycats, one group making many threats, or a state-backed hybrid-warfare operation designed to impose cost and rehearsal on Europe's security system. Every flight landed safely; the disruption was the payload.
