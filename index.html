Latest · Middle East · Iran Talks in Doha1 November 2026

Iranian Negotiators Spend a Third Day in Doha as Israel Completes Preparations for a Possible Renewed War

Diplomacy and military planning moved in parallel on Sunday. An official briefed on the discussions told Reuters that Iranian negotiators were in Doha for a third consecutive day of talks with Qatari mediators aimed at ending the US-Israeli war on Iran, following Qatari visits to Tehran on Friday and talks in Doha on Saturday. President Donald Trump has said Washington will not launch new attacks before Tuesday's US midterm elections and that talks have been productive, without ruling out further action afterwards. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump has not decided whether to order new strikes and would prefer a diplomatic outcome. Tehran, for its part, says it will answer any new attack by striking US and Israeli targets in neighbouring countries, and Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei on Saturday ordered the armed forces to be ready to respond. Against that backdrop, Israel's Yedioth Ahronoth reported that the Israeli military has completed both defensive and offensive preparations for a renewed confrontation — including rehearsal flights by dozens of fighter jets and refuelling aircraft through Syrian and Iraqi airspace towards Iran's borders — and that US Central Command chief General Brad Cooper has presented Trump with options for resuming attacks, attended by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman Dan Caine. No decision to resume operations has been made, the paper said, and ten US transport aircraft have arrived at Israeli bases in recent days as part of an airlift of equipment.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen Displacement1 November 2026

More Than 300,000 Flee Fighting in Western Yemen as the Saudi-Led Coalition Strikes Houthi Targets and Threats Against Aramco Are Renewed

The United Nations said on Sunday that more than 300,000 people have fled their homes in western Yemen as fighting intensifies between the Houthis and Saudi-backed government forces. Almost all were displaced in the past two months and urgently need shelter, food, clean water and health care, according to the International Organization for Migration, which is providing medical aid in five locations and says it needs $45 million over the next six months to keep operating. Yemen slid back into war in July after four years of relative calm, when the fragile 2022 truce fell apart; hostilities escalated in September with a Houthi offensive in the west. The city of Taiz is again reported to be surrounded, with acute shortages of fuel, food, water and medicine, and the World Health Organization warning of disease risk in a governorate already among the worst hit by cholera and home to about 126,000 displaced people. On Sunday the Saudi-led coalition said it had carried out a precision operation to neutralise vital resources used by the Houthis, releasing footage it said showed strikes in Hodeidah and Dhamar, and said it intercepted a missile and a drone launched towards Saudi Arabia's southern region. Houthi media, citing an unnamed military official, repeated threats against Saudi oil giant Aramco and aviation in the kingdom. The UN says more than 1,600 people have been killed since fighting reignited; an earlier UN humanitarian assessment put displacement since August at 290,000, including 6,100 pregnant women, and verified 270 civilian casualties.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza1 November 2026

Israeli Drone Strikes Kill Eight Palestinians in Gaza, Including Five Police Officers and a Four-Year-Old Boy

At least eight Palestinians were killed in Israeli drone attacks across Gaza on Sunday, hospital sources said, in the latest strain on the US-brokered ceasefire that took effect in October last year. Five Palestinian police officers were killed and several people wounded in al-Mawasi, west of Khan Younis in the south. Gaza's Interior Ministry said the officers were performing routine duties patrolling an area sheltering displaced civilians, and put the number of police and security personnel killed since the ceasefire began at at least 75. The Israeli army said its attack in the south was targeting a militant. In northern Gaza, three people — including a four-year-old boy, reported in some accounts as a father killed alongside his child — died in a drone strike on the Rimal neighbourhood of Gaza City, according to sources at al-Shifa Hospital. Tanks also fired towards areas south and east of Khan Younis. Gaza's Health Ministry figures cited in reporting put the toll since the ceasefire at 1,580 killed and 5,711 wounded, and the overall Palestinian death toll since October 2023 at more than 74,500 — figures the UN considers reliable.

Latest · Global Affairs · Crypto Heist1 November 2026

Binance Resumes Withdrawals After Covering 95% of the $6.3 Billion Stolen in the World's Largest Digital Heist, Which the FBI Blames on North Korea

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, resumed withdrawals on Sunday after covering about 95% of the $6.3 billion in tokens stolen from its wallets last week — a theft analysts describe as possibly the biggest financial robbery ever. The first phase reopened 15 cryptoassets including bitcoin, ether, Solana, XRP and dogecoin from 07:36 ET, and by 10:30 ET the exchange said it had processed about $4.6 billion in withdrawals against $1 billion in deposits. Binance said it bought replacement tokens using its roughly $1 billion Secure Asset Fund for Users, its Futures insurance fund and its corporate treasury, setting aside about $6.34 billion internally, and insisted it — not users — would absorb the financial impact. The remaining 5% consists of less liquid tokens that will take longer to source. The FBI said on Saturday that TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored unit active since at least 2020, carried out the 29 October attack across ten blockchain networks; investigators observed the stolen assets being converted mainly into ether and expect further laundering through decentralised-finance and instant-swap services. A coalition of crypto firms has frozen about $3 billion of the stolen assets. The bulk of the theft unfolded in around 20 minutes late on Thursday, and Binance's preliminary assessment linked the breach to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, though investigators are still examining how the attackers got in. President Trump said on Saturday the US was still not certain North Korea was involved. TraderTraitor was previously blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025; researchers at TRM Labs estimate $2.9 billion was stolen across nearly 150 crypto attacks last year.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India Voter Rolls1 November 2026

Thousands Rally in New Delhi Demanding India's Election Chief Resign Over a Voter-List Revision That Removed More Than 130 Million Names

Thousands of protesters marched in India's capital on Sunday to demand the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, in the latest of more than a month of near-daily demonstrations against a nationwide revision of electoral rolls. Led by Congress leader Rahul Gandhi and backed by the opposition INDIA bloc — with Sonia Gandhi, Akhilesh Yadav, Mamata Banerjee and Tejashwi Yadav among those at the Ramlila Maidan rally — protesters chanted against the government as hundreds of police with batons, shields and riot-control vehicles secured the site and traffic was diverted. Gandhi told the rally Kumar would have to vacate his post as the first step to putting the country back on the right track. The Special Intensive Revision has removed more than 130 million names from voter lists since last year — more than the population of Japan. Opposition parties allege the deletions disproportionately affected Muslims and other minorities unlikely to support Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party; one Congress student-wing member said four members of her family were deleted on the false claim they had moved away. The Election Commission denies favouring any party, says the exercise removes duplicate, deceased and ineligible entries, and says wrongly removed voters can re-register. The BJP accuses the opposition of undermining democracy by challenging legitimate procedures. Human Rights Watch said on Thursday the accusations raise serious questions about the fairness of future elections. The protests began after the Indian Express reported that Kumar's two deputies had raised internal concerns about the revisions, registration procedures and digital systems, and have drawn support from student organisations and the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party. India's next general election is due in 2029.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh Garment Unrest1 November 2026

Bangladesh Sets Up a Wage Board as Garment Protests Shut 529 Factories and Leave Nearly a Million Workers Idle

Bangladesh's government formed a six-member wage board on Sunday to review garment workers' pay and urged employees to return to work, but protests demanding higher wages continued across the country's main industrial belts. Workers are demanding a minimum monthly wage of 30,000 taka (about $245) — more than double the current 12,500 taka — saying the rising cost of food, housing, transport and healthcare has sharply eroded their purchasing power. The Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association said the unrest has forced 529 factories to shut and left 987,190 workers out of work. Police fired tear gas and water cannon after demonstrators threw bricks at officers, and a police vehicle and a police box were set on fire; in Gazipur, Border Guard Bangladesh platoons were deployed until 7 November and at least 45 factories declared holidays, while several hundred workers blocked Dhaka's Mirpur-10 intersection from around 9am. The unrest follows Saturday's demonstrations in Ashulia, the manufacturing hub outside Dhaka where dozens of factories first suspended operations. Labour and Employment Minister Ariful Haque Choudhury said the board — two representatives each from workers and employers, plus a government representative and a neutral member, headed by Senior District and Sessions Judge Sheikh Ashfaqur Rahman — was formed after talks with labour leaders and industry representatives and would begin work immediately. The garment sector is Bangladesh's key export industry, which makes the shutdowns an economic as well as a labour crisis.

Latest · Europe · Spain Train Disaster28 October 2026

Spain Mourns 69 Killed in a High-Speed Train Collision Near Urda, as Investigators Point to Four System Failures and a Cambridge Scientist Is Named Among the Dead

Residents of the village of Urda, in Toledo province, packed the church of San Juan Bautista on Saturday for a memorial Mass for the victims of Wednesday's high-speed train collision, which killed at least 69 people in one of Europe's deadliest rail disasters this century. The crash happened when a state-operated Renfe AVE service travelling from Madrid to Seville at about 160 km/h slammed into the rear of a stationary Iryo train near Urda, some 130 kilometres south of Madrid; more than 540 people were aboard the two trains — 397 on the Renfe service and 146 passengers plus an infant on the Iryo train — and more than 100 were injured, with dozens hospitalised and 15 in intensive care among the 34 still in hospital at the last update. Authorities have completed autopsies on all 69 bodies, with identifications still being finalised and families notified in stages. Among the dead was Dr Marta Shahbazi, a Cambridge cell-biology and embryology group leader at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, who was travelling to collect a prestigious award and attend a conference in Andalusia; her death was confirmed late on Saturday by the CiMUS research centre in Santiago de Compostela. A preliminary investigation reported by the Daily Telegraph found as many as four systems failed before the crash, with the failure of the LZB train-protection system and decisions at Madrid's control centre — including clearing a stalled train to restart at up to 160 km/h — at the heart of the inquiry. Transport Minister Óscar Puente has attributed the collision to human error and rejected calls to resign. Spain declared three days of national mourning, high-speed services between Madrid and Andalusia remain suspended, and the disaster is the country's second major rail crash this year after January's collision near Adamuz killed 46 — and its deadliest since Santiago de Compostela in 2013.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War1 November 2026

Ukraine Says It Liberated More Ground in October Than Russia Seized, as Oleshky Evacuees Reunite With Families and the Front Sees 264 Clashes in a Day

President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Ukraine's forces liberated 96.2 square kilometres of territory in October — a net gain of 10.4 square kilometres over what Russian forces captured in the same month — and inflicted record losses, which he put at 54,400 Russian troops killed, seriously wounded or missing in October, with a daily record of 2,250 on 21 October. Kyiv's General Staff reported 264 combat engagements on 31 October, the heaviest in the Pokrovsk sector where 55 attacks were repelled, alongside 74 Russian airstrikes dropping 230 guided bombs, 6,505 kamikaze drones and 2,533 other attacks; Ukraine's tally of Russian personnel losses since February 2022 reached about 1,590,270, up 2,100 in a day. Zelenskiy also confirmed Ukrainian strikes on the Kamensky chemical plant in Russia's Rostov region, involved in missile and rocket-fuel production, plus a Pantsir-S2 air-defence system and an S-400 command vehicle he valued together at $70 million. Behind the front, the human story of the week completed its arc: evacuees from the occupied town of Oleshky, who had lived with hunger and no utilities, reached Ukrainian-held territory on Saturday after a 19-hour journey by bus through Crimea to Anapa, a flight to Gomel in Belarus and a crossing in Chernihiv region, under a rare local ceasefire facilitated by Pope Leo XIV and US First Lady Melania Trump. About 750 people had registered; 11 buses headed to Ukraine and four to Russia. The oldest evacuee was 94 and the youngest just over one, families arrived with dogs, cats, a parrot and even a raccoon, and greeters held signs reading “You're home” and “You are Ukraine.” Fewer than 2,000 of Oleshky's pre-war 24,000 residents remained before the evacuation; some chose to stay, without gas, electricity or running water, as the six-day truce expired on Sunday.

Latest · Americas · US Midterms — Final Weekend1 November 2026

Final Weekend of the US Midterms: Democrats Lead by Eight Points Nationally, More Than 43 Million Have Already Voted, and the Senate Hinges on a Handful of Tight Races

Candidates spent Sunday making their closing appeals before Tuesday's vote, with the cost-of-living crisis — deepened by the US-Israel war on Iran — at the centre of the argument. The RealClearPolling generic-ballot average stands at 51.5% for Democrats to 43.1% for Republicans; a THP/MM survey puts the gap at 54% to 41% and an Emerson College poll at ten points. All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are in play. Democrats need a net gain of three House seats and four Senate seats; Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, and because Vice President JD Vance breaks a 50–50 tie, Democrats need 51 for outright control. Forecasts updated on Sunday project a median 233 Democratic House seats with a 93% chance of a majority, and 52 Senate seats at 71% — with ranges wide enough for a Republican hold in either chamber. State polls underline the uncertainty: in Kansas, a Washington Post–Schar School poll has Democrat Adam Hamilton leading Senator Roger Marshall 52% to 43%, while Emerson finds 50% to 47%, and prediction markets still narrowly favour Marshall; in Texas, Democrat James Talarico leads Republican Ken Paxton — by 49% to 43% in a CNN poll — in a state Democrats have not won a Senate race in since 1988; in Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed leads Mike Rogers by up to seven points. Polling averages tracked elsewhere show Iowa's Senate race at just +0.2 for the Democrat. President Trump's approval sits at about 37% against 60% disapproval in aggregated polling, and a Marquette Law School poll in Wisconsin found 37% approve to 61% disapprove. More than 43 million Americans had voted early by Friday, well above 2022 levels, and analysts warn close races may not be called on election night while mail and provisional ballots are counted.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria Runoff1 November 2026

Bulgarians Vote in a Presidential Runoff Split Over Russia, After Black Sea Drone Attacks on Shipping Left 21 Missing

Bulgarians voted on Sunday in a presidential runoff between two candidates offering opposite answers on Russia and the war in Ukraine. President Iliana Iotova, 61, who favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine, faces Andrey Gyurov, 50, a banker and former caretaker prime minister backed by the pro-Western opposition parties Continue the Change and Democratic Bulgaria. Iotova led the 25 October first round with 34.79% to Gyurov's 31.43% among 27 candidate pairs — far short of the outright-win threshold — but the gap was much narrower than the 20-point lead polls had predicted for her, and first-round turnout was a record low of about 23%. Polls opened at 7am and close at 8pm, with voting extendable to 9pm where queues remain; exit polls follow at close, official results are due within three days, and the winner takes office when the current term ends on 22 January 2027. The campaign was overtaken by a series of drone attacks on commercial vessels off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast in October that left 21 people missing, sharpening debate about whether the war is spilling into the waters of an EU and NATO member state. Analyst Teodor Slavev told AFP voters were delivering a verdict on Iotova's unconvincing response to the Russian threat in the Black Sea. Iotova, formerly vice-president and a Bulgarian Socialist Party figure until 2016, became president in January when Rumen Radev resigned; she is backed by Radev's Progressive Bulgaria and would be the country's first elected female president if she wins. Her running mate is Bulgarian News Agency head Kiril Valchev; Gyurov's is Georgi Kandev. Election officials noted voting machines would not reach three diaspora stations — Tenerife, Montreal and Atlanta — in time.

Latest · Global Affairs · G20 in Atlanta31 October 2026

G20 Foreign Ministers End Their Atlanta Meeting Without Consensus — No Joint Communiqué, and the Group Photo Cancelled Over Lavrov

The foreign ministers of the Group of 20 largest economies wrapped up two days of talks in Atlanta on Saturday without consensus on the central security questions before them, and without a joint communiqué. In its place, Secretary of State Marco Rubio issued a chairman's statement that announced no agreements, listing instead shared priorities from energy and food security to freedom of navigation. “I wouldn't pretend to you today that we are close to finding universally accepted solutions to these problems,” Rubio told reporters, while pointing to cooperation among some members on supply chains and critical minerals. The divisions ran through Russia's war on Ukraine, the US-Israeli war against Iran, US tariffs and Chinese activity in the South China Sea. Rubio had opened the meeting with an appeal for unity, then cancelled the traditional group photograph after European diplomats objected to the inclusion of Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and threatened to boycott or walk out. Poland's Radosław Sikorski, attending a G20 meeting as a guest for the first time, said the failure surprised no one: “We have wars going on, and we have participants in those wars around the table, so it's very difficult to agree on a common line.” Diplomats said a chairman's statement had long been the plan given the depth of disagreement, and that members nonetheless agreed to work towards making next month's G20 leaders' summit, hosted by President Trump in Florida, a success. On the sidelines Rubio met counterparts from Australia, India and Japan, and the final session focused on freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red, Black and South China seas, with critical trade routes and mineral supply chains framed as essential to growth.

Latest · Europe · London Protest — Final Toll1 November 2026

Final Count: 325 Arrested Outside the US Embassy in London for Backing Banned Group Palestine Action, Taking the Total Past 4,000

London's Metropolitan Police confirmed on Sunday that 325 people were arrested at Saturday's mass protest outside the US Embassy in Nine Elms in support of Palestine Action — a sharp upward revision from the preliminary figure of 139 reported while the action was under way. Of the total, 320 were detained for expressing support for a proscribed organisation under the Terrorism Act, an offence carrying a maximum sentence of 14 years. Hundreds had gathered with placards reading “I oppose genocide, I support Palestine Action,” in an action organised by Defend Our Juries under a Section 35 dispersal order covering the embassy area from 8am to 11pm. A smaller rally outside the US consulate in Edinburgh produced no arrests at the time, though Police Scotland said enquiries continue to identify people seen wearing t-shirts and holding banners supporting the group. Palestine Action was proscribed in July 2025 after actions against defence facilities, notably those of the Israeli arms company Elbit Systems, and a break-in at RAF Brize Norton in which two military planes were damaged; it sits on the blacklist alongside Hamas and Hezbollah, and the United States sanctioned it as a terrorist organisation in August. Organisers say Saturday's arrests take the total detained for defying the ban past 4,000 — including many older demonstrators, and, campaigners note, Holocaust survivors. The legal fight now moves to the Supreme Court, which begins a two-day hearing on Wednesday on the appeal against the designation: the High Court ruled the ban unlawful in February, before the Court of Appeal found it justified and proportionate in June.
