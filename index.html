Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Nasdaq Nears Bear-Market Ground25 November 2026

Tariff Shock and AI Rout Send Investors Scrambling for Safe Havens

Global markets endured another bruising session on Wednesday as Washington’s proposal for fresh tariffs on 14 trading partners landed on top of a faltering artificial-intelligence trade. The tech-heavy Nasdaq fell 2 percent on the day and is now down almost 20 percent from its early-October record, while Wall Street’s fear gauge, the VIX, has surged more than 60 percent in November. Investors moved into government bonds instead: benchmark US 10-year yields eased to 4.84 percent and German yields slipped to 3.22 percent. Chip shares have been hit hardest, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 50 percent from its June peak, and US markets now close on Thursday for Thanksgiving before a shortened Friday session.

Latest · Americas · New York — Guilty on 10 of 12 Counts25 November 2026

US Jury Convicts Huawei of Bank Fraud and Conspiring to Steal Trade Secrets

A federal jury in Brooklyn has found Chinese telecoms giant Huawei and three subsidiaries guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including bank fraud, wire fraud, money-laundering conspiracy and conspiring to steal trade secrets, while acquitting the company of racketeering conspiracy and obstruction of justice. Prosecutors argued over a trial lasting more than two months that Huawei misled banks including HSBC about its Iran business through its Skycom unit and took technology from US firms including Cisco, T-Mobile and Quintel. The case dates to a 2018 indictment; sentencing is scheduled for 14 September 2027, when fines, forfeiture and restitution will be decided. Huawei welcomed the racketeering acquittal and noted the trade-secret disputes had previously been handled as civil matters.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Power Cut in Five Regions25 November 2026

Russian Strikes Kill at Least Five in Ukraine and Damage Energy and Gas Sites

Overnight Russian attacks across Ukraine killed at least five people, wounded dozens and knocked out electricity in five regions, Ukrainian officials said on Wednesday. Strikes halted generation at a DTEK thermal plant, badly damaged its equipment and set a Naftogaz gas production site in the Poltava region ablaze, forcing it to stop work. In Odesa, Governor Oleh Kiper said two people were killed and three others, including a child, were injured as cars and private and industrial buildings were hit, while in Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said drone debris set the lower floors of a 16-storey apartment block on fire and injured two people. A morning strike on Zaporizhzhia also hit a residential building, injuring six people including two children.

Latest · Africa · Blue Nile State — 19 Dead, 20 Wounded25 November 2026

Drone Strikes Kill 19 Civilians in Sudan’s Blue Nile State, Rights Group Says

Drone strikes blamed on Sudan’s paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed 19 civilians and wounded 20 others in Blue Nile state on Tuesday, the Sudanese rights group Emergency Lawyers said on Wednesday. All 19 deaths, including six women, were reported in the state capital Damazin, where drones hit three residential neighbourhoods late on Tuesday night. Earlier the same day, five civilians were wounded in nearby Roseires, where the group said drones targeted the town’s dam and power facilities. The attacks mark a further escalation in the drone war between the RSF and Sudan’s army, a conflict under way since 2023 in which drones now account for the large majority of civilian deaths.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Canberra — A$740 Million Over Ten Years25 November 2026

Solomon Islands Signs Sweeping Treaty Making Australia Its Primary Security Partner

Solomon Islands and Australia signed a comprehensive treaty in Canberra on Wednesday under which the Pacific nation affirms it will host no foreign military or security base and names Australia its primary security partner. Canberra will provide A$740 million (about $490 million) over ten years, funding free education and other programmes, alongside support for policing and border management. The pact also requires Honiara to consult Australia before new security or sensitive-infrastructure deals outside the Pacific Islands Forum, though it does not apply retrospectively. The signing follows reports that Solomon Islands will let its 2022 security pact with China lapse when it expires in early 2027, a deal that had raised fears of a Chinese naval foothold in the South Pacific.

Latest · Europe · Canary Islands — More Than 2,100 Arrivals in a Day25 November 2026

Migrant Arrivals in Spain’s Canary Islands Break Single-Day Record

More than 2,100 migrants reached Spain’s Canary Islands in overcrowded boats within a single 24-hour period, maritime rescue data showed on Wednesday — a new daily record that the regional government described as an emergency. The latest crossings lift arrivals so far this year to 28,103, already above last year’s total, though still well short of the all-time annual high of 46,843 set in 2024. The Atlantic route from West Africa to the archipelago remains one of the busiest and most dangerous migration corridors into Europe.

Latest · Africa · Niger — Taken at the Weekend25 November 2026

Saudi National Kidnapped by Suspected Islamic State Militants in Niger

Militants believed to belong to an Islamic State affiliate active in West Africa have kidnapped a Saudi national in Niger and are holding the person hostage, two security sources said on Wednesday. The abduction took place at the weekend, on either 21 or 22 November, according to the sources. No group has publicly claimed responsibility and no ransom demand has been reported. Niger sits at the centre of a worsening Sahel insurgency in which jihadist groups linked to both Islamic State and al Qaeda regularly target soldiers, officials and foreign nationals.

The head of Barrick Mining’s newly separated international division, Sebastiaan Bock, met Mali’s Prime Minister Abdoulaye Maiga in Bamako on Wednesday — the first visit by a senior Barrick executive since the company’s standoff with the military-led government. Latest · Asia-Pacific · London & Manchester — Two Flights Cancelled25 November 2026

PIA Moves UK Flights to Its Own Aircraft After Norse Atlantic Suspends Operations

Pakistan International Airlines will operate its UK routes with its own Boeing 777 fleet from 26 November after wet-lease partner Norse Atlantic Airways abruptly suspended operations, the carrier said on Wednesday. Two services were cancelled at short notice — PK 786 from London Heathrow to Islamabad and PK 710 from Manchester to Lahore — after Norse notified PIA of the suspension without prior warning, just as passengers were preparing to board. PIA said affected travellers would be accommodated on its own aircraft as the schedule is rebuilt around the in-house fleet.

UN Warns of Further Atrocity Crimes in Gaza as Aid Crises Deepen in Yemen and Congo

The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights warned on Wednesday that new Israeli attacks in Gaza and the occupied West Bank point to further atrocity crimes being committed, leading a day of grim humanitarian updates from the United Nations.
