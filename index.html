“Maybe Tomorrow”: Trump Hints at Fresh Iran Strikes While Floating a Deal

President Donald Trump refused to say on Saturday whether the United States would resume strikes on Iran, telling reporters in Florida that an attack could come as early as Sunday — or that Washington might instead reach what he called a “nice, big, beautiful agreement” with Tehran. Speaking after casting his midterm ballot, Trump questioned whether Iran would honour any deal, a day after warning at a Florida rally that the US could launch “another little Epic Fury,” the name of the campaign that opened the war on February 28. Tehran has denied reports that it offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for lifting the US naval blockade. The eight-month conflict remains at a stalemate, with anxiety growing that strikes could resume around Israel's October 27 election or the November 3 US midterms.

Latest · Middle East · Israel25 October 2026

Israel to Close All West Bank Crossings on Election Day as Hebron Operation Widens

Israel said on Sunday that every land crossing with the occupied West Bank will close on Tuesday, October 27, when the country votes, and that Palestinian workers will be barred from settlements and industrial zones through election day. The lockdown follows a Friday checkpoint attack near Hebron in which gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three, and a military push into Hebron that has displaced about 2,000 residents. Defence Minister Israel Katz said those forced out would not be allowed back until Israel had finished “dealing with all the Hamas issues in Hebron,” while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu promised an operation on a scale not seen since 1967. Netanyahu faces a tight race for a new term, his security-first reputation badly damaged by the October 7, 2023 attack. The Palestinian Authority called for international pressure to protect residents caught up in the operation.

Source: AFP via The Hindu

Four Palestinians Killed in Gaza as Ceasefire Violations Mount

Israeli attacks across the Gaza Strip killed four Palestinians, including two women and a child, and wounded eight others on Saturday, medical sources told Anadolu Agency. A child died of wounds from army gunfire in Deir al-Balah, a woman was shot dead west of Khan Younis, and another woman was killed in the Tal al-Dhahab area of Beit Lahia. A young man died in a strike on Gaza City's al-Rimal neighbourhood, where witnesses said a drone fired on people near Palestine Stadium, while naval vessels shelled the city's coastline. Gaza's Health Ministry says Israeli fire has killed 1,547 Palestinians and wounded 5,559 since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025, with the overall toll since October 2023 now above 74,500 dead.

US Tells Citizens to Reconsider Travel to Saudi Arabia and UAE, Trims Embassy Staff

The United States has renewed Level 3 “reconsider travel” advisories for Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, citing a rising threat of missile and drone attacks on American interests by Iran and the Houthis, and is reducing staff at its missions in Jordan, Lebanon, Qatar and the UAE. The updated guidance, publicised on Saturday, keeps Level 4 “do not travel” warnings in place for Iran, Iraq, Lebanon, Syria and Yemen, and warns Americans across the region to prepare emergency departure plans that do not rely on US government help. A State Department official said the Houthi attacks “have the potential to escalate rapidly beyond that immediate area,” pointing to recent strikes on Saudi airports, including one on Dammam's King Fahd International Airport that killed two people. Officials denied the staff reductions were linked to possible US strikes on Iran.

Twelve Injured, Six of Them Children, in Shooting Near Florida Stadium

At least 12 people were injured in a shooting late on Saturday near Florida A&M University's Bragg Memorial Stadium in Tallahassee, during the university's homecoming weekend. Police said 11 of the injured had gunshot wounds; six were adults and six were juveniles, the youngest aged 11, and a twelfth person — a victim's grandmother — was hurt while fleeing. One victim was in critical condition and two others seriously injured. The gunfire broke out just before 8 p.m. near Perry and Manatee streets after a fight between two or three people, which Police Chief Lawrence Revell called an isolated incident. No arrests had been made. Officers carried victims to hospital through the crowds, and Revell appealed for witnesses to come forward.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Taiwan24 October 2026

Typhoon Champi Sweeps Past Taiwan Leaving One Dead and 527 Injured, Heads Toward China

Typhoon Champi, at its peak the strongest storm recorded anywhere in the world this year, brushed Taiwan's north-east coast on Saturday with gusts of about 227 km/h, killing one man swept away by waves on Liuqiu island and injuring 527 people. The storm made a rare loop offshore near Yilan before turning north, tearing down trees and power lines; at one point more than 288,000 homes lost electricity, though most were reconnected. Taiwan's transport ministry said 242 flights were cancelled on Saturday, down from nearly 1,000 on Friday, and northern and central districts including Taipei kept schools and offices shut for a second day. Champi is now tracking up the Chinese coast toward southern Japan. China's emergency ministry has readied 400 rescue workers in Zhejiang province, where more than 3,800 fishing boats returned to harbour and island visitors were evacuated.

Cyclone Arnab Bears Down on India's East Coast; Odisha Cancels Leave and Braces for Landfall

India's east coast is preparing for Cyclone Arnab, which the India Meteorological Department expects to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on Tuesday morning, October 28, as a very severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h and gusts to 140 km/h. Neighbouring Odisha has cancelled leave for district officials, positioned rescue teams and shut schools and colleges in five southern districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri — for October 28 and 29. Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said the state would take no chances despite the forecast landfall in Andhra Pradesh, restating Odisha's zero-casualty goal. Heavy rain and winds of 70–90 km/h are expected across Odisha's southern and coastal districts, and President Droupadi Murmu has postponed a planned visit to the state.

Latest · Global Affairs · Technology25 October 2026

Google DeepMind Researchers Quit Over Pentagon and Israel Military AI Deals

Several researchers at Google DeepMind have resigned in protest at the company's expanding military work, saying its artificial intelligence is being handed to the Pentagon and Israel's military without adequate safeguards. London-based research engineer Sofia Liguori, who quit this week after three years, told the BBC that realising Google was pushing AI into military operations made her “ashamed to work at Google.” AI researcher Andreas Kirsch, who left earlier in October, argued that Google has little ability to control or even monitor how its models are used once they enter classified systems. The anger centres on an April agreement allowing Google's AI to be used for “any lawful government purpose,” including classified work, and on Project Nimbus, the cloud contract supplying Israel's government and military. Google says it follows a consistent review process and its acceptable-use policies, and offers staff protected channels to raise concerns.

Latest · Europe · Business25 October 2026

Europe's Luxury Giants Shed €251 Billion as Chinese Shoppers Pull Back

Europe's six biggest listed luxury groups have lost a combined €251 billion in market value this year as the Chinese demand that powered a decade of growth continues to fade, the Financial Times reported. LVMH alone is down €131.6 billion and Hermès €93.3 billion, with Kering, Richemont, Prada and Moncler also caught in the slide. Third-quarter results from LVMH, Hermès and Kering this month missed even lowered expectations: LVMH said fashion and leather goods sales to Chinese clients fell by a mid-teens percentage worldwide, while Kering reported a 15–20% drop in sales to Chinese consumers. China's property slump and weak stock market have left monthly retail sales growth averaging under 0.5% over the past six months, and analysts now warn the industry faces a multiyear downturn rather than a pause.

Source: Financial Times via Traders Union
