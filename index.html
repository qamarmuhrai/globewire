Latest · Middle East · Gulf of Oman — Blockade Enforcement27 November 2026

US Forces Disable Ship That Tried to Run Naval Blockade of Iran as Tehran Vows to Break It

The US military said on Friday that its aircraft had disabled a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after the ship tried to run the American naval blockade of Iranian ports. US Central Command said precision munitions struck the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier M/V Pacific Archer after it sailed from Bandar Abbas and ignored repeated warnings; the ship’s propulsion was knocked out and its civilian crew was unharmed. Ship-tracking data listed Singapore as the vessel’s destination. Washington says the Pacific Archer is at least the 15th ship disabled since the blockade began in the spring, and the sixth since it was reimposed in July, while 158 compliant vessels have been redirected. An adviser to Iran’s supreme leader responded that Tehran would keep the Strait of Hormuz shut and work to break the embargo, as Iran’s Revolutionary Guards separately reported a tanker hitting a mine in the strait.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Black Sea Ceasefire Offer27 November 2026

Kyiv Offers Black Sea Ceasefire to Protect Grain Exports as Zelenskyy Rebukes Trump Over Putin

Ukraine has formally proposed a ceasefire in the Black Sea to restore freedom of navigation and keep grain exports moving, Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha announced on Friday at the “Food from Ukraine” conference, saying Kyiv is awaiting Moscow’s response. Russia has sought to fold oil shipments into any maritime truce, while Kyiv argues energy belongs in a separate, wider ceasefire track. The offer came as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, in a BBC interview broadcast the same day, sharply criticised US President Donald Trump for calling Vladimir Putin “my friend” at their recent meeting in China. “It’s strange. He is an aggressor and a killer. He can’t be a friend,” Zelenskyy said, likening it to calling Hitler a friend during the Second World War. He also complained of a severe shortage of Patriot and other anti-ballistic missiles, and argued Washington cannot act as a neutral mediator. The Kremlin called his remarks insulting, while Trump defended his approach, saying he wants a deal that stops the killing.

Latest · Middle East · Yemen — Strike Reported in Taiz27 November 2026

Strike Kills 11 People, Most of Them Children, in Yemen’s Taiz, Houthi Media Say

An air strike hit a residential building in al-Turbah, in Yemen’s southwestern Taiz governorate, on Friday, killing 11 people, most of them children, according to media affiliated with the Houthi movement, which blamed Saudi Arabia. The Houthi-run health ministry said the building, described as a former cinema partly used as lodgings by ice-cream vendors, was struck early in the morning; rescue teams recovered eight bodies, with others still missing under the rubble. The Saudi-led coalition made no public comment on the Taiz report, though it said it had destroyed a Houthi drone launched towards the Riyadh region the same day. The reported strike came a day after a Houthi attack on the Jazan oil refinery in southern Saudi Arabia killed a Nepali worker, part of a near-daily exchange of fire since Yemen’s civil war reignited over the summer. The casualty figures could not be independently verified.

Latest · Americas · Canada — Supreme Court Ruling27 November 2026

Canada’s Top Court Largely Upholds Quebec Ban on Religious Symbols for Public Workers

Canada’s Supreme Court ruled on Friday that Quebec’s secularism law, known as Bill 21, can largely stand, ending a seven-year legal fight over the first law of its kind in North America. The 2019 law bars people in certain public jobs — including teachers, police officers, judges and prosecutors — from wearing religious symbols such as hijabs, turbans, kippahs and crucifixes at work. Chief Justice Richard Wagner wrote that the law is rooted in Quebec’s “legitimate and historical commitment” to the secular character of the state, while acknowledging its “stigmatizing and deleterious effects,” identified as falling most heavily on Muslim women. The court found Quebec had validly used the constitution’s notwithstanding clause, which lets governments override some Charter rights for renewable five-year periods, and said courts cannot add extra limits to that power. It struck down only one element: the rule requiring faces to be uncovered will not apply to members of Quebec’s National Assembly, because it infringes democratic rights the clause cannot override. The National Council of Canadian Muslims called the ruling “a dark day in our constitutional democracy.”

Latest · Europe · Spain — Snap Election27 November 2026

Sánchez Vows to Defy the Polls as Spain’s Election Campaign Ends Before Sunday Vote

Spain’s political parties wrapped up campaigning on Friday ahead of Sunday’s snap general election, with Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez insisting he can still win despite polls pointing the other way. Speaking to SER radio, Sánchez said he could not conceive of losing and argued that internal data showed the Socialists on course to win the most votes and rebuild a left-wing majority with smaller allies. The last surveys published before a legal ban on polling instead favoured a coalition of the conservative People’s Party, led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo and polling around 30–31 percent, with the far-right Vox party of Santiago Abascal on 17–18 percent — a combination that would end eight years of Socialist government. Housing dominated Thursday night’s final four-way televised debate: Sánchez, who called the election after Congress rejected his emergency housing reforms, attacked his rivals over home costs, while Feijóo promised a million new homes. Closing rallies were held in Madrid, Seville and Valencia on Friday evening, before a legally required day of reflection on Saturday.

Latest · Europe · Russia — Naval Build-Up and Libya Convoy27 November 2026

Putin Unveils Hypersonic-Armed Nuclear Submarine as Russian Convoy to Libya Turns Back

Russian President Vladimir Putin attended a flag-raising ceremony on Friday aboard the Perm, a new nuclear-powered submarine that Moscow says is the first fitted with Zircon hypersonic missiles as standard, at the Sevmash shipyard in Severodvinsk. The Yasen-M class boat, the fifth delivered in six years, will join the Pacific Fleet, and Putin said five more missile-carrying submarines — including three of the same class — would reach the navy by 2030. The display of naval expansion came as a Russian flotilla carrying military vehicles towards Libya turned around in the Mediterranean, according to military and security sources cited by Reuters. The convoy reversed course after Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in Benghazi port on 22 November, some 2,000 km from the Ukrainian battlefield; a second flotilla, escorted by the frigate Admiral Golovko, also turned back near the Strait of Sicily and was reported paused off Algeria.

Latest · Americas · United States — Air Force Cloud Breach27 November 2026

Hackers Breach US Air Force’s Flagship Cloud One Platform, Sources Say

Hackers have broken into Cloud One, the US Air Force’s flagship cloud computing platform, according to three people familiar with the matter cited by Reuters. The intrusion was discovered within the past month during work to upgrade the system and has caused disruption across the platform. Cloud One underpins a wide range of sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data, which is why officials are treating the breach as potentially serious. Reuters reported that the identity of the intruders, the full extent of the access they obtained and whether any data was taken have not been established.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangladesh — Garment Orders Shift27 November 2026

Global Fashion Brands Shift Nearly a Tenth of Orders From Bangladesh to India Amid Unrest

Major fashion retailers including Zara, H&M, Marks & Spencer and Celio diverted nearly a tenth of their monthly orders out of Bangladesh in the first half of November, factory owners told Reuters, with most of the business going to neighbouring India and some to Vietnam, Cambodia and Indonesia. The diverted orders were worth about $254 million, while the unrest behind the shift has cost Bangladesh’s garment industry more than $400 million in lost production, according to the Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association. The sector is the backbone of the economy, accounting for more than 80 percent of export earnings and nearly 40 percent of manufacturing jobs, so buyers’ caution carries outsized weight. Inditex, Zara’s owner, declined to comment, and the other brands named did not respond to questions.

Latest · Africa · South Africa — Johannesburg Mayor27 November 2026

Johannesburg Elects Mashaba as Mayor in ANC-Backed Coalition That Shuts Out Election Winner

Johannesburg’s city council elected Herman Mashaba as mayor on Friday, returning the ActionSA leader to a job he held from 2016 to 2019, this time under a power-sharing deal that keeps the African National Congress influential in South Africa’s economic hub. Mashaba won 170 votes to 97 for the Democratic Alliance’s Helen Zille. No party won a majority in the 4 November local elections, and the coalition — spanning the ANC, ActionSA, the Economic Freedom Fighters and the MK party, among others — shuts out the pro-business DA even though it won the most votes. The ANC’s national vote share fell to about 31 percent in the elections, a steep decline for the party that ended apartheid. The Johannesburg coalition will control a council budget of nearly 100 billion rand (about $5.8 billion), and the same Gauteng arrangement also settled the mayoralties of Tshwane and Ekurhuleni.

Italy Says Drone That Killed Two Technicians in Kuwait Was Made in Iran

Italy’s Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said on Friday that the drone used in the attack in Kuwait that killed two Italian technicians was manufactured in Iran, hardening Rome’s position a day after Tehran denied any involvement. The two men, employees of the German defence contractor Rheinmetall, were killed on Thursday while maintaining air-defence equipment, in an incident Kuwait has protested formally to Tehran. Iran’s foreign minister has called the strike “suspicious,” suggested a false-flag operation and offered a joint investigation. Qatar’s emir sent condolences to Italy’s president and prime minister over the deaths. In Islamabad, meanwhile, officials from Türkiye, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia met under the Mecca defence pact, condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and pledged to speed up support for the kingdom, while Britain’s maritime agency reported a separate incident involving a cargo vessel and military forces in the Gulf of Oman.
