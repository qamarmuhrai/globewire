Latest · Middle East · Iran19 October 2026

Iran Says Its Seven-Day Plan to Reopen Hormuz Is “Still on the Table” as It Reviews Washington’s Reply

Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said Tehran’s proposed seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz remains “still on the table,” while confirming that Iran is still reviewing the response it received from the United States and that no agreement has been reached. Under the sequence Iran submitted in September, after an interim ceasefire collapsed, the strait would reopen on the sixth day and negotiations would begin on the seventh. Tehran’s conditions include lifting the US blockade of its ports, removing sanctions on Iranian oil exports and ending hostilities on all regional fronts. The United States has meanwhile renewed its warning to Americans in the Middle East to exercise “heightened vigilance,” citing possible flight cancellations and airspace closures. Iran maintains the strait is completely closed; Washington says commercial vessels have continued to operate through it.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has proposed a summit next month with US President Donald Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping to negotiate an end to the war in Ukraine, the Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, citing people familiar with the discussions. China’s Growth Picks Up to 4.5% in the Third Quarter, Beating Forecasts

China’s economy grew 4.5% year on year in the third quarter, official data showed on Monday, rebounding from a more-than-three-year low of 4.3% in the previous quarter and beating the 4.4% forecast in a Reuters poll. Growth for the first nine months of the year reached 4.6%, keeping Beijing within reach of its annual target of 4.5%–5.0%, while quarterly growth accelerated to 1.2%. The National Bureau of Statistics figures ease pressure on policymakers to deliver more aggressive stimulus, but the recovery remains uneven: exports and factory output have carried growth while domestic consumption and private investment stay weak, reflecting Beijing’s strategy of shifting credit from property toward high-tech industry. China’s trade surplus widened more than expected in September as exports surged, keeping the country on course to surpass last year’s record surplus and likely fuelling further trade friction with major partners.

Indonesian Markets Reel After MSCI Warns of Possible Expulsion From Its Indexes

Indonesia’s markets face a tense week after index provider MSCI warned it may remove the country from some of its benchmarks altogether, a threat that triggered an 11.6% plunge in Jakarta stocks on Friday — the benchmark’s biggest one-day fall in nearly three decades — and pushed year-to-date losses to 40%. MSCI said it is considering moving Southeast Asia’s largest economy to “standalone” status, a more severe step than the frontier-market downgrade it had threatened in January, citing a lack of progress on long-standing concerns over ownership opacity and trading behaviour. Indonesian regulators on Friday announced sanctions against those linked to alleged trading manipulation in three companies, but analysts say Jakarta must now prove its reforms are real and sustained.

Guatemala Declares 30-Day State of Emergency After Prison Riots and Police Killings

President Bernardo Arévalo has declared a 30-day state of emergency — a state of siege — after gang-linked inmates seized control of three maximum-security prisons, took dozens of guards hostage, and suspected gang members then killed at least seven police officers in retaliatory attacks across the capital. Hundreds of anti-riot police stormed the facilities, including the Renovación prison in Escuintla, and freed all the hostages; one gang member also died in the clashes and at least ten officers were wounded. The riots were a protest against the withdrawal of privileges for leaders of the Barrio 18 and MS-13 gangs, and Arévalo said the attacks were meant to intimidate the state into reversing its crackdown. The emergency restricts freedoms of movement, assembly and protest and allows detentions without prior court approval; it takes effect immediately but still requires congressional approval. Arévalo declared three days of national mourning, schools were suspended nationwide, and the army has been deployed to support the police.

Alberta Votes Today on Whether to Start Down the Path Toward a Separation Referendum

Albertans go to the polls on Monday in a ten-question referendum whose most closely watched item, Question 10, asks whether the province should remain part of Canada or instruct its government to begin the legal process toward a binding vote on separation. Polls are open from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Mountain Time, and the result is not binding. Every major poll since September has put “remain in Canada” far ahead — 70% to 22% in a Léger survey in early October, and 66% to 34% among decided voters in the latest Angus Reid polling. Premier Danielle Smith opposes separation and says she will vote to remain, while urging yes votes on the other nine questions, covering immigration and constitutional changes, to pressure Ottawa; she has also said she will not call another separation referendum if the remain side wins. Prime Minister Mark Carney campaigned in Alberta in the final days, calling the separation path a “dangerous gamble” and comparing it to Brexit.

President Donald Trump’s approval rating has fallen to 33%, with 66% of Americans disapproving, in a Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll released on Sunday — his lowest mark in the outlets’ polling across both of his terms, down from 37% in April and July. Around three in four Americans disapprove of Trump’s handling of the cost of living and inflation, and voters blame the Iran war and its effect on fuel prices.

Seven people, including organisers and private security staff, have been arrested in connection with the stampede at the Chorbagan Durga Puja pandal in north Kolkata that killed 19 people — among them 13 women and two minors — and injured 25 on Saturday evening. Latest · Africa · DR Congo18 October 2026

Congo’s Ebola Outbreak Is a Strain With No Approved Vaccine, as Kenya’s Cases Include Health Workers

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is being driven by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment, leaving health authorities to rely on isolation, contact tracing and infection prevention while vaccine research continues. The outbreak, declared in May, has become the largest and deadliest in Congo’s history and the second-largest globally after West Africa’s 2014–2016 epidemic; it has now reached 66 health zones across eight provinces, with cumulative totals of 9,415 confirmed cases and 4,536 deaths. Across the border, Kenya had confirmed seven cases as of October 17, including three health workers who treated the country’s first patient — a Kenyan man who travelled from Congo through Uganda and died on October 5. Uganda, which recorded 20 cases earlier in the year, declared its own outbreak over in August after 42 days without a new infection.
