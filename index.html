Paris Bans Protests Around Landmarks as Macron Rebukes Trump Over France's Unrest

French police have banned demonstrations across central Paris on Thursday, closing off the Élysée Palace, both houses of parliament, the Louvre and the Champs-Élysées, along with the Bastille and République squares and the Châtelet-Les Halles district that looters ransacked at the weekend. The ban runs from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. as the government, which admits it briefly "lost control" during the worst street violence in years, promises a massive security operation for further protests called on Saturday. What began in September as demonstrations over education and the cost of living has spiralled into nationwide rioting: some 2,500 people have been detained, 174 of them jailed and 917 formally prosecuted, according to Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin, while curfews remain in force in parts of several cities and labour unions have called a nationwide strike for 5 November. Speaking in Qatar on Wednesday, President Emmanuel Macron accused foreign leaders of exploiting the crisis with remarks he called "as unacceptable as they are stupid", after President Donald Trump claimed without evidence that France was being "conquered, looted and burned to the ground" by migrants; Macron said there was no visible foreign interference in the unrest itself, though he condemned foreign manipulation of information, including AI-generated images of scenes that never happened. India's embassy in Paris has advised its nationals to exercise caution while the restrictions last.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate21 October 2026

UN Warns 'Wrecking Ball' El Niño Will Push Pacific Temperatures to Record Highs

The El Niño weather pattern now under way will become extremely strong and drive central Pacific ocean temperatures to about 3.8 degrees Celsius above normal between November and January, the World Meteorological Organization said in a new seasonal outlook on Wednesday — beyond anything in its records. "We have not seen in our records 3.8. We have seen around 3 in some record El Niños before, with very substantive impacts across the world. So we are beyond that limit," WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said. The agency forecasts a near-100 percent likelihood that the event persists through February 2027, potentially making it one of the strongest since at least 1950, peaking around December. UN humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher called it a "behemoth" and a "wrecking ball" that will tear through Latin America, eastern and southern Africa, Asia and the Pacific with extreme heat, droughts and floods, and both officials urged governments to prepare now. The WMO expects flood risks to rise in southern Africa from November, hotter and drier conditions to strain agriculture and water supplies in Southeast Asia, and heavier rainfall across parts of Latin America; researchers at the University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab have estimated the event could cause as many as 451,000 excess deaths worldwide between June and February 2027.

Beatings, Cyberattacks and Threats Darken Serbia's Politics Days Before Pivotal Election

Activists, student protesters and opposition figures in Serbia describe a widening campaign of intimidation — beatings by police, masked officers bundling critics into unmarked cars, cyberattacks, social media bans and threats — as the country heads into an election on Sunday, 25 October, that poses the biggest challenge yet to Aleksandar Vučić's 12-year hold on power. A Reuters investigation published on Thursday recounts how popular commentator Marko Marjanović was seized on his way home from a supermarket in Belgrade and held in solitary confinement for two days in a police station basement under bright lights, months after officers beat him during anti-government protests; he and his lawyer describe a deliberate effort to silence him. Vučić, now a former president seeking to return as prime minister, and his Serbian Progressive Party deny accusations of repression and corruption, and his office and the interior ministry did not respond to requests for comment. The student-led protest movement that erupted after the Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse killed 16 people in 2024 is fielding 250 candidates of its own, and a poll this week put the students neck and neck with the ruling party.

Latest · Middle East · Energy & Shipping22 October 2026

Gulf Oil Flows Climb Above Pre-War Levels — but Tanker Attacks Are at a Record High

Middle East crude exports have rebounded to an average of 17.3 million barrels a day so far in October — above the 16.9 million averaged in the two months before US–Israeli strikes on Iran began on 28 February — yet analysts warn the recovery should not be mistaken for normality, with prices still near five-month highs and investors bracing for another escalation. Flows through the Strait of Hormuz have risen to 11.7 million barrels a day from 9.75 million in September, helped by convoys sailing close to the Omani coast under US naval protection, though that is still only about 82 percent of pre-war levels and roughly a third of crossings continue to use the Iran-controlled northern route. The danger is intensifying rather than fading: the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency counted 12 attacks in or around Hormuz last week, a record high, and believes the true number is higher because many shipowners do not report incidents. Saudi shipments have also recovered rapidly through the Red Sea port of Yanbu after an attack by Iran-backed Iraqi militias on a key pipeline crippled flows for weeks, while on Thursday Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed to have set four oil cargo ships ablaze in the northern Gulf and said three more vessels had struck mines near Hormuz.

Latest · Americas · US Politics21 October 2026

Trump's Approval Falls to 36 Percent as Democrats Open Double-Digit Lead Before Midterms

President Donald Trump's approval rating has fallen to 36 percent — the lowest recorded for him in the New York Times/Siena poll across both of his terms — with 59 percent of voters disapproving less than two weeks before the 3 November midterm elections. The survey of 3,494 voters, conducted from 12 to 19 October, put Democrats ahead of Republicans by about ten points in a named-candidate congressional ballot test and by five points across the 35 states holding Senate races. Voters were harshest on the economy: 69 percent disapproved of Trump's handling of the cost of living and 73 percent of his handling of gas prices, while nearly two-thirds disapproved of his handling of the war in Iran, which has pushed the national average petrol price above $4 a gallon. More than two-thirds of independent voters disapproved of his performance, though 82 percent of Republicans still approved. A separate CNN/SSRS survey put Trump's approval even lower, at 32 percent. Republicans currently hold a thin 218–214 majority in the House and lead the Senate 53–47, and the polls suggest Democrats are well placed to retake the House while the Senate fight remains tighter.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Energy Markets22 October 2026

Asia Buys More LNG Despite Near-Record Prices as Winter Demand Beats Cost Fears

Asia's imports of liquefied natural gas are on track to reach a three-month high in October even as spot prices have surged to their most expensive in almost four years, according to data compiled by commodity analysts Kpler. Arrivals across the world's top-importing region are estimated at 23.31 million metric tons, the strongest since July and the second-highest monthly total since the Middle East war began in February, when the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz largely cut off Qatar — the world's second-biggest LNG exporter, with about a fifth of the global market — from its buyers. Spot LNG for delivery into north Asia reached $29.30 per million British thermal units in the week to 16 October, the highest since December 2023 and nearly triple the $10.40 level that prevailed before the war. China, usually a price-sensitive buyer, is forecast to take 5.47 million tons, up from 5.20 million in September, a sign that stocking up ahead of the northern winter is outweighing the price shock; Japan's imports, by contrast, are expected to fall to a five-month low of 4.47 million tons, with utility inventories running 36 percent above last year's levels. The strengthening El Niño adds a further layer of uncertainty to demand forecasts for the months ahead.

Modi and Poland's Tusk Hold Talks in New Delhi as Both Sides Target $10 Billion in Trade

Prime Minister Narendra Modi held bilateral talks with his Polish counterpart Donald Tusk at Hyderabad House in New Delhi on Thursday, reviewing the India–Poland Strategic Partnership and looking for new momentum in defence, trade and investment, emerging technologies and renewable energy. Tusk, who arrived on Wednesday evening for a three-day visit — his second to India as Polish prime minister, after 2010, and the first by a Polish premier in 16 years — is accompanied by Finance and Economy Minister Andrzej Domanski and a large business delegation, with business forums planned in the capital and in Bengaluru. The two countries aim to lift annual bilateral trade from about $6 billion to $10 billion, and the leaders were also expected to exchange views on the Russia–Ukraine war and on strengthening the India–EU strategic partnership. Earlier on Thursday, Tusk paid tribute to Mahatma Gandhi at Rajghat, and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar called on him to discuss deepening cooperation across a wide range of areas. Bilateral ties were elevated to a Strategic Partnership during Modi's visit to Poland in August 2024.

Bank of Korea Pauses at 3.00 Percent After Back-to-Back Hikes, With Another Rise Forecast for November

South Korea's central bank left its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 3.00 percent on Thursday, pausing after quarter-point increases in July and August — its first consecutive hikes in more than three years — while keeping the door open to further tightening. All 34 analysts surveyed by Reuters had expected the hold. Headline inflation eased to 2.9 percent in September from 3.1 percent in August but remains above the bank's 2 percent target, and Governor Shin Hyun Song reiterated at an IMF event on 13 October that policy will stay restrictive until inflation converges to target. The economy's export engine is powered by global spending on artificial intelligence: shipments from January to September topped $800 billion for the first time ever. With growth expected to stay firm and inflation above target, the median forecast in the Reuters survey has the central bank raising its base rate to 3.25 percent at its November meeting, in line with its own projections.

Dollar's Slide Pauses Ahead of Central Bank Meetings as France's Borrowing Costs Hit New Highs

The dollar's recent downdraft took a breather on Thursday as investors settled in ahead of a crowded week of central bank meetings, with hawkish holds expected in Japan, the United States and Europe. Rate rises are expected elsewhere: the Reserve Bank of Australia's deputy governor Andrew Hauser said the inflation outlook was looking "materially worse", partly because of high oil prices, while the Bank of Canada is also expected to hike next week, leaving the Canadian dollar near a one-month top.

Chinese Quant Fund Lingjun Draws $300 Million From Overseas Investors as AI Re-Rates the Sector

The offshore arm of Beijing-based quantitative fund Lingjun Investment has attracted at least $300 million from US and Southeast Asian investors, a top executive told Reuters, as the firm resumes fundraising two years after Chinese regulators restricted its trading. Lingjun's assets fell to around 50 billion yuan ($7.49 billion) after the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges barred it from trading for three days in early 2024 for breaking rules, during a broader regulatory crackdown that blamed computer-driven funds for worsening a market selloff. Chairwoman and founder Cai Meijie said the Hong Kong unit, Lingjun Capital, has secured the money from a US institutional investor and several Southeast Asian clients, is in advanced talks over a further $150 million allocation from a Southeast Asian sovereign wealth fund, and is actively marketing to investors in the Middle East. She credited the success of AI startup DeepSeek — incubated by rival quant fund High-Flyer — with sparking a re-rating of Chinese assets and changing how global investors see the sector: "It's increasingly about how AI can be applied in investment," she said.
