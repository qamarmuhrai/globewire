Tibet Earthquake Injuries Rise to 76 as Rescuers Race Snow, Aftershocks and Rockslides

The number of people hurt in Thursday night's magnitude 7 earthquake in China's Tibet region has climbed to 76, local authorities said at a Friday press conference, while the confirmed death toll stayed at 16. The quake struck at about 10:33 p.m. local time with its epicentre in Nagqu, a sparsely populated city in northern Tibet some 4,800 metres above sea level, and was the strongest to hit China since January 2024. At least 339 homes collapsed, and officials warned that cracked or leaning buildings could fall in aftershocks, 413 of which have been recorded. Nagqu's acting mayor said rescue teams had completed three rounds of house-by-house searches by Friday afternoon, with more than 5,000 people from three counties deployed and over 12,000 residents relocated. Emergency deliveries included 2,000 prefabricated housing units, about 3,400 tents, folding beds, flashlights and winter clothing — supplies that matter more with intermittent snowfall forecast over the next three days. President Xi Jinping ordered all-out search and rescue efforts, and trains on the affected stretch of the Qinghai–Xizang railway resumed at reduced speeds on Friday morning.

Netanyahu Orders a Full West Bank Lockdown After Hebron Checkpoint Gunfight Wounds Five Israelis

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered Israeli forces to seal off Hebron immediately and prepare a full lockdown of the occupied West Bank after two Palestinian gunmen opened fire near a checkpoint north of the city on Friday morning, wounding five Israelis — two of them critically — before being shot dead in a prolonged exchange of fire. Israel's ambulance service said all five wounded were in their 20s. The army said troops raided homes of the assailants and other suspects and detained two Palestinians, while witnesses said the shooting lasted about 20 minutes, complicating evacuations. Hamas praised the attack without claiming responsibility. The lockdown comes four days before Israel's general election, with Netanyahu facing a tight race; he cancelled Likud campaign events on Friday and said troops had prevented a far larger attack, pointing to a large quantity of ammunition found in the gunmen's car. Far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for the Palestinian Authority to be dismantled. Tensions had already been running high across the West Bank, with attacks on Palestinian olive harvesters reported ahead of the vote.

Source: AFP via The Hindu
Latest · Asia-Pacific · Weather23 October 2026

Typhoon Champi Shuts Taiwan's Schools and Stock Market, Grounds More Than 900 Flights

Taiwan closed schools and offices across most of the island and shut its stock market on Friday as Typhoon Champi brushed past with strong winds and torrential rain. The storm, which reached super-typhoon strength earlier in the week with winds of 298 kph, had weakened to about 155 kph as it approached from the southwest, with its centre expected to stay offshore before curving north toward Japan's southwestern islands. The transport ministry said all 262 domestic flights were cancelled along with hundreds of international services. One person was killed after being swept away by waves on Liuqiu island on Thursday and 94 others were injured, according to the fire department, while almost 1,300 people were evacuated from vulnerable mountain areas. Parts of Taipei recorded more than 200 millimetres of rain in 24 hours, raising flood and landslide warnings. In Japan's Okinawa prefecture, a woman in her 70s broke a leg in the wind on Miyako island and nearly 2,000 homes on Ishigaki lost power.

Latest · Europe · France23 October 2026

Gunman Kills One and Wounds Four Near Paris as Prosecutors Open a Terrorism Investigation

A man opened fire from a moving vehicle in the towns of Trappes and La Verrière, west of Paris, on Friday morning, killing one person and seriously wounding four others before being arrested in the nearby town of Coignières. France's national anti-terrorism prosecutor's office has taken over the case, investigating murder in connection with a terrorist enterprise, with the added question of whether victims were targeted because of their race, ethnicity, nationality or religion. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said shots were fired at three locations with what appeared to be a military-grade or automatic weapon. Trappes mayor Ali Rabeh said the gunman fired at passersby near a doctor's surgery, a children's playground and a petrol station, calling it a miracle that more people were not killed. A 12-year-old girl was among the wounded and is in stable condition; two men, aged 29 and 49, were in critical condition. President Emmanuel Macron expressed deep sorrow and thanked police for the rapid arrest. The attack comes as France braces for further cost-of-living protests this weekend after riots last weekend.

Source: AFP via CNA
Latest · Europe · Black Sea23 October 2026

Romania Blames Russia After a Drone Strike Sinks a Cargo Ship, Killing One Sailor With Three Missing

Romanian President Nicușor Dan accused Russia of escalating its attacks on NATO's eastern flank on Friday after a drone strike sank a cargo ship off Romania's Black Sea coast, killing at least one sailor and leaving three missing. The Comoros-flagged vessel Khoryv, carrying 13 Ukrainian and Azerbaijani crew, sent a distress signal 13 nautical miles off the port of Constanța, just beyond Romanian territorial waters. Nine sailors were rescued from the water and one body was recovered, while rescue vessels searched choppy seas in high winds; poor visibility prevented helicopter deployment. Video released by Romanian authorities showed only a small part of the capsized hull still above the waves. The sinking is part of a sudden two-week surge in attacks on commercial shipping off Romania and Bulgaria, with at least seven cargo ships hit and some 30 sailors reported dead or missing. NATO said on Tuesday it would send more maritime patrol aircraft, radar systems and drones to the Black Sea, and Ukraine, Romania and Bulgaria have called on the alliance to do more to protect merchant shipping, much of it using the Danube-linked Sulina Canal that has become Ukraine's main export route.

Latest · Global Affairs · Diplomacy23 October 2026

Seven Foreign Ministers Meet in Istanbul in a First-of-Its-Kind Gathering on Regional Wars

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan hosted his counterparts from Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Britain, France and Germany in Istanbul on Friday for talks on the conflicts straining the region, the first meeting in this seven-country format, held at Turkey's initiative. The agenda covered the Israeli-Palestinian issue, the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, developments in Yemen, efforts to end the Russia-Ukraine war, and closer cooperation between regional and European states, according to a Turkish diplomatic source. Fidan was expected to press for a diplomatic solution to the conflict between the United States and Iran, stress freedom of navigation through Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, and restate Turkey's support for Saudi Arabia's security against Houthi attacks under the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signed by Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia. Ankara also planned to highlight its work to keep the Ukraine war from spilling further into the Black Sea, where attacks on merchant shipping have intensified in recent weeks.

US Orders Embassy Staff and Families Out of Saudi Arabia as Houthi Attacks Intensify

The US State Department has ordered the departure of non-emergency staff and their families from Saudi Arabia, citing intensifying attacks on the kingdom by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement. State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott said the Houthis have increasingly targeted civilian airports and other critical infrastructure, causing casualties and severe travel disruption; he said the formal order applies to Saudi Arabia only, though officials told Reuters that US missions in other Gulf states would also be sending family members home. The Riyadh embassy, badly damaged at the start of the Iran war, had been running on a skeleton staff for months. Houthi strikes have repeatedly hit Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, which was closed from October 10 after a severe attack, although a limited number of Saudi airline flights resumed this week while carriers such as Lufthansa and Air France have cancelled services into mid-November. On Wednesday, debris from an intercepted drone aimed at an oil facility killed two workers — a Filipino and a Nepali national — and injured two others near the Shaybah oilfield.

Kremlin Sets Trilateral Abu Dhabi Talks for November — and Says a First Russian Diesel Tanker Has Sailed for the US

The Kremlin confirmed on Friday that the next round of Russia–US–Ukraine negotiations will take place in Abu Dhabi in the first half of November in a trilateral format, while declining to rule out European participation later. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters the talks are expected to centre on post-war security guarantees for both Russia and Ukraine, an agenda that could also touch Europe's wider security arrangements. In the same briefing, Peskov said the first tanker carrying Russian diesel had already departed for the United States and that further shipments would follow regularly — a resumption of energy trade between Moscow and Washington even as the war continues. The announcement followed remarks by President Donald Trump in the Oval Office, where he warned President Volodymyr Zelensky that delaying a peace agreement could leave Ukraine in a weaker position, arguing that Russia's larger population and steady territorial gains made a negotiated settlement increasingly urgent.

Latest · Africa · Kenya23 October 2026

Rescuers Search Rubble After a Multi-Storey Building Collapses in Nairobi's Eastleigh District

A multi-storey building under construction collapsed in Nairobi's Eastleigh neighbourhood on Friday, with the county governor's office saying people were feared trapped beneath the rubble and rescuers reporting voices from inside the debris. The structure, on 9th Street, came down at around 1:40 p.m. local time; its ground floor was already occupied by shops even though the building had construction approval but no occupation certificate, Governor Johnson Sakaja's office said. One injured woman was pulled out and taken to hospital, police chief Issa Mohamud said, warning that the wreckage was leaning against a neighbouring building and could shift further. Kenya Red Cross teams joined a multi-agency search as excavators cleared debris, and police used tear gas to disperse large crowds that hampered access. No confirmed death toll had been established by Friday evening, and the cause of the collapse was not yet known. The disaster has renewed scrutiny of Nairobi's rapid high-rise construction boom, where safety concerns in fast-growing districts like Eastleigh have been raised for years.

Latest · Europe · Weather23 October 2026

Storm Austen Batters Britain Under a 'Danger to Life' Warning — and Storm Boelo Follows This Weekend

Storm Austen, the first named storm of the season, swept across southern Britain on Friday with gusts of 60 to 70 mph widely and up to 80 mph on exposed headlands in the southwest. The Met Office kept an amber wind warning — its second-highest tier — in force for Cornwall, Devon, Plymouth and the Isles of Scilly, warning of flying debris, tiles torn from roofs, road and bridge closures, power cuts and danger to life from large waves and beach material thrown onto coastal roads. A broader yellow warning covered much of southern England and Wales, and forecasters warned that with trees still in full leaf, fallen branches were likely to block roads and railways. People in dozens of areas were advised to keep torches and batteries ready and to stay away from cliff edges and stormy seas. There is little respite ahead: a second system, Storm Boelo, is forecast to intensify over the Atlantic and strike Northern Ireland, Scotland and northern England from Saturday afternoon, again with gusts that could reach 80 mph in exposed coastal areas.
