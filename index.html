Latest · Asia-Pacific · Myanmar — Wedding Venue Airstrikes28 November 2026

Myanmar Airstrikes Kill About 30 on Saturday, Including Around 20 at a Wedding Venue

About 30 people were killed in Myanmar military airstrikes on Saturday, witnesses said, lifting the toll from three days of bombing to roughly 60, a week after an international court found the state had committed genocide. The deadliest strike hit a venue in Moe Bye, on the border of Shan and Karenni states, where preparations were under way for two displaced people to marry; a rescue group leader put that toll at 21, including five children, with more than 40 wounded, while another witness said he counted 17 bodies at the scene. A further nine people were reported killed in bombing in western Rakhine state the same day. Two days earlier, according to the exiled National Unity Government, 20 civilians and nine members of the People’s Defence Forces died in a combined bomb, artillery and drone attack centred on a school and monastery in Magway region. The strikes are the latest mass-casualty incidents in the civil war that has followed the 2021 coup.

Latest · Europe · Spain — Migrant Boat and Election Eve28 November 2026

Fourteen Found Dead on Migrant Boat off Canary Islands as Spain Heads Into Sunday Election

Fourteen migrants were found dead aboard a boat carrying about 100 people that was rescued near Spain’s Canary Islands, Territorial Policy Minister Angel Victor Torres confirmed on Saturday, after the vessel was located on Friday 39 nautical miles south of El Hierro and towed toward the port of La Estaca. Six other people were airlifted to hospital in serious condition. Arrivals to the archipelago have surged since October, reaching a record 2,100 people in a single day on 25 November, most landing on El Hierro, an island of about 11,400 residents. The deaths land on the eve of Sunday’s general election, called early by Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, in which immigration has become a defining issue: Sanchez has championed the economic benefits of migration, while the right, ahead in most polls, is pressing for far tighter controls and is favoured to form the next government, likely in coalition.

Latest · Europe · France — Protests Ease28 November 2026

Turnout Falls Sharply at Largely Peaceful Marches Across France

Participation dropped sharply at demonstrations across France on Saturday, the Interior Ministry said, with 44,500 people marching nationwide, including 12,500 in Paris, compared with 82,500 nationally and 21,500 in Paris a week earlier. The protests, held over violence against women, the cost of living and student rights, were largely peaceful, although about 70,000 security personnel were deployed. Sporadic violence was reported in the southwestern city of Biarritz, where seven police officers were injured and one was taken to hospital after being struck in the elbow with a blunt object, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said. The movement began in September with student demands for renovated school buildings, more teachers and university reform, then broadened into wider cost-of-living protests backed by unions and activists, peaking at about 565,000 participants on 17 October.

Latest · Europe · Albania — Tirana Clashes28 November 2026

Police and Protesters Clash in Tirana as Thousands Rally Against Government on Independence Day

Police used water cannon and tear gas against protesters in central Tirana on Saturday after demonstrators threw stones and petrol bombs at street cameras and government buildings during an antigovernment rally called by students and opposition figures to mark Albania’s Independence Day. The health ministry said eight people were injured, including a police officer and two journalists, as thick smoke covered the main boulevard. The protest is the latest in a long series demanding the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama. The leaderless movement, known as the Flamingo Revolution, began in May over a luxury hotel project in a coastal nature reserve linked to US President Donald Trump’s family, then widened in October to grievances over healthcare and prospects for young people. November 28 this year marks 114 years since Albania declared independence, a date normally given over to national celebrations.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Zelenskyy on US Pressure28 November 2026

Zelenskyy Urges US to Pressure Putin and Calls Trump’s ‘My Friend’ Remark Strange

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has voiced frustration with Washington’s approach to Moscow, saying in a BBC interview broadcast on Friday that the United States should apply far more pressure on President Vladimir Putin to end the invasion, now approaching five years. He described President Donald Trump’s description of Putin as “my friend” at a recent summit in China as strange, arguing that an aggressor cannot be a friend and likening the remark to calling Hitler a friend during the Second World War. Trump defended the wording on Thursday, saying the aim was to reach a deal and stop the killing. Zelenskyy’s comments followed praise for Putin from US envoy Jared Kushner, who called him a grandmaster of politics, and from fellow envoy Steve Witkoff, who has described Putin as a humanitarian. The exchange comes as questions grow over Europe’s future financial commitments to Kyiv and as Zelenskyy also announced the latest Bring Kids Back UA reunifications, thanking First Lady Melania Trump for her role, with 43 children returned so far, according to the White House.

Latest · Middle East · Israel — Unity Talks Stall28 November 2026

Israel Unity Government Talks Stall as Netanyahu Signals Willingness to Let Eisenkot Serve First

Efforts to form a unity government in Israel remain unresolved after the October election produced no clear winner, with talks between caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud and Gadi Eisenkot’s centrist Yashar party ending without a deal and only a follow-up meeting promised. President Isaac Herzog has given Eisenkot until 6 December, extendable by 14 days, to form a government; Eisenkot’s presumed partners hold 48 seats against 53 for Netanyahu’s alliance. Yashar this week signed its first coalition pact, with Avigdor Lieberman’s Yisrael Beiteinu, while Israeli media reported Netanyahu was preparing to offer a rotation in which Eisenkot would serve as prime minister for the first two years and Netanyahu for the final two. Eisenkot accused Likud on Friday of stalling to force new elections, saying the sides were approaching a moment of decision over whether Likud would negotiate seriously and accept compromise.

Latest · Global Affairs · Aviation — Norse Atlantic Collapse28 November 2026

Norse Atlantic Files for Bankruptcy as Iran War Fuel Shock Claims Another Airline

Norwegian long-haul low-cost carrier Norse Atlantic has filed for bankruptcy for its main operating unit, cancelling all flights and halting ticket sales after failing to secure short-term financing. The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS petitioned the Agder District Court on Friday, a day after the airline grounded its fleet and two days after warning its position was critical; its shares had closed 85 percent lower on Thursday. Chief executive Eivind Roald said fuel costs toppled the company, and analysts noted Norse had not hedged fuel at all, leaving it fully exposed as jet fuel rose from about $99 a barrel before the US-Israel war on Iran began in late February to more than $200 in Europe by early October. The airline had already cancelled more than 700 flights since the war began and cut jobs, and lessors have issued termination notices for its Boeing 787 fleet. It follows Spirit Airlines, which ceased operations on 2 May, and restructuring at airBaltic, Volotea and Plus Ultra, while IATA expects the industry fuel bill to reach about $350 billion this year.

Latest · Africa · Kenya — Starvation Cult Verdict27 November 2026

Kenyan Cult Leader Paul Mackenzie Found Guilty of Manslaughter Over 123 Followers’ Deaths

A court in the coastal city of Mombasa has found Paul Mackenzie, founder and pastor of the Good News International Church, guilty of manslaughter over the deaths of 123 followers, convicting him on Friday alongside 29 followers including his wife, Rhoda Mumbua. Chief Magistrate Alex Ithuku said Mackenzie gave the directions to fast to death, according to Reuters; Mackenzie pleaded not guilty and a lawyer said he would appeal after sentencing. From the Shakahola Forest near Kenya’s Indian Ocean coast, Mackenzie told followers to starve themselves and their children to meet Jesus before the end of the world. The verdict is the latest in a series of cases: on 6 November he was sentenced to 200 years for the murder of 191 people, 190 of them children, and he has also been convicted of child cruelty, assault, radicalisation and terrorism-related offences. Prosecutors said post-mortem records showed 129 of 238 victims died of starvation.

Latest · Americas · Falklands — Oil Ruling28 November 2026

International Tribunal Rejects Argentina’s Bid to Halt UK Oil Drilling Near Falkland Islands

An international tribunal has rejected Argentina’s request for provisional measures to stop United Kingdom-authorised oil drilling near the Falkland Islands, clearing the way for the project off the disputed territory to proceed. Saturday’s ruling by the UN-backed International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea is a setback for Buenos Aires’s latest attempt to challenge economic activity around the islands, whose sovereignty remains contested between Argentina and Britain. The decision addresses only the interim request to halt activity, rather than the underlying sovereignty dispute itself, but it removes an immediate legal obstacle to exploration work authorised by London and the islands’ authorities.

Latest · Europe · EU — Hybrid Emissions Delay28 November 2026

EU Delays Plug-In Hybrid Emissions Rating Update Until 2030 in Relief for Automakers

The European Commission said on Saturday it will postpone until 1 January 2030 a planned update to the emissions rating for certain plug-in hybrid vehicles, a move expected to be welcomed by EU automakers. The update to the utility factor — the official share of distance assumed to be driven on electric power — had been due on 29 November and was intended to better reflect real-world driving, in which owners charge far less than assumed and electric shares are lower. Brussels said freezing the factor, retroactively from Sunday and only for vehicles with an electric range of at least 80 kilometres, gives manufacturers short-term flexibility to invest in improvements and rewards longer-range models; the proposal still needs to pass EU member states and the European Parliament, which may object. Campaign group Transport & Environment has argued against cancelling the updates, saying real-world CO2 emissions from plug-in hybrids average six times official test figures.
