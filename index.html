Latest · Middle East · Saudi Arabia — Missiles Intercepted12 November 2026

Saudi Arabia Says Debris From Two Intercepted Ballistic Missiles Fell in the Mecca Region as Houthi Attacks Escalate

Saudi Arabia says debris from two ballistic missiles intercepted by its air defences fell in several places in the Mecca region on Wednesday, including Sabuhah village, as its conflict with Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthi movement intensifies again. The kingdom’s civil defence, in a statement issued early on Thursday, called the targeting of civilian objects a flagrant violation of international humanitarian law. The Houthis have repeatedly denied Saudi accusations that they aim at Mecca and Medina, Islam’s two holiest cities, saying their operations are directed at military and strategic targets. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 had largely frozen the war that began after the Houthis seized Sanaa in 2014 and Saudi Arabia intervened the following year, but missile and drone attacks on Saudi territory and Saudi airstrikes in Yemen have resumed this year. The latest exchange also threatens the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the shipping route linking the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.

Latest · Global Affairs · G7 — Paris Declaration12 November 2026

G7 Ministers in Paris Reaffirm Support for Ukraine, Condemn China’s Military Aid to Russia and Warn Over Taiwan’s East Coast

Foreign ministers of the Group of Seven have closed talks in Paris with a joint declaration reaffirming support for Ukraine and criticising China both for supplying Russia with weapons that enable the war to continue and for increasingly assertive activity in the waters east of Taiwan. The ministers said China’s actions off Taiwan’s east coast, where regular coast guard patrols have stepped up since June, threaten regional stability and freedom of navigation, and they stressed the importance of peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait. China, which regards Taiwan as its own territory and has never renounced the use of force, did not immediately respond to a request for comment and recognises no Taiwanese sovereignty claims. Taiwan’s defence minister Wellington Koo said on Tuesday, after a reported rise in Chinese activity, that Beijing is pursuing what he described as area-denial operations around the island, where Chinese forces already operate on a daily basis. The United States, Britain, France and Germany have all previously raised concern about the east-coast patrols.

Latest · Americas / Asia-Pacific · Nvidia — Board Decision12 November 2026

Nvidia Shares Plunge Again in After-Hours Trade as the Board Meets to Decide Chief Executive Jensen Huang’s Fate

Nvidia shares slid more than 8 percent after the closing bell on Wednesday as the chipmaker’s board met late into the evening to decide whether to remove longtime chief executive Jensen Huang, according to a source familiar with the matter cited by Reuters. The meeting follows allegations that Huang had an affair with a businesswoman US authorities are reported to regard as a Chinese intelligence asset, and separate reporting of an affair with a company executive who reported to him. Asian markets opened fragile on Thursday, with S&P 500 futures down 0.4 percent, while South Korea’s Kospi rose 0.2 percent, Taiwanese shares gained 0.5 percent and Japan’s Nikkei 225 climbed 0.5 percent. President Donald Trump’s close ties to Huang have also come under renewed scrutiny: Trump said on 9 November that Huang told him he had no idea the woman was a spy, that he believed him, and that he knew the FBI was looking into it. On Wall Street on Wednesday the S&P 500 rose 1.3 percent, nearly erasing the losses since last week’s US midterm elections.

Latest · Middle East / Europe · Iran — Warning to US Bases11 November 2026

Iran’s Rezaei Warns US Bases ‘From West Asia to Europe’ Would Be Targets if Washington Attacks Again

A senior Iranian security figure, Mohsen Rezaei, a former commander-in-chief of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, has warned that any US military base involved in a fresh attack on Iran — from West Asia to Europe — would be treated as a legitimate target for Iranian strikes. In a post on X on Wednesday he wrote that there would be “no safe distance, no safe haven, no immunity,” and in an interview with Lebanon-based Al Mayadeen, broadcast on 11 November, he said Iran would strike bases even in European countries if American aircraft took off from them to bomb Iran, including more distant installations not previously attacked. Iran has published no authenticated target list, and the remarks are a warning of possible retaliation rather than confirmation of chosen targets. The warning comes as US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on 10 November that there was no need for another attack on Iran, pointing to the impact of American pressure on its economy, and as diplomatic efforts led by Qatar and Pakistan to end the war continue. Rezaei has separately said Tehran will not allow any enriched nuclear material to leave the country.

Latest · Europe · Spain — General Strike12 November 2026

Spain Stages Its First General Strike in 14 Years as Hundreds of Thousands March Over Rents and Pay

Workers across Spain have staged the country’s first general strike in 14 years, disrupting public transport and industry, while hundreds of thousands of people marched in Madrid, Barcelona and other cities demanding limits on rents and stronger purchasing power. Housing sits at the heart of the anger: protesters say homes have become unaffordable even for people in work, and the strike lands ahead of elections on 29 November. The scale of the turnout — described in morning roundups as hundreds of thousands on the streets — underlines how far the cost-of-housing crisis has moved from a local grievance to a national political test for the government. Services were cut back through the day as unions pressed demands for housing reform alongside higher wages.

Latest · Middle East · Hormuz — Navigation Systems Hit11 November 2026

Iran Attacked Shipping Navigation Systems on Oman’s Didamar Island, Sources Say, Testing the Hormuz Workarounds

Iran has attacked ship-navigation systems on the Omani island of Didamar in the Strait of Hormuz, three sources with knowledge of the matter have confirmed, in a move that demonstrates Tehran’s ability to target the infrastructure Gulf exporters are using to restore oil flows. The sources gave no damage assessment, Reuters could not independently determine whether any systems were destroyed in the attacks Tehran claimed on Tuesday, and Reuters did confirm smoke rising from the island in video footage without being able to tell what was burning. Two other shipping sources and a maritime security source said the impact on commercial shipping would likely be limited. Gulf exporters have increasingly relied on workarounds such as ship-to-ship transfers off Oman and the United Arab Emirates, and Gulf exports recovered to more than 80 percent of pre-war levels in September and October, helped by US military-assisted transits through the Strait and higher Saudi shipments via the Red Sea port of Yanbu.

DR Congo’s Ebola Cases Pass 11,000 as Kenya Begins a 42-Day Countdown, While the Dollar Holds Near a One-Month High

The government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo has reported that cumulative Ebola cases in the country have exceeded 11,000, while neighbouring Kenya has begun a 42-day countdown — the standard period, twice the virus’s incubation cycle, that must pass without a new case before an outbreak can be declared over. The figures appeared in Thursday’s curated daily roundup alongside market moves that show how widely the shockwaves run: the dollar index held steady in Asian trade at 102.87, its highest since April last year, as traders priced an 87.3 percent chance of a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike at the 9 December meeting, and foreign investors poured $1.34 billion into Indian equities on Wednesday alone, taking month-to-date inflows to just short of $8 billion after pulling more than $30 billion earlier in the year. Brent crude hovered near $92 a barrel, down from $118 less than two weeks ago.
