Latest · Middle East · Manama — Fire Over Sitra21 November 2026

Bahrain Says an Iranian Strike Set a Civilian Industrial Plant Ablaze; Tehran Says It Hit US Patriot Radars

Bahrain accused Iran of striking a civilian industrial facility on Saturday night, in the latest exchange of a war that keeps widening across the Gulf. The Interior Ministry said the attack caused a fire at the site and that Civil Defence teams were working to bring it under control, with preliminary reports pointing to material damage and no injuries. The ministry had earlier warned residents of a potential threat and urged them to move to the nearest safe place. Two residents told AFP they saw smoke rising above Sitra Island, on Bahrain’s east coast, home to several industrial sites including a refinery run by the state oil company Bapco. Bahrain’s Defence Force said its air defences intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks, and that shrapnel from the interceptions caused further material damage but no casualties. Tehran gave a different account: the Revolutionary Guards said they had attacked two US bases, Sheikh Isa and Juffair, and struck Patriot air-defence radars deployed in the kingdom, which hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, in response to American actions. Bahrain has reported repeated Iranian aerial attacks since 13 November, including strikes on 15, 17 and 18 November.

Latest · Middle East · Strait of Hormuz — Two Tankers in One Morning21 November 2026

Two Tankers Are Struck by Projectiles in Hormuz as Iran Says a Third Hit a Naval Mine

The world’s most important oil chokepoint took more fire on Saturday. The British maritime agency UKMTO said a tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was hit on the stern by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, on Oman’s northern coast, and that a second tanker was struck in its engine room about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab roughly 50 minutes later. No casualties were reported in either incident. Iran’s navy offered its own version, saying an oil tanker had caught fire after striking a naval mine in a restricted zone east of the strait, and accusing the vessel of ignoring warnings while travelling with its identification and navigation systems switched off. The Iranian account could not be independently verified, and it was unclear whether the burning ship was one of the two reported by UKMTO; a third tanker had already caught fire near Khasab after an attack on Friday. Analysts counting Iranian armed-forces statements put the number of shipping incidents at about 25 in the past week alone — roughly three a day. Before the war, the strait carried nearly a fifth of the world’s crude oil, and the US military has said it cleared all mines from the main shipping routes.

Latest · Europe · Warsaw — A “Grave Violation”21 November 2026

Two Russian Missiles and a Drone Came Down in Poland During the Barrage on Ukraine, and NATO Is Calling It a Provocation

Poland says Russian weapons did not just cross its airspace this time — they landed on its soil. Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz confirmed that two missiles came down at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone at Mołodycz, all in the country’s southeast, during Russia’s massive overnight attack on Ukraine on 20 November. One weapon was identified as a Kh-101 long-range cruise missile; it fell about 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border, and one missile crashed roughly 35 kilometres from where troops from 11 countries are holding an unprecedented two-week exercise, begun on 9 November, to prepare for a possible post-war stabilisation mission in Ukraine. No one in Poland was hurt and no infrastructure was damaged. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it an apparent “intentional provocation” timed to the drills, saying nobody could call it an innocent coincidence, and NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte labelled it a “grave violation” of Polish airspace and was expected in Warsaw over the weekend. Polish F-16s and Swedish Gripens, backed by a Dutch refuelling aircraft, patrolled through the night while Patriot systems near Rzeszów and Świdnik were activated, and Warsaw said it would ask NATO for more forces for Operation Eastern Sentry, the alliance mission guarding its eastern flank. The wider Russian barrage killed at least six people in Kyiv, hours after President Vladimir Putin vowed retaliation for Ukrainian strikes that severed the bridge linking occupied Crimea to the Russian mainland.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · The Hague / Cox’s Bazar — A First in 80 Years21 November 2026

“Justice Has Been Decided, but Not Yet Delivered”: Rohingya Hail the World Court’s Genocide Verdict Against Myanmar

In refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar, Rohingya families are celebrating a ruling nine years in the making. On Thursday the International Court of Justice found, by nine votes to six, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in its 2017 military crackdown in northern Rakhine State — the first time in the court’s 80-year history that it has held a state responsible for genocide, and only the fourth such finding by any international court since the Holocaust. Reading the verdict over three hours, President Yuji Iwasawa said the evidence showed Myanmar’s forces killed at least 10,000 Rohingya, committed widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and imposed measures intended to prevent births; more than 730,000 people fled to Bangladesh. The court concluded the “only reasonable inference” was an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group. The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The judges ordered Myanmar to stop all violence, investigate and prosecute those responsible, and provide reparations — including the safe, voluntary and dignified return of refugees and restitution of property — with a progress report due within two years. The ruling is final and cannot be appealed, but the court has no enforcement arm, and non-compliance would have to go to the UN Security Council. Former UN special rapporteur Yanghee Lee called it “a deeply emotional day for all Rohingya people,” adding that “justice has been decided, but not yet delivered.” Legal experts are also watching the judgment for signals on the separate Gaza genocide case before the court.

Latest · Global Affairs · Antalya — Into Overtime21 November 2026

COP31 Runs Past Its Deadline With No Deal, as Fossil Fuels Stay Missing From the Draft

The COP31 climate summit was meant to close on Friday. Instead, nearly 200 countries argued past the deadline in Antalya, with pavilions already being dismantled around them and a new draft not expected until Saturday morning. The sticking point has not moved: the presidency’s revised cover text drops every mention of fossil fuels — no coal, oil or gas by name, and no accelerated phase-out — after an earlier, longer draft had included exactly that language. The $120 billion annual adaptation-finance target has also disappeared, replaced by an unquantified discussion of tripling adaptation funding by 2035, and the proposal to lift electricity’s share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035 has been softened from a target into a call for cooperation. A bloc led by France and Panama has refused to accept a final decision silent on fossil fuels, Panama has threatened to block adoption outright, and civil-society groups have rejected the draft as a regression; on the other side, major producers including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iran and India have resisted any text that questions their energy revenues. Because COP decisions require consensus, a single holdout can sink the package. The draft does break one piece of new ground: for the first time, a COP text acknowledges that the 1.5°C warming limit has not been held.

Latest · Americas · Florida — The Wettest November on Record20 November 2026

A Second Storm Pushes Florida Past Every November Rain Record, and the St. Johns River Past Its 1953 Crest

Florida’s floods are not one storm but two. After the Election Day deluge of 1–3 November — when 24.69 inches of rain fell on Malabar in a day, a flash-flood emergency was declared for Palm Bay and two people died in Brevard County — a second system soaked the east coast again from 16 to 20 November. By 19 November Melbourne had recorded 22.59 inches for the month, roughly nine times its normal November rainfall and more than double its previous wettest November; its 11.51 inches on 2 November remains the wettest single day in the city’s records, which run back to 1937, beating the mark set by Hurricane Irma. Daytona Beach, West Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale and Vero Beach have also set November records with days of the month still to spare. On the morning of 20 November the St. Johns River near Cocoa stood at 18.63 feet, above its previous record crest of 17.55 feet set in October 1953. The Weather Prediction Center issued a rare high-risk outlook for excessive rainfall, Governor Ron DeSantis activated the National Guard to help residents, and the state of emergency now covers 21 counties through the end of the year. Forecasters warned of a further four to six inches on parts of the coast and gave the offshore low a 50% chance of becoming Subtropical or Tropical Storm Kyle. AccuWeather has estimated total damage and economic losses from the first storm alone at $3 billion to $5 billion.

Latest · Africa · Kinshasa — 11,000 Cases and Counting17 November 2026

Ebola Cases in DR Congo Have More Than Doubled in Three Months, and Children Are Paying the Highest Price

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has more than doubled in three months, the World Health Organization says, reaching more than 11,000 reported cases and about 5,400 deaths across 67 health zones in eight provinces. Opening the third meeting of the WHO Emergency Committee on the Bundibugyo virus epidemic, Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said children were “paying a terrible price”: they account for more than a quarter of confirmed cases and a third of deaths, and seven in ten infected children under five have died. Between 400 and 500 new cases are still being reported every week, and two-thirds of confirmed deaths occur outside Ebola treatment centres — a sign that infections are being found too late. There has been progress in the earliest epicentres, where rapid transmission has been halted, and the number of laboratories able to test for the virus has risen from two to more than two dozen. But the outbreak has crossed borders, with cases and deaths reported in Kenya and an imported case in Nigeria; Kenya has discharged its final patient, begun a 42-day countdown to Ebola-free status and imposed a 21-day quarantine on travellers returning from Congo. Trials of a vaccine specific to the Bundibugyo strain are set to begin in Congo, while Doctors Without Borders has called for wider access to the existing ERVEBO vaccine.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Manipur, India — Methanol Poisoning21 November 2026

Tainted Liquor Kills 11 in Manipur and Sends Hundreds to Hospital, Traced to One Town’s Supply

At least 11 people have died and hundreds have fallen ill in India’s northeastern state of Manipur after drinking suspected adulterated alcohol, in one of the country’s deadliest recent hooch tragedies. The first two deaths were reported on 18 November in Chandel district, and the toll reached 11 within 48 hours; the dead were all men aged between 32 and 50. Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh said more than 180 patients had been admitted to two hospitals in the capital, Imphal — 140 at Shija Hospital and 41 at Babina Hospital — while later district reports put the number treated across facilities at more than 300, many showing symptoms consistent with methanol poisoning. Police said the liquor was bought in Sugnu town, in neighbouring Kakching district, and was not brewed in Chandel itself. The chief minister visited both hospitals, ordered an inquiry to identify those responsible and promised action, while district officials issued a public-health advisory urging anyone who had drunk alcohol from Sugnu in the past week to seek medical evaluation, and warning residents to avoid locally brewed liquor or any drink of doubtful origin. The exact cause of the deaths remains under medical and scientific investigation.

Latest · Americas · Washington / Ottawa — “No Daylight”21 November 2026

The Pentagon Quietly Curbs Military Ties With Canada — and Ottawa Says Nothing Has Changed

The United States has imposed new restrictions on its defence relationship with Canada, its closest military partner, according to a Washington Post report on a classified memo circulated last week by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The precise scope is unclear — the Post said it had not seen the memo — but Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said the department was taking steps to ensure its engagements with Canada “advance tangible US defense objectives” and ultimately support a reinvigorated relationship, and he stressed that the guidance does not affect defence-intelligence cooperation, the binational NORAD command, or cooperation through NATO. In Halifax on Saturday, Republican Senator Mike Rounds, who was briefed on the memo, played it down further: the curbs, he said, largely require permission for US officials to attend think-tank events and social engagements rather than touching operations. Canada’s Defence Minister David McGuinty said he had not seen the document and had detected no change, insisting he saw “no daylight” between the two militaries, which continue to work “hand in glove” on Caribbean counter-trafficking patrols, integrated missile defence and scrambling jets across North America. The report lands on an alliance already strained by President Donald Trump’s trade war and his repeated threats to make Canada the 51st US state; one US official quoted in the coverage called the move a “slap in the face.”

Latest · Americas · Junín, Peru — The Morning-After Count21 November 2026

Peru’s Earthquake Toll Comes Into Focus: 128 Homes Affected, Four Destroyed, and No Deaths

A day after a powerful earthquake shook the Peruvian Andes and sent Lima residents into the streets, officials have put numbers to the damage — and the news is better than first feared. Peru’s civil defence agency, INDECI, said on Saturday that eight people were injured, 128 homes were affected and four were destroyed; the Health Ministry later raised the injured count to 15. The quake, now measured at magnitude 6.7 by Peru’s Geophysical Institute, struck at 7:09 p.m. on Friday about 30 kilometres northwest of the city of Junín, at a depth of 129 kilometres — deep enough to be felt strongly across central Peru while limiting surface destruction. In Pichanaki, in Chanchamayo province, a house wall and part of a hospital wall came down; two people were hurt during the evacuation there, and others were injured by falling rocks in Perené and Paucartambo, where rocks struck a tanker. A magnitude 5.0 aftershock followed early on Saturday. Defence Minister Rafael Belaunde reported minor damage to homes across Junín, power interruptions and restrictions on highway traffic, and INDECI said humanitarian aid was being coordinated from its warehouses in Junín and Pasco while damage assessments continued in vulnerable areas. No deaths have been reported, and Peru’s navy ruled out any tsunami risk from the inland quake.
