Latest · Global Affairs · Climate — COP31 Opens in Antalya9 November 2026

“Get Serious”: Climate Talks Open in Türkiye With Money Already the First Fight

The UN climate conference COP31 opened on Monday in the Mediterranean city of Antalya, running to 20 November under an unusual shared arrangement — Türkiye holds the presidency and hosts the talks, while Australia’s climate minister Chris Bowen leads the formal negotiations. UN Secretary-General António Guterres urged governments to get serious after a year marked by European heatwaves, major wildfires in Indonesia and catastrophic flooding in Nepal, worsened by a strong El Niño. The opening ceremony started about 90 minutes late, and delegates were already arguing over finance: how much richer countries should pay to help poorer nations adapt and cut emissions. Türkiye is pushing electrification as a flagship goal, aiming for electricity to supply 35% of global final energy demand by 2035, and bills this as an “implementation COP” focused on delivering past pledges rather than making new ones. A leaders’ summit follows on 11–12 November.

Latest · Europe · Poland — Kraków Gunman Dies9 November 2026

Kraków Gunman Dies in Hospital as Poland Mourns Its Worst Shooting in Decades

The man accused of killing six people in Kraków’s historic Main Market Square on Saturday has died in hospital from wounds sustained when police shot him during the attack, Polish police said on Monday. Named in Polish media as Michał Oksiński, 24, he is alleged to have driven into the crowded square in military-style clothing and opened fire at random, killing four women and two men — including a British national — and wounding 16 others, among them two police officers. Police later found the bodies of his father, mother and sister at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, and prosecutors say it is highly likely the same person carried out those killings; 11 firearms were recovered across the two sites. Authorities are investigating the case as a possible terrorist offence and say the suspect held radical far-right views. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it the deadliest such attack since the end of communism in 1989. Kraków has declared mourning until 11 November, and the hourly bugle call from St Mary’s Basilica was silenced for 12 hours — the first time since 1939.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Diplomacy — A Possible Three-Way Summit9 November 2026

Trump, Xi and Putin in One Room? Beijing Confirms Talks on a Rare Trilateral Meeting at APEC

President Donald Trump says he expects to meet both Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC leaders’ summit in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. Beijing confirmed on Monday it is “in communication with relevant parties” about a three-heads-of-state meeting and did not rule it out, though no final decision has been announced. Trump said on Sunday the agenda would include the wars in Ukraine and Iran and nuclear arms control, including denuclearisation. The visit would also be Trump and Xi’s third bilateral meeting this year, after Beijing in May and Washington in September, as both sides try to preserve a fragile trade truce due to expire in January. Analysts warn the three leaders’ interests clash sharply, so any session may amount largely to symbolism and a carefully choreographed photograph — with Putin, isolated by much of the West, gaining simply by being seen alongside the other two.

Latest · Europe · Diplomacy — G7 Meets in Paris9 November 2026

“Europe Must Have a Place at the Table”: G7 Ministers Press Rubio on Ukraine and Iran

G7 foreign ministers began two days of talks in Paris on Monday, their first meeting since the Republicans’ heavy defeat in the US midterms, with European ministers pressing Secretary of State Marco Rubio on two points: any Ukraine settlement must be fair to Kyiv, and Washington should avoid fresh military escalation against Iran. The meeting — moved from Marseille to Paris after weeks of protests across France — comes a day before US-mediated Ukraine–Russia talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday. French foreign minister Jean-Noël Barrot said Europeans will be represented in Abu Dhabi, with France taking part, while Germany’s Johann Wadephul warned the group “cannot afford” escalation with Iran. Rubio, on his first trip abroad since the midterms, said there had been no progress with Tehran and described some of its demands as unacceptable. Ukraine’s foreign minister joins a dedicated Ukraine session on Tuesday; Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea and Brazil were also invited to parts of the meeting.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Grid Struck Before the Talks9 November 2026

850,000 Without Power as Russia and Ukraine Trade Long-Range Strikes Before Abu Dhabi

Russia battered Ukraine’s power network again overnight and Ukrainian forces hit one of Russia’s largest oil refineries, officials said on Monday, hours before delegations were due to head to US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 317 drones, including 105 jet-powered types; Moscow said it targeted four electrical substations in the Kyiv, Dnipropetrovsk, Cherkasy and Vinnytsia regions. The operator DTEK said more than 600,000 people in Dnipropetrovsk region lost power after key facilities were damaged, and nearly 250,000 customers in Kharkiv region were cut off — about 850,000 in total in the east. Hospitals in Dnipro switched to diesel generators and electric transport stopped. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said 140 apartment buildings in Zhytomyr region lost heating and that the EU had activated an emergency mechanism to speed funding for grid repairs. Kyiv said a Ukrainian strike set fire to Russia’s Kirishi refinery in the Leningrad region. Zelenskyy accused Moscow of raising pressure deliberately ahead of the talks.

Latest · Europe · Nuclear Safety — Zaporizhzhia Back on the Grid8 November 2026

Power Restored to Zaporizhzhia After Its 27th Complete Blackout of the War

External electricity has been restored to the Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant, Europe’s largest, after it lost its only working power line for several hours on Sunday and relied on emergency diesel generators to keep cooling its fuel. Ukraine’s operator Energoatom said the 330-kilovolt Ferosplavna-1 line disconnected at 02:36 and was reconnected at 17:41, after an automatic reconnection attempt failed. The UN nuclear watchdog, the IAEA, counted this as the plant’s 27th complete loss of off-site power since the conflict began — and Energoatom’s 16th this year alone — and said the cause and location of the fault are still unknown. Director General Rafael Grossi called the safety situation precarious and stressed the need to keep the plant connected and diesel stocks topped up; the IAEA team on site was told fuel could run a limited number of generators for about two weeks. The plant’s main 750-kilovolt Dniprovska line has been down since October because of combat damage, leaving Ferosplavna-1 as its sole grid link. Its six reactors produce no electricity but need constant power for cooling and safety systems.

Latest · Middle East · Iran — Tehran Defends Its Offer9 November 2026

“The Most Reasonable Option on the Table”: Iran Defends a Two-Day Path Back to Talks

Iran on Monday defended a proposal to restart peace talks with the United States within two days as the most reasonable offer available, after Secretary of State Marco Rubio dismissed some of its conditions as “outrageous and ridiculous.” Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei asked whether it was unreasonable to demand that US sanctions and a naval blockade be lifted before negotiations start and that fighting end on all fronts. The two-day timetable compresses an earlier seven-step plan first put forward in September, under which Washington would halt hostilities, end the blockade on Iranian ports and waive oil sanctions, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and talks on other issues — including the nuclear programme — would follow. Messages are still being exchanged through Qatari mediators, but President Donald Trump rejected the original proposal, and Tehran says it will only return to nuclear talks once it has verified its conditions have been met. Baghaei also dismissed a new US task force aimed at restricting Iran’s land trade.

Latest · Middle East · Gaza — Winter Rains Flood the Camps9 November 2026

“Tents Don’t Protect Us”: Storm Floods 70,000 Shelters as Gaza Enters a Fourth Winter in Canvas

Gaza’s first heavy winter storm has flooded about 70,000 tents within roughly 12 hours, leaving an estimated 350,000 displaced people exposed, according to local authorities and aid agencies. Rain that began on Friday turned camps to mud, soaking mattresses, blankets and stored food and, in places, mixing floodwater with sewage from shattered infrastructure. In Deir al-Balah, a sand embankment collapsed onto tents, killing a woman and children sheltering there; civil defence teams said shortages of fuel and equipment meant they could answer only a fraction of more than 2,000 distress calls. The UN humanitarian office says about one million people are at risk of flooding this winter, most in low-lying sandy areas such as Al-Mawasi, and estimates Gaza needs around 121,000 tents and 561,000 tarpaulins. Save the Children says more than half a million children are living in critical or catastrophic shelter conditions, while only about 20,000 new tents have been allowed in and tens of thousands of mobile homes remain waiting on the Egyptian side of the border.

Latest · Europe · Migration — Deadly Days off Crete9 November 2026

At Least 21 Dead in Boat Emergencies South of Crete as Rescuers Save More Than 100

At least 21 people have died in a series of migrant boat emergencies south of the Greek island of Crete, the coastguard said on Monday, as strong winds complicated rescues on an increasingly busy route from North Africa. In the deadliest incident, a Morocco-flagged container ship spotted a boat about 15 nautical miles off Kaloi Limenes early on Monday; a coastguard vessel rescued 18 people and recovered 12 bodies, and one survivor later died in hospital. Authorities said that boat may have been at sea for several days after leaving Libya around 3–4 November, though how those on board died is not yet clear. In a separate weekend incident, rescuers saved 28 Sudanese nationals and recovered eight bodies, with two people still missing. Frontex aircraft and a drone located two further boats carrying roughly 47 and 40 people. In total more than 100 people were rescued. The UN refugee agency says over 37,000 migrants have arrived in Greece so far in 2026, more than 22,000 of them via Crete.

Google to Invest $15 Billion in Malaysia: Data Centres, Clean Power and 800,000 Trainees

Google says it will invest US$15 billion in Malaysia during 2027 and 2028 to expand the digital infrastructure behind search, maps and artificial-intelligence services. Announced in Kuala Lumpur in the presence of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, the plan adds new energy-efficient data-centre campuses in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan to a first campus at Elmina Business Park that is now operational, and the Selangor capacity will support Google’s planned cloud region for enterprise customers. The company says construction should contribute an average of about US$1.1 billion a year to Malaysia’s GDP and support more than 37,000 local jobs annually, working with over 80 Malaysian firms in its supply chain. The package also promises more than one gigawatt of new clean-energy capacity for the national grid, water-security work including shared infrastructure in Negeri Sembilan, and programmes intended to upskill roughly 800,000 Malaysians by 2031. It builds on a US$2 billion commitment announced in 2024.

Latest · Africa · Sport — Nigeria’s Sprint Double in Dakar8 November 2026

First Country to Sweep Both 100m Titles: Nigeria’s Teenagers Make Youth Olympic History

Nigeria won both 100-metre finals at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games on Sunday, becoming the first country to take the men’s and women’s sprint crowns at the same edition. Rosemary Nwankwo, 17, won the women’s race in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds — a following wind of +2.4 m/s put the time above the limit for record purposes — ahead of the UAE’s Arwa Ali and Qatar’s Dana Salem. She is Nigeria’s third women’s champion in the event, after Josephine Omaka in 2010 and Rosemary Chukwuma in 2018. Minutes later, 15-year-old Gift Bright won the men’s final in a personal best of 10.28 seconds, ahead of China’s Hangyu Zhang and Poland’s Szymon Dolega, becoming Nigeria’s first-ever men’s champion despite a thigh problem in the build-up. The two golds are Nigeria’s first medals of the Games, which run in Senegal until 13 November — the first Youth Olympics held in Africa.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · India — Delhi’s Worst Diwali Air in Five Years9 November 2026

Severe Smog Over Delhi After Diwali, With Hospitals Counting Burns and Monitors Under Scrutiny

Delhi woke on Monday to its most polluted post-Diwali morning since 2021, with the citywide Air Quality Index averaging close to 370 — deep in the “very poor” band — and several stations above 400, the “severe” threshold. Hourly fine-particle readings at one station peaked near 999 micrograms per cubic metre, many times the World Health Organization’s 24-hour guideline, and Stage III emergency restrictions remain in force across the capital region. Hospitals also counted the festival’s injuries: Safdarjung Hospital reported 135 burn cases over 7–8 November, most linked to firecrackers, and AIIMS reported 42 Diwali-related injuries by Monday, with one patient critical. The episode has become a data dispute as well as a health one — analysts noted that more than 20 of the city’s monitoring stations stopped reporting during peak firecracker hours, private platforms showed worse readings than official ones, and opposition and government parties traded accusations over data and stubble burning. The Supreme Court had permitted only approved “green” firecrackers in short windows over the weekend.
