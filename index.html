Latest · Africa · Cairo — Ring Road Inferno26 November 2026

Tanker Explosion Kills 31 on Cairo Highway as Egypt Demands Answers

Egyptians mourned on Thursday after a fuel tanker overturned and exploded on Cairo’s Ring Road during Wednesday evening rush hour, killing at least 31 people and injuring more than 20 in the deadliest of a recent string of transport disasters. Prosecutors said a car travelling at about 110 km/h struck the tanker from behind near the Warraq district of Giza, bursting a tyre and sending the truck into a concrete barrier; fuel spilled and ignited, charring 27 vehicles. The car’s driver has been arrested and, according to prosecutors, confessed to causing the crash, while DNA work continues to identify victims. Lawmakers demanded accountability, with one opposition party leader saying the cabinet bore full responsibility and should resign. Egypt recorded 5,829 road deaths in 2025, and the blast came less than three weeks after a deadly train collision, fuelling public anger over road safety.

Latest · Global Affairs · Trade — China Faces Highest Rate26 November 2026

US Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners Over Excess Factory Capacity

The Trump administration has proposed fresh tariffs on imports from 14 major trading partners, arguing that subsidised overproduction abroad is flooding world markets with cheap goods and undercutting American manufacturers. The duties, drawn up under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act after an eight-month investigation into 16 economies, would cover China, the European Union, India, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Cambodia, Bangladesh, Singapore, Switzerland and Taiwan; Mexico and Norway were investigated but spared. China faces the steepest proposed rate, 20 percent on top of existing duties, though rates are capped by recent trade deals — 15 percent for the EU, Japan and South Korea — and would not stack on earlier levies. If the US-China trade truce is not extended before it expires on 10 January, the China rate could climb to 40 percent. Beijing said it reserved the right to respond, and the EU insisted Washington honour its agreed 15 percent ceiling. A public hearing is set for 25 January, so the earliest start is late January.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Bangkok — Refinery Shut Down26 November 2026

Explosion at Bangkok’s Only Urban Refinery Injures Nine and Halts Production

An explosion and fire tore through the Bangchak oil refinery in eastern Bangkok on Thursday afternoon, injuring at least nine people, four of them seriously, and forcing residents nearby to evacuate. The blast, felt kilometres away, began around 2:45 p.m. local time and was brought under control within about half an hour. Bangchak said a preliminary investigation pointed to the plant’s hydrocracking unit, while Energy Minister Akanat Promphan said the fire appeared to start at a distillation tower roughly 100 metres from the oil storage area, which limited the damage. The company shut down every production unit at the site while the cause is investigated. The Phra Khanong refinery can process 120,000 barrels a day, is Thailand’s fifth largest, and is the country’s only refinery located inside a city.

Latest · Americas · Space — “Starmind” Constellation26 November 2026

US Regulators Clear SpaceX to Deploy One Million Data-Centre Satellites

The US Federal Communications Commission has authorised SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites that would work as data centres in orbit, a first-of-its-kind approval in the race to power artificial intelligence. The planned “Starmind” constellation would use solar-powered computing modules linked by laser connections to process data for users on Earth, which SpaceX says could cut the energy and environmental costs of ground-based facilities. The scale dwarfs the roughly 11,000 Starlink satellites already in orbit and has drawn scepticism and alarm from some scientists over congestion and debris. Under the licence, SpaceX must fly its first satellite by November 2033, have 100,000 in place by 2035 and half the fleet by 2038, completing the network by 2040; the company has said it aims to start launches in 2027, while cautioning investors that the technology is unproven.

Latest · Europe · Ukraine War — Deep-Strike Campaign26 November 2026

Ukraine Has Launched Nearly 500 Long-Range Strikes This Year, Monitor Finds

Ukraine carried out almost 500 strikes against Russian targets more than 500 kilometres away during 2026 — more than in the first four years of the war combined — according to figures from the ACLED conflict monitor analysed by AFP. Ukrainian drones have reached Moscow, Saint Petersburg and as far as Siberia, hitting targets up to about 1,500 kilometres from the border. The campaign has also gone to sea: attacks on Russian shipping in the Black Sea rose roughly five- to sixfold compared with 2025, to around 30 incidents, and a strike off the Libyan coast sank a Russian corvette-class warship in the Mediterranean. Analysts say the widening reach shows Kyiv steadily extending the war deep into Russian territory and across shipping routes, rather than confining its operations to the front line.

Latest · Europe · Germany — 17 Raids, Two Arrests26 November 2026

Germany Arrests Two Iraqis Over Alleged Plot to Bomb a Cologne Synagogue

German prosecutors said on Thursday that police had arrested two Iraqi nationals suspected of belonging to a terrorist organisation that planned attacks on Jewish and other targets across Europe, including a synagogue in Cologne. Officers detained Mostafa Al-R. in Heidelberg and Ameer J. in Cologne after 17 raids across three states. Prosecutors say Mostafa Al-R. had ties to Mohammad Al-Saadi, an alleged figure in the pro-Iranian group HAYI, which is blamed for a series of attacks on US, Israeli and Jewish sites in Europe since March, and that the two men helped found a separate cell in late 2025 to raise funds and recruit attackers. On two consecutive nights in late March, people believed to be acting for the group travelled to the Cologne synagogue and another European location apparently carrying explosives, but no attack took place, for reasons still unclear. Both suspects were due before a judge to decide on pre-trial detention.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Hong Kong — One Year On26 November 2026

Hong Kong Mourns 168 Fire Victims on Anniversary, With No Official Memorial

Hundreds of mourners laid flowers, toys and handwritten notes at a park facing the charred Wang Fuk Court housing estate in Tai Po on Thursday, one year after a fire swept through seven of its eight towers and killed 168 people in Hong Kong’s deadliest blaze in decades. The estate, home to nearly 5,000 residents, was undergoing a HK$336 million renovation when the fire broke out on 26 November 2025; an independent inquiry later blamed systemic failures across government and the construction industry, finding that non-fire-retardant scaffolding mesh and foam window boards helped the flames spread. Chief Executive John Lee apologised last month and the housing secretary resigned, two contractors face criminal proceedings, and more than 96 percent of households have accepted a government buy-out worth about HK$6.8 billion, with the site to become a park. The government held no official memorial, a decision some bereaved families called disrespectful, while Beijing warned against using the tragedy to stir unrest.

Latest · Europe · Ibiza — Pollution Plan Activated26 November 2026

Diesel Tanker Capsizes off Ibiza, Killing One Crew Member as Spain Guards Against a Spill

Spanish authorities raced to protect Mediterranean waters on Thursday after the Malta-flagged chemical tanker Med Emre capsized in the early hours about 30 nautical miles off Ibiza, possibly carrying up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel. All 17 crew members were rescued from the Algeciras-to-Barcelona voyage, but one died after reaching hospital in Ibiza and six others were treated for minor injuries including hypothermia. Officials said the modern, double-hulled ship, built in 2019, remained semi-submerged with its keel above the surface rather than sinking to the seabed, which should make salvage and containment easier. No significant spill had been detected, though small sheens were seen on the water. Spain sent the specialist oil-spill vessel Clara Campoamor and a survey aircraft to the scene and activated its national maritime pollution plan at level 2, the second-highest tier. The cause of the capsize is not yet known.

Latest · Global Affairs · Markets — Asia Rebounds26 November 2026

Asian Shares Bounce as Wall Street Recovers, but Tariffs and AI Costs Loom

Asian stock markets staged a rare rally on Thursday after Wall Street clawed back most of an overnight slide, even as investors weighed Washington’s new tariff proposals and doubts over the financing behind the AI boom. Japan’s Nikkei rose 1.6 percent and South Korea’s main index gained 1.9 percent, though both remain deep in the red for November, while S&P 500 futures added 0.5 percent. Nvidia ended Wednesday down 3.9 percent after S&P placed its credit rating on watch for a possible downgrade, citing weaknesses in the chipmaker’s management and board oversight. US data added to the unease: third-quarter growth was revised up sharply to 4.3 percent, but the Federal Reserve’s preferred core inflation measure came in at an uncomfortable 3.3 percent. Oil held firm near $94.81 a barrel for Brent on Middle East supply fears, while gold eased to about $3,566 an ounce.

Latest · Middle East · Gulf — Doha Grand Prix Weekend26 November 2026

War Squeezes the Gulf as Qatar’s Grand Prix Goes Ahead and Pledged US Trillions Stall

Qatar’s Formula One Grand Prix is going ahead in Doha this weekend after months of doubt caused by the regional war, but the paddock spectacle sits alongside a harder economic reality: the trillions of dollars Gulf states pledged to invest in the United States have largely failed to materialise as the Iran war squeezes their economies. The IMF projects the six Gulf economies will shrink 4.8 percent in 2026, with Qatar contracting by 15.6 percent, and governments that once competed to announce Washington deals are now tightening budgets. The conflict has also exposed the limits of US protection: Houthi attacks have struck deep inside Saudi Arabia, including a drone hit on Riyadh’s tallest tower that wounded at least 11 people, while in Yemen Houthi forces have advanced to within about 60 kilometres of Aden, the seat of the internationally recognised government, whose defenders are digging in. Doha’s streets, meanwhile, have filled with Saudi visitors taking advantage of the school break.
