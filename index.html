Latest · Europe · France19 October 2026

Protesters Block the Louvre as France’s Cost-of-Living Unrest Rolls On

Demonstrators blocked the entrance to the Louvre in Paris on Monday as France’s cost-of-living unrest continued into a new week, with students from the École du Louvre among those leading the action at the world’s most-visited museum. In Marseille, city authorities banned public gatherings between noon and 8 p.m. following the weekend’s violence. The anger is being driven by prices: diesel has risen nearly 40% and petrol about 25% since March, while food prices are up roughly 25% since 2021. President Emmanuel Macron has so far stopped short of declaring a state of emergency or a curfew, even as the unrest flares months before a presidential election he cannot contest again, with far-right leader Marine Le Pen riding high in the polls.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · Australia19 October 2026

Australian Lawmaker Fights for Her Life After a Deliberate Car Attack Outside Her Office

Independent MP Helen Haines, 65, is in a critical condition after being deliberately struck by a white Toyota Land Cruiser outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria, on Monday. Victoria Police said a 43-year-old man was arrested after bystanders detained the driver at the scene, and a knife was found inside the vehicle. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said a terrorism task force would investigate the attack, though the motive is not yet known, and he pointed to online anonymity for raising the temperature around politics. Haines was airlifted to hospital; attacks of this kind on politicians are extremely rare in Australia, where MPs still meet constituents in the street and at supermarkets.

Latest · Europe · Greece19 October 2026

Twelve Migrants, Including an Infant, Drown in Two Boat Disasters off Greek Islands

Twelve migrants drowned in two separate incidents in the northeastern Aegean Sea on Monday. Off Lesbos, four men, a woman and an infant died when their boat collided with a Greek coast guard vessel and overturned near Agrilia; eight survivors were taken to hospital, and a 37-year-old Turkish national among them was arrested as the suspected smuggler and operator of the boat. Off Samos, five men and a woman died when a speedboat carrying migrants from Turkey made a sudden manoeuvre and overturned during a coast guard pursuit; fourteen men survived and an Iranian survivor was arrested on suspicion of smuggling. Shipping Minister Vassilis Kikilias said the evidence so far indicates the Lesbos boat struck the coast guard vessel, and he vowed “zero tolerance” towards the smugglers he blamed for the deaths.

Latest · Europe · Spain19 October 2026

Storm Adam Floods Eastern Spain: Two Dead and 800,000 Told to Shelter as Rain Breaks Records

Helicopters rescued residents stranded on rooftops as Storm Adam swept torrential rain across eastern Spain on Monday, killing at least two people and prompting shelter-in-place orders for more than 800,000 residents. Some areas recorded over 600mm of rain in 48 hours — the most since 1987 — as rivers burst their banks, streets turned into torrents and several Mediterranean-coast towns were cut off. The national weather agency AEMET called conditions “extremely adverse,” issued red alerts and warned people to avoid all travel and seek higher ground. Classes were suspended for more than 100,000 students in Valencia, and the government deployed military and national police emergency units — scenes reminiscent of the catastrophic flooding that killed more than 230 people in the Valencia region two years ago.

Russian Drone Strike on a Village Park Near Kharkiv Kills Six, Including a Baby in Her Pram

A Russian drone struck a park in Nova Vodolaha, near Kharkiv, where families were playing with their children, killing six people including two children — among them a baby girl, just over a year old, who died in her pram. Fifteen more people were injured, seven of them children, Ukraine’s interior ministry said. The attack comes amid growing friction between Kyiv and Washington over Ukraine’s retaliatory strikes on Russian oil facilities, which President Donald Trump says are seriously hurting world diesel markets.

Latest · Asia-Pacific · China19 October 2026

China’s Economy Grows 4.5% in the Third Quarter as an Export Surge Masks Weak Spending at Home

China’s economy expanded 4.5% year on year in the third quarter, official data showed on Monday, picking up from 4.3% in the April-to-June period and keeping Beijing on track for its annual target of 4.5% to 5%. The engine was trade: the September surplus widened more than expected, putting China on course to surpass last year’s record $1.2 trillion surplus, while industrial output rose 5.5% in September, the fastest pace in six months. Retail sales also improved, rising 1.1%, but consumer spending overall remains subdued, underlining an uneven, production-led recovery. Policymakers have held back from broad stimulus, betting that export strength, boosted by the global AI-driven technology boom, will carry growth to target.

Latest · Global Affairs · Climate19 October 2026

El Niño Now Strengthening Could Be the Strongest Since at Least 1950, UN Weather Agency Warns

The World Meteorological Organization says the current El Niño is firmly established and strengthening rapidly, with a near-100% likelihood that it will persist through February 2027 and a chance of becoming one of the strongest since at least 1950. Sea-surface temperature anomalies in the central-eastern Pacific’s Niño 3.4 region jumped from +1.6°C above normal in June to +2.5°C in August, and the seasonal average is forecast to peak at about +3.7°C in October–December — the highest since observations began, well above the previous record of +2.6°C set in late 2015. The agency warns the event will raise global heat and shift rainfall patterns, increasing drought and flood risks worldwide, with a positive Indian Ocean Dipole also shaping weather around the Indian Ocean this season.

Latest · Africa · Kenya19 October 2026

Kenyan Court Convicts Starvation-Cult Leader Paul Mackenzie of Murdering 191 Followers

A court in Mombasa has convicted self-proclaimed pastor Paul Mackenzie of murdering 191 people in the Shakahola Forest starvation cult, dismissing his claim that his followers were not forced to fast. Prosecutors told the court that Mackenzie “initiated and directed” extreme fasting on his 800-acre ranch, describing a structured hierarchy of death under which children were expected to die first, followed by teenagers, women and then men. More than 400 people died in the Shakahola tragedy after Mackenzie told followers to starve themselves in order to “meet Jesus” before the end of the world. He was arrested in 2023 after a tip-off led police to mass graves in the forest near Kenya’s southeastern coast. Mackenzie and the other convicted defendants will be sentenced on November 6.

Latest · Americas · Canada19 October 2026

Grass Fire Sweeps Through Live-Fire Training at an Alberta Base, Killing Four Canadian Soldiers

Four soldiers from the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, were killed and 39 people injured — 19 of them critically — when a fast-moving grass fire overtook troops conducting live-fire training at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta. Military officials had monitored the fire for hours and believed it was sufficiently contained for training to continue, but a sudden wind shift drove the flames about a kilometre in 36 seconds, giving soldiers less than a minute’s warning; gusts reached up to 100 km/h and the fire crossed firebreaks into surrounding countryside. The response involved 22 ground ambulances, six airplanes and two helicopters, and some of the injured face weeks or months of treatment for severe burns. Prime Minister Mark Carney called it one of the worst peacetime tragedies for Canada’s military on domestic soil and ordered flags lowered to half-staff; a military inquiry will examine the fire’s spread and whether range safety protocols should change.
