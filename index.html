Latest · Europe · Ukraine — Abu Dhabi, Day Two11 November 2026

Day Two in Abu Dhabi: Ukraine, Russia and the US Return to the Table With Territory Still the Hardest Question

Negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States return for a second day of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, the first US-mediated round since February, with no agreement yet announced. Kyiv’s team, led by Rustem Umerov with chief of staff Kyrylo Budanov among its members, is pressing for an end to hostilities, firm post-war security guarantees — including arrangements discussed in NATO-style terms — economic recovery and humanitarian steps such as prisoner releases. Washington is represented by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, and Russia by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky. Moscow has described the opening day as difficult but constructive, yet its central demand has not moved: Ukrainian withdrawal from the Donbas, including parts of Donetsk and Luhansk that Kyiv still holds. European, NATO and EU officials are consulting on the sidelines but are not in the three-way room, and neither President Volodymyr Zelensky nor President Vladimir Putin is attending. Delegates have cautioned against expecting a final settlement from this round; progress on narrower issues is seen as the realistic test.

Latest · Europe / Middle East · France — Iran Fridge Cyberattack10 November 2026

France Blames Iran’s Revolutionary Guards for a Fridge Cyberattack on 700 Supermarkets; Tehran Calls It “Baseless”

France has formally accused Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps of sabotaging supermarket refrigeration across the country last month, and Iran on Tuesday rejected the charge as baseless and hostile. Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called the 11–12 October operation an attack of unprecedented scale and aggressiveness — the first large-scale cyberattack carried out purely for sabotage on French territory — in which hackers remotely triggered abnormal defrost cycles at about 700 stores, spoiling perishable food at a cost estimated in the tens of millions of euros. Paris says its investigators established with certainty that the IRGC’s Shahid Kaveh cyber unit was responsible, citing among other evidence Farsi use and IP addresses pointing to devices in Iran; the Paris prosecutor has opened an investigation into the unit’s head and two deputies. France summoned Iran’s ambassador and says it will seek retaliatory sanctions at European level. Similar attacks hit supermarkets in Sweden, Belgium and Ireland earlier this month, though Paris has not attributed that second wave.

Latest · Europe · Bulgaria — Motorway Pile-Up10 November 2026

At Least 10 Killed and 27 Injured as About 50 Vehicles Pile Up in Fog in Southeastern Bulgaria

At least 10 people were killed on Tuesday in a mass collision involving roughly 50 vehicles on both carriageways of a foggy motorway in southeastern Bulgaria, the BTA news agency reported, citing Yambol Regional Governor Georgi Chalakov. Police, firefighters and ambulance crews converged on the scene, where photographs in local media showed cars and trucks overturned, crushed or run off the road across both sides of the highway. Chief Inspector Todor Mishinev of the Yambol regional interior directorate told Nova TV that some occupants were still trapped in their vehicles and that rescue work took priority, with procedural and investigative steps to follow once it was complete. Twenty-seven injured people were taken to regional hospitals. Authorities have not yet given a cause beyond the dense fog reported on that stretch of road.

Latest · Europe · Spain — People’s Party Manifesto10 November 2026

Ahead of the 29 November Election, Spain’s People’s Party Promises Tighter Borders and Slower Climate Timetables

Spain’s main opposition People’s Party (PP) unveiled its election manifesto on Tuesday, promising tax cuts, tighter borders, stricter conditions on development aid and a slower timetable for climate targets ahead of the 29 November election. Polls put the conservatives ahead of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s Socialists, though on current numbers the PP would likely need the far-right Vox party to govern. On immigration, the programme would ban mass regularisation of migrants, tie work-visa quotas to countries that cooperate on returns, use the armed forces in border support, ban Islamic face veils in public spaces and restrict some welfare benefits to people with lasting, verifiable ties to Spain. On climate, it would halt what it calls impossible target timetables while still naming decarbonisation a key European objective. The manifesto also pledges zero income tax for minimum-wage earners, a VAT cut on basic foods while inflation pressures persist, higher military pay and troop numbers, continued support for Ukraine, and renewed dialogue with Britain over Gibraltar.

Latest · Americas / Asia-Pacific · Canada — Carney to Meet Xi10 November 2026

Carney Says He Will Tell Xi Directly That Outsiders Must Not Meddle in Canada’s Affairs

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Tuesday that he will tell Chinese President Xi Jinping face to face that China and other countries must not interfere in Canada’s internal affairs, when the two meet on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. Carney told reporters that the value of such meetings is the chance to raise issues directly and frankly, and that non-interference is a point he will stress. The meeting comes against a difficult backdrop: in April 2025 Carney called China one of the largest foreign-interference threats facing Canada, and a 2024 inquiry heard that Canada’s spy agency had concluded China interfered in the 2019 and 2021 elections — a charge Beijing has long denied. At the same time, Ottawa is seeking closer economic ties with China to reduce dependence on the United States after years of chilly relations, and in February Beijing said it would suspend some tariffs on Canadian farm products imposed during a trade dispute.

Latest · Middle East · Energy — UAE Sells Saudi Oil Cargo10 November 2026

In a Rare Tender, the UAE Is Selling 1.7 Million Barrels of Saudi Oil From a Tanker Struck Last Month

The United Arab Emirates’ energy ministry is selling 1.7 million barrels of Saudi Arab Light crude in a rare tender, according to a document seen by Reuters — oil loaded aboard the Gem No. 2, a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile last month. The tender, issued by the Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure under the judicial supervision of the UAE Federal Public Prosecution, closed on Monday and offered delivery between 10 and 20 November by ship-to-ship transfer from inside the Gulf. The UAE said last week that an oil leak from the vessel had been stopped. The sale underlines how the US-Israeli war with Iran has disrupted normal Gulf shipping: attacks on commercial vessels have raised security risks across the region’s lanes and heightened fears of environmental damage, and on Tuesday the state oil company Adnoc separately reported one of its vessels damaged in an attack in UAE waters, with response efforts under way.

Latest · Africa · Guinea-Bissau — Rapper Killing Inquiry10 November 2026

Guinea-Bissau Opens a 60-Day Inquiry Into the Killing of a Rapper Who Criticised the Coup Leaders

Guinea-Bissau’s public prosecutor has set up a commission to investigate the murder of rapper and activist Vigário Luís Balanta, following sustained pressure from the opposition and the media for those responsible to be held to account. Balanta, who had said he feared for his life before he was killed in March, founded the social and political movement Po di Terra, which was driven underground after his death and has accused the military-led government of protecting his killers. The government, which seized power in a coup in November 2025, has condemned the killing and said it could not comment further until investigations finished. Announced on Monday, the commission has 60 days to identify suspects, establish the crimes committed and present its conclusions. Po di Terra welcomed the step on Tuesday while demanding a rigorous, transparent and independent process, warning that it will follow the inquiry closely and that it must not become a device for political image-cleaning.

Latest · Africa · Nigeria — 66,000 Flee to Niger and Benin10 November 2026

UN Refugee Agency: 66,000 People Have Fled Violence in Northern Nigeria This Year, Many of Them Children

Escalating violence in northern Nigeria has forced some 66,000 people to flee into Niger and Benin during 2026, the UN refugee agency UNHCR told a Geneva briefing on Tuesday, warning of a growing protection crisis for those arriving. In Diffa, in Niger, about 23,000 people have arrived since the start of the year, more than two-thirds of them children, some suffering from severe malnutrition. Refugees described Boko Haram attacks on settlements for displaced people inside Nigeria. Niger already hosts more than half a million refugees from Mali and Nigeria, and UNHCR says it needs $11.4 million to strengthen emergency assistance across Niger, Nigeria and Benin. The same briefing heard that drones and drought are converging on children in Sudan, where UNICEF estimates 825,000 children under five are expected to suffer severe acute malnutrition in 2026 and where, in the last week of October, not a single day passed without an armed drone striking a power station.

Latest · Americas · California — Billionaire Tax Rejected10 November 2026

California Voters Reject Proposition 40, the One-Time 5% Tax on Billionaires, by 52.8% to 47.2%

California voters have rejected Proposition 40, a ballot measure that would have imposed a one-time 5% tax on residents worth more than $1 billion, with the Associated Press calling the result for the “no” side on Monday evening, nearly a week after the 3 November vote. With about 83% of ballots counted, the measure was failing 52.8% to 47.2%. Sponsored by the healthcare workers’ union SEIU-UHW and designed with economists Gabriel Zucman and Emmanuel Saez, it would have directed most of an estimated $100 billion in revenue to healthcare, with shares for food assistance and education. It was opposed by outgoing Governor Gavin Newsom and his elected successor Xavier Becerra, and opponents — led by Google co-founder Sergey Brin — raised about $378 million against it, compared with $33.5 million for the yes campaign, making it one of the most expensive ballot fights in US history. The result came even as Democrats swept California and retook both chambers of Congress, and the measure’s sponsor says it intends to return with a billionaire tax in 2028. A companion measure, Proposition 42, which would ban certain retroactive and personal-property taxes, was leading 51.6% to 48.4% but had not been called.

Latest · Americas · US Supreme Court — Big Bend Border Wall10 November 2026

Supreme Court Lets Big Bend Border Wall Construction Resume — and Texas Pushback Crosses Party Lines

The US Supreme Court has cleared the Trump administration to resume building roads and barriers in the Big Bend region of West Texas, including inside Big Bend National Park, pausing a district judge’s order that had halted the work while a lawsuit continues. In an unsigned ruling on 9 November, the court said Congress has expressed a clear preference for expeditious border construction, which it held outweighs the aesthetic, recreational and environmental harm alleged by the challengers — a riverboat guide, environmental groups and a group preserving a historic church in Ruidosa. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice to dissent publicly. The Big Bend sector spans 517 miles, more than a quarter of the US-Mexico border, yet records the lowest apprehensions of any Southwest sector, a point Judge Kathleen Cardone emphasised when she blocked the project in October. The ruling has drawn bipartisan criticism in Texas: outgoing Republican Senator John Cornyn warned it could hurt local businesses, while Democratic Senator-elect James Talarico and Representative-elect Katy Padilla Stout, whose district includes the park, also condemned it. Border officials say construction is moving forward.

Latest · Americas · Business — Boeing Workforce Cuts10 November 2026

Boeing to Cut About 5% of Its Commercial Airplanes Workforce — Roughly 2,500 Jobs — as the 737 Ramp Slips to 2027

Boeing will reduce staffing in its Commercial Airplanes division by about 5% over the coming months, division chief Stephanie Pope told employees on Monday, as the planemaker slows its production ramp to give factories and suppliers more time to stabilise. The unit employed 50,494 people at the end of 2025, so the cut equates to roughly 2,500 positions, to be achieved through a mix of attrition and involuntary layoffs, with notices beginning in December. Pope said adjusting production schedules and reducing output had left the division overstaffed, and promised employees would be treated with dignity and respect through the process. The move follows Boeing’s late-October earnings update, in which chief executive Kelly Ortberg acknowledged the company would miss its 2026 737 production and delivery targets; the target of 47 jets a month has slipped to early 2027, with a step to 52 a month now expected only in the second half of 2027. It is Boeing’s first major workforce reduction since the 17,000-job cut announced in 2024.

Latest · Global Affairs · Culture — Booker Prize 202610 November 2026

At 81, M. John Harrison Becomes the Booker Prize’s Oldest Winner for “The End of Everything”

British author M. John Harrison has won the 2026 Booker Prize for his dystopian novel The End of Everything, becoming at 81 the oldest winner in the award’s history — two years older than Margaret Atwood was when she shared the prize in 2019. Announced at Old Billingsgate in London on Monday night, the £50,000 prize went to Harrison’s 13th novel, a 198-page work that was the shortest on the six-book shortlist and was chosen unanimously from 163 eligible submissions. Set on a near-future English coast after an unspecified calamity, it follows a scavenger living with his aunt amid the arrival of enigmatic entities called the iGhetti, and the judges — chaired by Mary Beard, with poet Raymond Antrobus, musician Jarvis Cocker, journalist Rebecca Liu and novelist Patricia Lockwood — described it as readable as a thriller, a mystery, a satire, a dystopia and a road novel at once. Beard stressed the award was not a lifetime-achievement prize. Harrison beat five finalists including former winners Marlon James and Douglas Stuart, in what has been called the first Booker win for a work of science fiction, and the first for his publisher, Serpent’s Tail.
